# hazard.license-rental.electrician.indiana

## .what

"license rental" = an unlicensed or out-of-jurisdiction electrician uses another electrician's license number to pull permits and perform work they are not authorized to do.

## .why this matters

- homeowner believes they hired a licensed professional
- permit appears legitimate (license number on file)
- actual worker may lack skills, insurance, or local code knowledge
- if defects found, licensed holder may disclaim responsibility
- homeowner left with no recourse

---

## why this happens in indiana

indiana has **no statewide electrical license**. each municipality sets its own rules:

| jurisdiction | license required? | reciprocity |
|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| indianapolis | yes (marion county) | limited |
| carmel | yes | none with indy |
| fishers | yes | none with indy |
| noblesville | yes | none with indy |

a carmel-licensed electrician cannot legally pull permits in indianapolis without an indianapolis license.

## illegal vs legal: side-by-side

### scenario: carmel business wants electrical work done in indianapolis

#### ❌ illegal: license rental

```
carmel business (no indy license)
    │
    ├── pays fee to indianapolis electrician
    │   └── "let me use your license number"
    │
    ├── carmel electrician pulls permit (using indy license #)
    ├── carmel electrician does the work
    ├── indianapolis electrician never sees the job
    │
    └── RESULT: fraud, permit violation, no valid insurance
```

| who | does what |
|-----|-----------|
| carmel electrician | pulls permit, does work, deals with customer |
| indianapolis electrician | collects fee, provides license #, absent |
| permit | shows indy license, but indy licensee not present |

#### ✅ legal: true subcontract

```
carmel business (general contractor)
    │
    ├── hires indianapolis electrician as subcontractor
    │
    ├── indianapolis electrician pulls permit (their own license)
    ├── indianapolis electrician does the work (or supervises their crew)
    ├── indianapolis electrician present for inspection
    │
    └── RESULT: valid permit, valid insurance, clear responsibility
```

| who | does what |
|-----|-----------|
| carmel business | project management, customer relationship, pays sub |
| indianapolis electrician | pulls permit, does work, takes responsibility |
| permit | shows indy license, indy licensee performs work |

#### ⚠️ gray zone: permit legitimate, supervision fake

```
indianapolis electrician pulls permit (their own license)
    │
    ├── carmel electrician does all the work
    ├── indianapolis electrician never visits site
    ├── indianapolis electrician "signs off" without inspection
    │
    └── RESULT: permit looks valid, but still problematic
```

| who | does what |
|-----|-----------|
| indianapolis electrician | pulls permit, collects fee, rubber-stamps |
| carmel electrician | does all work unsupervised |
| permit | technically valid, but responsibility abandoned |

**why this is still bad:**

| issue | consequence |
|-------|-------------|
| license law violation | most codes require "responsible charge" — not just signature |
| insurance gap | GL policy may exclude work licensee didn't control |
| civil liability | if defect found, indy licensee liable but may have no knowledge |
| regulatory risk | board can revoke license for inadequate supervision |

**how it differs from true subcontract:**

| aspect | fake supervision | true supervision |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| site visits | none or rare | regular |
| work review | rubber stamp | actual inspection |
| decision authority | carmel decides all | indy directs work |
| defect knowledge | indy unaware | indy catches issues |

this is **license rental with extra steps** — the permit paperwork is cleaner, but the substance is the same.

---

#### how to prove supervision vs rental

when disputes arise (fire, injury, failed inspection), investigators look for evidence:

**evidence of genuine supervision:**

| evidence | what it shows |
|----------|---------------|
| site visit logs | licensee was physically present |
| photos with timestamps | licensee on site during work |
| text/email threads | licensee gave technical direction |
| material purchase records | licensee ordered or approved materials |
| inspection presence | licensee met inspector on site |
| employee records | worker on licensee's payroll |
| job diary / notes | licensee documented decisions |

**evidence of rental (red flags):**

| evidence | what it shows |
|----------|---------------|
| no site visits | licensee never present |
| flat fee payment only | fee-for-permit arrangement |
| no communication records | no technical direction given |
| worker has own business | not employee of licensee |
| licensee unaware of job details | can't describe work performed |
| different insurance | worker has own GL policy (or none) |
| customer never met licensee | only dealt with unlicensed party |

**who investigates:**

| trigger | investigator |
|---------|--------------|
| permit complaint | local permit office |
| license complaint | state/local license board |
| insurance claim | insurance adjuster / SIU |
| injury lawsuit | plaintiff attorney |
| fire investigation | fire marshal, insurance |

---

#### the key difference

| aspect | license rental | fake supervision | true subcontract |
|--------|----------------|------------------|------------------|
| who pulls permit | unlicensed party (fraud) | licensed party | licensed party |
| who does work | unlicensed party | unlicensed party | licensed party or supervised crew |
| licensee involvement | absent | absent (rubber stamp) | active control |
| insurance coverage | none (policy voided) | questionable | valid |
| legal status | **illegal** | **gray / risky** | **legal** |

## the rental arrangement

| party | role |
|-------|------|
| **license holder** | provides license number, may or may not supervise |
| **unlicensed worker** | performs the work, deals with customer |
| **homeowner** | believes they hired license holder |

variations:
- **fee-for-permit**: holder charges flat fee per permit pulled
- **percentage**: holder takes cut of job revenue
- **false subcontract**: holder claims worker is employee but has no control

---

## legal status

license rental is **illegal** in most jurisdictions:

| violation | authority |
|-----------|-----------|
| fraud | permit application requires actual licensee perform work |
| misrepresentation | customer deceived about who does work |
| license law violation | most codes require licensee to supervise or perform |
| insurance fraud | GL policy covers licensee's work, not renter's |

---

## what happens when caught

| consequence | who bears it |
|-------------|--------------|
| permit revocation | license holder |
| license suspension | license holder |
| fines | both parties |
| civil liability | both parties |
| no insurance coverage | homeowner exposure |
| code violation remediation | homeowner pays |

## legitimate alternative: true subcontract

a licensed electrician **can** legally subcontract work if:

| requirement | description |
|-------------|-------------|
| genuine supervision | RLI (Responsible Licensed Individual) oversees work |
| employee relationship | worker on licensee's payroll or W-2 |
| insurance coverage | worker covered under licensee's GL policy |
| code compliance | licensee responsible for all work |

the distinction: **control and responsibility**, not just a fee arrangement.

---

## red flags for homeowners

| red flag | what it suggests |
|----------|------------------|
| different person shows up than who was interviewed | possible rental |
| "i work under [name]'s license" without explanation | rental arrangement |
| license number from different jurisdiction | out-of-area rental |
| reluctance to provide license details | evasion |
| cash-only, no contract | avoiding paper trail |

---

## verification steps

1. **check permit**: verify license number on permit matches contractor
2. **call jurisdiction**: confirm license holder is aware of the job
3. **ask for COI**: certificate of insurance should name license holder
4. **verify supervision**: ask how RLI will oversee work

---

## liability exposure

if license rental discovered after work complete:

| party | exposure |
|-------|----------|
| **homeowner** | unpermitted work classification, remediation costs |
| **license holder** | license revocation, fraud charges |
| **unlicensed worker** | criminal charges, civil liability |

homeowner has weakest position — may have no recourse against any party.

## case studies: actual license rental cases

### case 1: Jeff Tracy Inc. v. City of Pico Rivera — $5.4 million disgorgement

**what happened:**
- Jeff Tracy Inc. (dba Land Forms Construction) won a contract to build a city park in 2011
- company claimed to have a Class A license using James Nale as "Responsible Managing Employee" (RME)
- dispute arose over $518,154 in withheld damages
- city counter-sued, alleging the license was a sham

**the license rental arrangement:**
- Nale "was never an employee of Land Forms"
- received only 20% stock that could be "returned upon demand"
- received no office, computer, email, or business cards
- never reviewed project plans
- couldn't name any subcontractors
- visited the job site only **three times**
- couldn't recall "giving any direction or advice" on the project

**ruling:**
- trial court found Nale was "neither a Responsible Managing Officer, nor a Responsible Managing Employee"
- ordered Land Forms to **disgorge $5,487,370.05** — the entire contract value
- Court of Appeal affirmed: if license is sham, **no payment allowed, no offsets, no apportionment**

**lesson:** license rental doesn't just void insurance — it can void your right to be paid at all.

source: [HBB Law - Sham RMO/RME Wake-Up Call](https://www.hbblaw.com/contractor-given-a-wake-up-call-for-using-a-sham-rmorme-09-22-2015/)

---

### case 2: Vascos Excavation Group v. Gold — $111,440 award vacated

**what happened:**
- Vascos Excavation won an arbitration award of $111,440.29
- opposing party challenged, alleging the RME was not legitimate
- Vascos claimed John Matthew Welch served as RME

**the license rental arrangement:**
- company could only show Welch appeared at jobsite on "at least two occasions"
- claimed he made "administrative decisions from a home office"
- could not produce payroll records, timesheets, or employment verification

**ruling:**
- appellate court vacated the $111,440.29 arbitration award
- held that producing a license certificate is "necessary but insufficient"
- contractor bears burden of proving RME is legitimate
- without proof, the contract is **illegal** and the contractor receives zero

**lesson:** "i have a guy with a license" is not enough. you must prove genuine supervision.

source: [Last Faoro & Whitehorn - Qualifier Not Acting As Manager](https://www.lastfaoro.com/articles/california-appellate-court-holds-that-contractor-must-return-all-payments-when-qualifier-is-not-acting-as-a-manager/)

---

### case 3: $8,000 remodel — neighbor complaint triggers investigation

**what happened:**
- contractor used a friend's license for an $8,000 remodel job
- neighbor filed complaint with California CSLB (Contractors State License Board)
- investigation revealed the license rental arrangement

**outcome:**
- contractor required to **return all payments**
- disciplinary action against the license holder (friend)
- potential criminal charges under Business and Professions Code 7027.3

**lesson:** license rental can be reported by anyone — neighbor, unhappy customer, competitor. one complaint triggers full investigation.

source: [Contractor Licensing Schools - Working Under Someone Else's License](https://contractorslicensingschools.com/blog/working-under-someone-elses-license-know-the-legal-risks/)

---

### case 4: general contractor hires unlicensed sub

**what happened:**
- general contractor hired subcontractor without verifying license
- subcontractor was using someone else's license number
- arrangement was discovered

**outcome:**
- GC required to **refund payments to clients**
- exposed to lawsuits from clients
- potential license suspension for the GC

**lesson:** the person who hires the license-rental contractor shares liability. "i didn't know" is not a defense.

source: [Contractor Licensing Schools - Working Under Someone Else's License](https://contractorslicensingschools.com/blog/working-under-someone-elses-license-know-the-legal-risks/)

---

### criminal penalties: California BPC 7027.3

license rental is a **crime** in California:

| violation | penalty |
|-----------|---------|
| fraudulent use of license number | up to 3 years in prison |
| allowing your license to be used | CSLB disciplinary action |
| aiding unlicensed contractor | misdemeanor, up to 6 months jail + $5,000 fine |

statute of limitations extended to **3 years** in 2024 (SB 862), specifically to crack down on license rental.

source: [Weintraub Tobin - California Cracks Down on Contractor Licensing Violations](https://www.weintraub.com/2025/02/california-cracks-down-on-contractor-licensing-violations/)

## how license rental hurts homeowners

| scenario | what homeowner expects | what actually happens |
|----------|------------------------|----------------------|
| defective work | sue contractor, insurance pays | license holder disclaims, unlicensed worker has no insurance |
| fire/damage claim | homeowner insurance covers | insurer investigates, discovers license fraud, may deny or subrogate with no target |
| worker injury | contractor's workers comp covers | homeowner becomes statutory employer (Labor Code § 2750.5) |
| permit inspection fails | contractor fixes | "licensed" party denies involvement, homeowner stuck with unpermitted work |
| years-later defect | contractor's completed ops coverage | coverage void — licensee didn't do work |

## why license rental evades detection: the inspection gap

license rental persists because **inspectors check the work, not the worker**.

### what inspectors verify

| inspectors check | inspectors do NOT check |
|------------------|-------------------------|
| code compliance | who performed the work |
| proper materials | license holder presence |
| safety standards | supervision arrangement |
| permit matches work | employer-employee relationship |

the inspection is a code compliance review, not a supervision audit.

### license holder presence requirements

**most jurisdictions do NOT require the license holder to be present at inspection.**

| jurisdiction | license holder required at inspection? |
|--------------|----------------------------------------|
| iowa city, IA | **YES** — licensed person present at all times |
| colorado | NO — contractor OR homeowner OR representative |
| new jersey | NO — unless specific tests needed |
| washington | NO — allows virtual inspections |
| indianapolis | not specified — practice is code compliance only |

### how license rental evades the system

```
1. permit application
   └── license number collected
       └── DBNS validates license is valid
       └── DBNS does NOT verify licensee will do work

2. work performed
   └── unlicensed party does work
   └── licensed party absent
   └── no one checks

3. inspection scheduled
   └── inspector arrives
   └── checks code compliance
   └── does NOT ask "who did this work?"
   └── does NOT require licensee present

4. permit closed
   └── work appears permitted
   └── license rental undetected
```

### the detection gap

| stage | what could detect rental | why it doesn't happen |
|-------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| permit application | verify licensee commitment | too burdensome, no requirement |
| work in progress | random site inspections | no resources, not standard practice |
| inspection | require licensee present | most codes don't require it |
| after completion | audit supervision records | no mechanism, no authority |

### when license rental IS detected

license rental typically surfaces only when a problem occurs:

| trigger | how rental is discovered |
|---------|--------------------------|
| permit complaint | investigation reveals licensee never visited |
| failed inspection | "licensed" party can't explain the work |
| injury lawsuit | discovery reveals no employment relationship |
| insurance claim | investigation finds licensee wasn't in control |
| fire investigation | records show licensee absent while work was done |

by then, the damage is done.

### jurisdictions with stronger safeguards

**iowa city** requires:
> "the licensed person for electrical work must be present at all times when unlicensed individuals do the work"

this is the exception, not the rule. most jurisdictions rely on:
- permit application attestation (easily falsified)
- post-incident investigation (too late)
