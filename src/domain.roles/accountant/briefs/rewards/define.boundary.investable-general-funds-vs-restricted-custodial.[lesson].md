# define.boundary.investable-general-funds-vs-restricted-custodial

## .what

the accountant redline for cash that offsets a points-liability: the sharp line between **investable
general funds** and **restricted custodial money**.

- **investable general funds** = an issuer's **own** money that merely carries an offset liability (a
  payable). it may be invested and the yield retained. no segregation, no commingle bar. → the
  accounts-payable shape.
- **restricted custodial money** = money a person **deposited** with an issuer; the issuer holds it for them.
  it must be segregated, matched to the outstanding obligation, and not freely invested. → the
  stored-value / money-transmitter shape.

## .why

- the free-to-invest-vs-restricted question rests entirely on **which side of this one line** the cash
  sits
- the element that separates the two is a single question of **whose money is it** — a debt the issuer owes
  (its own), or funds a person deposited (theirs)
- an earned-not-deposited point puts the offset cash on the investable-general-funds side; a deposited
  balance puts it on the restricted-custodial side — this brief draws the line either way

> **not legal advice. not tax / accounting advice.** informational groundwork for counsel and a CPA to
> validate.

## the redline

```
    INVESTABLE GENERAL FUNDS             │  THE LINE  │     RESTRICTED CUSTODIAL
──────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────┼──────────────────────────────────────
 the issuer's OWN money, with an offset   │          │  a person's money that the issuer HOLDS
 payable (a debt it owes)                 │  "whose  │  for them (a deposit)
                                          │   money  │
 no funds accepted, loaded, or prefunded  │   is     │  funds accepted / loaded / prefunded
                                          │   it?"   │
 free to invest; keep the yield           │          │  restricted; segregate; match the debt
                                          │          │
 an accounts-payable reserve              │          │  a custody / stored-value balance
```

**the element that separates the sides:** *whose money is it?* where an issuer **incurred a liability** for
a contribution, the cash is its own with an offset payable → investable general funds. where a person
**deposited funds**, the issuer holds someone else's value → restricted custodial money.

---

## one side — investable general funds (a debt the issuer owes)

a debt is the debtor's own obligation, not the creditor's money held:

> "Debt is a financial liability or obligation owed by one person, the debtor, to another, the
> creditor."

**source**: [debt — Cornell LII (Wex)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/debt)

an accounts-payable is that debt in its everyday form — the company's **own** short-term debt:

> "Accounts payable is an accounting term that describes the short-term debt that a company owes to its
> suppliers or vendors for products or services received before a payment is made"

and it is booked as the company's **own** current liability, not a custody balance:

> "Accounts payable entries are a type of current liability in the business's general ledger and
> balance sheet"

**source**: [accounts payable — Cornell LII (Wex)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/accounts_payable)

cash a company sets against its own payables is its own general capital. as a matter of law that cash may
be invested and the return follows title; no rule tells a company to fence off cash for its vendors or to
hand vendors its bank interest. that legal freedom marks the own-obligation side — mirrored on the other
side, where custody strips it away.

## the other side — restricted custodial money (funds a person deposited)

flip the fact: where a person **deposits** funds, the money is theirs and the issuer is a mere custodian.
FinCEN's prepaid-access definition names this — value **paid in advance** and held:

> "Access to funds or the value of funds that have been paid in advance and can be retrieved or
> transferred at some point in the future through an electronic device or vehicle, such as a card,
> code, electronic serial number, mobile identification number, or personal identification number"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ww)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

Reg E's prepaid-account definition names the same by the **load** of funds:

> "issued on a prepaid basis in a specified amount or not issued on a prepaid basis but capable of
> being loaded with funds thereafter"

**source**: [12 CFR § 1005.2(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.2)

florida's "stored value" is the state-law name for the held customer value:

> "'Stored value' means currency or monetary value represented in digital electronic format,
> regardless of whether it is specially encrypted, and stored or capable of storage on electronic
> media in such a way as to be retrievable and transferred electronically."

and "monetary value" is the medium-of-exchange it represents:

> "'Monetary value' means a medium of exchange, other than virtual currency, regardless of whether it
> is redeemable in currency."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.103(35), (22)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.103)

once the cash is the customer's, state money-services law caps how it may be invested — the licensee
must at all times hold matched permissible investments:

> "A licensee must at all times possess permissible investments with an aggregate market value,
> calculated in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, of at least the aggregate
> face amount of all outstanding money transmissions and payment instruments issued or sold by the
> licensee or an authorized vendor in the United States."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.210(1)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.210)

and for value-denominated obligations the duty tightens to near-total segregation — hold the **same
type and amount** owed:

> "Each money transmitter that receives virtual currency, either directly or through an authorized
> vendor, for the purpose of transmitting such virtual currency from one person to another location or
> person must at all times, until the transmission obligation is completed, hold virtual currency of
> the same type and amount owed or obligated to the other location or person."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.210(2)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.210)

## the deepest cut: title vs custody

the separator is a **title** question, not a redemption-rules question. deposit-insurance law states
the custody principle exactly — held funds stay the beneficial owner's:

> ""Pass-through" deposit insurance is a method of insuring depositors whose funds are placed and held
> at an FDIC-insured bank through a third party."

> "The underlying owners, rather than the third party that maintains the account at the insured bank,
> actually own the funds."

**source**: [Your Insured Deposits — FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/brochures/insured-deposits/)

restricted custodial money is a **title-elsewhere** balance: someone else owns it, the issuer holds it,
so the state / the customer constrains it. investable general funds is a **title-here** balance:
the issuer owns it, merely owes an offset, so it may be invested. the line is a whose-title line.

federal tax law then places the yield on the owner of title — interest is the earner's own income:

> "Except as otherwise provided in this subtitle, gross income means all income from whatever source
> derived, including (but not limited to) the following items:"

with item (4):

> "Interest"

**source**: [26 U.S.C. § 61 — gross income](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/61)

so on the own-obligation side the yield sits with the title-holder; on the custodial side title sits with
the customer and the yield follows the customer. either way the yield follows title, not custody — the law
draws the line, and does not favor a side.

## a real example on each side

| side | example | verbatim mechanic | why it sits here |
|------|---------|-------------------|------------------|
| **INVESTABLE GENERAL FUNDS** | Amazon Associates accrued commission | "the rights and obligations of the parties under Sections 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, and 11 of this Agreement and as specified in the Program Policies, together with any payable but unpaid payment obligations under this Agreement, will survive the termination" | the earned commission is a **payable the issuer owes** — its own liability. amazon does not fence off cash for it; the cash is amazon's own general capital |
| **INVESTABLE GENERAL FUNDS** | ASC 606 loyalty books | "Rewards program liability is also equal to the deferred revenue." / "The revenue gets recognized when points are redeemed." | the point is booked as the issuer's **own** liability, recognized on redemption — a debt, so its offset cash is the issuer's own |
| **RESTRICTED CUSTODIAL** | Starbucks Card balance | "The dollar value that you load onto your Starbucks Card is a prepayment for the goods and services of participating stores." / "capable of being loaded with funds thereafter" | the holder **loaded their own funds** → a deposit the issuer holds → stored value, the restricted side |
| **RESTRICTED CUSTODIAL** | FL-licensed money transmitter float | "A licensee must at all times possess permissible investments with an aggregate market value… of at least the aggregate face amount of all outstanding money transmissions" | customer transmission funds must be **matched by permissible investments** — the cash is not free to invest |

**sources**:
[Amazon Associates Program Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement) ·
[financial accounting for liability from rewards programs (ASC 606) — TrueLoyal](https://www.trueloyal.com/blog/financial-accounting-for-liability-from-rewards-programs) ·
[Starbucks Card Terms & Conditions](https://www.starbucks.com/terms/manage-gift-cards/) ·
[12 CFR § 1005.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.2) ·
[Fla. Stat. § 560.210](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.210)

note the Starbucks card carries a "no cash out" trait — "the dollar value on your Starbucks
Card is nonrefundable and may not be redeemed for cash" — yet it is still **restricted custodial**,
because the holder **loaded their own funds**. the cash-out rule is a guardrail, but the **separator is
the deposit**, not the redemption path. a program can carry the same redemption limits and still hold
restricted money if a person funded the balance.

## which side of the line a design lands on

| lands on investable general funds | lands on restricted custodial |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| points issued only as **earned** for a contribution | a person may **buy, load, or deposit** value to get points |
| the offset cash is the issuer's **own** capital against a payable | a person's funds are held as a **custody balance** |
| consumers redeem to **discount / goods** only | consumers may **cash out** a promotional balance |
| cash paid only for **services earned** (a trade debt) | anyone may **transfer / sell / send** points person-to-person |

FinCEN's service definition shows the first row is what keeps a design off the regulated shape — that
shape needs an **acceptance** of another's funds, which an earned point never has:

> "acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and the
> transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another location or
> person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(i)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

no acceptance, no load → no deposit → the reserve stays investable general funds.

## the honest gap

- the redline is text-strong but application-untested for a modern points reserve; a CPA and counsel
  should confirm the specific facts.
- an earned-commission **cash-out** path is the closest an own-obligation design comes to the line. it
  stays on the own-obligation side because the payee earned the amount **for services** — a trade debt the
  issuer owes — not because a consumer deposited funds. it reads as an earned-for-service payable, distinct
  from the consumer track.
- state law is not uniform. the FL permissible-investments text is a representative money-services
  regime, not a nationwide rule; per-state confirmation is required if the deposit side is ever in play.
- a single **load / buy / deposit** feature is the classic cause of drift — it converts an offset
  payable into a held customer balance, and the whole cash reserve flips to the restricted side.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what is the accountant redline? | investable general funds vs restricted custodial money |
| the separator? | whose money is it — a debt the issuer owes, or funds a person deposited |
| what makes cash "investable general funds"? | it is the issuer's own, with an offset payable — an accounts-payable reserve |
| what makes it "restricted custodial"? | a person deposited / loaded the funds — the issuer holds someone else's value |
| a real example on each side? | Amazon accrued commission = investable; Starbucks Card = restricted custodial |
| does a no-cash-out rule alone decide it? | no — Starbucks has it and is still custodial; the **deposit** is what matters |
| where does the yield land? | on title — the owner's income (26 U.S.C. § 61) |
