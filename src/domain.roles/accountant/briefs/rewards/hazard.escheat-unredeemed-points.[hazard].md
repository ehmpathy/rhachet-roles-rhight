# hazard.escheat-unredeemed-points

## .what

unclaimed-property (escheat) law can force a holder to hand dormant balances to the state. the hazard:
**do unredeemed points escheat?**

the answer splits by redemption path:

- **earned, discount-only points** — a point given for free that redeems **only** for goods, services,
  or a discount is a **loyalty card** under the revised uniform unclaimed property act (RUUPA, 2016) and
  is **excluded from "property"** — so it generally does **not** escheat.
- **a cash-out leg** — a balance that "may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the issuer"
  falls **outside** the loyalty-card exclusion. a cash-redeemable balance can become escheatable.

same point unit; the **cash-out feature** is the escheat exposure. a discount-only leg is outside escheat;
a cash-out leg is the one within it.

## .why

- a point **earned for free** and redeemed only for a **discount** fits the RUUPA loyalty-card exclusion →
  that leg does not escheat
- a **cash-out** feature makes a slice of value **redeemable for money** → outside the exclusion →
  the escheat exposure sits on that leg
- IN and FL both adopt the loyalty exclusion but by different mechanics — a per-state check is required

## the RUUPA loyalty-card exclusion (the safe leg)

the 2016 revised uniform unclaimed property act defines a **loyalty card** by two traits — given for
free, and redeemed only for goods/services/discount — and expressly excludes the monetizable case. the
D.C. enactment states it verbatim:

> "A record given without direct monetary consideration under an award, reward, benefit, loyalty,
> incentive, rebate, or promotional program that may be used or redeemed only to obtain goods or
> services or a discount on goods or services. The term does not include a record that may be redeemed
> for money or otherwise monetized by the issuer."

and it contrasts a **stored-value card** — a record backed by consideration, redeemable for money:

> "A record evidencing a promise made for consideration by the seller or issuer of the record that
> goods, services, or money will be provided to the owner of the record to the value or amount shown in
> the record."

**source**: [D.C. Code § 41-151.02 — RUUPA definitions](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/41-151.02)

a law-firm survey confirms the exclusion is near-universal in post-2016 states, tied to the
no-payment trait:

> "Every state that has revised or updated its escheat standards since 2016 has included an express
> exemption for loyalty cards in its statute or rules."

with the exclusion available only where the holder did not pay for the card:

> "given without direct monetary consideration under an award, reward, benefit, loyalty, incentive,
> rebate, or promotional program which may be used or redeemed only to obtain goods or services or a
> discount on goods or services."

**source**: [Eversheds Sutherland — unclaimed property, gift/payroll/loyalty/stored-value cards (JD Supra)](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/unclaimed-property-what-s-new-for-gift-66136/)

a tax/compliance analysis names the factors that decide — no payment, offered free, and no cash/gift-card
convertibility:

> "The term does not include a record that may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the
> issuer."

> "Balances cannot be converted to cash, cannot be converted to gift cards and must be used towards the
> purchase of merchandise."

> "Customers did not pay for the points and no consideration was exchanged."

> "Points are offered free of charge to everyone who signs up for the program."

**source**: [Sovos — are rebates, refunds and incentives considered unclaimed property?](https://sovos.com/blog/trr/are-rebates-refunds-and-incentives-considered-unclaimed-property/)

a point issued **free** for a contribution and redeemed **only** for a discount fits the loyalty-card
exclusion → that discount leg does not escheat.

## the cash-out leg is the exposure

the same definition draws the line: the exclusion **does not** cover a record that "may be redeemed for
money or otherwise monetized by the issuer." a cash-out leg redeems a point for **money** —
which puts that leg **outside** the loyalty-card exclusion. a cash-redeemable balance is the species of
value that can become escheatable if it goes dormant and unclaimed. this mirrors the money-transmitter
fault line: the cash-redeemable leg is the one that attracts regulation, on both escheat and
money-services fronts.

federal law confirms cash-out value is a real, owned obligation — money paid for a service is the
payee's gross income, not a mere discount:

> "gross income means all income from whatever source derived, including (but not limited to) the
> following items:"

with item (4):

> "Interest"

and cash for a service is compensation, the first enumerated class of gross income. so a
cash-out balance is a genuine money obligation an owner can claim — exactly the profile a state
unclaimed-property administrator reaches for when the owner does not claim it.

**source**: [26 U.S.C. § 61 — gross income](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/61)

the escheat trigger is **dormancy**: a promptly-settled earned commission is a payable that clears before
it can become a dormant unclaimed balance; a long-dormant cash-redeemable balance is the one at risk. so a
discount-only consumer leg and a promptly-settled commission both sit outside the dormant-balance profile;
a cash-redeemable balance left to sit is within it.

## FL vs IN — same exclusion, different mechanics

both states honor the loyalty exclusion, but reach it by different statutory routes.

### Florida — the gift-certificate route (§ 501.95 + § 717.1045)

Florida's gift-certificate statute carves out loyalty/promotional certificates where the recipient pays
no separate charge:

> "may have an expiration date if it is provided to the recipient...as part of a loyalty or promotional
> program when the recipient does not pay a separate identifiable charge for the certificate"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.95](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

and Florida's unclaimed-property chapter then exempts unredeemed gift certificates / credit memos from
escheat reports, and vests the leftover value in the issuer, not the state:

> "Notwithstanding s. 717.117, an unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo as defined in s. 501.95 is
> not required to be reported as unclaimed property."

> "The consideration paid for an unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo is the property of the
> issuer of the unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo."

> "An unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo is subject only to any rights of a purchaser or owner
> thereof and is not subject to a claim made by any state acting on behalf of a purchaser or owner."

with a carve-back that a certificate described in § 501.95(2)(b) **is** reportable:

> "However, a gift certificate or credit memo described in s. 501.95(2)(b) shall be reported as unclaimed
> property. The consideration paid for such a gift certificate or credit memo is the property of the
> owner of the gift certificate or credit memo."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 717.1045 — gift certificates and similar credit items](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/717.1045)

### Indiana — the direct RUUPA property-exclusion route (§ 32-34-1.5-3)

Indiana's revised unclaimed property act excludes a loyalty card from the very definition of
"property":

> "does not include...a financial organization loyalty program, a loyalty card, [or] a gift card"

and defines the loyalty card in the RUUPA terms, with the same monetizable carve-back:

> "a record given without direct monetary consideration under an award, reward, benefit, loyalty,
> incentive, rebate, or promotional program which may be used or redeemed only to obtain goods or
> services or a discount on goods or services."

> "does not include a record that may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the issuer."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 32-34-1.5-3 — RUUPA definitions (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-32-property/in-code-sect-32-34-1-5-3/)

Kentucky reaches the identical result to Indiana by the same direct route — a loyalty card is excluded
from "property" — a useful confirmation that the RUUPA property-exclusion mechanic is settled across the
states that adopt it:

> "A record given without direct monetary consideration under an award, reward, benefit, loyalty,
> incentive, rebate, or promotional program, which may be used or redeemed only to obtain goods or
> services or a discount on goods or services"

**source**: [Ky. Rev. Stat. § 393A.010 — loyalty card excluded from "property" (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ky/title-xxxiv-descent-wills-and-administration-of-decedents-estates/ky-rev-st-sect-393a-010/)

### the divergence

| state | route to the loyalty exclusion | the escheat carve-back |
|-------|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| **FL** | gift-certificate exemption (§ 501.95 + § 717.1045) — value vests in the **issuer** | a § 501.95(2)(b) certificate **is** reportable |
| **IN** | direct RUUPA exclusion from "property" (§ 32-34-1.5-3) | a record redeemable **for money / monetized** is not a loyalty card |
| **KY** | direct RUUPA exclusion from "property" (§ 393A.010) | a record redeemable **for money / monetized** is not a loyalty card |

same destination — free, discount-only points do not escheat — reached by two statutory mechanics. in
**both**, the **cash-redeemable** slice is the carve-back that can escheat.

---

## severity

**conditional.** for the **earned, discount-only** consumer leg, severity is **low** — the loyalty-card
exclusion fits and dormant points do not escheat. for the **cash-out leg**, severity **rises** — a
dormant, unclaimed, cash-redeemable balance can escheat, and the analysis is state-specific and
fact-driven. the hazard is a function of the **cash-out** design, not the point itself.

---

## red flags

| red flag | why it raises escheat exposure |
|----------|--------------------------------|
| a consumer can **cash out** a promotional balance | the balance is "redeemed for money" → outside the loyalty-card exclusion |
| a point can be **converted to a gift card** | monetized by the issuer → not a loyalty card |
| a cash-out balance is **let sit dormant** unclaimed | a dormant money-redeemable balance is the escheatable profile |
| a person **paid** a separate charge for the point | direct monetary consideration → not a loyalty card / not the § 501.95 carve-out |

## the honest gap

- no case law was found that applies the RUUPA loyalty-card exclusion to a modern earned-point program
  with a dual discount/cash-out design; the statutory text maps cleanly, but confirm with counsel per
  state before reliance.
- the exact escheat treatment of the **cash-out** leg is fact-specific — dormancy period, owner records,
  and whether the balance is "money-redeemable" at the dormancy trigger all matter, and differ by state.
- state law is not uniform. FL routes through the gift-certificate exemption (value vests in the issuer);
  IN and KY exclude a loyalty card from "property" directly. the destination aligns for discount-only
  points, but a 50-state program needs a per-state review.
- Florida legislation was queued that may narrow the expiration carve-outs; confirm the current § 501.95
  text before reliance.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| do earned, discount-only points escheat? | generally **no** — they fit the RUUPA loyalty-card exclusion |
| what is the exposure? | the **cash-out** leg — a balance "redeemed for money or otherwise monetized" |
| why is the cash-out leg different? | it falls **outside** the loyalty-card exclusion → can escheat if dormant |
| FL mechanic? | gift-certificate exemption (§ 501.95 + § 717.1045); value vests in the issuer |
| IN mechanic? | direct RUUPA exclusion from "property" (§ 32-34-1.5-3) |
| what removes a cash-out leg from exposure? | a promptly-settled earned commission clears before dormancy; a balance left to sit does not |
| severity? | conditional — low on the discount leg, higher on a dormant cash-out balance |
