# define.points-as-liability-vs-deferred-revenue-vs-cash-balance

## .what

there are **three ways to book a reward point** on the balance sheet. two of them place the point as the
issuer's **own** obligation; one places it as **someone else's money** the issuer custodies. the books
**mirror the legal character** of the balance — a debt owed, or funds held.

- **(a) accrued liability** — the issuer's **own** debt, a payable owed from general funds (an earned but
  unpaid commission sits here).
- **(b) deferred revenue / ASC 606 contract liability** — the issuer's **own** obligation to deliver a
  future discount/service; a slice of price deferred at issuance, recognized at redemption or expiry (a
  consumer discount option sits here).
- **(c) customer cash balance held (stored value)** — a person's **deposited** funds the issuer merely
  custodies; not the issuer's money. custodial: segregation, restricted-cash disclosure, fiduciary duty.

same dollars, **opposite regulatory posture**. (a) and (b) back the company's own obligation from
unrestricted general funds; (c) is a title-elsewhere balance the state constrains. which shape attaches
turns on whether value was **earned/deferred** or **deposited**.

## .why

- the choice among the three sets the recognition time (issuance vs redemption/expiry), the disclosure
  (unrestricted vs restricted cash), and whether a fiduciary/segregation duty attaches
- a point that is **earned or deferred** (not deposited) lands in (a)/(b); a point backed by **deposited**
  funds lands in (c) — the line is whose money the balance represents
- the books do not choose the posture; a single "load / deposit money then withdraw" feature is what moves
  a balance from (a)/(b) to (c) restricted custodial money

> **not legal advice. not tax / accounting advice.** informational groundwork for counsel and a CPA to
> validate.

## the three ways to book a point

| # | book shape | whose money | on the balance sheet | recognition time | posture |
|---|------------|-------------|----------------------|------------------|---------|
| **(a)** | accrued liability (own debt) | the issuer's own | a current liability (payable) | settled when paid out | unrestricted general funds |
| **(b)** | deferred revenue / contract liability (ASC 606) | the issuer's own | a contract liability (deferred revenue) | revenue at **redemption or expiry**, deferred at **issuance** | unrestricted general funds |
| **(c)** | customer cash balance held (stored value) | the customer's | a custody balance / restricted cash | held until the customer withdraws | segregation + fiduciary duty |

a point **earned** for a contribution and redeemed as a **discount** books to shape (b); one redeemed as
an **earned commission cash-out** books to shape (a); neither is a deposit. a point backed by **loaded /
deposited** funds books to shape (c).

---

## shape (a) — accrued liability (an issuer's own debt)

an earned commission is a debt the issuer owes — an ordinary, deductible business expense. the treasury
regulation lists commissions and selling expenses among deductible business expenses:

> "Among the items included in business expenses are management expenses, commissions (but see section
> 263 and the regulations thereunder), labor, supplies, incidental repairs, operating expenses of
> automobiles used in the trade or business, traveling expenses while away from home solely in the
> pursuit of a trade or business (see § 1.162-2), advertising and other selling expenses, together with
> insurance premiums against fire, storm, theft, accident, or other similar losses in the case of a
> business, and rental for the use of business property."

**source**: [26 CFR § 1.162-1(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.162-1)

an accrued-but-unpaid commission is the debtor's **own** payment obligation. an earned commission cash-out,
once earned, is reportable to the payee on Form 1099-NEC when the conditions are met:

> "If the following four conditions are met, you must generally report a payment as NEC. You made the
> payment to someone who is not your employee. You made the payment for services in the course of your
> trade or business (including government agencies and nonprofit organizations). You made the payment to
> an individual, partnership, estate, or, in some cases, a corporation. You made payments to the payee
> of at least $2,000 during the year."

and the reportable amounts expressly include commissions:

> "Include fees, commissions, prizes and awards for services performed as a nonemployee, and other forms
> of compensation for services performed for your trade or business by an individual who is not your
> employee."

**source**: [IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)

so shape (a) is a payable the issuer owes and reports — its own liability, settled from its own general
funds. no customer money is held.

## shape (b) — deferred revenue / ASC 606 contract liability

a consumer discount option is a **material right**. under ASC 606 the option to buy a future discounted
service is a performance obligation; a slice of the price is deferred at issuance and recognized only
at redemption or expiry:

> "An option that provides a customer with free or discounted goods or services in the future might be a
> material right."

> "A portion of the transaction price should be allocated to the material right (that is, the points)."

> "Revenue is recognized when the reporting entity has satisfied its performance obligation relating to
> the points or when the points expire."

**source**: [PwC Viewpoint — Revenue guide §7.2, customer options that provide a material right](https://viewpoint.pwc.com/dt/us/en/pwc/accounting_guides/revenue_from_contrac/revenue_from_contrac_US/chapter_7_options_to_US/72customer_options_t_US.html)

the deferred slice is a **contract liability** — an obligation to **perform**, not customer cash held:

> "A contract liability is an entity's obligation to transfer goods or services to a customer (1) when
> the customer prepays consideration or (2) when the customer's consideration is due for goods and
> services that the entity will yet provide (ASC 606-10-45-2)."

and the name for that balance is deferred revenue:

> "A contract liability may be called deferred revenue, unearned revenue, or refund liability."

**source**: [presentation of contract assets and contract liabilities (ASC 606-10-45-2) — RevenueHub](https://www.revenuehub.org/article/presentation-of-contract-assets-and-contract-liabilities)

a rewards-books explainer states the equivalence plainly — the rewards liability **is** the deferred
revenue, recognized on redemption, with the unredeemed slice (breakage) recognized as revenue under ASC 606
once redemption is remote (or in proportion to rights exercised), rather than held as a customer refund:

> "Rewards program liability is also equal to the deferred revenue"

> "The revenue gets recognized when points are redeemed"

> "Breakage is the percentage of unredeemed points (either issued or expired)"

**source**: [financial books for liability from rewards programs (ASC 606) — TrueLoyal](https://www.trueloyal.com/blog/financial-accounting-for-liability-from-rewards-programs)

so shape (b) is the issuer's **own** obligation to deliver a discount, recognized at redemption or expiry —
not a pot of customer cash.

## the real-world scale of an own-obligation liability — Delta SkyMiles

Delta carries its loyalty program as **deferred revenue** — a multi-billion-dollar own-obligation, not
customer money held. the recognition rule is redemption, not issuance:

> "We defer revenue for the miles when earned and recognize loyalty travel awards in passenger revenue
> as the miles are redeemed and transportation is provided."

the year-end 2024 liability was **$8,826 million**, per the loyalty-program roll-forward (balance at
January 1 $8,420M; miles earned $4,463M; travel miles redeemed ($3,841M); non-travel miles redeemed
($216M); balance at December 31 **$8,826M**). the split is current vs noncurrent:

> "The loyalty program deferred revenue classified as a current liability represents our estimate of
> revenue expected to be recognized in the next twelve months based on projected redemptions, while the
> balance classified as a noncurrent liability represents our estimate of revenue expected to be
> recognized beyond twelve months."

and breakage — the unredeemed slice — is modeled, not refunded:

> "Our estimate of ETV is adjusted for miles that are not likely to be redeemed ("mileage breakage")."

**source**: [Delta Air Lines FY2024 Form 10-K, Note 2 (Revenue Recognition — Loyalty Program) — SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27904/000002790425000004/dal-20241231.htm)

Delta holds an **$8.8B** loyalty liability yet freely uses its cash — because a loyalty liability is
the company's own obligation (shape b), not a custody balance. that is the general treatment of an
own-obligation loyalty balance.

## shape (c) — customer cash balance held (the custodial posture)

flip the fact: where a person **deposited / loaded** money and an issuer merely custodies it, the balance is
the customer's — stored value. that is a title-elsewhere, restricted custodial balance: it must be
segregated, disclosed as restricted cash, and carries a fiduciary duty. a point that is **earned, not
purchased** — no separate identifiable charge paid — does not reach this shape. the loyalty/promotional
carve-out in Florida's gift-certificate statute names exactly this earned, no-charge case:

> "A gift certificate purchased or credit memo issued in this state may not have an expiration date,
> expiration period, or any type of postsale charge or fee."

with the loyalty/promotional carve-out for a point the recipient did **not** pay for:

> "may have an expiration date if it is provided to the recipient...as part of a loyalty or promotional
> program when the recipient does not pay a separate identifiable charge for the certificate"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.95](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

no separate charge paid → no purchased stored value → shape (c) does not attach. the balance stays on the
(a)/(b) side.

## the books mirror the legal character of the balance

| leg of the point | legal character | books shape | recognition time |
|------------------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|
| consumer **discount** | a debt redeemed as a price reduction (rebate) | (b) deferred revenue / contract liability | recognized at **redemption or expiry** |
| earned **commission cash-out** | a debt redeemed as an earned commission | (a) accrued liability (payable) | settled when **paid out** |
| a person **loads / deposits** funds | stored value / deposited money | (c) customer cash balance held | held until **withdrawn** |

the legal character of the balance (earned/deferred vs deposited) is what places the books in (a)/(b) or
(c). the books do not create the posture — they **record** the legal shape.

---

## the honest gap

- the (a) vs (b) split is fact-driven and a CPA should confirm it per leg. a reward earned by a
  third party who brought in business reads closer to a **commission accrual (a)**; a discount granted
  as part of a paying customer's own transaction reads closer to a **contract liability (b)**. both are
  own obligations — the sub-classification differs, and the specific contract mechanics decide.
- the Delta figures and policy sentences are the verbatim FY2024 10-K note; a non-airline issuer's
  scale and breakage models differ, but the own-obligation treatment is the transferable point.
- shape (c) attaches where a design lets a person **load / buy / deposit** value. an earned-only, never-cash
  consumer path keeps a balance off (c); a load/deposit feature moves it onto (c).
- this is a text-level read of the standards and regulations, strong but CPA- and counsel-confirmable;
  no guidance addresses any specific earned-point books.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| how many ways to book a point? | three — (a) accrued liability, (b) deferred revenue, (c) customer cash held |
| which are own-obligation? | (a) and (b) — the issuer's **own** obligation, unrestricted general funds |
| which is custodial? | (c) — the customer's deposited money; segregation + restricted cash + fiduciary duty |
| when is revenue recognized under (b)? | at **redemption or expiry**, deferred at issuance (ASC 606 material right) |
| a real-world (b) liability figure? | Delta SkyMiles = **$8,826M** loyalty deferred revenue at 12/31/2024 |
| where does an earned commission cash-out sit? | (a) — an accrued commission, deductible, 1099-NEC reportable |
| what puts a point into (c)? | a person **loads / deposits** funds; an earned point does not |
| what governs the choice? | the legal character of the balance; the books mirror it |
