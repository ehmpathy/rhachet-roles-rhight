# hazard.zero-tax-still-owes-a-report

## .what

in Texas, an entity whose franchise tax is **$0** still owes an **information report** — the PIR or OIR under §171.203. skip it and the entity can forfeit its right to transact business, and **each officer and director becomes personally liable** for debts the entity incurs while the forfeiture stands.

the tax and the report are separate duties under separate sections. an answer to the first leaves the second open.

⚠️ **say "information report," not "annual report."** since 2024 the two have different answers — see the carve-out below.

## .why the trap is shaped this way

```
revenue is under the threshold
  -> no tax is due
    -> so no form is owed                   🚫 THE ERROR IS HERE
```

the tax is imposed by Tax Code §171.001 and relieved by §171.002(d); the **report** is imposed by §171.203, whose opening words defeat the inference:

> "A corporation, limited liability company, limited partnership, or
> professional association on which the franchise tax is imposed, **regardless
> of whether the entity is required to pay any tax**, shall file a report with
> the comptroller…"
>
> — [Tex. Tax Code §171.203(a)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.171.htm)

the report duty was never conditioned on the tax.

### ⭐ the 2024 carve-out PROVES the point rather than defeats it

Senate Bill 3 (88th Leg., 2nd C.S.) added a zero-tax exemption effective 2024-01-01, drafted to reach exactly two of the three report sections:

> "Sec. 171.2022. EXEMPTION FROM REPORTING REQUIREMENTS. A taxable entity that
> does not owe any tax under this chapter for any period is not required to file
> a report under **Section 171.201 or 171.202**. The exemption applies only to a
> period for which no tax is due."

§171.201 (initial report) and §171.202 (annual report) were each amended the same day to open "**Except as provided by Section 171.2022**." **§171.203 was not** — its "regardless of whether the entity is required to pay any tax" stands untouched.

| section | the report | zero-tax exempt since 2024? |
|---|---|---|
| §171.201 | initial | ✅ yes |
| §171.202 | annual | ✅ yes |
| **§171.203** | **public information (PIR / OIR)** | ⛔ **no** |

a legislature that amended the neighbors the same day left this one alone — strong evidence the survival of the PIR duty is deliberate.

⚠️ **the practical effect is a NARROWER trap, not a safer one.** a zero-tax entity now owes exactly one form — the quiet one. an entity that correctly learns "no franchise tax report is due" can walk straight into §171.255 personal liability.

## ⛔ THE SANCTION REACHES THE OFFICER PERSONALLY

> "Even if you do not have to file a franchise tax report because you are at or
> below the no tax due threshold or you file a franchise tax report and pay all
> taxes due, your entity **may forfeit its right to transact business** if you
> fail to file a completed and signed PIR or OIR. The effects of forfeiture
> include the **denial of your entity's right to sue or defend in a Texas
> court, and each officer, director, partner, member or owner becomes
> personally liable for certain debts of the entity**"
>
> — [Comptroller, *Public Information Report and Ownership Information Report*](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/pir-oir-filing-req.php)

the statute behind it:

> "If the corporate privileges of a corporation are forfeited for the failure
> to file a report or pay a tax or penalty, **each director or officer of the
> corporation is liable for each debt of the corporation** that is created or
> incurred in this state after the date on which the report, tax, or penalty is
> due and before the corporate privileges are revived."
>
> — [Tex. Tax Code §171.255(a)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.171.htm)

§171.255(b) sets the measure: liability runs "in the same manner and to the same extent **as if the director or officer were a partner and the corporation were a partnership**." the liability shield — the reason the entity exists — undone by an unfiled form that carries no money.

## 🚩 REVIVAL DOES NOT UNDO IT — and this is where Texas differs from Delaware

Delaware's revival is total:

> "…the corporation shall be revived with the same force and effect **as if its
> certificate of incorporation had not been forfeited or void**… Such revival
> shall **validate all contracts, acts, matters and things** made, done and
> performed… **as if the certificate of incorporation had at all times remained
> in full force and effect**."
>
> — [8 Del. C. §312(e)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc12/index.html)

Texas says the opposite about the officer:

> "If a corporation's charter or certificate of authority and its corporate
> privileges are forfeited and revived under this chapter, **the liability
> under this section of a director or officer of the corporation is not
> affected by the revival** of the charter or certificate and the corporate
> privileges."
>
> — [Tex. Tax Code §171.255(d)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.171.htm)

| | Delaware §312(e) | Texas §171.255(d) |
|---|---|---|
| does revival reach back? | ✅ validates every act in the window | ⛔ **officer liability survives it** |
| what the cure buys you | the window is treated as though it never happened | the entity's standing, and **only** that |

a Texas lapse is **not** curable the way a Delaware void is: reinstatement restores the entity, not the officer.

## .no judicial proceeding, and a 45-day fuse

> "The forfeiture of the corporate privileges of a corporation is effected by
> the comptroller **without a judicial proceeding**."
>
> — §171.257

§171.251 forfeits if the entity "does not file… **within 45 days after the date notice of forfeiture is mailed** or provided by electronic means, a report required by this chapter."

the notice is the last warning, easy to miss at a stale address of record, and the clock is 45 days.

## .the current rules, and what changed in 2024

Senate Bill 3 (2023) reshaped this; a pre-2024 memory of the rules is wrong in both directions:

> "In July 2023, the legislature passed Senate Bill 3, which increased the No
> Tax Due Threshold and eliminated reporting requirements for certain entities."
>
> "For the 2024 report year and later, a taxable entity whose annualized total
> revenue is less than or equal to the No Tax Due Threshold **is not required to
> file a No Tax Due Report. However, the entity is required to file Form 05-102,
> Public Information Report or Form 05-167, Ownership Information Report.**"
>
> — [Comptroller, *No Tax Due Reporting for Report Year 2024 and Later*](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/ntd-rpt-updates-2024.php)

the change **removed one form and left another**:

| report year | under the threshold, you file… |
|---|---|
| through 2023 | the No Tax Due Report (Form 05-163) |
| 2024 onward | **the PIR (05-102) or the OIR (05-167)** — 05-163 is discontinued |

which one depends on entity type, per §171.203 and the Comptroller:

- **PIR (05-102)** — a corporation, LLC, limited partnership, professional association, or financial institution
- **OIR (05-167)** — every other taxable entity

both are due on the annual franchise tax report date, **May 15**.

## .the threshold

§171.002(d) relieves the tax on either of two independent grounds:

> "(d) A taxable entity is not required to pay any tax and is not considered to
> owe any tax for a period if:
> (1) the amount of tax computed for the taxable entity is **less than $1,000**; or
> (2) the amount of the taxable entity's total revenue from its entire business
> is **less than or equal to $2.47 million** or the amount determined under
> Section 171.006 per 12-month period on which margin is based."

⚠️ **$2.47 million is the STATUTORY BASE, not necessarily the operative figure.** §171.006 indexes the §171.002(d)(2) amount to CPI on January 1 of each even-numbered year, and §171.006(e) makes the Comptroller's determination "final and may not be appealed." **take the year's threshold from the Comptroller, and record which year it belongs to.**

## 🎯 the antecedent question is NEXUS, and it governs everything above

§171.001(a) imposes the tax on "each taxable entity that **does business in this state** or that is chartered or organized in this state." for an entity chartered elsewhere, the first prong is the whole question — and the Comptroller's exclusion list drops the report duty with it:

> "Entities Not Required to File a PIR or an OIR: **Entities that are not
> organized in Texas and/or do not have nexus in Texas.**"

```
1. is there nexus?          -> Franchise Tax Accountability Questionnaire
2. if yes, is tax due?      -> §171.002(d), against THAT YEAR's threshold
3. either way, file the report -> PIR or OIR, annually, May 15
```

step 3 does not depend on step 2. that is the whole hazard.

⚠️ a **business address in the state** is not one of the activities any safe harbor protects, and it is a far stronger fact than the economic-nexus tests that catch remote sellers. an entity with an in-state office should presume nexus and disprove it.

### ✅ the nexus rule — 34 TAC §3.586

two **independent tests**; an entity escapes only if it falls outside both:

| test | §3.586 | escapes when |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **economic** | (f)(1) | Texas gross receipts **under $500,000**, for any period that ends 2019 or later |
| **physical** | (d) | none of **21 listed activities** occurred |

- ⛔ **the threshold is no defense against presence.** (f)(1) grants nexus "**even if it has no physical presence**" — an *additional* basis, never a floor under subsection (d)
- subsection (d) is **functional, not formal**: entry (20) reaches "a telephone number that is **answered** in Texas"; (13) reaches a "**place of business**", not an address of record. **a Texas address used only for mail is not among the twenty-one**
- ⚠️ the list is expressly "**not limited to**", so its silence is a strong indication, never a safe harbor. an address paired with *any* activity — someone who worked there, a call answered there, a service delivered from there — lands on (5), (13), (16), or (20)

## .how to check where an entity actually stands

do not reason about it — the Comptroller publishes the answer:

- **account status lookup** — [`coas-instructions.php`](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/coas-instructions.php). the status you want is **"active right to transact business"**
- **the nexus determination** — [Franchise Tax Accountability Questionnaire](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/questionnaire.php)
- **a lapsed account** — [`current-2024.php`](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/current-2024.php)

⚠️ that status string is load-bearing beyond tax: the Secretary of State demands a screen print of it before it will cap late **registration** fees at five years. see the registrator brief below.

## ⛔ THERE IS NO STATUTE-OF-LIMITATIONS DEFENSE — the clock never started

read alone, the four-year limit suggests an old lapse aged out:

> "Sec. 111.201. ASSESSMENT LIMITATION. No tax imposed by this title may be
> assessed after **four years** from the date that the tax becomes due and
> payable."
>
> — [Tex. Tax Code §111.201](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/TX/htm/TX.111.htm)

four sections later:

> "Sec. 111.205. EXCEPTION TO ASSESSMENT LIMITATION. (a) The limitation provided
> by Section 111.201 of this code **does not apply and the comptroller may
> assess a tax imposed by this title at any time** if:
>
> (1) with intent to evade the tax, the taxpayer files a false or fraudulent
> report;
> **(2) no report for the tax has been filed;** or
> (3) information contained in the report of the tax contains a gross error."

(a)(2) is self-executing — no fraud, no intent, no finding. an entity that filed no report **is** the exception.

```
no report filed  ->  §111.205(a)(2) applies
                 ->  §111.201's four years does NOT apply
                 ->  assessable AT ANY TIME
```

limitations periods reward the taxpayer who filed and got it wrong; they offer no protection to the one who filed none. an unfiled year from a decade ago is as assessable as last year's. the only way to start the clock is to file — which is also the only way to reach the "active right to transact business" status the Secretary of State demands.

### ⭐ one administrative cap exists where the statute has none

Texas is a member of the **MTC's National Nexus Program**, which publishes a Texas look-back of **4 complete prior tax years** on income/franchise tax.

- a program term the state may decline, forfeited by a single prior contact, and withheld without notice from an applicant whose Texas nexus is already under investigation
- it does not change a word of §111.205(a)(2); it changes what a taxpayer may be able to negotiate

⛔ do not cite it as a limitations period. see `limitations/state=texas/lever.mtc-disclosure-caps-an-uncapped-window`.

> 🚩 **the same fact drives §9.052.** the Business Organizations Code civil penalty is measured by "all fees and taxes that would have been imposed… **had the entity registered when first required and filed all reports required by law**." with §111.205(a)(2) open-ended, that measure has no time floor either. see the registrator brief below.

⚠️ this section is about **assessment**, not collection. §111.202 sets a separate three-year suit limit that runs from a determination, and §111.207 tolls periods during administrative proceedings. neither rescues an unfiled year — they operate after an assessment exists.

## .enforcement

- a conclusion that "$0 tax means no filing" = **blocker**
- a §111.201 four-year defense asserted for a year in which no report was filed = **blocker** (§111.205(a)(2) — the limitation does not apply)
- "annual report" used where §171.203's information report is meant, for a period from 2024-01-01 = **blocker** (§171.2022 exempts the first, not the second)
- a franchise-tax review of an out-of-state entity that never asked the nexus question first = **blocker**
- a threshold figure quoted from §171.002(d) without the report year and a Comptroller confirmation = **blocker** (§171.006 indexes it)
- a Texas lapse treated as fully curable by revival, on a Delaware analogy = **blocker** (§171.255(d))
- an entity with an in-state business address presumed to lack nexus = **nitpick**
