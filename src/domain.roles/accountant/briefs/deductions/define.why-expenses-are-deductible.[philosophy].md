# define.why-expenses-are-deductible

## .what

the fundamental principle behind business deductions: **tax falls on profit, not on revenue.** a deduction is simply the mechanism that subtracts the cost of income earned from the income itself, so tax lands on the true economic gain.

## .the core idea: net-income taxation

the income tax targets **net income** — what a business actually earned — not **gross receipts** — everything that flowed in.

```
gross receipts
  − cost of the revenue earned   (deductions)
  ─────────────────────────────
  = net income (taxable profit)
```

if the tax hit gross receipts, a company that spent $100 to earn $101 would be taxed as if it made $101 — a penalty on the act of trade itself. deductions exist so the tax reflects the **$1 of real gain**, not the $101 of turnover.

> "There shall be allowed as a deduction all the **ordinary and necessary** expenses paid or incurred during the taxable year in carrying on any trade or business."
> — [IRC § 162(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/162)

everything about deductions flows from this one sentence.

## .why "ordinary and necessary"

the standard is deliberately broad because business is diverse. two words gate it:

| word | meaning | test |
|------|---------|------|
| **ordinary** | common and accepted in your line of work | would other businesses in your field normally incur this? |
| **necessary** | appropriate and helpful for the business | does it help the business operate or earn income? |

note "necessary" does **not** mean indispensable — just helpful and appropriate. and the cost must tie to an active, profit-seeking enterprise, not personal life.

so the fundamental question for any cost is:

> **"was this an ordinary, helpful cost of the business's income?"**

if yes → it offsets income (deductible). if it is personal, or a capital asset, or barred by policy → it does not.

## .why some costs are denied even if "business"

a handful of costs are ordinary/necessary yet still **not deductible**. these are policy overrides — the tax system refuses to *subsidize* certain conduct by letting it reduce tax:

| denial | statute | the "why" |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| illegal payments (bribes, kickbacks) | [§162(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/162) | will not subsidize crime |
| lobbying & political spend | §162(e) | will not subsidize political influence |
| fines & penalties to a government | §162(f) | will not soften the cost of a broken law |
| exec comp over $1M (public cos) | §162(m) | will not subsidize excessive pay |

the principle: **a deduction is a public subsidy of a private cost.** the government declines to subsidize illegal, political, or (for large public companies) excessive costs — so those are carved out regardless of the ordinary-and-necessary test.

## .why capital costs are different (not "denied" — deferred)

the purchase of a long-lived asset (equipment, a building, land) is **not** a cost of *this* year — it is an investment that helps earn income over *many* years. so instead of a full deduction now:

- **depreciable assets** (equipment, buildings) → the cost is spread over the asset's useful life (depreciation)
- **land** → never deducted or depreciated (it does not wear out)

this is the same net-income logic applied over time: match the cost to the years it helps earn income.

## .why documentation matters

a deduction is a **claim** that a cost was an ordinary, necessary, business cost. on audit, the burden is on the taxpayer to prove it. receipts, invoices, and statements are the evidence that turns a claim into a defensible fact.

> no record → no proof → the deduction can be disallowed even if it was legitimate.

## .the mental model

for every dollar the business spent, ask, in order:

1. **is it a business cost at all, or personal?** (personal → not deductible)
2. **is it ordinary + necessary for this business?** (no → not deductible)
3. **is it a same-year cost, or a multi-year capital asset?** (capital → depreciate, do not expense)
4. **is it barred by policy?** (illegal / political / fine / >$1M exec → denied)

pass all four → it offsets income.
