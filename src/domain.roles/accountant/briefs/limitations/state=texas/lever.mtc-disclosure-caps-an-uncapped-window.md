# lever.mtc-disclosure-caps-an-uncapped-window

## .what

Texas gives a non-filer **no statutory way back**. Tex. Tax Code §111.205(a)(2)
leaves the assessment window open "**at any time**", and ch. 111 subchapter C —
the whole of the Comptroller's settlement power — caps no look-back for a
taxpayer who never filed.

but Texas **is** a member of the Multistate Tax Commission's permanent National
Nexus Program, and that program publishes a look-back for Texas of
**4 complete prior tax years** on income/franchise tax, **48 complete prior
months** on sales/use.

so the cap exists. it is **administrative**, not statutory — a program term
rather than a right — and it is forfeited by a single prior touch.

⭐ **and the Comptroller runs its OWN program with the same cap** (found
2026-10-04, after this brief was first written — the gap row below is closed):

> "The Comptroller's office will limit its review to reports due **four years
> from the date the business initially contacts us**. However, there is no
> limit on the lookback period for taxes collected and not remitted to the
> Comptroller's office."
>
> — Comptroller Pub. 96-576, *Voluntary Disclosure Program* (r. 08/26),
> `franchise-tax/state=texas/refs/pulledAt=2026-10-04.tx-pub-96-576-vda.r0826.txt`

it carries the same forfeiture shape — a business qualifies only if *"it has not
been previously contacted by the Comptroller, either verbally or in writing,
concerning a liability or estimated liability"* — and it waives *"Statutory
penalties and interest … except interest on taxes collected and not remitted."*
⇒ two doors to one four-year cap: the MTC's (anonymous, multistate) and the
Comptroller's own (direct). the statute still offers neither.

## .what it changes

- ch. 111 has **no statutory way back**: searches for `voluntary disclos` and `before the comptroller contacts` both return null
- the MTC's permanent program moves the Texas exposure from *unbounded* to *bounded at four years, conditionally*
- ⚠️ **do not conflate two MTC instruments.** STAR 201808030L says the MTC's 2017 **limited-time** marketplace-seller initiative "does not apply to the Texas franchise tax." that letter is scoped to the amnesty. the **permanent** National Nexus Program has its own member list and covered taxes, and the chart below is its own publication

## .the grid — a statute with no cap, beside a program that has one

| instrument | what it bounds | Texas | source |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **statute** — a report was filed | assessment window | 4 years | §111.201 |
| **statute** — no report filed | assessment window | **at any time** | §111.205(a)(2) |
| **statute** — a voluntary disclosure | assessment window | ⛔ **no such provision** | two negative probes over ch. 111 |
| **program** — MVDP, income/franchise | look-back | **4 complete prior tax years** | MTC NNP look-back chart |
| **program** — MVDP, sales/use | look-back | **48 complete prior months** | MTC NNP look-back chart |
| **program** — the Comptroller's own VDA | review | **reports due four years** from first contact | Pub. 96-576 (r. 08/26) |

⚠️ the two kinds of row do not sit on the same footing. a statutory limit is a
right a taxpayer asserts. a program look-back is a term a state offers and may
decline to offer, and the chart says so in terms.

## .the operative text

### Texas is a member — and membership is the gate

> "The Commission offers services to the public and member states. **The
> Commission does not facilitate voluntary disclosures for non-member
> states.**"
>
> — [MTC, National Nexus Program member states](https://www.mtc.gov/nexus/member-states/)

the same table lists `Texas … Yes`. so the sentence above, which would
otherwise be the disqualifier, is satisfied.

### the program reaches income/franchise tax, not sales tax alone

> "A state's sales/use tax and **income/franchise tax** (including the Hawaii
> GET and Washington B&O tax) are the tax types generally subject to a
> voluntary disclosure agreement (VDA)."
>
> — [MTC, Multistate Voluntary Disclosure Program](https://www.mtc.gov/nexus/multistate-voluntary-disclosure-program/)

this is the sentence that separates the permanent program from the 2017
initiative STAR 201808030L addressed.

### the published look-back

```
   Nexus Program States   Income/franchise tax   Sales/Use Tax
                          Lookback period        Lookback period
                          (complete prior tax    (complete prior
                          years)                 months)

Texas                                4                 48
Florida                              3                 36
Delaware                             5                 n/a
```

> — MTC, *Lookback Periods for States Participating in National Nexus Program*,
> chart dated 1-1-25

the Florida row carries a note the Texas row does not: *"Lookback period
includes the 3 years immediately preceding the date of the VDA request."*
Texas's row is bare, so the chart's general definition governs it.

### what a look-back period is, per the program's own definition

> "The lookback period includes the prior complete tax filing periods for which
> a taxpayer applying for voluntary disclosure relief must generally file
> returns and pay the past-due tax liability plus interest in return for the
> state's waiver of tax liability for periods prior to the lookback period and
> penalties. The lookback period **also includes the current incomplete tax
> filing period**, the return for which must be timely filed and tax paid when
> due."

so "4 years" is four **complete prior** years **plus** the current one. it is
not four years counted back from today.

### Texas runs its own paperwork inside the program

> "3.1.5 The Texas Comptroller participates in the MVDP but **prepares its own
> voluntary disclosure agreements**, and applicants will need to adhere to its
> procedures in completing those."
>
> — MVDP Procedures, revised July 21, 2025, §3.1.5

Texas advertises no voluntary-disclosure program document (four nav walks and a
STAR search found none); its agreements are reached through the Commission.

## ⛔ the lever is spent by a single prior touch — and the list is broad

> "4.2 A taxpayer who has had **prior contact** with a participating state
> concerning a tax type is **ineligible** to participate in the MVDP with
> respect to that state and tax type. 'Prior contact' includes **at any time in
> the past registering, filing a tax return or making a tax payment** with
> respect to that tax type and state, or **receiving a contact** … from that
> state"
>
> — MVDP Procedures §4.2

and what counts as a contact **from** the state is wider than a bill:

> "12.1 'State contact' means any communication with respect to a tax type from
> state personnel to a person with respect to that person's actual or potential
> tax obligation … Examples of a state contact include but are not limited to:
> a telephone call or correspondence from a state revenue official, **a nexus
> questionnaire mailed or e-mailed to the taxpayer**, and a notice of audit or
> assessment."

### 🚩 this collides with the advice in the nexus shield brief

`nexus/state=texas/shield.no-duty-below-the-bright-line` recommends the
Comptroller's Franchise Tax Accountability Questionnaire as *"the cheapest
confirmation — the agency answering the question, in writing, for free."*

a nexus questionnaire is a **named example** of a state contact, and any reply
from Comptroller personnel about a potential tax obligation fits §12.1. so the
cheap confirmation may **forfeit** the four-year cap. the order is unsettled:

```
ask the Comptroller first   ->  a free, written, authoritative nexus answer
                            ->  but a state communication now exists
                            ->  MVDP eligibility is at risk

apply through the MTC first ->  anonymous until a VDA is signed
                            ->  the 4-year cap is preserved
                            ->  but you pay for years you may not owe
```

which path is right turns on the **nexus** question — see the decision tree in
the shield brief. if no duty ever attached, the questionnaire is the cheaper
and better move and the cap guards no exposure. if a duty did attach, the order
is reversed.

⛔ **do not send either one without a determination on nexus first.**

### 🚩 two Texas-only footnotes make this worse than the general rule

**1. Texas excludes taxpayers it is already investigating, and will not tell
you.**

> "Texas extends this policy to exclude from eligibility those whose nexus to
> the state **is being investigated** by the state but who have not yet been
> contacted. **Such persons will ordinarily not know of their ineligibility
> before they apply.**"
>
> — MVDP Procedures, footnote 2

so a Texas applicant cannot verify eligibility in advance.

**2. a contact about one tax type may be construed to reach all of them.**

> "A state contact with Texas may at the state's option be construed to be with
> respect to **any tax type**, without regard to whether that tax type is
> included in an enumeration of tax types accompanying the contact. For
> example, Texas may interpret a communication to a taxpayer that references
> only corporate franchise tax to also include sales and use tax."
>
> — MVDP Procedures, footnotes 1 and 3

so a **franchise-tax** letter can spend the **sales-tax** lever. treat any
Texas communication as one that touched every tax type until shown otherwise.

## ⚠️ a floor that may moot the whole instrument

> "NNP staff **do not process** voluntary disclosure applications when the
> good-faith estimate of tax due to a state for the lookback period is **less
> than $500**. Taxpayers with minimal tax liability should pay such liability
> upon filing an initial return directly with the state."

a taxpayer whose Texas estimate is under $500 (e.g. $0) is **below the floor**:
there is no cap left to guard, and the state's own nexus questionnaire is the
right move.

## ✅ what the program does give you

| term | text |
|---|---|
| **anonymity** | "The Commission will disclose an applicant's identity to a state **only after** the applicant has entered into a VDA … known to that state only by its voluntary disclosure **Case Number**" |
| **penalty waiver** | "the state will waive penalties (unless otherwise indicated in the agreement), back taxes owed for time periods prior to the state's lookback period, and interest on such back taxes" |
| **no fee** | "There is no charge to the taxpayer for participation in the MVDP." |
| **protection from discovery** | begins "12:01 AM (Eastern time) on the calendar day following the Commission's receipt of the electronically submitted application" |

⛔ the waiver does **not** reach tax you collected from someone else:

> "Collected but unremitted tax plus interest must be paid **without regard to
> the lookback period** and if the agreement provides, **without waiver of
> penalties**."

## ⚠️ the chart disclaims itself — read it before you rely on it

> "Each state determines its own lookback period. Lookback periods may vary
> between states. The lookback periods published in the chart below are
> provided **for guidance purposes only** … **As each state makes the final
> determination of the lookback period** following review of the taxpayer's
> application, the guidance provided in the chart below **does not constitute
> specific advice and accordingly should not be relied upon without further
> confirmation from the state(s)**."

so `Texas 4` is a published expectation, not a commitment. it is far better
than the statute's "at any time" and it is not a right.

## .enforcement

- "Texas has no way back" asserted without the MVDP qualification = **blocker**
- the MVDP look-back cited as a **statutory** limit = **blocker**
- STAR 201808030L read to answer the permanent program = **blocker**
  (it addresses the 2017 limited-time initiative)
- a Texas nexus questionnaire sent before a nexus determination, where a
  material franchise-tax duty may exist = **blocker** (it may spend the lever)
- a Texas contact treated as scoped to the tax type it names = **blocker**
  (footnotes 1 and 3)
- MVDP eligibility asserted as verified in advance for Texas = **blocker**
  (footnote 2 — the applicant "will ordinarily not know")
- the "4 years" read as four years back from today rather than four complete
  prior years plus the current period = **nitpick**

## 🚩 what is NOT established here

| gap | why it is open |
|-----|----------------|
| what the **Texas Comptroller's own** VDA terms say | ⚠️ **partly closed 2026-10-04** — Pub. 96-576 states eligibility, the four-year review, and the penalty + interest waiver (quoted under `.what`); the agreement form itself was not obtained |
| whether Texas honors the published 4-year look-back in practice | the chart disclaims itself; only the state's response to an application settles it |
| the **full** delta between the 2022 and 2025 procedure editions | every provision this brief quotes was checked and is unchanged; a normalized whole-document comparison was not run |
| whether a **submitted** Comptroller questionnaire triggers §12.1 | §12.1 defines a contact as a communication **from** state personnel; whether a reply to a taxpayer-initiated inquiry counts was not determined |
| whether Texas has an **independent** VD program outside the MVDP | ✅ **closed 2026-10-04** — it does: Comptroller Pub. 96-576 (r. 08/26), quoted under `.what`. the earlier *"four nav walks and a STAR search found no such Texas program document"* was a miss of the search, not an absence |
| whether the $500 floor is per state or per application | the sentence reads "tax due **to a state**"; not confirmed against the application form |
| whether Texas would waive **interest** as well as penalties | the program waives penalties and pre-look-back tax and interest; interest **inside** the look-back is due "unless expressly waived by the state" |
