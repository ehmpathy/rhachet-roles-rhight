# lever.disclose-first-to-cap-the-lookback

## .what

in Florida, a taxpayer who never filed a return faces an assessment window
that is **open-ended**. one move closes it to **3 years** — a written
disclosure to the Department of Revenue, made **before** the Department makes
contact.

it is a **race**, not a preference. the lever is spent the moment DOR reaches
out first.

## .why it is a trap

the naive read is "old years age out." §95.091(3)(a) says the opposite for a
non-filer, then, in the **same sentence**, hands the limit back to anyone who
discloses first. quote either half alone and the answer is wrong.

## .the grid — four clocks, not one

| clock | what it bounds | the period | source |
|-------|----------------|-----------:|--------|
| **assessment** — a return was filed | how far back DOR may assess | **3 years** | §95.091(3)(a)1 |
| **assessment** — no return filed | how far back DOR may assess | **at any time** | §95.091(3)(a)5 |
| **assessment** — no return, **but disclosed first** | how far back DOR may assess | **3 years** | §95.091(3)(a)5 |
| **collection** — the lien | how long DOR may pursue what it assessed | **20 years** | §95.091(1)(b) |

⚠️ the last row is the one that gets skipped. **assessment and collection are
separate clocks**, and to close the first does not close the second.

## .the operative text

### the default 3 years

> "1.a. … for taxes due on or after July 1, 1999, within **3 years** after the
> date the tax is due, any return with respect to the tax is due, or such
> return is filed, **whichever occurs later**"
>
> — [Fla. Stat. §95.091(3)(a)1](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/95.091)

⚠️ *"whichever occurs later"* — the clock starts at the **latest** of three
dates, not the earliest. a late-filed return restarts it from that date.

### the non-filer exception, and the escape inside it

> "5. **At any time** after the taxpayer failed to make any required payment
> of the tax, **failed to file a required return**, or filed a fraudulent
> return, **except that** for taxes due on or after July 1, 1999, the
> limitation prescribed in subparagraph 1. applies **if the taxpayer disclosed
> in writing the tax liability to the department before the department
> contacts the taxpayer**"

read the shape carefully:

```
no return filed
  -> assessable AT ANY TIME                      the default
    -> UNLESS disclosed in writing first
      -> the 3-year limit of subparagraph 1 APPLIES
```

⭐ the verb is **"applies"** — a **statutory right**, not agency grace. see the
comparison below.

### the collection clock runs 20 years

> "(b) Any tax lien granted by law to the state … for any tax enumerated in
> s. 72.011 … expires **20 years** after the last date the tax may be
> assessed, after the tax becomes delinquent, or after the filing of a tax
> warrant, **whichever is later**."

⚠️ a hazard that compounds: the 20 years runs from *"the last date the tax may
be assessed."* where no return was filed, that date is **unbounded** under
(3)(a)5 — so the collection clock has **no fixed start** either. the lever
fixes both, because it fixes the assessment date.

### the clock pauses on a protest

> "(4) If administrative or judicial proceedings for review of the tax
> assessment or collection are initiated by a taxpayer within the period of
> limitation … the running of the period is **tolled** during the pendency of
> the proceeding."

so a protest pauses the clock. do not treat an open protest as time served.

## 🎯 TWO independent routes to 3 years — and they are NOT the same strength

Florida offers the relief twice, in two chapters, and the difference matters.

| | §95.091(3)(a)5 | §213.21(7)(a) |
|---|---|---|
| what it is | a **limitation on assessment** | a **power to compromise** |
| the verb | the limit **"applies"** | the department **"may settle and compromise"** |
| discretion | ⭐ **none** — it is a statutory bar | ⚠️ **discretionary** |
| trigger | disclosed **in writing** before contact | before the department "has contacted or informed" you |
| the window | 3 years, per subparagraph 1 | "the 3 years immediately preceding" first contact |

> "(7)(a) When a taxpayer voluntarily self-discloses a liability for tax to the
> department, the department **may settle and compromise** the tax and interest
> due … to those amounts due for the **3 years immediately preceding** the date
> that the taxpayer initially contacted the department … **A voluntary
> self-disclosure does not occur if the department has contacted or informed
> the taxpayer** that the department is inquiring into the taxpayer's liability
> for tax or whether the taxpayer is subject to tax in this state."
>
> — [Fla. Stat. §213.21(7)(a)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/213.21)

**the practical read:** apply through the program (§213.21) for the penalty
relief and the assistance, but understand that the 3-year cap you actually
stand on is **§95.091(3)(a)5**, which no one has discretion to withhold.

🚩 **inferred, not tested.** that §95.091 protects you even if DOR declines a
§213.21 compromise is a read of "applies" against "may," with no case or DOR
determination behind it. treat it as the stronger argument, not a settled one.

## 💰 the penalty relief is separate — and it is the program's real offer

> "(6) The Department of Revenue may modify the reporting or filing periods …
> for purposes of facilitating the calculation of penalty and interest due on
> tax payments made as a result of a taxpayer's **voluntary self-disclosure**
> … **Interest or penalty calculations may not be based on a filing period
> longer than 1 year.** Modified reporting periods apply only to taxpayers
> **not previously registered** for the specific tax disclosed and to
> registered taxpayers with annual gross receipts of **less than $500,000**."
>
> — §213.21(6)

and from the Department's own program page:

> "When the tax and interest liabilities have been paid, **all penalties will
> be waived** unless tax has been collected and not remitted. In those
> instances, a 5% penalty will be imposed, per Rule 12-13.0075(8), F.A.C.,
> unless reasonable cause is provided."

⚠️ **interest is NOT waived.** the waiver is penalty-only, in both the statute
and the brochure. budget for interest on every disclosed year.

### ✅ the rule behind it, pulled — Rule 12-13.0075, F.A.C. (eff. 2022-05-23)

the program page and the brochure name this rule as co-authority. it makes the
waiver **mandatory**:

> "(7) Voluntary Self-Disclosure of Liability.
> (a) When payment of delinquent tax and interest results from a voluntary,
> written self-disclosure, the Department **will compromise all penalties**.
> (b) If the taxpayer has **collected, but failed to remit** tax, the
> Executive Director or the Executive Director's designee has the authority to
> compromise penalties to **no less than 5%** of the delinquent tax, absent
> extenuating circumstances."

- *"will compromise"* is an obligation; §213.21(7)(a) says the department *"may settle and compromise"* the **tax**. so the **penalty** relief is firmer than the tax look-back relief
- (7)(b) is a **floor, not a rate**: "no less than 5%" caps the relief, not the penalty — the Department may compromise to 5%, or grant less relief

### ⛔ (11) — the hard bar on the whole compromise regime

> "(11) Subsections (2) through (10) are intended to provide examples and
> guidance … but should not be construed to limit the compromise of penalties
> to only those circumstances described in such subsections. **However, no
> compromise is authorized in situations involving fraud, willful negligence,
> or willful neglect on the part of the taxpayer.**"

two halves, both material:

| half | effect |
|------|--------|
| "examples and guidance … not … limit" | ✅ the list is **not exhaustive**; a fact pattern outside (2)–(10) may still be compromised |
| "no compromise is authorized" for fraud / willful neglect | ⛔ a **hard bar**, and it reaches the whole rule |

⚠️ this bar has **no counterpart in §95.091(3)(a)5**. so a taxpayer barred
from penalty relief by (11) may still hold the statutory 3-year assessment
cap — a second, independent reason the §95.091 backstop matters.

### ✅ and (11) does NOT reach a voluntary discloser — Rule 12-13.007(9)

the worry: a taxpayer who skipped years is, on that fact alone, guilty of
"willful neglect," which would revoke the waiver from the person the program is
for. neither §213.21 nor 12-13.0075 defines the term; the grounds rule one
section over answers it in the taxpayer's favor:

> "**(9) Reasonable cause shall be PRESUMED to exist whenever a taxpayer
> voluntarily self-discloses liability for tax, interest, or penalty by
> contacting the Department in writing to disclose and pay tax and interest
> due prior to any contact by the Department concerning such liability.**"
>
> — Rule 12-13.007(9), F.A.C., eff. 2022-05-23

⭐ **a presumption, not a factor.** and it interlocks with the statute's own
words. §213.21(3)(a) makes the test **conjunctive**:

> "…due to **reasonable cause AND NOT to** willful negligence, willful
> neglect, or fraud"

reasonable cause and willful neglect are the two poles of one determination, so
a presumption of the first is a presumption against the second. **(9) resolves
the (11) bar** for a voluntary discloser.

### the standard is softer than it first reads

> "(1) … The standard used in this determination is whether the taxpayer
> exercised **ordinary care and prudence** and was nevertheless **unable to
> comply**."

read alone, "unable to comply" is severe. three later subsections pull it
back, and each is independently useful:

| subsection | what it allows |
|---|---|
| **(2)** | reasonable cause may exist "**even though** … **slight negligence, inadvertence, mistake, or error** resulted in noncompliance" |
| ⭐ **(3)** | "**Ignorance of the law** or an erroneous belief as to the need to comply … **constitutes reasonable cause**" where ordinary care was exercised — and it expressly names "limited experience in **Florida tax matters**" |
| **(6)** | reliance on another person "to comply with filing requirements … or to properly prepare returns" |
| **(14)** | (3)–(13) "are intended to provide **examples and guidance** … Penalty **may be compromised whenever the facts and circumstances demonstrate reasonable cause**" |

(3) inverts *ignorantia juris non excusat*: ignorance of a revenue law is an
**enumerated ground**, subject to the ordinary-care test. an out-of-state
company that did not know Florida reached it fits (3), not fraud.

### a skipped return is never named as willful neglect

a search of all of 12-13.007 for `failure to file` and `did not file` returned
**null** for both. an unfiled return appears only at (6), as a ground **in the
taxpayer's favor**. an absence is weaker than a statement — DOR still decides
on "the facts and circumstances of the specific case" — but with the (9)
presumption, the feared read has no textual support in the operative rule.

### ⛔ the bar is scoped to PENALTIES — verified by enumeration

the willful-neglect condition, counted end to end in both texts:

| text | occurrences | where |
|---|---:|---|
| Fla. Stat. §213.21 | **1** | (3)(a) — the sentence about **penalties** |
| Rule 12-13.0075 | **1** | (11) |

and (11)'s own antecedent is "**Subsections (2) through (10)**" — which
**excludes (1), "Tax and Interest."** so on the rule's own terms the bar
governs the penalty subsections and not the tax-and-interest one. §213.21
agrees: its first sentence compromises tax and interest on "**doubt as to
liability for or collectibility**", a separate ground that carries no
willful-neglect condition at all.

⭐ so the bar cannot reach §95.091(3)(a)5, which is a statute of limitations
rather than a compromise, and does not reach the tax compromise either.

### ⚖️ and a DOR determination of willful neglect is not final

> "The facts and circumstances are subject to **de novo review** to determine
> the existence of reasonable cause in any administrative proceeding or
> judicial action challenging an assessment of penalty…"
>
> — Fla. Stat. §213.21(3)(a)

no deference. an ALJ or court re-decides reasonable cause from scratch.

the same sentence adds two more:

- a **hard safe harbor** — "a taxpayer who establishes reasonable reliance on the **written advice issued by the department** to the taxpayer **will be deemed** to have shown reasonable cause." mirrored at 12-13.007(5)
- a **mandatory tier** — penalties "**in excess of 25 percent of the tax shall be** settled or compromised" on the same test. so 12-13.0075(7)(a)'s "will compromise all penalties" sits **above** a statutory floor

### 🚩 the brochure's subsection cite does not match the current rule

- the brochure (R. 03/24) attributes the 5% to **`12-13.0075(8)`**; in the current edition (8) is "Self Audits/Self-Analysis of Books" and the voluntary-disclosure 5% sits at **(7)(b)**
- the answer is unchanged: `(7)(b)` and `(8)(b)` are **verbatim identical** in the pulled text
- not established: whether the brochure mis-cites or tracks the **2013 edition**. the earlier editions (IDs `13676920`, `1260629`) were **not** opened

## ⛔ the eligibility carve-out that can disqualify you

> "Anyone is eligible who has any tax liability for a tax or fee administered
> by the Department and **who has not been previously contacted by the
> Department concerning the liability**. Disclosures relating to delinquencies
> or deficiencies **that are obvious and would routinely generate a billing,
> if not otherwise self-disclosed, are not eligible** for the program."
>
> — [Voluntary Disclosure Program brochure GT-800053, R. 03/24](https://floridarevenue.com/Forms_library/current/brochure/gt800053.pdf)

the second sentence is the trap:

| the taxpayer | would DOR "routinely generate a billing"? | likely eligible? |
|---|---|---|
| **registered** for the tax, simply did not file | ✅ yes — DOR knows the account exists and expects a return | ⚠️ **doubtful** |
| **never registered**, out-of-state, unknown to DOR | 🚫 no — DOR has no account to bill | ✅ **the intended case** |

### ✅ the rule draws the line — and narrower than the brochure

the second sentence of the subsection that grants the presumption:

> "The presumption **does not apply** when the taxpayer **is registered with
> the Department or has routinely filed returns** with the Department **and**
> the taxpayer's self-disclosure relates to a delinquency or deficiency
> **that is obvious and would routinely generate a billing** if not otherwise
> self-disclosed."
>
> — Rule 12-13.007(9), second sentence

⭐ **the test is CONJUNCTIVE:**

```
       ( registered  OR  has routinely filed returns )
  AND  ( obvious     AND would routinely generate a billing )
```

**both** sides must hold. so mere registration does **not** forfeit the
presumption — the delinquency must *also* be the obvious, auto-billed kind. a
registered taxpayer with a liability DOR would never have billed on its own
keeps the presumption on the rule's own words.

loss of the presumption is not loss of the program; the brochure and the rule
state different consequences:

| source | consequence of the carve-out |
|---|---|
| brochure GT-800053 | such disclosures "**are not eligible** for the program" |
| Rule 12-13.007(9) | "the **presumption does not apply**" |

the rule's consequence is the **narrower** one: a taxpayer outside the
presumption falls back to proof of reasonable cause, which (14) preserves —
"Penalty may be compromised **whenever the facts and circumstances demonstrate
reasonable cause**."

### 🚨 DOR's own materials state the carve-out THREE ways

DOR's live voluntary-disclosure page (pulled 2026-08-31):

> "Disclosures relating to delinquencies or deficiencies **that would routinely
> generate a bill** if not otherwise self-disclosed **are not eligible** for
> the program."
>
> — [DOR, *Voluntary Disclosure of Tax Liabilities*](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/compliance/Pages/voluntary_disclosure.aspx)

side by side, the conditions **drop away one at a time**:

| source | the test it states | conditions |
|---|---|---:|
| **Rule 12-13.007(9)** | ( registered **OR** routinely filed ) **AND** ( obvious **AND** routine-bill ) | **3** |
| brochure GT-800053 | obvious **AND** routine-bill | **2** |
| **the web page** | routine-bill | **1** |

the tests genuinely differ: the most-read statement (the web page) is the
broadest, the operative rule the narrowest. a never-registered, never-filed
taxpayer is **outside** the rule's carve-out (its first conjunct fails) yet
could be read **into** the web page's.

### ✅ the never-registered posture satisfies all three

the term common to all three is *"would routinely generate a bill,"* and it
cannot be met by an entity DOR has never heard of:

```
a bill is generated from an ACCOUNT.
no registration -> no account -> no return expected -> no bill to generate.
```

- confirm the posture with a registry probe, never assume it — see `../../registration/howto.check-a-registration-before-you-disclose.md`
- this argument rests on the plain words of every version; no DOR determination construes the phrase, so it is untested
- 🚩 not established: which version controls at *intake*. the rule is the operative law; a screen clerk reads the web page. the question bites only for a taxpayer **already inside** DOR's system

## ⚠️ the trap that forfeits the lever by accident

> "A completed Florida Business Tax Application (Form DR-1), if you need to
> register for sales and use tax. **Do not register for sales and use tax
> online if you are applying for voluntary disclosure.**"

an online registration is **contact**: do it first and you may hand DOR the
notice that ends the race. the disclosure goes first; the registration rides
*with* it, on paper.

## .how to apply — the eight required items

> "You must apply by **written request**. Your request must include the
> following information:
>   • A statement that you have **not been previously contacted** by the
>     Department about the tax liability you are disclosing.
>   • The **tax type and applied period(s)** being disclosed.
>   • A statement as to what amount, if any, of tax was **collected and not
>     remitted**.
>   • The **city, county, and state** of the taxpayer (for sales and use tax).
>   • **Type of taxable activities** or transactions (for sales and use tax).
>   • Your sales and use tax certificate number and **Business Partner
>     number**, if applicable.
>   • A completed **Florida Business Tax Application (Form DR-1)**, if you
>     need to register for sales and use tax. …
>   • **Any other facts** regarding the disclosure."

that last sentence is the Department's own confirmation that the unlimited

## .which taxes are covered

corporate income tax is **named explicitly**, alongside sales and use,
discretionary sales surtax, documentary stamp, communications services,
insurance premium, gross receipts, fuel, reemployment, solid waste, and
dry-cleaning gross receipts — "any tax administered by the Florida Department
of Revenue," per §213.21(7)(a) and the brochure.

## 🧭 the order of work

```
0. STOP. has DOR contacted you about this liability?
      yes -> the lever is SPENT. the program is closed and §95.091(3)(a)5
             no longer helps. go straight to the merits
      no  -> the clock is live; every day is a day of risk

1. do NOT register online. do NOT file a return yet.
      either act is CONTACT and can forfeit the cap

2. draft the written disclosure — the eight items above

3. send it, and KEEP THE POSTMARK. the look-back is measured from it

4. file the returns and pay tax + interest for the 3 disclosed years

5. penalties are waived on payment (5% if tax was collected and not remitted)
```

step 0 is the only step that can invalidate every step after it.

## 🌎 the contrast that makes this state-specific

do not carry one state's answer across the border:

| | Texas | Florida |
|---|---|---|
| default assessment limit | 4 years — Tex. Tax Code §111.201 | 3 years — Fla. Stat. §95.091(3)(a)1 |
| no return filed | **at any time** — §111.205(a)(2) | **at any time** — §95.091(3)(a)5 |
| a statutory way back to the limit | ⛔ **none found in the container** | ✅ **written disclosure before contact** |

both states open the window for a non-filer; only Florida writes the door back
in. **never assume a state has a statutory VD benefit** — Florida's is unusual
because it sits in the limitations statute, not only in agency policy.

## .enforcement

- "the old Florida years aged out" asserted without a check of §95.091(3)(a)5
  = **blocker**
- a Florida VD approached **after** DOR contact, treated as still available
  = **blocker**
- an **online registration** filed before the written disclosure = **blocker**
- interest treated as waived because "penalties are waived" = **blocker**
- the 20-year §95.091(1)(b) collection clock omitted from an exposure estimate
  = **blocker**
- a §213.21(7) compromise relied on as if it were non-discretionary, with no
  §95.091 backstop named = **nitpick**
- any multi-year tax, penalty, or interest figure computed by hand = **blocker**
  (`rule.forbid.hand-computed-figures`)
