	
Hawaii General Excise Tax (GET)
The General Excise Tax (GET) is Hawaii’s tax on business activity. It is imposed on the gross income or gross receipts of virtually all business activity conducted in Hawaii, including sales of goods, services, rents, commissions, construction, and other business transactions. Unlike a traditional sales tax, GET is levied on the business rather than the customer, though many businesses choose to pass the cost on to customers.

What is Taxed & GET Rates

GET applies to the gross income or gross receipts from all business activities conducted in Hawaii. This includes (but is not limited to):

Sales of goods and services
Services and professional fees
Rental income from property
Commissions and fees
Construction contracts
Business interest and other income earned

All gross receipts from these activities are subject to GET unless a specific exemption applies.

Gross income is the total of all business income before deducting business expenses.

  GET is NOT a sales tax.

A sales tax is a tax on the customer, which businesses must collect. GET is a tax on the business itself. Businesses may choose to pass the GET on to customers, but they are not required to do so.

If GET is passed on to customers, it must be included in gross receipts.

Hawaii’s GET Rates Vary by Type of Activity
Activity Type	Tax Rate
Selling retail goods and services, renting, leasing real property, construction contracting, earning commissions, all others	4.5%*
Wholesaling goods, producing, providing wholesale services, business activities of disabled people	0.50%
Insurance Commissions	0.15%

*This 4.5% rate includes a 0.5% county surcharge. The county surcharge does not apply to activities taxed at the 0.5% rate (for example selling wholesale goods) or insurance commissions.

Exemptions, Deductions, and Exclusions

All gross receipts from these activities are subject to GET unless a specific exemption applies. Certain types of income may be exempt or subject to deduction under Hawaii law.

Registration Requirements
An individual, business, or other legal entity must register for a GET license if they are: engaging in business activities in Hawaii, have physical nexus, or meet/exceed the economic nexus threshold.
Engaging in Business Activities

Examples of taxable activities include:

Sales of goods and services
Services and professional fees
Rental income from property
Commissions and fees
Construction contracts
Business interest and other income earned
Physical Nexus

Nexus can be established through physical presence such as having an office,  employees or representatives, inventory or other property in Hawaii, or providing services in Hawaii such as installation,  training, maintenance, or repair services.  Once nexus is established, then all your transactions in Hawaii are subject to Hawaii taxes.

Economic Nexus Threshold

Out-of-state or remote businesses that engage with Hawaii customers are subject to the economic nexus threshold. If the business has gross income or receipts of $100,000 or more, or has 200 or more transactions in the current or preceding calendar year, economic nexus is established.

How to Register for a GET License

1. Complete the State of Hawaii Basic Business Application (Form BB-1).

2. Pay the one-time $20 license fee.

3. Submit the application in person, by mail to the Department of Taxation, or online via Hawaii Tax Online (recommended).

Processing times:

In person submission is immediate issuance
Hawaii Tax Online takes 5-7 business days
By mail takes 4-6 weeks

Once issued, your GET license and associated Hawaii Tax ID remain active until you close or surrender them, or until they are revoked due to abandonment.

Display your GET license at EACH place of business

If you have multiple business locations or more than one business name, such as a trade name or doing business as (DBA) name, you must get an additional branch license for each location.

To apply for a branch license, use Form G-50. There is no additional charge to obtain a branch license.

Filing Requirements

GET returns are filed periodically and annually. Periodic General Excise/Use Tax Return (Form G-45) is filed semiannually, quarterly, or monthly depending on expected annual GET liability. Annual Return & Reconciliation of General Excise/Use Tax Return (Form G-49) is filed yearly to reconcile gross receipts, exemptions, and taxes for the taxable year.

Taxpayers must file a return even if they have no income (also known as a “zero return”).

Ways to Submit/File Returns
Online filing via Hawaii Tax Online
Paper filing by mail

Certain taxpayers are required to file electronically (e-file), particularly those with higher annual liabilities. Penalties may apply for failing to e-file when required.

Periodic Returns – File Form G-45

Deadline to file Form G-45 is 20 days after the tax period ends. If the deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, it will be due on the next business day.

If Expected Annual GET Liability ≤ $2000, then file Semi-Annually

A semi-annual tax period covers 6 months. For example, a semi-annual return for a January to June period is due July 20th.

Taxpayers may submit a paper return or file online at Hawaii Tax Online (recommended).

If Expected Annual GET Liability ≤ $4000, then file Quarterly

A quarterly tax period covers 3 months. For example, a quarterly return for a January to March period is due April 20th.

Taxpayers may submit a paper return or file online at Hawaii Tax Online (recommended).

If Expected Annual GET Liability > $4000, then file Monthly

A monthly tax period covers 1 month. For example, a monthly return for January is due February 20th.

Taxpayers are REQUIRED to file monthly returns online at Hawaii Tax Online.

Annual Returns – File Form G-49

All taxpayers must file form G-49 annually.

Deadline to file Form G-49 is the 20th day of the fourth month following your tax year end. If the deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, it will be due on the next business day. For example, calendar year (January-December) filers must file by April 20th.

Payment Requirements

GET payments are due on the same dates as the corresponding return (periodic or annual). Taxes must be paid with the return by the due date. Taxpayers required to file electronically under Hawaii law must also make electronic payments if applicable.

Ways to Submit Payment
Electronically through Hawaii Tax Online (ACH and other electronic payment options available)
By mail with a check or money order
In person at state tax office locations (cash or check payments are accepted only when paying in person)

Follow instructions on the return or in the Hawaii Tax Online portal. Prompt payment by the due date helps avoid penalties and interest.

Penalties & Interest Rates

If a GET return or payment is late:

Late filing penalty: 5% of unpaid tax per month (or part of a month), up to a maximum of 25%
Late payment penalty: A 20% penalty may be applied if the tax on a timely filed return is not paid within 60 days after the due date
Interest: 2/3 of 1% per month on unpaid tax and penalties, starting after the due date

Additional penalties may apply, such as:

2% penalty for failing to e-file or e-pay when required
10% penalty for failing to properly complete Part V of the return
