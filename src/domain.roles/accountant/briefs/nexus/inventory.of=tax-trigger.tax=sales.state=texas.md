# inventory.of=tax-trigger.tax=sales.state=texas

## .answer

**yes, SaaS is taxable** in Texas — as a data-process service, on **80%** of the charge. a seller must register and collect if it has a Texas **place where business is conducted**, or if its **total Texas revenue** (taxable and nontaxable) in the **prior twelve calendar months** reaches **$500,000** — then permit + collection by the **first day of the fourth month** after the month it crossed.

## .is the product taxable

> "“Taxable services” means: … (10) information services; … (12) data processing services;"
>
> — Tex. Tax Code §151.0101(a)

> "(6) Internet hosting, as defined by Tax Code, §151.108 (Internet Hosting), is a taxable data processing service when the user stores data on the service provider's hardware, or processes data on software that is owned, licensed, or leased by the user or provider."
>
> — 34 TAC §3.330(a)

> "There is exempted from the taxes imposed by this chapter 20 percent of the value of information services and data processing services."
>
> — Tex. Tax Code §151.351

⚠️ §3.330 also carves out *"the display of a classified advertisement, banner advertisement, vertical advertisement, or link on an Internet website owned by another person"* — an ad product may not be data processing.

## .the trigger

> "(1) maintains, occupies, or uses in this state permanently, temporarily, directly, or indirectly or through a subsidiary or agent by whatever name, an office, distribution center, sales or sample room or place, warehouse, storage place, or any other physical location where business is conducted;"
>
> — Tex. Tax Code §151.107(a)(1)

> "The comptroller will not enforce the permit requirement of this subsection or the collection obligation of subsection (d) of this section on a remote seller whose total Texas revenue in the preceding twelve calendar months is less than $500,000."
>
> — 34 TAC §3.286(b)(2)(B)(i)

> "Your total Texas revenue is based on gross revenue from taxable and nontaxable sales of tangible personal property and services into Texas."
>
> — Comptroller, *Remote Sellers*

## .thresholds

| figure | which sales count | period | effective |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$500,000** total Texas revenue | all sales into Texas, taxable **and** nontaxable, resale and exempt sales too | the prior twelve calendar months, rolled monthly | rule text: STAR 202001001R (2020-01-01) |
| no transaction count | — | — | — |

## .when collection must start

> "No later than the first day of the fourth month after the month in which a remote seller exceeds the safe harbor amount in subparagraph (B) of this paragraph, the remote seller shall obtain a permit and begin collecting use tax."
>
> — 34 TAC §3.286(b)(2)(E)

register via the Comptroller's *Texas Online Tax Registration Application* or Form AP-201 (both named on the *Remote Sellers* page).

## .what does NOT trigger it

- under $500,000 of Texas revenue in the prior twelve calendar months, with no Texas place of business
- servers alone: *"A person whose only activity in this state is conducted as a user of Internet hosting is not engaged in business in this state."* (§151.108(b))
- sales only through a certified marketplace: such a seller *"is not required to hold a Texas tax permit"* (*Remote Sellers*)

## ⛔ a permit taken "to be safe" opens franchise tax

> "A foreign taxable entity with a Texas use tax permit is presumed to have nexus in Texas and is subject to Texas franchise tax."
>
> — 34 TAC §3.586(e)

## 🚩 open

| gap | why |
|---|---|
| a mail-only address under §151.107(a)(1) | "where business is conducted" is the test; no STAR document on a forward-only mailbox was found |
| an online lead marketplace or ad product | data processing vs the §3.330 ad exclusion vs information service turns on the product's facts |
