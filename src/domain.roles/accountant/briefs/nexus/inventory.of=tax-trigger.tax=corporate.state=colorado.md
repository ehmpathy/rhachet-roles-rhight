# inventory.of=tax-trigger.tax=corporate.state=colorado

## .answer

Colorado **corporate income tax**: a foreign C corporation has substantial nexus once its Colorado **sales exceed $500,000** in the tax period, or property / payroll over $50,000, or 25% of any total — and it is subject to tax when it also exceeds P.L. 86-272, which a software or services seller always does.

## .apportionment

**single sales factor** (a receipts factor), SaaS receipts sourced **market-based** (where the service is delivered, the location of the market).

> "All business income must be apportioned using a receipts factor."
>
> — CDOR, *C Corporation Apportionment* page

> "In calculating its Colorado tax, a C corporation’s apportionable income must be apportioned by multiplying it by a fraction representing the proportion of its total sales that were derived from Colorado sources."
>
> — CDOR, *Corporate Income Tax Guide* (Revised March 2026), Part 6

> "Receipts from the sale of services are included in the numerator of the apportionment fraction if and to the extent that the service is delivered to a location in Colorado. In general, the location to which a service is delivered is the location of the C corporation’s market for the service, which may not be the location of C corporation’s employees or property."
>
> — CDOR, *Corporate Income Tax Guide* (Revised March 2026), Part 6

> "The C corporation whose only activities in Colorado consist of making sales, that does not own or rent real estate within the State of Colorado, and that generates annual Colorado gross sales of $100,000 or less, may elect to pay tax on 0.5% of the annual Colorado gross receipts in lieu of paying the normal income tax."
>
> — CDOR, *C Corporation Apportionment* page, § Gross Receipts

## .the threshold

> "a corporation organized outside of Colorado has substantial nexus with Colorado for any tax period in which the corporation’s property, payroll, or sales within Colorado exceed any of the following thresholds"
>
> — CDOR, *Corporate Income Tax Guide*, Part 1 (Rule 39-22-301(1))

> "For Colorado corporate income tax purposes, a C corporation is doing business in Colorado whenever the minimum standards of Public Law 86-272 are exceeded and the C corporation has substantial nexus with the State of Colorado."
>
> — CDOR, *Corporate Income Tax Guide*, Part 1

## .details

| item | value |
|---|---|
| figure | **$500,000** sales · $50,000 property · $50,000 payroll · or 25% of any total |
| receipts that count | gross receipts sourced to Colorado — incl. *"the sale of software or digital products for primary use by a purchaser known to the seller to be in Colorado"* and services *"for primary use by a purchaser known to the seller to be in Colorado"* |
| period | the tax period |
| effective | current guide, pulled 2026-10-05; the guide states no index |
| physical presence also triggers | yes — organized or commercially domiciled in Colorado; or property / payroll over the lines |
| P.L. 86-272 | part of the test, but it reaches tangible-goods solicitation only — never software or services |
