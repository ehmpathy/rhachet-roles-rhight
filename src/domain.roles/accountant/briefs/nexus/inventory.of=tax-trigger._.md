# inventory.of=tax-trigger._

## .what

per state, WHEN a corporation chartered elsewhere — one that sells services or software to customers in that state — first owes the state (a) its corporate-level tax (income, franchise, or gross-receipts) and (b) a sales-tax collection duty. one entry per cell; each states the trigger in its first two lines and carries the state's own text, quoted, with the year the figure applies to.

⚠️ the tax answer is not the registration answer — different agency, different test. see `define.three-duties-three-tests` and the registration twin below.

⛔ in every state, P.L. 86-272 shields only solicitation of orders for **tangible** goods — never services or software (15 U.S.C. §381).

## .the axes

| axis | positions |
|---|---|
| `tax=` | `corporate` (income / franchise / gross-receipts) · `sales` |
| `state=` | the 50 states and `district-of-columbia` — 51 positions |

2 × 51 = 102 cells. occupied: 102. forbidden: 0. a state with no such tax still has its entry, which says so with a source.

## .the grid

each cell is the entry's own `.answer`, verbatim — generated by `.scratch/research/build-tax-trigger-root.js`, never typed by hand. read the entry before you act on a cell: the quote, the effective year, and the open items live there.

the **apportionment** column answers *how much* of a remote SaaS seller's income the corporate tax reaches, once it applies: the formula, and whether SaaS receipts are sourced to where the **customer** is (market-based) or where the **seller works** (cost of performance). it is the first paragraph of the corporate entry's `## .apportionment` section, verbatim. at zero profit the apportioned tax is $0 under any formula — but a minimum tax and the return are still owed.
