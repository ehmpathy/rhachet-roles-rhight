# inventory.of=tax-trigger.tax=sales.state=new-mexico

## .answer

New Mexico has **no sales tax**; its **gross receipts tax** (GRT) is levied on the seller. **SaaS: yes, by the general rule** — gross receipts reach licenses of property employed in NM and services whose product is first used in NM; no SaaS-specific statement was pulled. remote threshold: **≥ $100,000** of **taxable** NM gross receipts in the **previous calendar year** (from July 1, 2019).

## .the threshold

> "For those that lack physical presence in New Mexico, including a marketplace provider, it means having at least $100,000 of taxable gross receipts from sales, leases and licenses of tangible personal property, sale of licenses and sales of services and licenses for use of real property sourced to New Mexico in the previous calendar year. Taxable gross receipts are only those gross receipts that are not otherwise exempt or deductible."
>
> — FYI-105, *Gross Receipts & Compensating Taxes – An Overview* (08/11/2025), *Engaging in Business*

> "“Gross receipts” means the total amount of money or the value of other consideration received from selling property in New Mexico, leasing or licensing property employed in New Mexico, from granting a right to use a franchise employed in New Mexico, from selling services performed outside New Mexico, the product of which is initially used in New Mexico, or for performing services in New Mexico."
>
> — FYI-105 (08/11/2025), *What is Gross Receipts?*
