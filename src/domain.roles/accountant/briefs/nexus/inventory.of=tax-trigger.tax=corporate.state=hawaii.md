> ⚠️ **not legal advice**, and not tax advice. this entry helps you find what to ask; confirm the answer for your facts with a licensed attorney or CPA.

## .answer

two business-level taxes: the **general excise tax (GET)**, a gross receipts tax on the business itself, owed once Hawaii gross income reaches **$100,000** or **200 transactions** in the current or prior calendar year (services used in Hawaii count); and the **corporate net income tax** (HRS ch. 235) — ⚠️ no dollar bright line found for it.

## .apportionment

**equal three-factor formula** (property + payroll + sales, ÷ 3), SaaS receipts sourced **market-based** (where the service is used or consumed in Hawaii, or an intangible is used there), for taxable years after Dec. 31, 2019.

> "All business income shall be apportioned to this State by multiplying the income by a fraction, the numerator of which is the property factor plus the payroll factor plus the sales factor, and the denominator of which is three."
>
> — HRS §235-29, Apportionment of business income; percentage

> "Sales, other than sales of tangible personal property, are in this State: (1) In the case of intangible property, to the extent the intangible property is used in this State; or (2) In the case of a service, to the extent the service is used or consumed in this State."
>
> — HRS §235-37, Apportionment; sales factor; nontangible personalty (am. L 2019, c 96)

> "The 2019 amendment applies to taxable years beginning after December 31, 2019."
>
> — HRS §235-37, Note

> "In the case of sales of other than tangible personal property, there shall be included, in addition to amounts includible under rules prescribed on the sale of tangible personal property above, all sales or receipts from: (A) property located and contracts performed in Hawaii, irrespective of the place where the contract is made, (B) communications transmitted from a point in Hawaii, (C) intangible property used in Hawaii, (D) services used or consumed in Hawaii, (E) all other activities engaged in or transactions conducted in Hawaii."
>
> — Department of Taxation, Instructions for Schedules O and P (Form N-30), Rev. 2024, Sales Factor

## .the threshold

> "Act 41 provides that a person is engaging in business in the State, regardless of whether the person is physically present in the State, if in the current or preceding calendar year: (1) The person has gross income of $100,000 or more from the sale of tangible personal property delivered in the State, services used or consumed in the State, or intangible property used in the State; or (2) The person has entered into 200 or more separate transactions involving tangible personal property delivered in the State, services used or consumed in the State, or intangible property used in the State."
>
> — Department of Taxation Announcement No. 2018-10 (amended July 10, 2018), Act 41 SLH 2018, eff. July 1, 2018

> "GET is a tax on the business itself. Businesses may choose to pass the GET on to customers, but they are not required to do so."
>
> — Department of Taxation, General Excise Tax page

> "The tax rates for corporations are 4.4% up to $25,000, 5.4% over $25,000 but not over $100,000, and 6.4% over $100,000 of taxable income."
>
> — Outline of the Hawaii Tax System as of July 1, 2025, (1) Net Income, HRS ch. 235
