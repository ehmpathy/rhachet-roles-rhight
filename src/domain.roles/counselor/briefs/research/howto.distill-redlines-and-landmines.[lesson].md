# howto.distill-redlines-and-landmines

## .what

the method for the last step of any research pass: distill the findings into **two paired artifacts**
— a `ref.landmine-inventory` (the facts that defeat a careful reader) and a `floor.redlines` (the
lines drawn from them) — and keep them **separate**, with the second cited into the first by ID.

⭐ **run this on every research subject, not only legal ones.** the pattern is subject-agnostic: any
domain with a body of authority — a regulation, an API contract, a protocol spec, a tax code, a
vendor agreement — has landmines, and they are the part a synthesis brief loses.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

> ⚠️ **a note on gerunds.** this file trips `rule.forbid.gerunds` on terms of art quoted from the
> worked example (`telemarketing`, `holding` in its judicial sense).

---

## ⭐ .why — a synthesis brief systematically loses the most valuable half

a research pass naturally produces a **synthesis**: here is the area, here are the layers, here is
what to build. that artifact is necessary and it has a specific blind spot.

a synthesis is organized by **what the law requires**. so it carries every requirement, and it drops
the thing a reader most needs: **which of those requirements they would have gotten wrong on their
own**. that information exists only as a *relation between the source and a reader* — it is invisible
in the source, so a faithful summary of the source cannot contain it.

⭐ **that relation is exactly what the research pass discovered, and no other artifact records it.** a
second reader can re-derive every requirement from the sources. they cannot re-derive *where a
competent person's intuition diverges from the text* without repeating the whole pass.

⚠️ **and it is the half a client can act on immediately.** a synthesis says "build these six layers".
an inventory says "the belief you are operating on today is false." the second changes behavior this
week; the first changes a roadmap.

## ⭐ two walkers, and the file serves both

⛔ **before the kinds of landmine, settle who steps on them** — because the answer decides what may
enter the inventory at all.

| the walker | what they hold | ⛔ what defeats them | the mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🌞 **lightwalker** | a live model of the domain, learned in the trade | the **surprise** — where the model is wrong, or has a hole | ⚡ |
| 🌑 **nightwalker** | ⭐ **no model at all** — they may not know the instrument reaches them | the **plain rule**, in the instrument's most obvious words | 🪨 |

⭐ **the rule: enumerate every mine, and mark only the surprises.** a plain mine is **never omitted for
obviousness** — state it flatly, mark it 🪨, and a lightwalker skims past in a glance. mark each
surprise ⚡, and a nightwalker knows which rows their intuition cannot carry them through.

## the three kinds of landmine

⛔ **most hazard lists capture only type I, miss type II entirely, and never think to ask about ⓪.**

| | 🪨 **type ⓪** — **no belief** is held | ⚡ **type I** — the belief is **wrong** | ⚡ **type II** — the belief is **absent** |
|---|---|---|---|
| what it is | ⭐ the **plain rule** — obvious to a reader of the instrument, invisible to anyone else | a correct read of a **partial** source yields a false conclusion | a requirement obvious **once stated** — never encountered |
| the tell | *"is this even about us?"* | *"we checked that"* | *"nobody raised it"* |
| ⭐ how it is found | ✅ ask *"what does this demand of someone who has **never heard of it**?"* — ⛔ **the question an expert forgets to ask** | ⛔ **only by contradiction** — a re-read cannot find it; your read was correct on its own terms, and the **scope** was wrong | ✅ **by an exhaustive read** of the instrument, end to end |
| who steps on it | 🌑 the nightwalker | 🌞 the lightwalker | 🌞 the lightwalker |
| what fixes it | an **orientation** | a correction | a checklist |
| ⚠️ does it recur? | **no** — once you know the instrument is about you, you know it | **yes** — the intuition survives and reasserts on the next adjacent question | **no** — once written down, it stays |

⭐ **type II is the half a hazard list built from incidents will miss**, because an absent belief fails
**quietly**: the requirement is simply never met, and no alarm sounds. a list of "what went wrong"
only captures beliefs that failed loudly.

⚠️ **and neither ⓪ nor II is always a new duty.** either can cut in your favor — an unknown threshold
that makes a whole class of events non-actionable means an unaware program **over-reserves**. record
those too; they are the entries that pay for the exercise.

### ⛔ why type ⓪ is the one a method loses by default

⭐ **a catalogue's blind spot is proportional to its authors' expertise.** a research pass is run by
people who have just read the instrument, so **what is obvious to the author is invisible to the
artifact** — and that is precisely what a newcomer walks into.

⚠️ **the worked example shipped without its plain rules for exactly this reason, and the mechanism
deserves a name because it is not carelessness.** the original rung-① admission test was *"did a source
contradict a competent reader's belief?"* — **reader-relative**. rung ②'s test is
**reader-independent**. because ② traces into ①, ①'s test **silently narrowed** ②'s: ⛔ **a rule nobody
was wrong about could not enter, however large it was.** the artifact then promised *"not defeated"* to
a reader for whom the promise was false.

⛔ **so rung ①'s admission test is two questions, and you owe both.**

## the procedure

### step 0 — 🪨 state the plain rules, before you know too much to see them

⭐ **do this FIRST, and if you cannot, do it with someone who has not read the instrument.** ask what
the domain demands of a reader who has never heard of it, and write those answers down flatly:

- *does this instrument reach me at all?* — the **trigger**: scope, thresholds, who is covered
- *what does it forbid outright?* — the headline prohibition, in the instrument's own words
- *what does it demand that I would not think to ask about?* — the second track, the parallel duty

⛔ **the honest problem: you cannot ask this question well once you have read the source.** an author
a week into an instrument has lost the state of mind the question requires. ⭐ **the only real
instrument is a genuinely uninitiated reader** — and where none is available, a projection of the
domain's own **trigger** material is the substitute. ⚠️ **label it as the substitute it is**, because a
projection returns what the trigger brief happened to cover, never what a newcomer would actually ask.

⭐ **and mark these 🪨.** they are the rows a practitioner skips and a newcomer cannot do without.

### step 1 — sweep for type II, per instrument

for each primary instrument the subject rests on, read it **end to end** and list every operative
requirement, whether or not anyone asked about it. ⭐ **this is mechanical and it is the highest-yield
hour in the pass.** a regulation that enumerates its own minimum standards is a type II generator: it
wrote the checklist, and a reader who arrived via a summary has met perhaps half of it.

⚠️ **record the sweep's bound.** the type II set is complete **for instruments actually swept** and
empty for the rest. that asymmetry must be stated, or a reader will read absence as safety.

### step 2 — harvest type I from the pass's own corrections

⛔ **you cannot sweep for these.** go back through the research trail and collect every place a source
**contradicted** an expectation — including your own. the tells:

- a section that begins "this is a correction"
- a claim that had to be demoted, re-scoped, or withdrawn
- a footnote that inverted the naive read of a case
- ⭐ **an over-tag, a wrong ratchet, or a defect your own gate caught** — these are landmines about the
  method rather than the subject, and they belong in the inventory if the artifact ships

### step 3 — write the inventory: facts, and no instruction

group by subject (the tracks, the layers, the phases — whatever axis the domain already has), and
give every entry a **stable ID**. each row: the belief, the correction with its authority, the type,
and the blast radius.

⛔ **the inventory prescribes no action.** the moment it says "so you should…", it stops being a
catalogue of facts and starts to carry appetite — and appetite is what makes an artifact unpublishable.

### step 4 — apply the redline test

for each entry, ask **one** question:

> **if you cross this line, are you defeated *whatever else you do*?**

| answer | verdict |
|--------|---------|
| yes — **the text forbids it** | ⛔ a **hard** redline |
| yes — **your defense becomes unprovable** | ⛔ a **proof** redline |
| yes — **the claim itself is false**, so what is built on it inherits the error | ⛔ a **claim** redline |
| no — it raises risk, or it is one of several permitted options | ✅ **not a redline.** it is a judgment |

⭐ **the test is the whole method, and it does two jobs at once.** it produces a short, high-signal
list *and* it makes the artifact publishable — because a preference among options the law permits
**fails the test by construction**. appetite is excluded by the rule of admission, not by a scrub
afterwards.

⚠️ **so where the sources permit a choice, the redlines file must be silent.** that silence is the
guarantee, not a gap.

### step 5 — trace every line back by ID

each redline names the inventory entries it defuses. ⛔ **a redline with no landmine behind it is
invented caution**, and the trace column is what makes that visible to a reviewer at a glance.

⚠️ **the classification axes are independent, and say so.** a landmine is typed by **how you would
find it**; a redline is typed by **what happens if you cross it**. a type II can be a hard redline. ⛔
**the type is never a severity** — a fact you merely never met defeats you as completely as one you
believed wrongly.

### step 6 — state what a clean sheet does NOT mean

⛔ **the most likely misuse of a redlines file is as a certificate.** say plainly, in the file: to hold
every line means you have not been **defeated** — never that you are **compliant**. compliance needs
the affirmative build, which lives in the `floor.howto.*` briefs.

## ⭐ why two files rather than one

the split is not organizational tidiness. it is what keeps the pair honest.

| | the inventory | the redlines |
|---|---|---|
| holds | facts, with authority | imperatives |
| prescribes | ⛔ no action at all | the lines to hold |
| its completeness | bounded by what was swept and met | bounded by the inventory |
| if bundled | ⛔ the imperatives would inherit the facts' authority, and a reader could not tell which sentences the sources **force** from which we **chose** |

⭐ **the trace by ID is what a bundle cannot provide.** separated, a reviewer can check every line
against a fact. bundled, a line and a fact read alike, and invented caution becomes invisible.

## the naming convention

| file | what it is |
|------|-----------|
| `floor.landmines.[lesson].md` | the catalogue of what **defeats** you — facts, IDs, no instruction. ⭐ **both sweeps live here**: one group holds what the sources **grant** |
| `floor.redlines.[lesson].md` | the lines — imperatives, each traced by ID |
| `floor.mustcants.[lesson].md` | the **todos** those lines force — bucketed must/can't × establish/eliminate, each traced by line ID |

⭐ **the `floor.` prefix marks a promise**: the best minimum found, every element traced to a named
authority, and its own failures named. **a floor, never a peak.** ⚠️ note it marks the *promise*,
not the mood — two of the four hold **facts** rather than imperatives, and carry the prefix all the same.

⚠️ **an earlier cut of this method named a fourth file `floor.northstar`, which held the same material
as *properties* ("the record is per-number") rather than as *todos* ("write a per-number record — done
when you can produce one"). it was dropped as a duplicate.** ⭐ the lesson is worth the sentence: a
**property** states a shape and leaves the reader to derive the action; a **todo** states the action and
its done-condition. the second is strictly more useful and no less publishable, because the rule of
admission is unchanged.

### ⭐ the grants sweep is the step this method originally missed

⛔ **step 1 as first written sends you to sweep for requirements, and a sweep returns what it was sent
to find.** run only that way, the pass produces a catalogue of ways to lose and calls itself complete.

⚠️ **the same instrument almost always gives as well as takes** — exemptions, thresholds, safe
harbours, cure windows, definitional exclusions. those have the **same type II character**: obvious
once stated, invisible until swept for. and the cost of ignorance runs the other way, so it is
**silent**: an unknown duty eventually announces itself in a demand letter; an unknown protection
announces itself never, and you simply over-reserve forever.

**so sweep each instrument twice — once for what it demands, once for what it grants.** the second
sweep's admission test is the mirror of the redline test:

> **does the source itself name this as an exemption, exclusion, threshold, defense, or cure?**

that excludes "arguments we think would work", which is appetite, exactly as the redline test excludes
a preference among permitted options.

### ⛔ the grants sweep is a GROUP, not a file — and that correction cost a rewrite

⚠️ **an earlier cut of this method gave the grants sweep its own file** (`floor.armory.for=$party`),
split into two classes: **armor** — a protection said to be a fact about your situation, unloseable —
and **shield** — a defense you must plead and prove.

⛔ **the armor class was false on its own entries.** every protection in the worked example carried
conditions the reader has to establish: a ported-number grace with four conjunctive tests, a numerical
limit that demands a per-number call count, an exemption whose availability is the entire subject of a
supreme court case. **no protection is a fact you have; each is a condition you must prove.** with the
armor half gone, the split had one class in it, and a one-class taxonomy is not a taxonomy.

⭐ **so the grants sweep folds back into the landmine catalogue as its own group.** the entries were
never a different kind of fact — they are false beliefs about *escape*, which is exactly what a
landmine is. and the defect itself was recorded as an entry in that group, because a method's own
wrong belief is the same species as the ones it catalogues.

| ⛔ what the split claimed | what survived contact with the entries |
|---|---|
| armor is a fact you hold, unloseable | ❌ **no protection is free** — every one has conditions **you** must establish |
| a separate file keeps the sweeps legible | ❌ it **hid** the grants half from a reader who opened the catalogue |
| the party qualifier (`for=$party`) marks one cell of a grid | ✅ **this part held** — protections are rarely symmetric between parties; say which side a group is written for |

⛔ **the one sentence to carry across domains: a protection you cannot prove is a protection you do not
have.**

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| ⭐ why not just write the synthesis? | a synthesis is organized by what the sources **require**, so it structurally loses **where a reader's intuition diverges from them** — the pass's most perishable finding |
| what is the highest-yield hour? | the **type II sweep** — read each instrument end to end and enumerate what it actually demands |
| which half can you not sweep for? | ⛔ **type I** — a belief wrong on a correct read is invisible to a more careful read |
| ⭐ what admits a line to the redlines? | **"if you cross it, are you defeated whatever else you do?"** — and no other criterion |
| why does that test matter beyond concision? | it excludes **appetite by construction**, which is what makes the artifact publishable |
| why two files? | so a reviewer can check every imperative against a fact; ⛔ bundled, invented caution is invisible |
| what must the redlines file refuse to imply? | ⚠️ that a clean sheet means **compliant** — it means **not defeated** |

## the honest gap

- ⬜ **this method has been run once**, on one subject (US phone-contact law). it produced 56 landmines
  and 46 redlines, and the type I / type II split was **added after the first draft** when a reader
  pointed out that the original definition excluded half the population. that correction is the
  strongest evidence the method needs a second run before it is trusted as general.
- ⭐ **but one result inside that single run is worth more than the run's own numbers.** a *second*
  step-1 sweep — of paragraphs the first pass had never asked for — added **10 entries, 8 of them
  type II**, and one of those (`I3`) **contradicted a hard redline the file had already published**.
  ⚠️ **so the sweep is not a one-time step**, and its yield does not decay the way an incident log
  does: it is a function of **what remains unread**, which the first sweep does not measure. a method
  that presents step 1 as done-once will systematically under-run it.
- ⚠️ **the redline test has a soft edge.** "defeated whatever else you do" is crisp for a forbidden act
  and softer for a proof failure, where the honest answer is often "defeated *if the question is ever
  litigated*." the three-way split (hard / proof / claim) manages the ambiguity rather than resolves it.
- ⬜ **no method is proposed for finding type I mines nobody stepped on.** by construction they surface
  only on contradiction, and this brief offers no way to manufacture one. a blind second reader is the
  closest available substitute, and that is a different technique.
- ⬜ **the type marks were assigned by judgment, not by a rule.** several entries have both faces, and
  the brief marks them for the face that governs discovery. no test was applied and none is proposed.
- ⚠️ **the file layout in this brief has now been wrong twice** — once with a `floor.northstar` that
  duplicated the todos as properties, once with a `floor.armory` whose armor class was false on its own
  entries. ⛔ **both were caught by a reader's question, not by the method**, and this brief proposes no
  test that would have caught either. a layout that survives two retractions is not thereby validated.
