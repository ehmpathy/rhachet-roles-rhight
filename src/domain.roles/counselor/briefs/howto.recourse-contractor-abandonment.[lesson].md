# howto.recourse-contractor-abandonment

## .what

practical guide to recover money when a licensed contractor takes payment but abandons the job — options range from $0 demand letters to $50-150 small claims filings.

## .why

- contractor abandonment is common (10-15% of residential projects experience significant disputes)
- licensed contractors have more leverage points than unlicensed
- small claims court is designed for exactly this scenario
- most homeowners don't know the full range of options
- order matters: free options first, paid options if needed

---

## the situation

| fact | implication |
|------|-------------|
| licensed contractor | board complaint + bond claim available |
| pulled permit | permit is in their name (their problem) |
| partial payment made | you have damages |
| work incomplete | breach of contract |

## recourse options (in order)

### 1. demand letter ($0)

**success rate**: 20-30% settled here

send certified mail with return receipt:

| element | content |
|---------|---------|
| facts | contract date, amount paid, work promised |
| breach | work not completed by [date] |
| demand | complete work within 14 days OR refund $X |
| consequences | licensing board complaint, bond claim, legal action |
| deadline | 14 days from receipt |

**why it works**: licensed contractors fear board complaints more than the money.

### 2. contractor licensing board complaint ($0)

**timeline**: 30-90 days for outcome

| state | licensing authority |
|-------|---------------------|
| Indiana (electrical) | local jurisdiction (not state) |
| Indianapolis | [Indy.gov Contractor Licenses](https://www.indy.gov/activity/contractor-licenses) |
| most states | state contractor licensing board |

**what the board can do**:

| action | effect |
|--------|--------|
| suspend license | contractor can't work |
| revoke license | permanent |
| order restitution | sometimes available |
| mediate dispute | informal settlement |
| public record | damages contractor's reputation |

**note**: electricians in Indiana are licensed locally, not at state level. file with Indianapolis DBNS for Marion County.

### 3. contractor bond claim ($0) — best option when available

**why bonds are your best recourse**:

| factor | bond claim | small claims |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| cost to you | $0 | $50-150 |
| timeline | 30-60 days | 60-120 days |
| collectability | guaranteed (surety is solvent) | uncertain (contractor may have no assets) |
| effort | paperwork only | court appearance required |
| no-show defendant | doesn't matter | still need to collect |

**how bonds work**:

```
YOU ──file claim──▶ SURETY COMPANY ──pays you──▶ YOU
                          │
                          ▼
                   SURETY ──recovers from──▶ CONTRACTOR
```

the surety company pays you directly, then pursues the contractor to recover. their problem, not yours.

**typical bond amounts**:

| bond type | typical amount | notes |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| electrical contractor | $5,000-15,000 | varies by jurisdiction |
| plumber contractor | $5,000-15,000 | varies by jurisdiction |
| HVAC contractor | $5,000-15,000 | varies by jurisdiction |
| general contractor | $10,000-25,000 | often higher |
| residential remodeler | $10,000-25,000 | state dependent |

**how to find bond info**:

| source | how |
|--------|-----|
| your contract | may list surety company and bond number |
| license office | call and ask what bond is on file |
| state contractor board | online lookup (some states) |
| contractor directly | request certificate of bond |

**how to file a bond claim**:

| step | action | details |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | identify surety company | from contract or license office |
| 2 | request claim form | call surety or download from website |
| 3 | complete claim form | describe abandonment, list damages |
| 4 | attach documentation | see below |
| 5 | send via certified mail | keep track number |
| 6 | surety investigates | 30-60 days typical |
| 7 | receive payment | if claim validated |

**documentation to include**:

| document | purpose |
|----------|---------|
| signed contract | proves scope and price |
| payment records | proves what you paid |
| photos of incomplete work | proves abandonment |
| communication records | shows contractor stopped contact |
| permit records | shows work started |
| written timeline | dates of payments, work, last contact |
| demand letter (if sent) | shows you attempted resolution |

**how the surety validates your claim**:

the surety company doesn't just take your word for it — they investigate:

| step | what surety does |
|------|------------------|
| 1 | review your documentation |
| 2 | contact contractor for their side |
| 3 | request contractor's records (invoices, receipts, schedule) |
| 4 | may inspect the property |
| 5 | compare stories and evidence |
| 6 | decide based on preponderance of evidence |

**what protects against false claims**:

| safeguard | how it works |
|-----------|--------------|
| contractor gets to respond | surety contacts them before payment |
| documentation required | you must prove payment and non-performance |
| signed contract | proves what was agreed |
| permit records | public record of work started |
| surety investigation | they verify facts independently |
| fraud penalties | false claim = fraud (criminal liability) |

**if contractor disputes your claim**:

| scenario | outcome |
|----------|---------|
| contractor provides evidence of completion | claim denied |
| contractor proves you didn't pay | claim denied |
| contractor shows scope was different | partial payment or denied |
| contractor doesn't respond | claim likely approved |
| conflicted evidence | surety decides based on weight |

**bond claim success rates**:

| scenario | success rate |
|----------|--------------|
| clear abandonment + good documentation | 60-80% |
| disputed scope or quality | 30-50% |
| contractor contests claim | takes longer, often still paid |
| insufficient documentation | low |

**if loss exceeds bond amount**:

| your loss | bond amount | outcome |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| $3,000 | $10,000 | fully covered |
| $8,000 | $10,000 | fully covered |
| $15,000 | $10,000 | get $10K from bond, pursue $5K via small claims |

**bond claim vs small claims comparison**:

| factor | bond claim | small claims |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| who pays | surety company (always solvent) | contractor (maybe insolvent) |
| court appearance | no | yes |
| need attorney | no | no (usually not allowed) |
| judgment collection | not needed | your problem |
| time to money | 30-60 days | 90-180+ days |
| if contractor contests | surety decides | judge decides |

**bottom line**: if contractor is bonded and your loss is under the bond amount, file the bond claim first. it's faster, free, and you don't have collectability risk.

### 4. small claims court ($50-150)

**when to use**: if options 1-3 fail and loss exceeds filing cost

| factor | typical value |
|--------|---------------|
| filing fee | $30-100 |
| service fee | $20-50 (sheriff serves defendant) |
| total cost | $50-150 |
| limit | varies by state |
| attorney | not required (often not allowed) |
| timeline | 30-60 days to hearing |

**state small claims limits**:

| state | limit |
|-------|-------|
| Indiana | $10,000 |
| California | $12,500 |
| Texas | $20,000 |
| Florida | $8,000 |
| New York | $10,000 |
| Illinois | $10,000 |

source: [Nolo: Small Claims Court Limits](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/small-claims-suits-how-much-30031.html)

## small claims process

### step 1: gather documentation

| document | purpose |
|----------|---------|
| contract | proves agreement and price |
| payment proof | cancelled checks, receipts, bank statements |
| photos | shows incomplete work |
| texts/emails | shows contractor stopped responding |
| permit records | shows work was started |
| timeline | when paid, when abandoned |

### step 2: calculate damages

| calculation | formula |
|-------------|---------|
| amount paid | $X |
| value received | $Y (estimate of work completed) |
| damages | $X - $Y |

**example**:
- contract: $6,000
- paid: $3,000 (50%)
- work completed: ~25% ($1,500 value)
- damages: $3,000 - $1,500 = $1,500

### step 3: file claim

| where | your county small claims court |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| forms | available at courthouse or online |
| fee | $30-100 |
| defendant | contractor name and address |
| amount | your calculated damages |

### step 4: serve defendant

| method | cost |
|--------|------|
| sheriff service | $20-50 |
| certified mail | $10-20 (some jurisdictions) |
| process server | $30-75 |

### step 5: hearing

| what happens | your role |
|--------------|-----------|
| present your case | show documents, explain timeline |
| contractor responds | may not show (default judgment) |
| judge decides | same day ruling typical |

### step 6: collect judgment

**if you win, collection options**:

| method | how it works |
|--------|--------------|
| voluntary payment | contractor pays |
| wage garnishment | court orders employer to withhold |
| bank levy | seize funds from contractor's account |
| property lien | lien on contractor's real estate |

## the collectability problem

**winning is easy. collecting is hard.**

| contractor situation | collectability |
|---------------------|----------------|
| established business with assets | good |
| owns real property | good (lien it) |
| sole proprietor, no assets | poor |
| LLC with no assets | poor |
| multiple prior judgments | very poor |
| closed business | near zero |

**before you sue, ask**:

| question | how to check |
|----------|--------------|
| do they own property? | county assessor records |
| do they have a business location? | drive by |
| are there prior judgments? | court records search |
| are they still operating? | check license status |

## cost-benefit analysis

| your loss | recommended action |
|-----------|-------------------|
| < $500 | demand letter + board complaint only |
| $500-1,000 | add bond claim |
| $1,000-3,000 | add small claims if collectible |
| $3,000-10,000 | definitely small claims |
| > $10,000 | civil court (attorney advisable) |

## realistic success rates

| action | success rate | notes |
|--------|--------------|-------|
| demand letter | 20-30% | higher for licensed contractors |
| board complaint | 10-20% get outcome | may get restitution order |
| bond claim | 40-60% if bond extant | limited by bond amount |
| small claims judgment | 70-80% | if you show up with docs |
| actually collecting | 30-50% | depends on contractor assets |

## the open permit problem

when contractor abandons with permit open:

| issue | implication |
|-------|-------------|
| permit in their name | technically their liability |
| tied to your property | shows up in title search |
| blocks sale/refinance | may need closure |
| expires eventually | 6-12 months typical |

**closure options**:

| option | process |
|--------|---------|
| contractor closes permit | unlikely if they abandoned |
| new contractor takes over | pulls new permit, finishes work |
| permit expires | building dept may void |
| you request closure | explain abandonment to building dept |

## prevention for next time

| practice | protection |
|----------|------------|
| never pay > 10% deposit | limits exposure |
| progress payments only | pay for completed milestones |
| verify license before hiring | licensing board website |
| verify insurance | request certificate |
| written contract | specifies scope, price, timeline |
| payment tied to inspections | pay after inspection passes |

**payment structure recommendation**:

| milestone | payment |
|-----------|---------|
| contract signing | 10% |
| materials delivered | 30% |
| rough-in inspection passed | 30% |
| final inspection passed | 30% |

## checklist: contractor abandoned job

- [ ] document everything (contract, payments, photos, communications)
- [ ] calculate your damages (paid - value received)
- [ ] send certified demand letter (14 day deadline)
- [ ] file licensing board complaint
- [ ] check for contractor bond and file claim
- [ ] if unresolved, file small claims (if collectible)
- [ ] address open permit (contact building dept)
- [ ] keep all records for 7+ years
