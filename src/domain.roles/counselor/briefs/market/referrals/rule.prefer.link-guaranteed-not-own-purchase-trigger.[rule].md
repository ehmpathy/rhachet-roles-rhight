# rule.prefer.link-guaranteed-not-own-purchase-trigger

## .what

three trigger attributes each place a referral program on the outside-the-ban side of a distinct
statutory element; a program that has all three at once lacks every element the referral-selling
statutes require:

- **(1) a per-referrer LINK / code** — versus a collected list of prospect names.
- **(2) a GUARANTEED reward to every qualified referrer** — versus a chance / drawing / raffle.
- **(3) a reward earned on a friend's PURCHASE** — versus a reward earned on the referrer's OWN purchase.

one trigger design carries three simultaneous element-absences. the adjacent briefs draw each redline
on its own; this brief stacks all three and shows why a program with the stack sits outside the
referral-selling statutes in both states, while a program absent any one re-opens that element.

## .why

the referral-selling statutes in **both** states rest on an **element stack** — they attach
only when a bundle of elements combine: **names-of-prospects** + inducement of the referrer's **own
purchase** + a **contingency** (and in FL, a **chance** element that turns it into a lottery). where
any load-critical element is absent, the statute does not attach.

- a trigger with all three attributes lacks **each** element at once, so a program with it sits
  **outside** the statutes in both states — the absences are independent, not a single-thread bet.
- indiana treats the banned design as a **deceptive act** under Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9), which
  arms the attorney general **and** private plaintiffs (Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4).
- florida goes further: § 849.0915 **declares** the banned design a **lottery** and a **first-degree
  misdemeanor** — a **criminal** exposure. so in FL the trigger attributes bear on criminal exposure,
  not merely civil risk.
- the link-based, guaranteed, earn-on-a-friend's-purchase trigger is the Uber / DoorDash model — a proven,
  common market pattern.

## the element stack and the trigger attribute that removes each

the two statutes share the same skeleton. each trigger attribute corresponds to the absence of one
element.

| element | what the statute needs | the attribute under which it is absent |
|---------|------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **names-of-prospects** | the referrer hands the seller "names of prospective purchasers / consumers" | a **link / code** — the friend self-identifies by use; no roster is handed over |
| **own-purchase inducement** | the reward is "an inducement for [the buyer's own] sale" | a reward **earned on a friend's purchase**; the referrer's own purchase is not the object induced |
| **contingency / chance** | a reward "contingent upon … an event subsequent" — and FL deems it a **lottery** (needs a chance element) | a **guaranteed** reward to every qualified referrer; no draw, so the **chance** leg that makes it a lottery in FL is absent |

note: the **contingency** alone is present in nearly every referral program (the friend must transact),
so contingency is not the tripwire — the statute needs the **other** elements too. the three trigger
attributes correspond to the absence of those other elements and, in FL, the absence of the **chance**
leg that the lottery label rests on.

### the statutes, verbatim

**indiana — a deceptive act.** Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9) enumerates the banned design:

> "That the consumer will receive a rebate, discount, or other benefit as an inducement for
> entering into a sale or lease in return for giving the supplier the names of prospective
> consumers or otherwise helping the supplier to enter into other consumer transactions, if
> earning the benefit, rebate, or discount is contingent upon the occurrence of an event
> subsequent to the time the consumer agrees to the purchase or lease."

and the umbrella prohibition at subsection (a):

> "A supplier may not commit an unfair, abusive, or deceptive act, omission, or practice in
> connection with a consumer transaction."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-3/)

**florida — declared a lottery.** Fla. Stat. § 849.0915(1) both defines the banned design and
attaches the **chance / lottery** label:

> "Referral selling, whereby the seller gives or offers a rebate or discount to the buyer as an
> inducement for a sale in consideration of the buyer's providing the seller with the names of
> prospective purchasers, is declared to be a lottery if earning the rebate or discount is
> contingent upon the occurrence of an event subsequent to the time the buyer agrees to buy."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.0915](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.0915)

so both texts share **names-of-prospects** + **own-purchase inducement** + **contingency**; FL adds
the **lottery** label, which turns on a **chance** element. under the three trigger attributes, names
are absent (link), the own-purchase inducement is absent (earn-on-a-friend's-purchase), and chance is
absent (guaranteed reward). with those gone, neither text attaches.

### why "guaranteed" removes the FL chance element

the FL statute **deems** a contingent referral rebate a "lottery." florida's general lottery
prohibition, Fla. Stat. § 849.09, forbids one to "Set up, promote, or conduct any lottery for money
or for anything of value" — and a lottery classically needs three legs. per a secondary that states
the test:

> "An illegal lottery exists when **all three** of the following elements are present: **Prize**
> **Chance** **Consideration**"

> "If _any one_ of these elements is removed, the promotion no longer qualifies as a lottery under
> the law."

**source**: [The Social Media Law Firm — the three elements of an illegal lottery](https://thesocialmedialawfirm.com/blog/sweepstakes-law/the-three-elements-of-an-illegal-lottery-prize-chance-and-consideration/)

a reward **guaranteed** to every qualified referrer — no draw, no raffle — is a reward from which the
**chance** leg is absent. without chance, the lottery label the FL statute rests on has no leg to
stand on.

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.09 — general lottery prohibition](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.09)

a florida criminal-defense summary confirms the referral-selling statute is a **lottery** statute,
and defines the florida lottery it prohibits:

> "A game of chance in which smaller sums of money or things of smaller value are risked for the
> chance of getting money or property of greater value upon the happening of an uncertain event."

the same firm notes that "referral selling lotteries" are prohibited "under Florida Statute
849.0915, alongside pyramid lotteries and chain letter lotteries."

**source**: [Florida illegal lottery / referral-selling crimes — law-firm analysis (R.P. Foley)](https://www.rpfoley.com/illegal-lottery-or-raffle-crimes-florida-statute-849-09.html)

### the trigger attributes are the proven market pattern

the link-based, guaranteed, earn-on-a-friend's-purchase trigger is not theoretical — it is the
term-of-service template of the largest referral programs. each term below was fetched from the
program's own page and quoted verbatim.

**uber — a personal link / code, guaranteed on the friend's action:**

> "you can share your referral link or code only with people you know"

> "you should receive your Referral Reward after your referee/invitee uses your link or code to sign
> up with Uber and/or to activate the referral offer, and complete the requirements"

and it is capped, not a random draw:

> "For Rider, Driver, and Courier referrals: Referrers are limited to payouts in connection with a
> combined total of 10 referral offers."

**source**: [Uber Referral Program Rules](https://www.uber.com/legal/referral-program-rules/referral-terms-us-can-en/)

**doordash — a unique link, credit on the friend's own order:**

> "The Referrer provides the Referrer's unique referral link (the \"Referral Link\") directly to the
> New User through email, text, or the Share Your Link option."

> "The New User (a) registers on the DoorDash platform using the Referral Link provided to the New
> User by the Referrer; and (b) meets the offer requirements set forth in the Referral Link, which
> typically includes placing an order on the DoorDash platform with a certain minimum subtotal
> (excluding taxes and fees)."

**source**: [DoorDash Consumer Referral Program Terms](https://help.doordash.com/consumers/s/referral-terms-english?language=en_US)

both pay for a **link** (not names), reward every qualified referrer (**guaranteed**, no draw), and
turn on the **friend's** action (not the referrer's own purchase). both illustrate all three
trigger attributes element-for-element.

## IN vs FL

one trigger design sits outside both states' bans, but the **stakes** differ: IN is a civil deceptive
act; FL is a criminal lottery. that asymmetry is why the classification turns on the **structure**,
not on disclosure.

| dimension | Indiana | Florida |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| character | civil **deceptive act** | criminal **lottery** — first-degree misdemeanor |
| statute | § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9) | § 849.0915 |
| exposure a design inside the ban invites | AG injunction + private (even class) suit | criminal charge + injunction, on top of civil |
| enforcer | attorney general + private consumers | attorney general / state attorney / Div. of Consumer Services |

**indiana's teeth are civil but real.** Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4 arms both a private plaintiff and the
state:

> "A person relying upon an uncured or incurable deceptive act may bring an action for the damages
> actually suffered as a consumer as a result of the deceptive act or five hundred dollars ($500),
> whichever is greater."

> "The attorney general may bring an action to enjoin an unfair, abusive, or deceptive act, omission,
> or practice in connection with a consumer transaction."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4 — remedies (AG action + private suit)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-4/)

**florida's teeth are criminal.** § 849.0915(2) makes one who conducts a referral-selling lottery
"guilty of a misdemeanor of the first degree," and § 849.0915(3) hands three enforcers an injunction
that "shall issue without bond." because in FL a lottery cannot be cured by disclosure, only a
structure that lacks the lottery elements sits clear of that criminal edge.

## severity

**prefer → nitpick.** this is a strong recommendation, not an absolute bar — a differently
structured referral program is not automatically unlawful, and counsel may clear an alternative on
its own facts. but it is a **load-critical** choice.

**why it matters:** a program with all three trigger attributes sits **outside** the referral-selling
statute in **both** states at once — and, in florida, outside the **criminal lottery exposure** (a
first-degree misdemeanor) that a chance-based, names-based, or own-purchase design invites. a design
absent any of the three attributes re-opens exactly the element the statute needs.

## the honest gap

- **no case law tests these statutes against a modern loyalty / link program.** the referral-selling
  statutes predate app-era referral links; no court opinion or AG guidance was found that applies
  them to a link-based, guaranteed, earn-on-a-friend's-purchase trigger. the text-level read is strong
  (a link is not a name-roster; a guaranteed reward is not a chance draw), but the reach of the
  statutes onto modern loyalty mechanics is a **counsel judgment call**, not a settled rule.
- **indiana's "or otherwise helping the supplier" clause is broader than FL's "names" language.** a
  design that leans on the referrer to actively work leads — rather than merely share a link — edges
  back toward that clause; the **own-purchase** element (element 2) is the sharper dividing line
  under IN than the names/link element.
- **florida's FDUTPA backdrop is broad.** even where § 849.0915 does not attach, deceptive market
  copy can draw a separate FDUTPA claim; the trigger attributes bear on the criminal statute, but
  market copy is a separate review surface.
- this brief maps the trigger attributes onto settled statutory text; it does not certify any design
  for a specific set of facts. counsel must confirm before any reliance.
