# define.boundary.marketing-bonus-vs-purchase-inducement

## .what

the second redline that separates a lawful referral reward from banned referral selling: the reward
is a **marketing bonus for a referral**, not an **inducement for the referrer's own purchase**.

## .why

- the referral-selling bans target a rebate given "as an inducement for [the buyer's own] sale"
- where the referrer earns by referral without any obligation to buy, that element is absent
- this separates the modern refer-a-friend program from the classic banned scheme

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

---

## the redline

```
     OUTSIDE THE BAN                     │   THE LINE    │       INSIDE THE BAN
─────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────────────────────
 referrer earns by REFERRAL              │              │  referrer must BUY to earn, and
 (a current/past customer, or a          │ "inducement  │  the reward is dangled to
 non-purchaser advocate)                 │  for the     │  induce that purchase
                                         │  referrer's  │
 the reward is a MARKETING BONUS         │  OWN sale"   │  the purchase comes first and
 for spread of the word                  │              │  is the object induced
                                         │              │
 no purchase by the referrer is a        │              │  "buy this, then earn rebates
 condition to earn                       │              │  as you refer others in"
```

**the element that splits the two:** is the reward an inducement for **the referrer's own
purchase**? if the referrer need not buy, it is a marketing bonus — not the banned inducement.

## the statutory hook

### Florida

Fla. Stat. § 849.0915 frames referral selling as a scheme where:

> "the seller gives or offers a rebate or discount to the buyer as an inducement for a sale in
> consideration of the buyer's providing the seller with the names of prospective purchasers"

and it "is declared to be a lottery if earning the rebate or discount is contingent upon the
occurrence of an event subsequent to the time the buyer agrees to buy."

### Indiana

Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9) forbids a supplier from a representation:

> "That the consumer will receive a rebate, discount, or other benefit as an inducement for
> entering into a sale or lease in return for giving the supplier the names of prospective
> consumers or otherwise helping the supplier to enter into other consumer transactions, if
> earning the benefit, rebate, or discount is contingent upon the occurrence of an event
> subsequent to the time the consumer agrees to the purchase or lease."

**sources**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.0915](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.0915);
[Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-3/)

both frame the illegal reward as one that induces the **buyer's own** purchase. a bonus paid to a
person for a referral — where that person's own purchase is not the object bought — is a
different animal.

## the federal frame: reward the referral, not the referrer's own buy

the FTC's multi-level marketing guidance draws the same line from a different angle. it condemns a
structure where the reward flows from a participant's own purchase rather than from a real sale to
someone else. per the FTC:

> "At the most basic level, the law requires that an MLM pay compensation that is based on actual
> sales to real customers, rather than based on mere wholesale purchases or other payments by its
> participants."

the harm the FTC names is a structure that:

> "incentivizes participants to buy product, and to recruit additional participants to buy product,
> to advance in the marketing program rather than in response to consumer demand in the
> marketplace"

the parallel is exact: a lawful reward turns on a **real sale to a real other person** (the friend
who buys), not on the referrer's own purchase used as the ticket to earn.

**source**: [FTC, Business Guidance Concerning Multi-Level Marketing](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing)

## the classic banned scheme vs the modern program

| the banned scheme | the modern program |
|-------------------|--------------------|
| "buy this $2,000 system now" | (no purchase required to refer) |
| "…and we'll rebate you $100 for every neighbor who also buys" | "share your link; earn when a friend buys a subscription" |
| the **purchase is induced** by future referral rebates | the **referral is rewarded**; any later purchase is optional |
| referrer is a buyer, hooked by the rebate | referrer is an advocate / past customer |

---

## which side real programs sit on

each row below is quoted from the program's own terms, fetched verbatim.

| program | does referrer buy to earn? | verbatim trigger | side |
|---------|----------------------------|------------------|------|
| Chase Refer-A-Friend | no — friend applies and is approved | "When your friend applies for a Chase credit card using your URL and their application is approved, both you and your friend receive the promotional rewards active at that time." | OUTSIDE |
| Uber referral | no — a current user shares a link | "you should receive your Referral Reward after your referee/invitee uses your link or code to sign up with Uber and/or to activate the referral offer, and complete the requirements" | OUTSIDE |
| Robinhood invite-a-friend | no — friend signs up and links a card | "As soon as your friend signs up, gets approved, and links their bank or debit card, then you'll both receive gift stock." | OUTSIDE |
| Cash App referral | no — friend acts within 14 days | "For both parties to claim their referral bonuses, your invited friend must complete the steps below." | OUTSIDE |
| pyramidal "buy + refer for rebates" | yes — purchase is the induced act | the statute's target | INSIDE |

each outside-the-ban row shares one trait: the **referrer's own purchase is not the trigger**. the
trigger is a friend's action. a compliance overview of referral programs frames the reward as a way to
"[t]urn happy customers and partners into advocates, offer a reward, and watch new leads roll in"
— the reward flows to those who **recommend**, not to their own purchase. ([Referral Factory, Legal
Considerations of Referral Programs](https://referral-factory.com/learn/legal-considerations-of-referral-programs))

---

## the credit-toward-future-purchase nuance

a program that gives the **referrer** credit toward their **own next purchase** stays
outside the ban — the credit is spendable on a future purchase (like a rider credit is spendable on a
future ride) — **so long as** the referrer earns it **by referral, not as a condition of a purchase**.
the redline is not the *form* of the reward (credit toward a future order) but the *trigger*:
earn-by-referral sits outside the ban; earn-by-your-own-purchase-plus-referrals sits inside it.

> a reward tied to the **referral**, paid to a past/current customer or advocate, sits on the
> outside-the-ban side; a reward structured as "make your purchase now and we'll rebate you as you
> refer" sits on the inside side.

---

## the honest gap

as with the link/names line, no case law applies this element to a modern credit-toward-future-
purchase reward. the position is text-strong but counsel-confirmable.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| second dividing element? | reward is a marketing bonus, not an inducement for the referrer's own purchase |
| does credit-toward-a-future-order put it inside the ban? | no — the form is immaterial; the trigger is what matters |
| what crosses the line? | "buy now, earn rebates as you refer others" |
| the outside-the-ban trigger? | earn by referral; no purchase required to earn |
