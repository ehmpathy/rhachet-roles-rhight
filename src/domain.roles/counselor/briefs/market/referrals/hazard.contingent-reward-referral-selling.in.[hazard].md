# hazard.contingent-reward-referral-selling.in

## .what

in Indiana, a referral reward structured the wrong way is a **deceptive act** under the Deceptive
Consumer Sales Act — actionable by the Attorney General and by consumers.

the act tells suppliers plainly: "a supplier may not commit an unfair, abusive, or deceptive act,
omission, or practice in connection with a consumer transaction" (Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(a)). the
contingent-reward referral design is one of the enumerated deceptive acts under subsection (b)(9).

## .why

- Indiana's ban is civil (contrast a stricter state like Florida, where the same design is a criminal lottery)
- but it is still an enforceable deceptive act with AG + private remedies
- the elements that trigger it match the Florida counterpart — so both bans turn on one common element set
- Indiana's AG "takes legal action on behalf of the state against individuals and companies that
  violate Indiana's Deceptive Consumer Sales Act", so a design inside the ban invites public
  enforcement on top of private suit

              │

              │

              │

              │
              ▼

## the statute verbatim

the enumerated deceptive act, quoted exact from Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9):

> "That the consumer will receive a rebate, discount, or other benefit as an inducement for
> entering into a sale or lease in return for giving the supplier the names of prospective
> consumers or otherwise helping the supplier to enter into other consumer transactions, if
> earning the benefit, rebate, or discount is contingent upon the occurrence of an event
> subsequent to the time the consumer agrees to the purchase or lease."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-3/)

the umbrella prohibition sits at subsection (a): "a supplier may not commit an unfair, abusive, or
deceptive act, omission, or practice in connection with a consumer transaction". a "consumer
transaction" per § 24-5-0.5-2 reaches "a sale, lease, assignment, award by chance, or other
disposition of an item of personal property, real property, a service, or an intangible" — so
consumer sales and services fall inside the act's scope generally, not outside it.

---

## the element analysis — the two sides of each element

| # | element | inside the ban | outside the ban |
|---|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | benefit as an inducement for the **consumer's own** sale/lease | referrer must buy; benefit induces it | referrer earns via referral, need not buy |
| 2 | **names of prospective consumers** (or "otherwise helping" land transactions) | referrer submits names | referrer shares a **link/code** |
| 3 | **contingent on a later event** | prospect later buys | present on both sides — not sufficient alone |

note IN's "or otherwise helping the supplier to enter into other consumer transactions" is broader
than FL's "names" language — so a link-share (rather than a name-list) does less of the work under
IN, and the **inducement-of-own-purchase** element (element 1) is the sharper divide in IN.

---

## severity

**blocker** (if the design has all elements) — a deceptive act with AG + private-suit exposure.
avoid via structure, not disclosure.

the remedy stack is real. per § 24-5-0.5-4, a consumer may recover "the damages actually suffered as
a result of the deceptive act or five hundred dollars ($500), whichever is greater", trebled up to
"three (3) times the actual damages" for willful conduct, plus "reasonable attorney's fees". the AG
may "bring an action to enjoin an unfair, abusive, or deceptive act, omission, or practice in
connection with a consumer transaction" and, for knowing violations, seek civil penalties "not
exceeding $5,000 per violation".

## IN vs FL divergence

| dimension | Indiana | Florida |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| character | civil deceptive act | criminal lottery (1st-deg misdemeanor) |
| statute | § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9) | § 849.0915 |
| breadth | "names… or otherwise helping… other transactions" (broader) | "names of prospective purchasers" |
| enforcer | AG + private consumers | AG / state attorney / Div. of Consumer Services |
| outside-the-ban elements | same: link-not-names + earn-via-referral + guaranteed | same |

the FL counterpart, quoted exact from § 849.0915(1), shows the parallel design trigger but a lottery
label rather than a deceptive-act label:

> "Referral selling, whereby the seller gives or offers a rebate or discount to the buyer as an
> inducement for a sale in consideration of the buyer's providing the seller with the names of
> prospective purchasers, is declared to be a lottery if earning the rebate or discount is
> contingent upon the occurrence of an event subsequent to the time the buyer agrees to buy."

and FL § 849.0915(2) supplies the criminal edge that IN lacks: "Any person conducting a lottery by
referral selling is guilty of a misdemeanor of the first degree".

**one common element set governs both:** the presence of names/code, own-purchase inducement, and a
chance element determines which side of each ban a program falls on.

---

## the honest gap

no Indiana case law was found that applies § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9) to a modern link-based program. IN's
broader "otherwise helping… transactions" language deserves specific counsel attention — a
link-share arguably "helps" the supplier, so element 1 (no inducement of the referrer's own
purchase) is the sharper dividing line under IN than the names/link element.

practitioner commentary treats the act as a "sleeping giant" that is "liberally construed" to
protect consumers, and one firm counts "43+ enumerated unfair practices" — so an under-lawyered
referral design has real surface area even without published referral-selling precedent.

## the five-state baseline

IN is one cell of the five-state sweep required by `rule.require.five-state-baseline`. across TX, IN,
FL, CA, NY the same contingent-reward structure carries very different labels:

| state | label | strict? |
|-------|-------|---------|
| TX | DTPA deceptive act (civil) + pyramid = state-jail felony | |
| IN | civil deceptive act — **this brief** | |
| FL | criminal lottery (1st-deg misdemeanor) | |
| CA | criminal endless chain + private rescission | ⭐ |
| NY | chain distributor scheme deemed a security | |

**baseline = California** — the only one that stacks a crime with a private consumer rescission. IN is
the lighter-touch civil end of the range; a design built to clear CA's endless-chain line clears IN by
a wide margin. the full matrix with verbatim cites is in
