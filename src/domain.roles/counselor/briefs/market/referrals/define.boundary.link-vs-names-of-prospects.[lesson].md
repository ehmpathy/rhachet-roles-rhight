# define.boundary.link-vs-names-of-prospects

## .what

the sharpest redline that keeps a referral reward out of the "referral selling" ban: the referrer
shares a **link/code**, not a **list of names of prospective purchasers**.

## .why

- both IN and FL referral-selling bans hinge on the referrer's supply of "names of prospective
  purchasers" / "names of prospective consumers"
- a link the friend chooses to click is not "names the referrer hands to the seller"
- this is the single cleanest element on which the two sides diverge — and the one the major
  programs land outside of

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

**the separating element:** did the referrer **hand the seller names of prospective purchasers**?
a shared link does not; a submitted contact list does.

## why the statutes turn on "names"

### Florida

Fla. Stat. § 849.0915 declares referral selling a lottery. the statutory hook is the referrer's
supply of names, verbatim:

> "the seller gives or offers a rebate or discount to the buyer as an inducement for a sale in
> consideration of the buyer's **providing the seller with the names of prospective purchasers**"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.0915](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.0915)

### Indiana

Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9) treats the same conduct as a deceptive act, verbatim:

> "That the consumer will receive a rebate, discount, or other benefit as an inducement for
> entering into a sale or lease in return for **giving the supplier the names of prospective
> consumers** or **otherwise helping the supplier to enter into other consumer transactions**, if
> earning the benefit, rebate, or discount is contingent upon the occurrence of an event subsequent
> to the time the consumer agrees to the purchase or lease."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-3/)

both require the referrer to **hand over names**. a link-share flips the initiative: the **friend**
self-identifies by the choice to use the link — the referrer supplies no roster. note that Indiana
reaches one step further with "or otherwise helping the supplier," so the fact that the referrer
transfers **no names** does most of the work under both texts.

## which side real programs sit on

every major consumer program pays for a **link/code**, never for a prospect roster. each term below
was fetched from the program's own page and quoted verbatim.

| program | mechanic (verbatim) | side | source |
|---------|---------------------|------|--------|
| Uber | "share your referral link or code and earn Referral Rewards" | OUTSIDE (link) | [Uber referral terms](https://www.uber.com/legal/referral-program-rules/referral-terms-us-can-en) |
| DoorDash | "The Referrer provides the Referrer's unique referral link… through email, text, or the Share Your Link option" | OUTSIDE (link) | [DoorDash referral terms](https://help.doordash.com/consumers/s/referral-terms-english?language=en_US) |
| Airbnb | "Referral links should not be distributed where there is no reasonable basis for believing that all or most of the recipients are personal friends or family" | OUTSIDE (link) | [Airbnb referral terms](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3613) |
| PayPal | "A referrer may refer a referee by sending a personal message via the Program referral prompts ('Referral Link')" | OUTSIDE (link) | [PayPal Refer a Friend terms](https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/invite/terms) |
| Dropbox | "the users the option to copy and paste their unique referral link anywhere" | OUTSIDE (link/space) | [Dropbox referral program (referralrock)](https://referralrock.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program/) |
| old "name 10 friends for a rebate" schemes | submit a prospect list | INSIDE (names) | the classic target of the statute |

note: two big programs even **forbid** broadcast of the link to strangers — Uber says referral
codes "must be used only for personal and non-commercial purposes," and PayPal says "you must not
publish or distribute your Referral Link in public locations… where the audience is likely to
include people who are not your family or personal friends/acquaintances." a private, opt-in link
is the norm, not the exception.

## why the mechanic matters (compliance analysis)

modern link programs also lean on an **opt-in** step: the friend acts first, so no third party's
data reaches the seller unbidden. one compliance analysis frames the privacy default this way,
verbatim:

> "you cannot automatically add a friend's email to a marketing newsletter just because a customer
> provided it."

the same guide notes the disclosure norm — that a referral message must state something like:

> "Refer a friend and we'll both get a discount."

**source**: [are referral programs legal? — Growave](https://www.growave.io/blogs/growth-and-retention/are-referral-programs-legal-a-guide-to-compliance-and-growth)

the takeaway: a link program on the outside-the-ban side never receives a roster of un-consented
prospect names; the friend's own click is the trigger.

## where the two sides fall, element by element

| outside the ban | inside the ban |
|-----------------|----------------|
| a per-referrer **link or code** | a **list of prospects' names/contacts** collected to earn |
| the **friend** initiates by use of the link | the referrer **submits** people for solicitation |
| attribution via the link the friend used | attribution via a roster the referrer handed the seller |

> a "customer-stated" attribution (the friend names the referrer at checkout) stays outside the ban: the
> **friend** identifies the referrer, and no roster of prospect names is supplied. a design where the
> **referrer** hands over prospect names crosses this line.

---

## the honest gap

no case law was found that tests whether a modern link-share meets the "names of prospective
purchasers" element. the textual read is strong (a link is not a name-roster), but it remains a
counsel-confirmable position, not a settled holding. Indiana's broader "or otherwise helping the
supplier" clause is the softer edge — a design that leans on the referrer to actively work leads,
rather than just share a link, is where the analysis gets closer to the line.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what's the sharpest dividing element? | link/code, not names of prospects |
| why? | both statutes require the referrer to "provide names of prospective purchasers/consumers" |
| is customer-stated attribution outside the ban? | yes — the friend identifies the referrer, not a roster |
| what crosses the line? | a referrer submits a prospect list that the seller then solicits |
| do the major programs illustrate the outside-the-ban side? | yes — Uber, DoorDash, Airbnb, PayPal, Dropbox all pay for a link, never a roster |
| Indiana's extra reach? | its "or otherwise helping the supplier" clause reaches past mere name-lists |
