# hazard.contingent-reward-referral-selling.fl

## .what

in Florida, a referral reward structured the wrong way is not just a civil violation — it is
**declared an illegal lottery** and to conduct it is a **first-degree misdemeanor**.

the source statute sits inside **chapter 849 — gambling**, confirmed verbatim on the official
florida senate chapter index. a naive "give $10 when your friend buys" design maps
element-for-element onto the ban.

## .why

- Florida is an example of a stricter state on this point — it criminalizes the design, where a state like Indiana treats it as a civil deceptive act
- the statute maps almost element-for-element onto a naive "give $10 when your friend buys" design
- criminal exposure means this hazard turns on the structure itself, not on disclosure

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

## the hazard

```
NAIVE DESIGN: "collect your friends' names → get a rebate when they buy"
              │
              ▼
   rebate/discount to a buyer
              │
              ▼
   in exchange for NAMES of prospective purchasers
              │
              ▼
   contingent on the prospect later buying
              │
              ▼
   FL § 849.0915 → "declared to be a lottery"
              │
              ▼
   first-degree misdemeanor (s. 775.082 / 775.083)
              │
              ▼
   + AG / state attorney / Div. of Consumer Services injunction
```

## the statute

fla. stat. § 849.0915 has three subsections. all three are quoted verbatim below from the official
florida senate text.

subsection (1) — the definition and the lottery declaration:

> "Referral selling, whereby the seller gives or offers a rebate or discount to the buyer as an
> inducement for a sale in consideration of the buyer's providing the seller with the names of
> prospective purchasers, is declared to be a lottery if earning the rebate or discount is
> contingent upon the occurrence of an event subsequent to the time the buyer agrees to buy."

subsection (2) — the criminal penalty:

> "Any person conducting a lottery by referral selling is guilty of a misdemeanor of the first
> degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082 or s. 775.083."

subsection (3) — the injunction authority:

> "In addition to the penalty provided herein, the Attorney General and her or his assistants, the
> state attorneys and their assistants, and the Division of Consumer Services of the Department of
> Agriculture and Consumer Services are authorized to apply to the circuit court within their
> respective jurisdictions, and such court shall have jurisdiction, upon hearing and for cause
> shown, to grant a temporary or permanent injunction restraining any person from violating the
> provisions of this section, whether or not there exists an adequate remedy at law, and such
> injunction shall issue without bond."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.0915](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.0915)

so subsection (3) hands three separate enforcers — the attorney general, the state attorneys, and
the division of consumer services — a low-friction path to an injunction that "shall issue without
bond."

---

## what the penalty statutes actually say

the § 849.0915(2) cross-reference points to two penalty statutes. both were fetched from the
official florida senate text:

- **jail term** — per fla. stat. § 775.082(4)(a), a first-degree misdemeanor is punishable
  "by a definite term of imprisonment not exceeding 1 year".
- **fine** — per fla. stat. § 775.083(1)(d), the fine cap is "$1,000, when the conviction is
  of a misdemeanor of the first degree."

so the maximum criminal exposure per count is up to one year of jail plus a $1,000 fine, before
the separate injunction track.

## why it counts as a lottery (prize + chance + consideration)

florida's general lottery prohibition, fla. stat. § 849.09, forbids to "Set up, promote, or conduct
any lottery for money or for anything of value." a criminal-defense law firm summary of that chapter
defines an illegal florida lottery as:

> "a game of chance in which smaller sums of money or things of smaller value are risked for the
> chance of getting money or property of greater value upon the happening of an uncertain event."

that same firm notes that "referral selling lotteries are prohibited under Florida Statute
849.0915, alongside pyramid lotteries and chain letter lotteries." the classic lottery test is
prize + chance + consideration; § 849.0915 is the legislature's decision to **deem** a contingent
referral rebate a lottery, which is why the presence or absence of the chance element (a guaranteed
reward removes it) is decisive to the classification.

a second, unrelated florida sweepstakes-law firm frames the same three-part test. per the social
media law firm:

> "An illegal lottery exists when all three of the following elements are present: Prize, Chance,
> Consideration."

and, on how a lawful promo escapes that classification:

> "If any one of these elements is removed, the promotion no longer qualifies as a lottery under the
> law."

so two firms — from separate practices — converge on the same rule: remove one leg of the tripod
and the lottery ban falls away. for a referral reward, the chance leg is absent when the reward is
guaranteed to every qualifier.

## how Florida separates a lawful game promotion from an illegal lottery

Florida does permit prize-and-chance promos when they drop the consideration leg. fla. stat.
§ 849.094 defines the lawful vehicle:

> "a contest, game of chance, sweepstakes, or gift enterprise, conducted by an operator within or
> throughout the state and other states in connection with and incidental to the sale of consumer
> products or services, and in which the elements of chance and prize are present."

the florida department of agriculture & consumer services, which enforces § 849.094, spells out what
tips such a promo back into the unlawful zone. on its list of acts that make a game promotion
unlawful sits this one:

> "Requiring an entry fee, payment or proof of purchase to enter."

that is the consideration leg. it confirms the mirror-image lesson for a referral reward: a
purchase-gated reward supplies consideration, and a random-draw reward supplies chance — stack both
onto a prize and the promo collapses into the lottery bucket that § 849.0915 already deems referral
selling to be.

## the element analysis — the two sides of each element

| # | element | inside the ban | outside the ban |
|---|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | rebate/discount to a **buyer** | referrer must buy; rebate induces it | referrer earns via referral, need not buy |
| 2 | **as an inducement for a sale** | the reward is the carrot to buy | reward is a promo bonus |
| 3 | **names of prospective purchasers** | referrer submits a name list | referrer shares a **link/code** |
| 4 | **contingent on a later event** | prospect later buys | present on both sides — not sufficient alone |
| 5 | → deemed a **lottery** (needs chance) | sweepstakes / prize-draw flavor | reward **guaranteed** to every qualifier |

**where element 3 (a name list) and element 2 (a purchase inducement) are absent, and no chance
element is present, FL § 849.0915 does not attach; where all elements are present, it does.**

---

## severity

**blocker** (if the design has all elements). a first-degree misdemeanor + injunction risk is not a
disclosure-curable problem — the structure itself must avoid the elements.

---

## red flags

| red flag | why it's dangerous |
|----------|--------------------|
| "submit your friends' contact info to earn" | supplies "names of prospective purchasers" (element 3) |
| "buy now, earn rebates as you refer" | inducement for the referrer's own sale (element 2) |
| "refer a friend, be entered to win" | chance element → lottery (element 5) |
| reward paid only if the prospect later buys, AND referrer had to buy | full element stack |

## the parallel civil track (FDUTPA)

even where the criminal § 849.0915 elements do not stack, a deceptive referral promo can still draw
a civil claim under the florida deceptive and unfair trade practices act. fla. stat. § 501.204(1)
declares:

> "Unfair methods of competition, unconscionable acts or practices, and unfair or deceptive acts or
> practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful."

§ 501.204(2) adds that "due consideration and great weight shall be given to the interpretations of
the Federal Trade Commission and the federal courts". so FDUTPA is a second, civil layer to keep in
view for how a program is marketed, separate from the criminal lottery track.

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.204](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/501.204)

---

## the honest gap

no Florida case law was found that applies § 849.0915 to a modern link-based digital program. the
text maps cleanly onto the *naive* design; a link-based, guaranteed, earn-via-referral design lacks
the key elements — but that read remains a counsel-confirmable position. the FDUTPA overlay above is
the most likely fallback theory a regulator would reach for, so market copy is a separate review
surface.

## the five-state baseline

FL is one cell of the five-state sweep required by `rule.require.five-state-baseline`. across TX, IN,
FL, CA, NY the same contingent-reward structure carries very different labels:

| state | label | strict? |
|-------|-------|---------|
| TX | DTPA deceptive act (civil) + pyramid = state-jail felony | |
| IN | civil deceptive act | |
| FL | criminal lottery (1st-deg misdemeanor) — **this brief** | |
| CA | criminal endless chain + private rescission | ⭐ |
| NY | chain distributor scheme deemed a security | |

**baseline = California** — FL is criminal, but CA is stricter still: it pairs the crime (Penal § 327,
a wobbler up to three years) with a **private consumer rescission** (Civ § 1689.2) that FL's criminal
track does not add. a design built to clear CA's endless-chain line clears FL's lottery ban too. the
full matrix with verbatim cites is in `hazard.contingent-reward-referral-selling.five-state.[hazard].md`.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| how bad is FL? | referral selling = illegal **lottery**, first-degree misdemeanor |
| what is the maximum? | up to 1 year jail (§ 775.082) + $1,000 fine (§ 775.083) per count |
| does disclosure cure it? | no — the classification turns on the elements, not disclosure |
| the two elements that split the sides | names vs link, and own-purchase inducement vs promo bonus |
| plus | a guaranteed reward vs a random draw |
| civil fallback | FDUTPA § 501.204 can still reach deceptive promo copy |
