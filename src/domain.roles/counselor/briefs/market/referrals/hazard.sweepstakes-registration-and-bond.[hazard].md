# hazard.sweepstakes-registration-and-bond

## .what

once a referral or rewards promo adds a **chance** element — a random draw, a "refer a friend, be
entered to win" mechanic, a prize wheel — it can cross from a *guaranteed* reward (outside the lottery
bans) into a **game promotion / sweepstakes**. two of the five baseline states then impose an
**up-front registration + bond** duty *before* the promo launches, backed by criminal penalties. this
brief runs the five-state sweep (TX, IN, FL, CA, NY) required by `rule.require.five-state-baseline`
and names the strictest.

the punchline: a **guaranteed** reward for every qualifier carries no chance element and triggers no
registration. the moment the reward is a **prize awarded by chance** above a dollar threshold, NY and
FL demand a pre-launch filing, a trust account or surety bond equal to the prize pool, and expose the
operator to a misdemeanor for a missed filing.

## .why

- registration/bond duties bite **before** the promo runs — a launch without the filing is itself the
  violation, so this is a design-time gate, not a clean-up item.
- the trigger is the **chance** element (element 4 in `hazard.contingent-reward-referral-selling.five-state`)
  — this brief is the overlay for what happens once that element is present.
- the duty attaches per **served state**, and the two states that impose it (NY, FL) are high-
  population markets, so the strictest-common-denominator posture is to build to their bar.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate; verify current text and
> current dollar thresholds before any reliance.

## the five-state matrix

| state | pre-launch registration / bond? | verbatim-cited authority | trigger | strict? |
|-------|--------------------------------|--------------------------|---------|---------|
| TX | **no** registration/bond regime (mail-sweepstakes content rules only) | Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 622.051 | — | |
| IN | **no** general sweepstakes-registration statute found | (none found) | — | |
| FL | **yes** — file + establish a trust fund / bond for game promotions | Fla. Stat. § 849.094 | prize pool over $5,000 | ⭐ (tie) |
| CA | **no** registration/bond regime (official-rules + record-retention duties only) | Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17539.1 et seq. | — | |
| NY | **yes** — register 30 days pre-launch + trust account or bond = full prize value | N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 369-e | total announced prize value over $5,000 | ⭐ (tie) |

**baseline = NY (with FL close alongside)** — NY and FL both impose a pre-launch registration + bond;
NY adds a fixed **30-day** advance-filing window and a **class B misdemeanor** for a failure to file.
TX, IN, and CA regulate sweepstakes *content and fairness* but impose **no** registration or bond, so
they are outside this particular gate. a promo built to clear NY + FL clears the nationwide floor for
the chance overlay.

## the statutes verbatim

### NY — N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 369-e (registration + bond + criminal backstop)

the registration trigger — a prize pool over $5,000:

> "total announced value of the prizes offered is in excess of five thousand dollars"

the advance-filing window:

> filing "at least thirty days prior to the commencement of such game, contest or promotion."

the trust-or-bond duty:

> the operator must "establish and maintain a special trust account … with a balance sufficient to
> pay or purchase the total value of prizes offered," or, in the alternative, furnish "a bond, with
> sufficient sureties, in an amount equal to the total value of all prizes offered."

the criminal backstop:

> "Failure to file such statement shall be a class B misdemeanor."

**source**: [N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 369-e](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/369-E)

### FL — Fla. Stat. § 849.094 (game-promotion trust fund / bond)

Florida's game-promotion statute defines the lawful vehicle and, over the same $5,000 threshold,
requires the operator to file the rules and establish a trust fund or surety bond. the statutory
definition of the vehicle:

> "a contest, game of chance, sweepstakes, or gift enterprise, conducted by an operator within or
> throughout the state and other states in connection with and incidental to the sale of consumer
> products or services, and in which the elements of chance and prize are present."

the Florida Department of Agriculture & Consumer Services, which enforces § 849.094, lists the act
that tips a game promotion back into the unlawful zone:

> "Requiring an entry fee, payment or proof of purchase to enter."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.094](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.094)
· [FDACS — what makes a game promotion unlawful](https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Game-Promotions-Sweepstakes/What-makes-a-game-promotion-unlawful)

### TX — no registration/bond analogue

Texas Business & Commerce Code chapter 622 ("Sweepstakes") exists but imposes **no** pre-launch
registration or bond. it is limited to deceptive-content regulation of mail sweepstakes:

> "This chapter applies only to a sweepstakes conducted through the mail."

**source**: [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 622.051](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_622.051)
(fallback mirror — official portal not machine-readable.) **no TX registration/bond analogue found.**

### CA — official-rules regime, no bond

California regulates sweepstakes **disclosure and fairness** (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17539.1 et seq. —
official rules, no-purchase-necessary parity, prohibited practices) but imposes **no** pre-launch
registration or prize bond. **no CA registration/bond analogue found** for the bond duty specifically.

**source**: [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17539.1](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17539.1.)

### IN — no general registration statute

no Indiana general sweepstakes-registration statute was found; Indiana reaches deceptive promo copy
through the Deceptive Consumer Sales Act (Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5) rather than a pre-launch filing.
**no IN registration/bond analogue found.**

## how to stay outside this gate

| lever | keeps the promo outside registration |
|-------|--------------------------------------|
| **guarantee the reward** | every qualifier earns it → no chance element → not a sweepstakes at all |
| **cap the prize pool** | keep the total announced prize value at or under the $5,000 NY/FL threshold (a per-state check) |
| **drop the chance mechanic** | no draw, no wheel, no "be entered to win" — a fixed bonus is not a game promotion |

the first lever is the cleanest: a **guaranteed** referral or review reward has no prize-by-chance, so
none of the five states' game-promotion regimes attach. the registration duty is a hazard only for a
program that deliberately adds a draw.

---

## severity

**blocker** (if a chance-based promo over the threshold launches in NY or FL without the filing +
bond). a missed NY filing is a class B misdemeanor on its own; the bond ties up capital equal to the
full prize pool. this is a pre-launch structural gate, not disclosure-curable.

---

## the honest gap

- the **$5,000** thresholds and the **30-day** NY window are current as quoted; dollar thresholds and
  windows move — counsel should verify the live text before a launch.
- the **Texas** and some **California** citations rest on public-law / official-portal fetches noted
  above; confirm TX against the official pdf.
- states outside the five (e.g. Rhode Island's retail-store sweepstakes registration) add their own
  gates — the five-state sweep is the high-population baseline, not the whole map.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| strictest of the five? | **NY** (30-day filing + bond + class B misdemeanor); FL close alongside |
| the trigger? | a **chance** element with a prize pool over $5,000 |
| which states have no bond gate? | TX, IN, CA (they regulate content/fairness, not registration) |
| the clean lever? | **guarantee** the reward — no chance, no game promotion |
