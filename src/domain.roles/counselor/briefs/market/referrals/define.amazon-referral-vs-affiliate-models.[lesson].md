# define.amazon-referral-vs-affiliate-models

## .what

amazon runs **two legally distinct kinds** of pay-for-a-referral program, and they classify
differently:

- a **referral program** (Prime refer-a-friend) — a consumer refers a friend, both earn a
  platform credit. this is a lawful marketing reward, not compensation.
- an **affiliate program** (Amazon Associates) — a publisher drives a sale through a tracked link
  and earns a **commission** (a percentage of the sale). this is **nonemployee compensation** — a
  routine payable the issuer funds from its own revenue, reported on **1099-NEC**.

this brief maps the two and draws the line between a **referral reward** and an **affiliate
commission**. the same "pay for a referral" surface can carry either legal character; the line
decides the tax character and the disclosure duty for each.

## .why

- amazon is a reference model for a program whose rewards are earned via reviews or referrals
  attributed to sales
- a consumer refer→reward is a lawful marketing credit
- an affiliate cash-out is a lawful, routine commission the issuer pays out of its own revenue
  (NOT money transmission), with an unconditional 1099-NEC duty
- the two shapes look similar (both pay for a referral) but sit in different legal buckets; the
  bucket decides the tax character and the disclosure duty

## the two amazon models, side by side

| attribute | Prime refer-a-friend (REFERRAL reward) | Amazon Associates (AFFILIATE commission) |
|-----------|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| who earns | a **consumer** who refers a friend | a **publisher / creator** who drives a sale |
| what they share | an **invite link** to a friend they know | a **tracked link** (Special Link) to any audience |
| trigger | friend signs up for Prime as a new member | anyone clicks the link and buys a qualifying item |
| reward form | an **Amazon account credit** (no cash value) | **cash commission** — a percentage of the sale |
| funded by | amazon (its own promo budget) | amazon (its own revenue share) |
| legal character | a marketing reward | **nonemployee compensation** |
| tax duty | none to the consumer (a discount) | **1099-NEC** to the associate |

both pay for a referral. the fork is **who earns and in what form** — a consumer earns a capped
account credit; a publisher earns a cash commission the issuer books as a payable.

---

## model 1 — Prime refer-a-friend (the referral reward)

a consumer shares an invite link with a friend; when the friend joins Prime as a new member, the
consumer earns an amazon credit. the reward is a **capped account credit**, never cash — the same
shape as the Uber rider credit.

verbatim analysis of the program confirms the credit form (not cash):

> "The benefit of the friendship was the $5 credit for me."

and that a plain amazon account was the only entry requirement (no separate publisher onboarding):

> "All you needed to make a referral was an Amazon.com account."

**source**: [Amazon Prime referral program analysis (ReferralCandy)](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/lessons-from-amazon-primes-referral-program)

the credit lands in the referrer's amazon account after the referred friend's first order ships,
and it functions as a one-time, non-refundable, non-cash discount — not a payment:

> "Upon the new cardmember's approval for the Amazon Visa, the $50 Amazon Gift Card will be loaded
> into your Amazon.com account."

**source**: [Amazon refer-a-friend reward mechanics (Doctor of Credit)](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/amazon-prime-cardholders-get-50-when-referring-a-friend-to-amazon-visa-up-to-500/);
[Amazon Prime Referral Program Terms and Conditions](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=202075790)

### why this classifies as a referral reward, not compensation

| element | Prime refer-a-friend | reading |
|---------|----------------------|---------|
| what is shared | a link to a friend | not a name list, not a service |
| what the referrer does | refers, does not sell | no service performed for amazon |
| reward form | capped account credit, no cash value | a discount, not a payment |
| tax character | none | a discount reduces price; it is not income for services |

a consumer credit is a price discount, not a fee for services. it does not become nonemployee
compensation, so amazon owes the referrer no 1099. this is the **referral-reward side** of the line
— a fixed, capped, non-cash consumer credit.

---

## model 2 — Amazon Associates (the affiliate commission)

a publisher enrolls in Associates, generates **tracked links** to amazon products, and earns a
**commission** — a percentage of each qualifying sale the link drives. amazon describes the program
as an affiliate program in its own words:

> "Welcome to one of the largest affiliate marketing programs in the world."

> "Earn up to 10% in associate commissions from qualifying purchases and programs."

**source**: [Amazon Associates home](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/)

the commission is earned when a customer engages a tracked link and buys — the operating agreement's
own words:

> "When our customers click through or engage with the Special Links to purchase an item sold or
> services offered on the Amazon Site or take other actions, you can receive commission income for
> qualifying purchases."

**source**: [Amazon Associates Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement)

### why this classifies as a commission (nonemployee compensation)

the associate performs a **service** — the referral of a sale — and earns a **fee** measured as a
percentage of that sale. that is the textbook definition of nonemployee compensation. the IRS
instructions name the exact category:

> "Include fees, commissions, prizes and awards for services performed as a nonemployee, and other
> forms of compensation for services performed for your trade or business by an individual who is
> not your employee."

**source**: [Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (IRS)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)

so amazon reports it. amazon's own tax help states the duty plainly:

> "Form 1099-NEC is used to report nonemployee compensation (e.g. service income) to U.S. payees."

> "If you are a U.S. payee and received nonemployee compensation totaling $600 or more, Amazon is
> required to provide you a 1099-NEC form."

**source**: [Amazon Associates tax information help](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GW3SWP5L5WL5G9HY)

and the associate is not an employee — the agreement states the independent-contractor status:

> "You and we are independent contractors, and nothing in this Agreement will create any
> partnership, joint venture, agency, franchise, sales representative, or employment relationship
> between you and us."

**source**: [Amazon Associates Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement)

## the line — referral reward vs affiliate commission

```
              a consumer refers a friend                a publisher drives a sale
                        |                                          |
              shares an INVITE LINK                       shares a TRACKED LINK
                        |                                          |
          friend joins Prime as new member          any buyer clicks + buys qualifying item
                        |                                          |
              earns an ACCOUNT CREDIT                    earns a CASH COMMISSION (% of sale)
                (capped, no cash value)               (paid from amazon's own revenue share)
                        |                                          |
              => a marketing DISCOUNT                    => nonemployee COMPENSATION
                        |                                          |
                   NO 1099 duty                            1099-NEC duty (>= $600)
                        |                                          |
              the REFERRAL-REWARD side                    the AFFILIATE-COMMISSION side
                 (a consumer credit)                       (a cash commission for a service)
```

| test | referral reward | affiliate commission |
|------|-----------------|----------------------|
| is a service performed for the issuer? | no — a friend referral | yes — a sale driven for a fee |
| reward measured against a sale? | no — a fixed credit | yes — a percentage of the sale |
| reward form | capped account credit, no cash value | cash, paid to the earner |
| funded by | issuer promo budget | issuer revenue share |
| income to the earner? | no (a discount) | yes (nonemployee compensation) |
| 1099 duty | none | 1099-NEC at/above threshold |

the affiliate commission is a **percentage of a sale, paid in cash, for a service** — every marker
of nonemployee compensation. the referral reward is a **fixed, capped, non-cash credit for a friend
referral** — a marketing discount. same "pay for a referral" surface, opposite tax buckets.

---

## which classification attaches to which reward form

the legal character of a pay-for-referral reward follows its **form and trigger**, not its label. the
two amazon models mark the two ends:

| reward form | amazon parallel | classification | tax character |
|-------------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| a consumer refer→reward (fixed, capped credit) | Prime refer-a-friend | referral reward | discount, no 1099 |
| a %-of-sale cash payout for a service | Amazon Associates | affiliate commission | nonemployee comp → 1099-NEC |

a %-of-sale cash commission is a lawful, routine payable an issuer pays from its own revenue for a
sale the earner's review/referral helped drive. it is **not** money transmission — amazon does not
hold the earner's money and hand it back; amazon owes its own money as a payable for a service. that
payable carries an unconditional 1099-NEC duty, exactly as amazon issues to its associates. see the
rewards-debt brief for why an issuer-owed payable stays off the money-transmitter, stored-value, and
escheat regimes.

---

## a lawful reward still triggers a disclosure duty

both models make the earner a paid endorser under FTC rules — a fact to disclose, not a reason the
reward is unlawful. 16 CFR § 255.5 states the trigger:

> "When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product that
> might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not
> reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously."

and it names the very benefit these programs pay:

> "Material connections can include monetary payment or the provision of free or discounted products
> (including products unrelated to the endorsed product)."

**source**: [16 CFR § 255.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5)

for the affiliate model, amazon operationalizes this with a mandated on-site statement (covered in
the associates case study). the point: disclosure attaches to **both** models; the tax character
diverges.

---

## the honest gap

amazon's public materials do not label the Prime credit "not a 1099 event" in so many words — that
reading follows from tax character (a price discount is not income for services), not from an amazon
statement. and the Associates 1099-NEC duty is stated plainly by amazon, but the precise reportable
category (NEC vs MISC) has shifted over the years and depends on the payment type. the classification
here is text-strong but remains a **counsel judgment call** on a program's exact facts, not settled
case law applied to a services rewards program.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| does amazon run one referral program or two kinds? | two — a consumer referral reward and an affiliate commission |
| what is the consumer refer-a-friend reward? | a capped, non-cash account credit — a marketing discount |
| what is an Associates payout? | a cash commission (% of sale) — nonemployee compensation |
| what distinguishes an affiliate commission? | a %-of-sale cash payout for a service |
| is that commission money transmission? | no — an issuer-funded payable for a service |
| does it carry a 1099 duty? | yes — 1099-NEC at/above the threshold |
| does a lawful reward still trigger any duty? | yes — FTC material-connection disclosure, for both models |
