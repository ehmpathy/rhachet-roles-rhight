# define.referral-reward-vs-referral-selling

## .what

a modern "refer a friend, both get $10" reward and an old-school illegal "referral selling" scheme
look similar but are legally opposite. this brief draws the line.

## .why

- Indiana and Florida are two examples of states with a "referral selling" ban on the books
- a naive reward design can fall inside it; a link-based, guaranteed reward does not
- the difference is structural, not cosmetic — the statute draws a line by its elements

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

---

## the two things, side by side

| attribute | referral REWARD (lawful) | referral SELLING (banned) |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| what the referrer gives | a **link/code** the friend chooses to use | **names of prospective purchasers** |
| is the referrer induced to buy? | no — earns by referral, need not purchase | yes — rebate is an inducement for the referrer's OWN sale |
| chance element? | no — guaranteed to every qualified referrer | often a lottery/drawing |
| contingent on a later event? | yes (friend buys a subscription) | yes (prospect later buys) |

the contingency (last row) is present in BOTH — so contingency alone is not the tripwire. the ban
needs the **other elements too** (names-of-prospects + inducement-of-own-purchase, and it deems a
chance element a lottery).

## the statutes that define "referral selling"

### Florida — deemed an illegal lottery

> "Referral selling, whereby the seller gives or offers a rebate or discount to the buyer as an
> inducement for a sale in consideration of the buyer's providing the seller with the names of
> prospective purchasers, is declared to be a lottery if earning the rebate or discount is
> contingent upon the occurrence of an event subsequent to the time the buyer agrees to buy."

the criminal exposure is explicit:

> "Any person conducting a lottery by referral selling is guilty of a misdemeanor of the first
> degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082 or s. 775.083."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.0915](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.0915)

### Indiana — a deceptive act

> "That the consumer will receive a rebate, discount, or other benefit as an inducement for
> entering into a sale or lease in return for giving the supplier the names of prospective
> consumers or otherwise helping the supplier to enter into other consumer transactions, if
> earning the benefit, rebate, or discount is contingent upon the occurrence of an event
> subsequent to the time the consumer agrees to the purchase or lease."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-3(b)(9)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-3/)

### why the Indiana label carries teeth

a "deceptive act" is not a dead letter — the remedy statute arms both the state and private
plaintiffs. the private hook:

> "A person relying upon an uncured or incurable deceptive act may bring an action for the damages
> actually suffered as a consumer as a result of the deceptive act or five hundred dollars ($500),
> whichever is greater."

and the state hook:

> "bring an action to enjoin an unfair, abusive, or deceptive act, omission, or practice in
> connection with a consumer transaction."

so a mislabeled program invites both an AG injunction and a private (even class) suit.

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-4/)

## the element test — a program is "referral selling" ONLY if ALL are true

| # | element | how a design falls on either side |
|---|---------|-----------------------------------|
| 1 | benefit to a **buyer** | inside: the benefit is conditioned on the referrer's own purchase / outside: the referrer earns via referral, not as a condition of their own purchase |
| 2 | as an **inducement for [the buyer's own] sale** | inside: the reward is a carrot to make the referrer buy / outside: the reward is a marketing bonus |
| 3 | in return for **names of prospective purchasers** | inside: the referrer submits a list of names / outside: the referrer shares a **link/code** |
| 4 | **contingent on a later event** | present on both sides — NOT sufficient on its own |
| 5 (FL) | plus a **chance** character → lottery | inside: a drawing / raffle / outside: the reward is **guaranteed** to every qualified referrer |

if element 2 or 3 is absent (and no chance element is present), the program sits outside the ban;
if all are present, it sits inside.

### the lottery frame, confirmed by secondary analysis

the FL statute deems referral selling a "lottery." the classic lottery test needs three things —
and the absence of any one is fatal to the label:

> "An illegal lottery exists when **all three** of the following elements are present: **Prize**,
> **Chance**, **Consideration**. This definition is consistent across U.S. jurisdictions and is
> grounded in long-standing gambling statutes. If _any one_ of these elements is removed, the
> promotion no longer qualifies as a lottery under the law."

a guaranteed, skill-free reward to every qualified referrer removes the **chance** element:

> "To offer lawful promotions, brands rely on two alternative legal structures: **Contests**, which
> eliminate chance by relying on skill; **Sweepstakes**, which eliminate consideration while
> retaining chance."

**source**: [The Social Media Law Firm — the three elements of an illegal lottery](https://thesocialmedialawfirm.com/blog/sweepstakes-law/the-three-elements-of-an-illegal-lottery-prize-chance-and-consideration/)

## the outside-the-ban structure, in the words of the programs that use it

the "share a link, not names" structure is not theoretical — it is the term-of-service template of
the largest referral programs, and it illustrates the outside-the-ban side of each element.

### Uber — a personal link/code, not a name list

> "As long as you and your referee/invitee follow these Rules...you should receive your Referral
> Reward after your referee/invitee uses your link or code to sign up with Uber...and complete the
> requirements."

and it is capped and non-public:

> "Referrers are limited to payouts in connection with a combined total of 10 referral offers"

**source**: [Uber Referral Program Rules](https://www.uber.com/legal/referral-program-rules/referral-terms-us-can-en/)

### DoorDash — link direct to a known person, credit on the friend's order

> "the Referrer's unique referral link directly to the New User through email, text, or the Share
> Your Link option."

credit turns on the friend's own order, not on any name handed over:

> "registers on the DoorDash platform using the Referral Link provided...and meets the offer
> requirements set forth in the Referral Link, which typically includes placing an order on the
> DoorDash platform with a certain minimum subtotal."

**source**: [DoorDash Consumer Referral Program Terms](https://help.doordash.com/consumers/s/referral-terms-english?language=en_US)

### Chase — the referrer need not buy at all

the friend applies through the referrer's link; the referrer's own purchase is irrelevant:

> "When your friend applies for a Chase credit card using your URL and their application is
> approved, both you and your friend receive the promotional rewards"

and the link is the sole trigger — no name list, no referrer sale:

> "If your friend does not use the unique URL you will not receive credit for the referral."

**source**: [Chase Refer-A-Friend](https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/basics/chase-refer-a-friend)

## a reward triggers a disclosure duty (a related, separate rule)

even a lawful reward makes the referrer a paid endorser under FTC rules — a fact to disclose, not a
reason the reward is illegal:

> "When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product that
> might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not
> reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously."

the rule names the very benefit a referral pays out:

> "monetary payment or the provision of free or discounted products"

**source**: [16 CFR § 255.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5)

## the honest gap

no court opinion or AG guidance was found that applies these older statutes to a modern link-based
program. the text-level analysis is strong (link ≠ names; marketing bonus ≠ inducement-to-buy), but
**application to a modern program is a counsel judgment call, not settled case law.** the FL
consumer-protection backdrop (FDUTPA) is broad enough to reach conduct the referral-selling statute
does not name outright:

> "Unfair methods of competition, unconscionable acts or practices, and unfair or deceptive acts or
> practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.204](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/501.204)

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| is "refer a friend, get credit" inherently illegal? | no |
| what makes it "referral selling"? | names-of-prospects + inducement of the referrer's own purchase (+ chance in FL) |
| does the contingency (friend must transact) alone trip it? | no — every program has it |
| what design sits outside the ban? | a **link**-based, earn-via-referral (not purchase), guaranteed reward |
| does a lawful reward create any duty? | yes — FTC material-connection disclosure |
| is this settled? | text is clear; application to modern link programs is untested |
