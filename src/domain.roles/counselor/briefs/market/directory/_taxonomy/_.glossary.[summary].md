# _.glossary — the directory term index

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate. each term points to the
> brief that defines it; verify current text there before reliance.

> scan-first index of the terms used across the `market/directory/` law briefs. one line each, with
> the legal lens that owns it and a link to the brief that defines it.
>
> this is a **navigation artifact** — it makes no independent factual claim, so it inherits its
> citations from the briefs it points to (per `rule.require.seven-distinct-citations` exception,
> same posture as `market/_taxonomy/_.glossary`).

## why a glossary first

a directory says things about people and companies. **which** body of law owns a given statement
turns entirely on terms that look interchangeable and are not: *unknown* is not *unverified*,
*claimed* means two different things, and **a business is not a consumer**. the wrong label imports
the wrong regime — and one of these terms decides whether a federal statute applies at all.

name the things before we classify them.

## ⭐ the two terms that carry the most weight

### `consumer` is not a synonym for "the subject"

> ⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE CANONICAL HOME — for the *analysis*, not for the words.** § 1681a(c) is a
> **canonical atom**, not canonical analysis: restate it wherever it helps, **always with its section
> number**, so one grep finds every copy. see § *canonical atom vs canonical analysis* below.
>
> seven briefs in this cluster turn on the same six words. one statute, one home for the *analysis*
> of what it does — and seven honest restatements of the six words themselves.

`[observed]` **15 U.S.C. § 1681a(c): "The term 'consumer' means an individual."**

so a business entity is **not** a consumer, and a report about one is **not** a consumer report,
whatever the reader intends to do with it. the same distinction runs through Cal. Civ. Code
§ 1798.99.80(c), which defines a data broker by reference to *"the personal information of a
consumer."*

**this single definition is why subject grain sits above every other axis.** get the grain wrong and
the brief cites a statute that does not apply.

⭐ **the seven that restate it** — `define.boundary.entity-grain-vs-person-grain`,
`define.boundary.who-speaks-decides-the-regime`, `define.boundary.consent-cures-privacy-not-fcra`,
`hazard.consumer-report-gate`, `hazard.go-private-request`, `hazard.reincorporation-blind-spot`, and
this glossary. ⚠️ **the list is derived by command, not by memory** — `rhx grepsafe` on the verbatim
string.

⚠️ **why one home matters more here than for a typical shared quote:** this is not a convenience —
**it is the hinge the entire entity-grain posture hangs on.** if § 1681a(c) were ever amended, six
briefs would need to change together, and a copy missed is a brief that still asserts an exit from a
regime the directory no longer has.

### ⭐ canonical atom vs canonical analysis — the distinction that governs which one you may copy

| | **canonical analysis** | ⭐ **canonical atom** |
|---|---|---|
| example | the grain→regime table; the five-state ROP sweep; the § 1798.99.80(c) conjunctive prongs | § 1681a(c) — *"The term 'consumer' means an individual."* |
| what a copy risks | ⛔ **interpretive drift** — two copies reach different conclusions and neither knows | ⚠️ **version drift only** — the words are the statute's, not ours |
| how long it is | paragraphs to pages | **six words** |
| what a pointer costs the reader | little — they meant to read the analysis anyway | ⛔ **more than the quote** — a jump to another file for a phrase shorter than the link |
| verdict | ⛔ **never duplicate** | ✅ **duplicate freely, under one control** |

⛔ **and the harm runs the other way for an atom.** a brief that asserts *"FCRA cannot reach an
entity"* and makes the reader leave to learn why is **more** likely to be misapplied, not less — the
six words *are* the reason, and a reader who skips the pointer never sees that the whole posture
rests on a definition rather than on an argument.

### the control that actually answers the amendment risk

the glossary's stated fear is precise and correct: *an amendment, and a copy missed.* **but the
control for that is not scarcity — it is greppability.**

> ✅ **every restatement carries the section number in the identical form `§ 1681a(c)`.** verified:
> all seven do. so `rhx grepsafe --pattern '1681a\(c\)'` returns the complete change set in one
> command, and an amendment becomes a mechanical sweep rather than a memory exercise.

⭐ **so the requirement is not *"quote it once"* — it is *"never quote it without its section
number"*.** that is checkable, cheap, and it survives any growth in the file count.

**the corrected rule, and it applies to every short statutory definition in this cluster:**

- ⛔ **canonical analysis** — one owner, cite it, never restate it. unchanged.
- ✅ **canonical atom** — a verbatim statutory phrase under ~20 words may be restated anywhere,
  **provided it carries its section number verbatim**, so a grep finds every instance.
- ⛔ **a restatement without the section number = blocker** — that, and only that, is what makes an
  amendment unsweepable.

> ⚠️ **this is the cluster's one enforceable check that sits OUTSIDE a `.enforcement` block**, and it
> hid from `hazard._.catalogue` § *the AUDIT INDEX* for exactly that reason — an index built by a scan
> for enforcement sections cannot see a rule stated inline in prose. it now carries a row there.
> **the check stays here, where the convention it enforces is defined; the index knows about it.**

⚠️ **and the meta-lesson matches the citation audit's exactly:** the declaration was made and the
population never checked. **the structure is the easy half.** here the population was not merely
incomplete — it was *universally* contrary, which is the tell that the rule, and not the authors, was
the defect.

### `verified` — the usage rule, because the word is already taken

⚠️ **`verified` is already a term of art in this brief tree.** `market/reviews/define.boundary.
verified-vs-fake-review` uses it for a **verified purchase** — the platform confirmed a transaction
occurred.

`[inferred]` **the settlement is not a rename — it is a usage rule**, because the two senses are
actually the *same* sense: *"verified X" = "X was confirmed."* the ambiguity comes from the bare word,
not from the concept.

> ⭐ **`verified` is never used alone. it always qualifies the noun it confirmed.**
> ✅ `verified license` · `verified permit` · `verified registration` · `verified purchase`
> ⛔ a bare `verified` badge, or "a verified pro"

this satisfies `rule.require.ubiqlang` (one term, one sense) and `rule.require.treestruct`
(`[...noun][state]`) at once, and it stays consistent with the extant `market/reviews/` usage rather
than a fork from it. ⚠️ the alternative — a rename to `confirmed` — was declined because it would
have created exactly the synonym `rule.forbid.term.addition.synonym` prohibits.

## how the terms relate

```
subject grain ────────────── decides which regime can apply at all
  ├─ business entity ─────── no FCRA, no ROP, no broker duty; defamation + accuracy remain
  └─ natural person ──────── the full privacy stack; entered via the qualifier, the sole prop, the DBA

fact class ───────────────── decides whether a favorable result is publishable
  ├─ presence-fact ───────── a record exists, and it carries a STATUS
  │    ├─ status favorable ─ → assertable → `verified $noun`
  │    └─ status adverse ─── → NOT assertable → `unknown`   ⭐ the correction
  └─ absence-fact ────────── favorable = no record exists → never assertable → `unknown`

assertion state (internal) ─ verified-positive · self-attested · verified-negative · unknown
        │
        └── lossy projection ──> badge state (public) — verified $noun · claimed · unknown
```

### ⛔ two synonym collisions, resolved rather than left to drift

⭐ **per `rule.forbid.domain-term-synonyms`: adhere, or argue — never drift.**

| the collision | resolution |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **`linkage` (dimension) vs `linkage key` (attribute)** | **both kept, and they are NOT synonyms.** a *linkage key* is a specific attribute (an officer name, a qualifier's licence); *linkage* is the **dimension** that asks whether a datum is self-keyed or match-required. ⭐ **the dimension classifies; the key is an instance.** ⛔ do not collapse them — `linkage key` is person-grain by nature and `linkage` is grain-neutral |
| ⚠️ **`claimed` (badge state) vs `claimed profile` (relationship) vs `claimed tenure` (a self-attested fact)** | **three senses, one word, and the glossary already flagged two of them.** ⭐ the shared root is correct and load-bearing — all three mean *"the subject said so, and we attribute it to them"*, which is the § 230(f)(3) line. ⚠️ **so the overload is disciplined rather than accidental**, and the rule is: `claimed` never stands alone — always `claimed profile`, `claimed tenure`, or the badge state `claimed` in a rendered row |

⛔ **and one term was RENAMED this round, for a reason worth a note.** the applied inventory was
`ref.clearance-inventory`. `ref.` in this tree means *an external public lookup* — every other `ref.*`
is a case study or a statute table — so the prefix asserted **fullsun by nature** for a file that was
🔒 protect. ⭐ **the type error and the publishability error were the same error.** renamed to
`inventory.of=clearance` per `rule.require.order.noun_adj` ([noun][adj]) and moved out of the
shippable tree.

### ⭐⭐ `floor` — the third artifact shape, and the cluster has no member for it

⚠️ **a `floor` is not a `rule` and not an `inventory`, and the cluster carries the first two while it
names the third nowhere.** the three are distinct **shapes of the same knowledge**:

| shape | what it is | reader's question | this cluster's member |
|---|---|---|---|
| **rule** | a **test** — conjunctive gates, run per datum | *"does THIS datum clear?"* | ✅ `rule.require.opt-out-bounded-to-entity-registry-facts` |
| **inventory** | a **result set** — the test already run, per datum | *"what did the test say about the 40 datums?"* | ✅ `inventory.of=clearance` (🔒, out of tree) |
| ⭐ **floor** | the **line under which we never go** — declarative, one screen, no test to run | *"what will you never do?"* | ⛔ **absent** |

⭐ **the distinction is legible in who reads each.** a rule is read by an implementer with a datum in
hand. an inventory is read by an auditor. **a floor is read by whoever must decide whether to trust
the design at all** — a wisher, a client, adverse counsel — and none of them will run a four-gate
test to find out what we promise.

> ⛔ **so a cluster that owns a rule and an inventory has stated its floor twice and declared it never
> once.** the floor is derivable from the rule, and *derivable* is the defect: the whole
> asymmetric-disclosure defence rests on prior announcement of the policy, and an announcement nobody
> can read in one screen is not one.

⚠️ **the term is settled; the BRIEF TYPE is not.** `define.` / `rule.` / `hazard.` / `ref.` has no
member that carries a declarative floor, so the artifact has no home. **carried to council as a
type-vocabulary gap** — tracked with the vocabulary gaps below, because that is exactly what it is.

### ⭐⭐ the gap kinds — a `G-*` key is not one kind of item

`hazard._.catalogue.[summary].md` tracks every open question under a `G-<slug>` key. ⚠️ **the keys
read as a flat list and they are not** — they split on **what is absent**, which decides **who can
close it**. a tracker that hides this ranks a wisher call beside a lookup and puts both in a
research queue where one of them can never be closed.

| kind | what is absent | who closes it | worked example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fact gap** | a datum about the world | a researcher, per state — cheap and bounded | `G-qualifier-key` — does a state licence search return the qualifier's number, or is it a records request? |
| ⭐ **vocabulary gap** | a **word** in *our own* model | the wisher — ⛔ **no capture helps** | `G-attested-provenance` — an insurer's certificate is neither `verified` (us) nor `claimed` (them); the fourth state does not exist |
| **authority gap** | a legal source we have not captured | a capture pass | `G-nominative-fair-use` — the doctrine exists in case law under § 1125(a); the case law was not pulled |

⭐ **the vocabulary gap is the one to name loudest**, because it is the only kind that a research pass
**cannot** close and therefore the only kind that survives an otherwise-complete sweep. it looks
identical to the others in the tracker and behaves not at all like them.

⚠️ **and the three carry different risk if left open.** a fact gap leaves a row `unsettled`. an
authority gap leaves a defence `[unverified]` — flagged, never relied on. a vocabulary gap leaves a
**product capability unbuildable**, which no amount of legal work reveals.

> ⭐ **the counselor-root corollary:** an authority gap is the tell of a **won defence** — we need
> the case law precisely because the predicate attaches. a design that reaches an **unmet element**
> instead has no authority gap to open. see `rule.prefer.unmet-element-over-won-defense`.
