# rule.prefer.metadata-over-republication

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate; verify current text before
> reliance. every claim is tagged `[observed]` (quoted from a captured source), `[inferred]`
> (reasoning over those sources), or `[unverified]` (not yet sourced).

## .what

**where a third party's expression carries a fact we want, publish the FACT and decline the
EXPRESSION.**

- ⛔ **republication** — *"Great crew, showed up on time, fixed the panel same day. — J. Martinez"*
- ✅ **metadata** — *"412 reviews on Google from 380 customers, 4.6 average, read 2026-08-04"*

⭐ **the two are not a quality gradient. they sit on opposite sides of copyright's subject-matter
line**, and the second never enters it.

## ⭐ .why this is worth a rule rather than a preference

`[observed]` **17 U.S.C. § 107** makes fair use a defense with mandatory factors — *"the factors to be
considered **shall include**"* — and two of those factors point the wrong way for a commercial
directory:

- factor (1): *"whether such use is of a **commercial nature**"*
- factor (4): *"the effect of the use upon the **potential market** for or value of the copyrighted
  work"*

`[inferred]` verbatim republication of a review substitutes for a visit to the source platform, which
is precisely the market-effect factor (4) measures.

> ⭐⭐ **a defense you must prove is strictly worse than a scope you never enter.** fair use is
> fact-intensive, decided late, and costs the same to win as to lose up to the judgment. metadata does
> not reach it, because it does not reach copyright.

## .the three layers, and they must be answered separately

⛔ **a "yes" on copyright is not a "yes" overall.** the metadata form clears the first layer outright,
narrows the second, and creates a duty at the third.

### 1. copyright — ✅ the COUNT is outside the subject matter; ⛔ the SCORE is not

`[observed]` **§ 102(a)** extends protection to *"**original works of authorship** fixed in any
tangible medium of expression."*

⭐ **and the Supreme Court says the rest of it outright.** `[observed]` **Feist Publications v. Rural
Telephone Service Co., 499 U.S. 340 (1991)**:

> *"The **sine qua non** of copyright is originality… **No one may claim originality as to facts.**
> This is because facts do not owe their origin to an act of authorship. The distinction is one
> between **creation and discovery**: the first person to find and report a particular fact has not
> created the fact; he or she has merely discovered its existence… **The discoverer merely finds and
> records.**"*

⛔⛔ **and one line of Feist decides this rule's whole question**, because it addresses a **competing
compiler** by name:

> `[observed]` *"the copyright in a factual compilation is **thin**. Notwithstanding a valid
> copyright, **a subsequent compiler remains free to use the facts contained in another's publication
> to aid in preparing a competing work**, so long as the competing work does not feature the same
> **selection and arrangement**."*

`[observed]` § 102(b)'s list is *"idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept,
principle, or discovery"* — **"fact" is absent from it as a word.**

> ⛔ **CORRECTED — this line previously concluded *"which is why Feist rather than § 102(b) is the
> citation."* that framed them as rivals and they are not.**
>
> `[observed]` **Feist**, 499 U.S. at 356: *"**§ 102(b) is universally understood to prohibit any
> copyright in facts.** … Accord, Nimmer § 2.03[E] (equating facts with 'discoveries')."*
>
> ⭐ **so a fact enters § 102(b) as a *"discovery"* — the last item in the very list quoted above.**
> Feist is not a substitute for the statutory citation; it is **what tells you how to read it**. cite
> both. see `define.boundary.promotional-vs-sensitive` § *CORRECTED*, which carries the full account.

`[observed]` **§ 103(b)** supplies the statutory half: a compilation copyright *"extends **only to the
material contributed by the author** … and **does not imply any exclusive right in the preexisting
material**."*

#### ⛔⛔ CORRECTED — the aggregate score is NOT the same case as the count

**an earlier form of this rule treated "count and score" as one row and marked the score a close call
under `G-aggregate-score`. the captures settle it, and they settle it the other way.**

⭐ **Feist protects exactly what an aggregate score is made of.** the protected element of a factual
compilation is the *"selection and arrangement"* — and `[observed]` Yelp's terms claim precisely
that vocabulary:

> `[observed]` *"We own the Yelp Content, including … **compilation (including, but not limited to,
> our selection, coordination, aggregation, and arrangement of User Content and other Service
> Content)** … **aggregate star ratings**, and all other elements … you may not sell, license, copy,
> publish, modify, reproduce, distribute, create derivative works or adaptations of, **publicly
> display** or in any way use or exploit any of the Yelp Content."*

⛔ **"aggregate star ratings" is named expressly**, and the claim tracks Feist's protected category
rather than overreaches it.

> ⭐⭐ **and the sanity check found the two texts share their WORDS, not merely their concept.** Feist
> protects a compilation's *"**selection and arrangement**"*; the clause claims *"our **selection**,
> coordination, aggregation, and **arrangement** of User Content."*
>
> **the term is drafted to the case.** that matters for how the claim should be weighed: a
> boilerplate assertion of ownership over every element is routinely discounted, and a clause that
> tracks the governing decision's protected element is the opposite of boilerplate. ⛔ **do not read
> this term as a platform's overreach and plan around it. it is well-founded, and it was written by
> someone who had read Feist.**

so:

| datum | copyright status |
|---|---|
| **review count** | ✅ a fact — *"the discoverer merely finds and records"* |
| ⛔ **aggregate score** | ⚠️ their claimed compilation output, and the claim is well-founded under Feist |

> ⭐ **the count and the score are not two grades of the same risk. they are on opposite sides of
> Feist's line**, and a rule that groups them will publish the wrong one.

⚠️ **what survives of the old advice:** where a score is published, it is **attributed** — *"4.6
average as published by Google"* — never adopted bare, which would make it our own assertion under
`define.boundary.who-speaks-decides-the-regime`. **but attribution answers § 5, not copyright**, and
the copyright answer is now the one that binds.

### 2. ⛔ contract — the layer copyright does NOT answer

⛔ **a platform's terms bind by agreement, not by copyright.** so a fact that is free of copyright may
still be closed by contract, and the two analyses share no elements.

⛔⛔ **CORRECTED — this layer was `[unverified]` and is now `[observed]`, and it DEFEATS the copyright
answer for the count.**

an earlier form of this section reasoned about how contract law works and flagged that no agreement
was captured. one is now captured, and it is more specific than the inference expected:

> `[observed]` **Yelp Terms of Service** — *"use any automated means or form of scraping or data
> extraction to access, query or otherwise collect **Yelp data, content and/or reviews** from the
> Consumer Site or the Business Site, **except as expressly permitted by Yelp** (for example, as
> described at www.yelp.com/robots.txt)"*

⭐⭐ **note the enumeration: "Yelp DATA, content and/or reviews."** the term names **data** as its own
category, separate from content and from reviews. **so it reaches a COUNT, which is a datum and not
content.** the question this rule flagged as *the whole question* — *does the term bind on content, or
on the access method?* — is answered: **it binds on the access method, and its object is data.**

> ⛔ **Feist frees the fact and the contract closes the pipe.** both are true simultaneously. a
> correct copyright answer proves the wrong point.

⚠️ **and a second clause reaches display, independent of collection:** `[observed]` *"Modify, adapt,
appropriate, reproduce, distribute, translate, create derivative works or adaptations of, **publicly
display**, sell, trade, or in any way exploit the Service or Service Content (other than Your
Content), except as expressly authorized by Yelp."*

the corrected table — `[observed]` for Yelp, `[unverified]` for every other platform:

| access method | contract exposure |
|---|---|
| an **official API** under its published terms | ✅ bounded by terms we accepted knowingly. ⭐ the ToS itself names the escape — *"except as expressly permitted"* |
| ⛔ **automated collection** from the rendered page | ⛔ **`[observed]` forbidden, and the prohibition names "data"** — so the count is inside it |
| the **subject supplies it** on their claimed profile | ✅ **outside the platform relationship entirely** |

> ⭐ **the third row is the one the design should reach for**, and it converges with the opt-in
> posture already chosen: a claimed subject who links their own review profile has given us the datum
> directly. **no platform term is implicated because no platform was the source.**

### ⭐⭐ and the platform's own terms show the mechanism at work

`[observed]` the same agreement contains, in its business-owner section:

> *"you agree that Yelp **may access and retrieve content and/or information about your Business from
> your website or publicly available sources**, and may reproduce, adapt, distribute, publicly
> perform, and publicly display that content and/or information on the Service"*

⭐ **that is precisely the posture this directory wants — and Yelp's authority for it is the business
owner's AGREEMENT, not a copyright answer.** the strongest available example of the third row, drawn
from the same document that closes the second.

⚠️ `[unverified]` **only ONE platform is captured.** Google, Angi, BBB, and every trade-specific
platform are un-swept, and terms differ. **the pattern is now evidenced rather than assumed, and the
sweep is still open** — tracked as `G-platform-tos`.

### ⛔⛔ .but does the term BIND us? — hiQ v. LinkedIn, and what it did NOT decide

**the analysis above assumed the platform's terms reach us. that assumption was never tested, and the
case everyone cites for the opposite conclusion says something narrower than its reputation.**

`[observed]` **hiQ Labs, Inc. v. LinkedIn Corp., 31 F.4th 1180 (9th Cir. 2022)** is routinely
summarized as *"public data is free to collect."* the opinion's own description does not say that:

> *"the panel again affirmed the preliminary injunction, concluding that Van Buren reinforced its
> determination that hiQ had raised **serious questions** about whether LinkedIn may invoke the
> **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act ("CFAA")** to **preempt hiQ's possibly meritorious tortious
> interference claim**."*

⛔ **three limits, each of which the popular read drops:**

| the popular read | `[observed]` what the opinion says |
|---|---|
| *"the court held scraping is legal"* | it affirmed a **preliminary injunction** on a *"**serious questions**"* standard under a *"**sliding scale**"* — expressly not a merits decision |
| *"it decided the right to collect public data"* | the question was whether **the CFAA's *"without authorization"*** reaches a site where *"prior authorization is not generally required but a particular person—or bot—is refused access"* |
| *"so the terms of service do not matter"* | ⭐⭐ **the CFAA was at issue only as a possible PREEMPTION of hiQ's own tortious-interference claim.** the contract question was never reached |

⭐⭐⭐ **and the first sentence of the case summary is the contract claim, unresolved:**

> `[observed]` *"LinkedIn Corp. sent hiQ Labs, Inc. a cease-and-desist letter, asserting that hiQ
> **violated LinkedIn's User Agreement**."*

> ⛔ **hiQ answers an ANTI-CIRCUMVENTION statute. it does not answer a contract.** the two claims share
> no elements: the CFAA asks whether access was *"without authorization"* to a protected computer;
> breach asks whether a party who **assented** to terms then broke them. **a win on the first is not a
> defense to the second**, and the Yelp term above is a contract term.

#### ⭐ the question hiQ makes visible, which this rule had not asked

**assent.** a contract binds a **party**. so:

| our posture toward the platform | does the term bind? |
|---|---|
| we hold an **account** and accepted the terms | ✅ **yes** — plainly a party |
| we use an **API** under its published terms | ✅ **yes** — acceptance is the price of the key |
| ⚠️ we collect from public pages, **no account, no click** | ⚠️ **`[observed]` Nguyen says a bare browsewrap does not bind — but see the trap below: we now have ACTUAL knowledge, and that is the other route to assent** |
| ✅ the **subject supplies** the datum on their claimed profile | ✅ **moot** — the platform was never the source |

#### ✅ G-browsewrap-assent — CLOSED, and it moves the third row

`[observed]` **Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble Inc., 763 F.3d 1171 (9th Cir. 2014)** answers it directly, and
answers it against enforceability:

> *"where a website makes its terms of use available via a **conspicuous hyperlink on every page** of
> the website but otherwise **provides no notice to users nor prompts them to take any affirmative
> action to demonstrate assent**, even **close proximity of the hyperlink to relevant buttons** users
> must click on — **without more — is insufficient to give rise to constructive notice**."*

⭐⭐ **the bar is higher than instinct suggests: a conspicuous link on every page is NOT enough.** the
opinion also records *"no evidence that the website user had **actual knowledge** of the agreement"*,
so the two routes to assent are **actual knowledge** or **constructive notice**, and a bare browsewrap
supplies neither.

⛔ **but note the second half of that sentence, and it is the trap for us:** *"nothing in the record
to suggest that those browsewrap terms at issue are enforceable **by or against** plaintiff."*
`[inferred]` **actual knowledge is fatal to the defense.** we have now read Yelp's terms and quoted
them in this brief — so the *"no actual knowledge"* route is closed **to us specifically**, whatever
it might offer an ignorant collector.

> ⭐ **a brief that reads a platform's terms in order to plan around them has created the very
> knowledge that makes them enforceable.** that is not an argument against the research; it is the
> reason the answer must be *avoid the surface*, not *rely on non-assent*.

⚠️ `[unverified]` **three limits on how far Nguyen carries:**

- it is **9th Circuit** — the split among circuits on browsewrap is un-swept
- ⛔ it construes a **consumer** browsewrap. `[inferred]` a **commercial** collector who reads terms,
  engineers around a `robots.txt`, and operates at scale presents different facts on every element
- Yelp's own term points at `www.yelp.com/robots.txt` as where permission is expressed — `[inferred]`
  which is an **express notice channel** aimed at automated agents, and *"without more"* is exactly
  what Nguyen said was missing. **an explicit robots directive may be the "more".** un-captured;
  tracked as `G-robots-as-notice`

⚠️ `[unverified]` **and the hiQ story does not end at the injunction.** the capture is the 2022
appellate opinion on preliminary relief; **what happened to LinkedIn's contract claim afterward is
NOT in it.** ⛔ **do not cite hiQ for a final outcome on any theory** — this capture supports the
scope of the CFAA question and no more. tracked as `G-hiq-disposition`.

> ⭐ **the practical takeaway is unchanged and better founded:** the design should reach for the
> **fourth row**. subject-supplied provenance does not need hiQ, does not need a browsewrap decision,
> and does not need a fair-use argument. **it is the only row where every one of these questions is
> moot rather than won.**

### 3. ⚠️ accuracy — the metadata form CREATES a duty the republication form does not

⛔ **a count is a claim about a number, and a number goes stale silently.** review text does not change
after we copy it; a count changes every day.

`[observed]` **15 U.S.C. § 1681k(a)(2)** sets the standard the cluster has already adopted for public
records — an item is up to date only where *"the **current public record status** of the item at the
time of the report is reported."*

`[inferred]` the same discipline applies here by analogy rather than by force, because a directory
outside the consumer-report gate is not bound by § 1681k. **but the exposure it guards against —
a stale number published as current — is identical.** so:

- ⭐ **an as-of date is mandatory** on any published count or score
- **the source is named** — *"on Google"*, not a bare *"412 reviews"*, which reads as ours
- ⛔ **a count that only ever rises** is a defect: reviews are removed, and a monotonic counter
  silently asserts a corpus that no longer exists

## .the test

> **"can we say the true fact without a copy of the sentence that carried it?"**

- yes → publish the metadata, attribute the source, stamp the date
- no → the fact and the expression are inseparable, so **decline the datum**, do not reach for fair use

⚠️ **and one honest limit:** the metadata form is **less useful** to a reader than the reviews are. it
answers *how much feedback exists*, never *what the feedback says*. that is a real product cost and
this rule does not pretend otherwise — it is the same trade the asymmetric-disclosure rule makes, for
the same reason.

## .what this rule does NOT own

the exact copy, the refresh cadence, the API-vs-link choice, the visual treatment. per
`hazard._.catalogue` § *what is NOT a gap*: **a mechanism is stated where the rule is FALSE without
it, and declined where the rule is merely unimplemented without it.**

⚠️ **the refresh cadence is the closest call**, and it stays open for the same reason the dispute SLA
does: *"current"* is the standard and a number of days is a build choice. tracked as
`G-metadata-refresh`.

## .enforcement

- ⛔ **verbatim republication of third-party review text** without an affirmative right (a licence, an
  API's terms, or the subject's own supply) = **blocker** — § 107 is a defense, not a permission
- ⛔ a published **count or score with no as-of date** = **blocker** — a stale number asserted as
  current is a false statement about a live fact
- ⛔ a count or score **published without its source named** = **blocker** — an unattributed number
  is our assertion, which forfeits the § 230(f)(3) posture the attributed form keeps
- ⛔⛔ an **aggregate score republished at all**, absent an API licence or the subject's own supply =
  **blocker** — `[observed]` Yelp names *"aggregate star ratings"* as its own Content and forbids
  public display of it. ⚠️ **this is stricter than the earlier attribution-only rule, which was
  written before the terms were read**
- ⛔ **automated collection** from a platform surface whose terms forbid it = **blocker** — and
  `[observed]` the Yelp prohibition names *"Yelp **data**"*, so **it reaches a bare count** and is not
  answered by the count's freedom from copyright
- ⛔⛔ **hiQ v. LinkedIn cited as authority that a platform's TERMS do not bind** = **blocker** —
  `[observed]` it is a **preliminary-injunction** decision on *"serious questions"* about the
  **CFAA**, reached only as a possible preemption of hiQ's own tortious-interference claim. **it
  answers an anti-circumvention statute, never a contract**, and the User Agreement claim that opens
  the case was not resolved in it
- ⚠️ a contract-layer conclusion that assumes the terms **bind us** without a stated basis for
  **assent** = **nitpick** — an account or an API key makes us a party; a public-page read may not.
  see `G-browsewrap-assent`
- ⚠️ reliance on **fair use** where the fact could have been stated as metadata = **nitpick**
- ⚠️ a review count that **never decreases** = **nitpick** — reviews are removed; a monotonic counter
  drifts upward from the truth

## severity

**prefer → nitpick for the form; require → blocker for the guardrails.** the choice of metadata over
republication is a design preference and it is priced as one. the four bullets that carry **blocker**
do so because each is an assertion that can be **false** — a stale count, an unattributed number, an
adopted computation, or a use the terms forbid.

## .note on the five-state rule

⚠️ **copyright is federal and needs no state sweep** — `rule.require.five-state-baseline` does not bind
that layer, which is why it was never run here. ⛔ **but the contract layer is not one body of law with
five state variants — it is one agreement per platform**, so the five-state frame does not fit it at
all. the sweep it needs is **per platform**, and exactly one platform is captured. tracked as
`G-platform-tos`.
