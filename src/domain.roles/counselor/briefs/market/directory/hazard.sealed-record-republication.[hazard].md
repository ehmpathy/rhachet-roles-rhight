# hazard.sealed-record-republication

## .what

a record captured while it was public may later be **sealed, expunged, or restricted**. the question
is not what the order does to the court file — it is what, if anything, the order obliges **the
holder of the copy** to do about it.

⭐ **the sweep's central result: the five states do not regulate the same actor, or even the same
act.** this is the one layer in the whole directory where the five-state matrix is **not** a set of
stricter-and-looser variants of one rule. it is five structurally different regimes.

> **recommendation disclaimer.** the mitigations below are offered to the best of our knowledge,
> based on the information found in research — they carry **no guarantee of success or outcome**, and
> they must be reviewed by a licensed professional (attorney / CPA / licensed advisor) against your
> own real scenario and current law before any reliance. this is groundwork for that review, not a
> substitute for it.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate; verify current text before
> any reliance. claims are tagged `[observed]` / `[inferred]` / `[unverified]`.

## ⭐ .the five-state sweep — and the shape is the result

`rule.require.five-state-baseline`. posture: **strictest-common-denominator**.

| state | who does the statute bind? | the operative duty | gated on a consumer report? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TX** | ⭐ **the publisher directly** — Tex. Bus. & Com. ch. 109, *"a business entity that … publishes criminal record information"* | may not publish where an expunction or nondisclosure order issued **and the publisher has notice**; plus an affirmative accuracy duty and a dispute channel | ⛔ **no** `[observed]` |
| **IN** | ⭐ **the publisher directly** — Ind. Code ch. 24-4-18, *"Criminal History Providers"*, sited in the **trade-regulation** title | may not provide expunged/restricted records; ⭐ **60-day** staleness bound | ⛔ **no** `[observed]` |
| **CA** | **the reporter** — Cal. Civ. Code § 1786.18 (ICRAA) | enumerated record classes barred outright; ⭐ **30-day** re-verify window | ✅ **yes** `[observed]` |
| **NY** | **whoever owed a seal duty** — N.Y. Civ. Rights Law § 50-g | damages, but only against a party who **owed a duty of care** under CPL § 160.57 | ⛔ no, but ⚠️ narrow another way |
| **FL** | ⛔ **criminal justice agencies** — Fla. Stat. § 943.0585(6) | *"must be physically destroyed or obliterated by **any criminal justice agency having custody**"* | ⛔ n/a — it never reaches a publisher |

### ⭐⭐ strictness does NOT travel across layers

**Florida was the strictest state on right-of-publicity scope** — *"any natural person"*, the narrowest
news carve-out of the five. here it is the **most permissive**: it has no criminal-history-provider
statute at all, and its expunction statute's effect provisions bind **agencies**, not publishers.
`[observed]` § 943.0585(6)(b) grants the **subject** a right — *"may lawfully deny or fail to
acknowledge the arrests covered by the expunged record"* — which is a shield for the subject, not a
duty on a publisher.

⚠️ **so a per-layer baseline is required; a per-state one would be wrong.** a design that anchored on
"FL is the strictest state" would be well-defended on publicity and blind here. the strictest cell has
to be re-derived for **every** layer.

## ⭐⭐ .Texas has TWO exits from the entire chapter — and one of them is § 230's own test

`[observed]` **§ 109.002(a)(1)** gates the chapter's main duties on a **conjunction**: a business
entity that (A) publishes criminal record information **and** (B) *"requires the payment: (i) of a fee
in an amount of **$150 or more** … to remove criminal record information; or (ii) of a fee or other
consideration to **correct or modify** criminal record information."*

⭐ **that is the mugshot-site business model, named with precision.** a directory that charges **no
fee to remove and no fee to correct** falls outside § 109.002(a)(1) entirely.

`[observed]` **§ 109.002(b)(4)** is the second exit, and it is broader: *"This chapter does not apply
to … an **entity that provides an information service or that is an interactive computer service**."*

⚠️ **but note the two Texas carve-outs are written differently, and the difference matters:**

| | § 109.002(b)(4) — chapter-wide | § 109.0045(e)(3) — juvenile records only |
|---|---|---|
| the text | *"an entity that … is an interactive computer service"* | *"is an interactive computer service, as defined by 47 U.S.C. Section 230, **and published material provided by another person**"* |
| what it turns on | ⭐ **status alone** | status **+ who authored** |

`[inferred]` so (b)(4) reads **broader than § 230 itself** — it does not carry the
*"provided by another person"* qualifier. ⚠️ that is a favorable read of a state statute against a
federal analogue and it is **exactly the kind of read counsel should test**, not one to build on.

⛔ **and the exits do not reach every case.** § 109.002(a)(2) — confidential juvenile and child
criminal record information — applies *"regardless of … whether the business entity charges a fee."*
`[observed]` **there is an unconditional floor for juvenile records that no fee-model choice escapes.**

## ⭐⭐ .Indiana sets a hard number, and it needs no consumer report to bite

`[observed]` **Ind. Code § 24-4-18-7(a)**: *"A criminal history provider may not knowingly include
criminal history information in a criminal history report if the criminal history information fails to
reflect material changes to the official record **occurring sixty (60) days or more** before the date
the criminal history report is delivered."*

⭐ **a statutory decay deadline, in days.** `hazard.stale-public-record-status.[hazard].md` argued
`[inferred]` that **expiry beats re-check** but had no number to hang it on. Indiana supplies one.

`[observed]` **§ 24-4-18-6(a)** bars a provider from a record *"expunged by … removing the record from
public access"*, one *"restricted by a court"*, certain reduced felonies, and — ⭐ notably —
*"(4) A record that the criminal history provider **knows is inaccurate**."*

⛔⛔ **the sharp part: neither section has a consumer-report gate.** they bind a *"criminal history
provider"* who delivers a *"criminal history report"* — full stop. **so this trigger sits LOWER than
FCRA's.** the federal analysis in `hazard.consumer-report-gate.[hazard].md` is therefore **incomplete
for Indiana**: one can be outside FCRA and still inside ch. 24-4-18.

⚠️ `[unverified]` — whether a directory of this shape meets the § 24-4-18-2 definition of *"criminal history provider"* and
§ 24-4-18-3 of *"criminal history report"* was **not captured**. those two definitions decide the whole
Indiana cell and they are the highest-value open row on this layer.

### and the state remedies STACK on the federal one

`[observed]` **Ind. Code § 24-4-18-8**: *"(a) A violation of section 6 or 7 … is a **deceptive act**
that is actionable under IC 24-5-0.5-4. (b) This section **does not prohibit** an individual from
bringing an action on the individual's own behalf under the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act."*

`[observed]` **Tex. Bus. & Com. § 109.008**: *"The actions and remedies provided by this chapter are
**not exclusive** and are in addition to any other action or remedy provided by law."*

⭐ **so the state layers are additive, not alternative.** to clear FCRA is to clear FCRA. it does not
clear these.

### ⭐⭐ and the Indiana remedy has TEETH — already priced in this tree

`[observed]` § 24-4-18-8(a) routes a violation to **IC 24-5-0.5-4** (the Deceptive Consumer Sales
Act). ⭐ **that statute is already researched here** —
`../rewards/ref.udap-remedy-teeth.five-state.[ref].md` quotes it verbatim:

> a consumer may recover *"the damages actually suffered as a result of the deceptive act or **five
> hundred dollars ($500)**, whichever is greater"*, trebled up to *"three (3) times the actual
> damages"* for willful conduct, plus *"reasonable attorney's fees"*.

⛔ **so an Indiana violation is not an abstract exposure.** it carries a **$500 statutory floor per
consumer**, **treble on willful**, and **fee-shift** — which is the combination that makes a
small-per-head claim viable as a class at coverage scale.

⚠️ **and note the TX parallel.** the same extant brief records that **Tex. Bus. & Com. § 17.50
(DTPA)** allows an **uncapped treble** on a *"knowing"* violation plus **mandatory** fees to a
prevailing consumer. ⭐ **the two states that impose criminal-history duties directly on a publisher
are also the two states with the sharpest UDAP remedies.** that is not a coincidence a design should
discover late.

⭐ **cited, not re-derived** — the five-state remedy matrix belongs in that brief; this one only needs
to record that § 24-4-18-8 lands in it.

## .California: a second gate with a tighter clock

`[observed]` **§ 1786.18(c)**: an ICRA *"shall not furnish an investigative consumer report that
includes information that is a matter of public record and that relates to an arrest, indictment,
conviction, civil judicial action, tax lien, or **outstanding judgment**, unless the agency has
**verified the accuracy** of the information during the **30-day period** ending on the date on which
the report is furnished."*

⭐ **so the two captured decay bounds are 30 days (CA) and 60 days (IN), and they attach to different
triggers:**

| which side of the gate | which clock binds |
|---|---|
| **outside** the consumer-report gate | ⭐ **IN's 60 days**, for a *"criminal history provider"* |
| **inside** it | ⭐ **CA's 30 days**, and it is a **re-verify** duty, not merely a decay limit |

⚠️ note the difference in kind: IN says *do not publish data this stale*; CA says *do not publish
unless you re-checked within 30 days*. **CA's is affirmative work; IN's is a bound.** a badge that
expires to `⬜` at a fixed age satisfies IN's shape natively and does **not** satisfy CA's.

`[observed]` § 1786.18(a) also bars, inside the gate: *"(2) **Suits** that, from the date of filing,
and satisfied judgments that, from the date of entry, antedate the report by more than **seven
years**"*; *"(3) **Unsatisfied judgments** … more than seven years"*; and *"(7) … in the case of an
arrest … **a conviction did not result**"* — barred regardless of age.

⭐ **(a)(2) reaches the commonest adverse fact pattern in the trade** — the civil suit for *money
taken, work never done*. inside the CA gate, a suit older than seven years from its filing date may
not be reported at all, whatever its merits.

## .New York is narrow, but check where the narrowness lies

`[observed]` **§ 50-g(2)**: a party owes the duty *"when the party is under an obligation pursuant to
subdivision two of such section to seal information … or when the party **obtains access** to records
of such conviction for a specified purpose"* under CPL § 160.57.

`[inferred]` so a third party who copied a record from a **public** source **before** the seal does not
obviously fall inside § 50-g — the duty attaches to seal obligations and to authorized access, not to
prior public capture. ⚠️ this is a defense about the **source and date of the copy**, not about the
publisher's conduct after the seal, and it is `[inferred]` from the section's own text rather than
from any case law.

## .the mitigations, ranked

`[inferred]`:

1. ⭐⭐ **do not publish adverse items at all** — `rule.require.asymmetric-disclosure`. this layer
   exists **only** for adverse records. the asymmetry does not merely reduce the exposure; it
   **removes** it, because a sealed record is by definition an adverse item that never publishes.
2. ⛔ **charge no fee to remove and no fee to correct** — TX § 109.002(a)(1)(B) gates the chapter on
   exactly that model. the same lever also blunts the *"other valuable consideration"* prong of
   CCPA's sale definition (below).
3. **badges expire to `⬜` at a fixed age** — satisfies IN's 60-day bound natively; ⚠️ does **not**
   satisfy CA's 30-day *re-verify* duty, which needs affirmative work.
4. ⭐⭐ **a conspicuous dispute channel with prompt removal** — TX §§ 109.003–109.004 require it.
   ⛔ **cited, not restated: `rule.require.dispute-channel.[rule].md` is canonical.**
5. **entity grain as the subject of record** — a business has no criminal history to seal, so the
   layer does not arise. structural, and it is the same #1 as every other layer.
6. **never publish juvenile or child records under any model** — TX § 109.002(a)(2) is the one floor
   that no fee-model or grain choice escapes.

⛔ **what does NOT mitigate it:** FCRA compliance (`[observed]` the state remedies are expressly
cumulative — IN § -8(b), TX § 109.008); a disclaimer; the subject's non-participation.

## ⚠️ .the DISPUTE CHANNEL — sourced here, OWNED elsewhere

⭐ **this brief is where the duty is SOURCED** — `[observed]` TX §§ 109.003–109.004 make the absence
of a channel actionable, and § 109.005 prices it per day. **it is not where the duty is owned.**

> ⛔ **`rule.require.dispute-channel.[rule].md` is the canonical statement. cite it; do not restate
> it.**

⚠️ **the reason for the split is that the duty is BROADER than the chapter that supplies it.** TX
ch. 109 reaches *"criminal record information"*, but the same channel is what a subject needs to
correct a lapsed licence, a stale permit, or a mis-matched identity — and it is cited as canonical by
**four** peer briefs across three bodies of law. `rule.prefer.most-common-denominator` places it at
their common ancestor, not inside the one that happened to supply the citation.

⛔ **a duty filed inside a brief titled for sealed criminal records reads as bounded to them.** it is
not, and that mis-read is exactly why it registered as a courtesy in each of the four briefs that
leaned on it.

> of it sat in this brief because that is where it was discovered — beside the fee-model prong it

> it.**

what this brief keeps is the single interlock that is genuinely about the **criminal-record** layer:

⭐ **the no-fee design choice carries weight in two statutes, for two unrelated reasons.**

| statute | why a fee hurts |
|---|---|
| `[observed]` **Tex. Bus. & Com. § 109.002(a)(1)(B)** | a fee to remove or correct is one of the conditions that pulls a business **into** the chapter. no fee, no hook |
| `[observed]` **Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ad)(1)** | a fee is the cleanest possible case of *"monetary … consideration"*, the prong that turns publication into a **sale** |

⚠️ **so one product decision defends two unrelated regimes, and a change to it breaks both quietly.**
that is why the no-fee rule is enforced below rather than left as a preference.

## .the test

> **"is this an adverse record about a natural person — and does any state bind the actor as the
> publisher rather than as a reporter?"**

- the subject is an entity → the layer does not arise
- it is a **favorable** record → the layer does not arise (a seal order concerns adverse items)
- adverse, about a person, and published → ⛔ TX and IN reach the publisher **without** any
  consumer-report gate, and their remedies stack on FCRA's

## .enforcement

> ⭐ **these are DERIVED checks, not original mandates** — each says what to verify so this body of
> law is not tripped. the convention is declared in `hazard._.catalogue` § *the convention*; the
> original mandates live in `rule.*` files. **a `[hazard]` that enforces is checkable, not normative.**

- publication of an **adverse** record about a natural person = **blocker**
  (`rule.require.asymmetric-disclosure` — this layer's whole surface)
- any **fee to remove** or **fee to correct** a record = **blocker** (TX § 109.002(a)(1)(B) pulls the
  business into the chapter; it also feeds CCPA's *"valuable consideration"* prong)
- ⛔ **absence of a conspicuous dispute / correction channel = blocker** (TX §§ 109.003–109.004).
  ⭐ **cited, not restated** — the four elements and their checks are owned by
  `rule.require.dispute-channel.[rule].md`
- publication of **juvenile or child** criminal record information = **blocker**, under every model
  (TX § 109.002(a)(2) applies *"regardless of … whether the business entity charges a fee"*)
- continued publication after **notice** of an expunction or nondisclosure order = **blocker**
  (TX § 109.005: up to **$500 per violation** and **$500 per day** of continuing violation)
- reliance on **FCRA compliance** as the answer to a state claim = **blocker** (IN § -8(b),
  TX § 109.008 — expressly cumulative)
- a person-grain public page without opt-in, where CCPA applies = **blocker**
  (§ 1798.140(ad)(1) — *"making available"* is a sale absent the (ad)(2)(A) direction)
