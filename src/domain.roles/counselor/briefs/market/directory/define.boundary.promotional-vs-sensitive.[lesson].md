# define.boundary.promotional-vs-sensitive

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate; verify current text before
> reliance. every claim is tagged `[observed]` (quoted from a captured source), `[inferred]`
> (reasoning over those sources), or `[unverified]` (not yet sourced).

## .what

**a second axis, orthogonal to subject grain: is the material we republish a RECORD about the
subject, or the subject's own PROMOTIONAL expression?**

- **sensitive** — a licence status, a permit, a civil judgment, a criminal record, an identity match
- **promotional** — a logo, a tagline, site copy, a linktree, review text a customer wrote

⭐ **the axis does NOT change the data-broker answer.** it changes **which body of law owns the
material**, and it runs opposite to intuition.

## .why it is a separate brief

`define.boundary.entity-grain-vs-person-grain` is the axis that exits four regimes at once, all on the
same *"natural person"* hinge. **this axis exits none of them and opens two more.** two axes that
behave that differently cannot share a file without one being read as a special case of the other.

## ⛔ .the first thing this axis does NOT do

> **promotional material buys no exit from the data-broker definition.**

`[observed]` **Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.99.80(c)** defines a data broker as a business that *"knowingly
**collects and sells** to third parties the **personal information of a consumer** with whom the
business does not have a direct relationship."*

⛔ **content-kind is not one of the elements.** the elements are (1) collect **and** sell, (2)
personal information of a **consumer**, (3) no direct relationship. **a scraped logo is outside the
definition for exactly the reason a scraped criminal record about a company is outside it — there is
no natural person in either.**

⭐ **and the converse is the trap.** *personal* promotional material — a sole proprietor's headshot, a
person's linktree — **is** personal information, and `[observed]` **§ 1798.140(i)** confines
*"consumer"* to *"a natural person"* without regard to whether the datum flatters them.

> ⛔ **privacy law does not gate on whether the fact is nice.**

## .the table — which regime each side answers to

| | **sensitive** (records, status, background) | **promotional** (logo, review text, taglines, site copy) |
|---|---|---|
| privacy · FCRA · ROP · data-broker | ⛔ the whole apparatus (at person grain) | ✅ inert **at entity grain**; ⛔ fully live at person grain |
| defamation / trade libel | ⛔ sharp — an adverse record wrongly matched is provable economic harm | ⚠️ small — their own words rarely defame them |
| ⭐ **copyright** | ✅ largely out of scope — see the qualifier below | ⛔ **a logo, review text, and site copy are original works of authorship** |
| ⭐ **trademark** | ✅ n/a | ⛔ `[observed]` § 1125(a) false association — **the Lanham claim with no statutory fair-use exclusion** |
| ⭐ **contract / platform terms** | ✅ registries publish for the express purpose of public notice | ⛔ `[unverified]` platform terms restrict republication of hosted reviews |
| § 230 | shields third-party speech; **never our own badge** | same seam, but far more of the content is genuinely theirs |

> ⭐⭐ **promotional material is SAFER on privacy and MORE DANGEROUS on IP and contract — and this
> cluster has mitigations for the first and none for the second.** a directory that concludes
> *"promotional = safe"* has swapped a regime it has answered for two it has never opened.

## ⛔ .the copyright cell needs its qualifier stated, because the obvious citation is wrong

⛔ **a draft of this analysis cited § 102(b) as the reason facts are uncopyrightable. the capture does
not support it.**

`[observed]` **17 U.S.C. § 102(b)**: *"In no case does copyright protection for an original work of
authorship extend to any **idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle,
or discovery**, regardless of the form in which it is described…"*

⭐ **"fact" is absent from that list.** § 102(b) is the idea/expression dichotomy — the House report
captured with it says exactly that: *"the basic dichotomy between expression and idea remains
unchanged."*

`[inferred]` the exit for a bare fact is **one section earlier and structurally different**:
`[observed]` § 102(a) extends protection to *"**original works of authorship** fixed in any tangible
medium of expression."* a measurement is not authored, so it never enters the subject matter at all.

### ⛔⛔ .CORRECTED — the correction above was textually right and doctrinally WRONG

> **`G-facts-originality` is CLOSED, and it closed against the paragraphs above. § 102(b) IS the
> provision. the Supreme Court says so in terms.**

`[observed]` **Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone**, 499 U.S. 340, 356 (1991), which quotes
§ 102(b) in full and then holds:

> *"**§ 102(b) is universally understood to prohibit any copyright in facts.** Harper & Row, supra, at
> 471 U.S. 547, 556. Accord, Nimmer § 2.03[E] (equating facts with 'discoveries')."*

⭐ **the mechanism is named, and it resolves the apparent contradiction rather than a reversal of it.**
"fact" is absent from § 102(b)'s list **as a word** and present **as a category** — via
***"discovery"***, the last item in the list this section quoted and read past. so the textual
observation was accurate; the inference drawn from it was not.

`[observed]` and the syllabus states both routes as one: *"copyright extends to 'original works of
authorship,' 17 U.S.C. § 102(a), and **there can be no copyright in facts, § 102(b)**."* they are
parallel, not rival — § 102(a) is the threshold a fact fails, § 102(b) is the exclusion it falls under.

⛔ **so the enforcement bullet this section produced was a defect: it made a Supreme-Court-endorsed
citation a blocker.** it is corrected below.

> ⭐⭐ **the lesson, now that this cell has been wrong twice in opposite directions:** the first draft
> cited § 102(b) from memory and was right by luck. the correction read the text and was wrong by
> rigor. **the capture of the CASE settled it — and only the case could, because the answer lives in
> how a court reads one word, which no amount of care with the statute would recover.**
>
> ⚠️ **the generalizable form: a text read closely still under-determines a doctrine.** where a brief
> reasons from statutory text alone to a conclusion about what a provision *reaches*, the case law is
> not a nice-to-have citation — it is the one that carries the weight, and its absence should be an
> `[unverified]` on the **conclusion**, not merely on the authority under it.

> ⭐ **what survives from the original lesson:** a defense recalled from its reputation rather than
> read from its source will be wrong about **which provision** carries it — as the Lanham analysis
> found, where the nominative-fair-use exclusion turned out to sit inside the dilution subsection.
> **that lesson holds; this cell was simply not an instance of it.**

## ⭐ .the compilation rule cuts BOTH ways

`[observed]` **§ 103(b)**: *"The copyright in a compilation or derivative work extends **only to the
material contributed by the author** of such work, as distinguished from the preexisting material
employed in the work, and **does not imply any exclusive right in the preexisting material**."*

two consequences, and the second is the one nobody expects:

1. **it bounds what a source platform can claim** over a corpus of reviews its users wrote — its
   compilation copyright reaches its own selection and arrangement, not the underlying facts
2. ⛔ **it bounds what WE could claim over our own directory** — which matters the moment anyone
   proposes a moat argument built on copyright rather than on coverage

⛔ **and § 103(a) carries a penalty clause that is easy to miss.** `[observed]`: *"protection for a
work employing preexisting material in which copyright subsists **does not extend to any part of the
work in which such material has been used unlawfully**."*

> `[inferred]` **an unlawfully-sourced corpus poisons our own copyright in the part built from it.**
> so republication is not merely a claim risk — it forfeits the protection of the aggregate that
> republication was meant to build.

## ⚠️ .fair use is the fallback, and both of its structural questions are now settled from the text

`[observed]` **§ 107**: *"the fair use of a copyrighted work … for purposes **such as** criticism,
comment, news reporting, teaching …, scholarship, or research, is not an infringement… In determining
whether the use made of a work in any particular case is a fair use the factors to be considered
**shall include** —"* the four factors.

| question | answer from the text |
|---|---|
| are the four factors mandatory? | ✅ **yes** — *"shall include"* |
| are they exhaustive? | ⛔ **no** — *"include"* is a floor, not a ceiling |
| do the preamble purposes bound the defense? | ⛔ **no** — *"purposes **such as**"* is illustrative |

⚠️ **but two factors point the wrong way for a commercial directory:**

- `[observed]` factor (1) asks *"whether such use is of a **commercial nature**"*
- `[observed]` factor (4) asks *"the effect of the use upon the **potential market** for or value of
  the copyrighted work"* — and verbatim republication substitutes for a visit to the source

> ⭐⭐ **which is why the design answer is to stay OUT of copyright's subject matter rather than to
> win a fair-use argument inside it.** a defense you must prove is strictly worse than a scope you
> never enter. see `rule.prefer.metadata-over-republication`.

## .the test

> **"is this material a RECORD about them, or something they (or their customers) AUTHORED?"**

- a record → the sensitive column; run the grain test first, because grain decides most of it
- authored → the promotional column; ⛔ **grain buys almost none of it here**, and the questions are
  copyright, trademark, and contract

⚠️ **and the two are routinely adjacent on one profile**, so the test is applied **per datum**, never
per page.

## .enforcement

- ⛔ a design that treats promotional material as safe **because** it is favorable = **blocker** —
  the data-broker elements do not include content-kind (§ 1798.99.80(c))
- ⛔ republication of **personal** promotional material (headshot, personal linktree) without the
  subject's opt-in = **blocker** — § 1798.140(i) reaches it as personal information
- ⛔ **~~a copyright analysis that cites § 102(b) for the proposition that facts are uncopyrightable
  = blocker~~ — RETRACTED, the bullet was wrong.** `[observed]` Feist, 499 U.S. at 356: *"§ 102(b) is
  universally understood to prohibit any copyright in facts."* the citation this bullet blocked is the
  Supreme Court's own. see § *CORRECTED*
- ✅ **replaced by:** a copyright analysis that reaches the fact exit **without** either § 102(b) (via
  *"discovery"*, per Feist) or § 102(a)'s originality threshold = **blocker** — the two are parallel
  routes to one answer, and a brief that cites neither has asserted the conclusion rather than sourced
  it
- ⛔ a moat or defensibility argument built on **compilation copyright** = **blocker** — § 103(b)
  reaches only our own contribution, and § 103(a) withdraws even that where the source was unlawful
- ⚠️ reliance on **fair use** where the material could be reduced to metadata that never enters
  copyright's subject matter = **nitpick** — ⭐ **a special case of
  `rule.prefer.unmet-element-over-won-defense`**, which is now canonical at the counselor root. this
  bullet stays here because copyright is where the choice actually presents; **the principle is
  general and is stated once**
- ⛔ a brief that applies the **grain** test to promotional material and stops = **blocker** — grain
  answers privacy; it does not answer IP or contract

## severity

**define → the boundary itself is not a check**; the enforcement bullets above are. the axis is
stated as a `define.boundary` because it **selects** which rules apply, in the same way
`define.boundary.entity-grain-vs-person-grain` does.
