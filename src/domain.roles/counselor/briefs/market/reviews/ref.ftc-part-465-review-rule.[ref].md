# ref.ftc-part-465-review-rule

## .what

the 2024 ftc rule on the use of consumer reviews and testimonials — 16 cfr part 465 — laid out
verbatim, section by section. this is the federal redline that governs any program that rewards or
hosts reviews. a verified-purchase check is necessary but not sufficient: the rule reaches
sentiment-conditioned rewards, insider reviews, and review suppression too.

## .why

- part 465 is the rule that says which review practices are lawful and which are an unfair or
  deceptive act
- the rule carries civil-penalty teeth (~$51,744 per violation), so the cost of a wrong design is
  not hypothetical
- each § 465.x subsection is a **distinct** prohibition; a program must clear all of them, not just
  the fake-review one

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate; verify current text.

## the statutory anchor — ftc act § 5

part 465 is a trade-regulation rule under the ftc act. the act declares the root wrong unlawful,
verbatim, 15 u.s.c. § 45(a)(1):

> "Unfair methods of competition in or affecting commerce, and unfair or deceptive acts or practices
> in or affecting commerce, are hereby declared unlawful."

and the act supplies the civil-penalty hook for a rule violation, verbatim, 15 u.s.c. § 45(m)(1)(A):

> "The Commission may commence a civil action to recover a civil penalty in a district court of the
> United States against any person, partnership, or corporation which violates any rule under this
> subchapter respecting unfair or deceptive acts or practices"

**source**: [15 u.s.c. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45)

each § 465.x subsection restates this frame — "it is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a
violation of this part" — which is what ties the per-violation penalty to each prohibition.

---

## § 465.1 — definitions (scope)

the rule's reach turns on a few defined terms. verbatim:

> **Consumer review** means "a consumer's evaluation, or a purported consumer's evaluation, of a
> product, service, or business that is submitted by the consumer or purported consumer and that is
> published to a website or platform dedicated in whole or in part to receiving and displaying such
> evaluations."

> **Clear and conspicuous** means "a required disclosure is easily noticeable (i.e., difficult to
> miss) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers."

> **Business** means "an individual who sells products or services, a partnership that sells products
> or services, a corporation that sells products or services, or any other commercial entity that
> sells products or services."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.1)

note the phrase "or purported consumer" — a review need not be from a real customer to fall inside
the rule; a fabricated one is squarely in scope.

---

## § 465.2 — fake or false consumer reviews

the core prohibition. verbatim, subsection (a):

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to write,
> create, or sell a consumer review, consumer testimonial, or celebrity testimonial that materially
> misrepresents, expressly or by implication:
> (1) That the reviewer or testimonialist exists;
> (2) That the reviewer or testimonialist used or otherwise had experience with the product,
> service, or business that is the subject of the review or testimonial; or
> (3) The reviewer's or testimonialist's experience with the product, service, or business that is
> the subject of the review or testimonial."

subsection (b) reaches purchase and dissemination of such reviews the business "knew or should have
known materially misrepresented" the same three points.

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2)

the three-part test is the redline: a review is fake/false if the reviewer does not exist, did not
have the experience, or misrepresents the experience. a law-firm summary frames the same target,
verbatim:

> "The final rule addresses reviews and testimonials that misrepresent that they are by someone who
> does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews, or who did not have actual experience with the
> business or its products or services, or that misrepresent the experience of the person giving it."

**source**: [ftc bans fake reviews and testimonials (fourscore)](https://www.fourscorelaw.com/resources/ftc-bans-fake-reviews-and-testimonials)

---

## § 465.4 — buying positive or negative reviews (sentiment)

the sentiment-conditioned-incentive ban. verbatim:

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to
> provide compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned expressly or by
> implication on, the writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment,
> whether positive or negative, regarding the product, service, or business that is the subject of
> the review."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.4)

this is the section a rewards program most easily trips. the reward must be blind to sentiment —
paid for a review, never for a **good** review. "by implication" reaches designs that pay only when
a review reads favorably, even without an explicit "5 stars = points" rule.

---

## § 465.5 — insider reviews and testimonials

reviews from inside the business must disclose the relationship. verbatim, subsection (a):

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for an officer or
> manager of a business to write or create a consumer review or consumer testimonial about the
> business or one of the products or services it sells that fails to have a clear and conspicuous
> disclosure of the officer's or manager's material relationship to the business, unless, in the case
> of a consumer testimonial, the relationship is otherwise clear to the audience."

subsection (c)(1) extends the ban to an officer or manager who solicits reviews from immediate
relatives, employees, or agents without disclosure. verbatim:

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for an officer or
> manager of a business to solicit or demand a consumer review about the business or one of the
> products or services it sells from any of their immediate relatives or from any employee or agent
> of the business, or to solicit or demand that such employees or agents seek such reviews from their
> relatives, when: (i) The solicitation or demand results in ... writing or creating such a review
> without a disclosure of the reviewer's material relationship to the business"

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.5)

so an employee or manager who transacted as a customer may review — but the relationship must be
disclosed clearly and conspicuously.

---

## § 465.6 — company-controlled review sites

a business may not pose an owned site as independent. verbatim:

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to
> materially misrepresent, expressly or by implication, that a website, organization, or entity that
> it controls, owns, or operates provides independent reviews or opinions, other than consumer
> reviews, about a category of businesses, products, or services including the business or one or more
> of the products or services it sells."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.6)

so a business that hosts its own reviews must not dress that surface as a neutral third-party rater
of its own product category.

---

## § 465.7 — review suppression

two prongs: threats to kill a review, and selective removal of negatives. verbatim, subsection (a):

> "For anyone to use an unfounded or groundless legal threat, a physical threat, intimidation, or a
> public false accusation in response to a consumer review that is made with the knowledge that the
> accusation was false or made with reckless disregard as to its truth or falsity, in an attempt to:
> (1) Prevent a review or any portion thereof from being written or created, or
> (2) Cause a review or any portion thereof to be removed"

verbatim, subsection (b):

> "For a business to materially misrepresent, expressly or by implication, that the consumer reviews
> of one or more of the products or services it sells displayed in a portion of its website or
> platform dedicated in whole or in part to receiving and displaying consumer reviews represent most
> or all the reviews submitted to the website or platform when reviews are being suppressed (i.e., not
> displayable) based upon their ratings or their negative sentiment."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.7)

the section carves out neutral moderation — reviews withheld for defamation, confidential data, or a
seller's reasonable belief the review is fake are not "suppressed" if the criteria apply equally
regardless of sentiment. the line: a business may not filter out low-star reviews while it shows
high-star ones as the full set.

---

## § 465.8 — fake indicators of social-media influence

the influence-inflation ban. verbatim:

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for anyone to:
> (a) Sell or distribute fake indicators of social media influence that they knew or should have
> known to be fake and that can be used by individuals or businesses to materially misrepresent their
> influence or importance for a commercial purpose; or
> (b) Purchase or procure fake indicators of social media influence that they knew or should have
> known to be fake and that materially misrepresent their influence or importance for a commercial
> purpose."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.8)

fake followers, likes, or views bought to inflate perceived reach fall here.

## the honest gap

- **this brief is a text-faithful reference, not enforcement analysis.** counsel must apply each
  section to a program's specific design and facts.
- **the $51,744 figure is inflation-adjusted** and moves over time; verify the current per-violation
  cap before any reliance.
- **the ftc.gov primary pages 403-block automated fetch**, so the effective-date and penalty quotes
  here rest on law-firm secondaries (goodwin, fourscore) that restate them; the § 465.x text itself is
  quoted from the codified cfr on cornell.
- **"knew or should have known"** appears across the sections — a scienter standard counsel should
  weigh against a program's verification and moderation controls.
