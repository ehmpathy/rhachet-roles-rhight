# define.boundary.disclosed-vs-undisclosed-incentive

## .what

a review redline under the endorsement rules: where a reward creates a **material connection**
between the reviewer and the seller, that connection is either **disclosed clearly and conspicuously**
on the review (lawful) or left **undisclosed** (unlawful).

- **lawful side**: a reviewer earns a reward for a real, verified transaction, and the review carries a
  clear-and-conspicuous disclosure of that reward / material connection (16 cfr 255.5).
- **unlawful side**: a reviewer earns a reward and the connection is hidden — a plain 255.5 violation
  and a deceptive act under ftc act § 5 (15 u.s.c. 45).

the element that splits the two: does a reader of the review learn about the reward, on the review
itself, clearly and conspicuously?

## .why

- a reward paid when a customer leaves a review is a **material connection** the ftc requires be
  disclosed, whatever the actor or the reward form
- an undisclosed incentive makes the review deceptive — it inflates the weight a reader gives it
- the fix is inexpensive on either side of the line: a badge or line of text on each review carries
  the disclosure, while silence about the reward is what breaks it

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

**the element that splits the two:** is the reward / material connection **disclosed clearly and
conspicuously on the review**? if yes, the review is contextualized and lawful; if hidden, it is a
deceptive endorsement.

## the statutory hook — § 255.5 requires disclosure of a material connection

16 cfr 255.5 states the duty verbatim:

> "When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product
> that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is
> not reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and
> conspicuously."

and it names the reward forms — money or a free / discounted good — that count as a material
connection, verbatim:

> "They can include monetary payment or the provision of free or discounted products (including
> products unrelated to the endorsed product) to an endorser."

the rule also reaches other benefits, verbatim:

> "Material connections can also include other benefits to the endorser, such as early access to a
> product or the possibility of being paid, of winning a prize, or of appearing on television."

and it sets the bar for what the disclosure must convey, verbatim:

> "A disclosure of a material connection does not require the complete details of the connection,
> but it must clearly communicate the nature of the connection sufficiently for consumers to
> evaluate its significance."

**source**: [16 cfr § 255.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5)

a reward for a verified transaction is a monetary-value benefit — squarely a material connection. so any
reward-linked review must carry a clear-and-conspicuous disclosure of that reward.

---

## the enforcement hook — an undisclosed incentive is a deceptive act under § 5

the ftc act, 15 u.s.c. 45(a)(1), declares the root wrong verbatim:

> "Unfair methods of competition in or affecting commerce, and unfair or deceptive acts or
> practices in or affecting commerce, are hereby declared unlawful."

**source**: [15 u.s.c. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45)

an undisclosed material connection makes an endorsement deceptive — it misleads a reader about the
weight to give the review. that is the § 5 hook the 255.5 guides operationalize.

the 2024 reviews rule (16 cfr part 465) layers a further, related prohibition: a review must not
misrepresent a real experience. 16 cfr 465.2 opens verbatim:

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to
> write, create, or sell a consumer review, consumer testimonial, or celebrity testimonial that
> materially misrepresents, expressly or by implication:"

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2)

a reward-linked review that hides the reward can read as a misrepresentation of an ordinary,
unincentivized experience — the exact deception 465.2 and § 5 target.

---

## disclosure is the cure — but only the cure for the *disclosure* wrong

the ftc's guidance draws the practical line: an incentive must be surfaced. per a national law
review analysis of the rule, the incentive-for-a-review path stays open **so long as** it is not
sentiment-gated, verbatim:

> "The Rule does not prohibit giving incentives for reviews, as long as there is not an express or
> implied requirement that the reviews have to express a particular sentiment."

**source**: [national law review — fifteen things advertisers need to know about the ftc's consumer reviews and testimonials rule](https://natlawreview.com/article/what-are-fifteen-things-advertisers-need-know-about-ftcs-consumer-reviews-and)

a morgan lewis analysis confirms the two ftc concerns sit as distinct duties — one about disclosure
of a material relationship, the other about sentiment — and describes the sentiment prohibition
verbatim:

> "prohibits businesses from providing compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or
> conditioned on (either expressly or by implication), the writing or creation of consumer reviews
> that express a *particular sentiment*"

**source**: [morgan lewis — ftc issues final rule on consumer reviews and testimonials](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)

> **critical scope note.** disclosure cures the **undisclosed-incentive** wrong on *this* redline.
> it does **not** cure a **sentiment-gated** reward — that is a per-se part 465 violation regardless
> of disclosure. see the companion brief
> `define.boundary.sentiment-blind-vs-sentiment-gated.[lesson].md`.

## where real programs fall on the line

real programs illustrate both sides. amazon vine discloses the material connection on **every**
review via a uniform badge; a program that pays for reviews but hides the reward does not. a davis
wright tremaine summary of the 2024 rule frames the penalty a program risks if it drops disclosure,
verbatim:

> "The potential cost? As much as $51,744 per violation, and how a violation will be calculated is
> not specified."

**source**: [davis wright tremaine — ftc finalizes rule banning fake consumer reviews](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews)

a goodwin analysis states the same penalty cap, verbatim:

> "The Final Rule additionally authorizes the FTC to seek civil penalties of up to $51,744 per
> violation"

**source**: [goodwin — ftc finalizes rule on consumer reviews](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/09/alerts-practices-cldr-ftc-finalizes-rule-on-consumer-reviews)

| program | reward for a review? | connection disclosed on the review? | side |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------------------------|------|
| amazon vine | yes — a free product | yes — "vine customer review of free product" badge on every review | SAFE |
| amazon early reviewer (discontinued) | yes — a $1–$3 gift card | yes — a program label on the review | SAFE-on-this-line |
| a program that pays for reviews but hides the reward | yes | no | UNSAFE |

the safe rows share one trait: a reader **learns** of the reward on the review. the unsafe design
is the one that hides it.

---

## severity: BLOCKER

an undisclosed reward-linked review is a live § 255.5 / § 5 violation the day it publishes. it is
not a design nuance to revisit later — it is a launch gate.

| dimension | reading |
|-----------|---------|
| is a reward for a verified transaction a material connection? | yes — a monetary-value benefit under § 255.5 |
| does the reward need on-review disclosure? | yes — clear and conspicuous, on the review itself |
| what happens if it is hidden? | deceptive endorsement under § 5; misrepresentation risk under § 465.2 |
| is this a launch blocker? | yes — disclosure must ship with the first reward-linked review |

---

## the honest gap

- **no case law applies § 255.5 to a "reward for a verified transaction" review.** the text
  and the guides are strong, but the specific application is counsel-confirmable.
- **"clear and conspicuous" is a standard, not a template.** a badge on the review is safe; a
  reward buried in terms with no on-review mention is not. the exact on-review disclosure text is a
  counsel-confirmable choice for any actor.
- **disclosure fixes only this redline.** a disclosed reward can still be unlawful if it is
  sentiment-gated. this brief settles the disclosure line only; the sentiment line is separate and
  sharper.
- **the $51,744 figure is the part 465 penalty cap**, quoted from law-firm summaries; the precise
  per-violation math is, per dwt, "not specified."

---

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what does § 255.5 require here? | the reward / material connection be disclosed clearly and conspicuously on the review |
| is a reward for a verified transaction a material connection? | yes — a monetary-value benefit under § 255.5 |
| what crosses the line? | a reward-linked review with the reward hidden from readers |
| where does the lawful side sit? | the reward / material connection is surfaced on the review itself |
| does disclosure cure it all? | no — it cures only the disclosure wrong, not a sentiment-gated reward |
