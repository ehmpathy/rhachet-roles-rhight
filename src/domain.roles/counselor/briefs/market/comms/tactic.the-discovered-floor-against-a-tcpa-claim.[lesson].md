# tactic.the-discovered-floor-against-a-tcpa-claim

## .what

the **floor** this engagement's research actually supports — the minimum stack a sender can build
where **every layer traces to a named authority**, assembled in one place.

⛔ **this is a floor, not a guarantee, and the difference is the whole point of the brief.** the
research found **no mechanism that is sure**. what it found is four independent layers, each of which
defeats a different track, each of which fails for a different reason — plus two named gaps that
remain open even when all four are in place.

> **recommendation disclaimer.** this stack is offered to the best of our knowledge, based on the
> information found in research — it carries **no guarantee of success or outcome**, and it must be
> reviewed by a licensed professional (attorney / broker / licensed advisor) against your own real
> scenario and current law before any reliance. it is groundwork for that review, not a substitute
> for it.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

> ⚠️ **this is a floor, not a peak.** it is the best minimum we have found so far, and every
> element traces to a cited authority. it is **not** a guarantee, **not** a certification, and
> **not** complete — the gaps below are named because we found them, not because they are the only
> ones that exist. it is dated, and the law it rests on moves. **treat a counter-example as the most
> valuable reply this document can receive.**

> ⚠️ **a note on gerunds.** this file trips `rule.forbid.gerunds` on terms that cannot be altered:
> verbatim regulatory text (`"bearing the signature"`, `"maintains records documenting this process"`),

> name of a regulation's own standard (`training of personnel`), and a case name
> (`Insurance Marketing Coalition`). to reword a quote is citation fraud; to rename a term of art

## ⭐ why no other brief holds this

the eight `comms/` briefs and the three `insurer/` briefs each answer one slice, and the index orders
them. **none of them stacks them** — and the reason is structural rather than editorial:

⭐ **the four tracks are defeated by four different things.** a reader who solves one and assumes it
carries is wrong on the other three.

| the track | what actually defeats it | what does **not** |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **`§ 227(b)`** — autodialer or artificial/prerecorded voice to a wireless number | ⭐ **prior express written consent**, tied to that number, still producible | a suppression program |
| **`§ 227(c)`** — the do-not-call track | ⭐ a **suppression program**, established *and* implemented with due care | ⛔ **consent — the statutory defense has no consent element at all** |
| **the multiplier** — class aggregation, which is where the dollars are | an **arbitration clause with a class waiver whose formation reaches the claimant** | a perfect consent record |
| **the cost** of any of the above | **cover bought before the claim**, verified on the grant's subject | a standard CGL, which names the TCPA in its exclusion |

⚠️ **read the second row twice.** it is the single most likely misread in this subject. a sender with
flawless per-number written consent is **still exposed** on `§ 227(c)`, because that subsection is
framed around objections rather than agreement.

## the floor — six layers, each with its authority

### layer 1 — prior express written consent, per number

`47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(9)` enumerates it, so this is a **checklist the regulation wrote**, not a
design choice:

- an agreement *"in writing, bearing the signature of the person called"*
- that **clearly authorizes** the seller to deliver advertisements or telemarketing messages by
  automatic telephone dialing system or artificial/prerecorded voice
- which **names the telephone number** to which they are authorized
- with the disclosure that the person is **not required to sign** as a condition of purchase
- e-signature counts

⭐ **this layer survived the 2025 vacatur, and the popular read says otherwise.**
`Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC` vacated the FCC's *added* one-to-one restrictions — and
its footnote 1 records that *"The 2012 Order is not at issue in this case."* the 2012 Order is what
makes consent **written** for a telemarketing purpose. see
`define.what-the-2025-vacatur-did-and-did-not-do.[lesson].md`.

⚠️ **the burden sits on the sender.** consent is an affirmative defense, so the record must be
producible per number, years later. see `hazard.unprovable-consent-record.[hazard].md`.

### layer 2 — revocation, honored inside ten business days

`§ 64.1200(a)(10)`. two operative terms that a keyword allowlist alone fails:

- revocation may be by **any reasonable method**, not a list you choose
- ⭐ *"may not designate an exclusive means to request revocation of consent"* — a program that
  accepts only literal `STOP`, or routes revocation solely to a web form, is **non-compliant by
  construction**
- honored *"within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days from receipt"*

⚠️ **and ten business days is not the only clock.** `Fla. Stat. § 501.059(10)(c)` makes a `STOP` reply
a **condition precedent** to any text-message damages action and gives the sender **15 calendar
days** to cure — a different length, a different character, and a different unit. **build to the
strictest operative deadline across your served states**, which `§ 501.059` alone does not tell you.

### layer 3 — ⭐ the internal suppression program, actually run

`§ 64.1200(d)`'s **six minimum standards**. this is the layer that consent does not reach, and the
one the statute names a defense for.

| # | standard | the operative requirement |
|---|----------|---------------------------|
| 1 | written policy | *"available upon demand"* |
| 2 | training of personnel | informed and trained in the list's existence and use |
| 3 | record + disclose | list the number *"at the time the request is made"*; honor within ten business days |
| 4 | identification of callers | the individual caller's name, the entity, a contact number — *"may not be a 900 number"* |
| 5 | affiliated persons | a request reaches affiliates only where *"the consumer reasonably would expect them to be included"* |
| 6 | maintenance | honored for **5 years** |

⛔ **the trap inside standard 3:** where a vendor keeps the list, *"the person or entity on whose
behalf the call is made **will be liable** for any failures to honor the do-not-call request."* you
may outsource the list; you may not outsource the liability.

### layer 4 — registry scrub, no older than 31 days

the regulatory safe harbour at `§ 64.1200(c)(2)(i)` requires a registry version obtained *"no more
than 31 days prior to the date any call is made"*, and that the caller *"maintains records
documenting this process"*. ⚠️ **the record is part of the standard, not evidence of it.**

### layer 5 — an arbitration clause whose **formation** reaches the claimant

this is the layer that governs the size of the number, and the research found the axis is **not**
customer status.

| the notice was… | the outcome |
|-----------------|-------------|
| a labeled button, terms disclosed adjacent, the act of the click tied to the terms | assent formed |
| a reference set apart from the act, smaller type, separate medium, reachable only by an independent step | ⛔ assent **not** formed |

`Soliman v. Subway Franchisee Advert. Fund Tr.` is the second row: *"Subway has failed to demonstrate
that such terms and conditions would be clear and conspicuous to a reasonable person in Soliman's
position"* — a short-code enrollment off an in-store poster produced no inquiry notice.

⚠️ **and a clause can fail before conspicuity is ever reached.** in `Knutson v. Sirius XM Radio Inc.`
the claimant had no contract with the caller at all: *"A reasonable person in Knutson's position could
not be expected to understand that purchasing a vehicle from Toyota would simultaneously bind him or
her to any contract with Sirius XM."* a purchased-list recipient sits in exactly that posture.

see `define.boundary.formation-not-customer-status-is-the-axis.[lesson].md`.

### layer 6 — cover verified **before** the claim

⛔ **not curable after.** the grant is settled by the law of the state where suit lands; the exclusion
by language bought years earlier.

- the standard CGL **names the TCPA** in its exclusion — that is the default, not an accident
- ⭐ read the grant's nouns: keyed to **information** (a breach product — does not reach a TCPA claim)
  or to **contact**. `Doctors Direct Ins., Inc. v. Bochenek` construed a real cyber endorsement keyed
  to data and **affirmed** the denial — which is why "we have cyber" is not an answer to a robotext
  class action
- a buy-back is worth what its **replacement** text says, never what its deletion says

⛔ **and layer 6 is the least sure layer in this stack.** `Yahoo Inc. v. National Union Fire Ins. Co.`
is the case where a buyer **did** negotiate the TCPA exclusion out — and the same endorsement
*"expressly carved out"* privacy injuries caused by material in an advertisement, a second endorsement
in the same policy added a statutory exclusion back, and the buyer still needed the Ninth Circuit and
the California Supreme Court to learn what it had bought. **an endorsement is a real mechanism and a
poor guarantee.**

see `../../../../insurer/briefs/hazard.either-question-can-defeat-gl-coverage-of-a-tcpa-claim.[hazard].md`
for the two questions a policy must answer, both quoted from the forms and the decisions.

## ⛔ what the floor still does NOT cover

the honest bound. these stay open **even when all six layers stand**, and each is named rather than
papered over.

| the gap | why it is open |
|---------|----------------|
| ⭐ **`§ 227(c)(5)`'s "due care" is unconstrued here** | the defense's text is quoted; **no decision that applies the standard was read.** so what a court demands of the program is unknown |
| ⭐ **California requires a live mid-call voice announcement** (`§ 2874`) — and **Indiana states a live-operator route as an alternative** to consent | **no written record satisfies California's announcement**, so a program built to layers 1–4 is compliant federally and can still fail in California on the consent step itself. ⚠️ Indiana's route is disjunctive (`(1)` consent **or** `(2)` live operator), so a valid written record still reaches it — it is not the same gap as California |
| the internal-list failure has **no enumerated shelter** | per `Dish`: *"The FCC Rule safe harbor only applied to Registry Calls."* layer 4's checklist does not cover a layer-3 failure |
| the state layer is **statutory text only** | no case law was read in any of the five states, and five is not fifty |
| the residential/wireless boundary in `(d)` | `(d)` is written to a *"residential telephone subscriber"*; whether these duties reach a wireless number was not researched |
| what cover **costs**, and who writes it | ⬜ unresearched — every coverage decision found is a dispute over an instrument already bought |
| individual/officer exposure | whether liability reaches past the corporate entity was not researched |

⚠️ **the first two rows are why this brief is titled a floor.** the layer with a **named statutory
defense** is the layer whose standard **no court has been read to apply**, and the layer that is
strongest federally is the one California can defeat outright — because its live-announcement route
takes no written record.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| ⭐ **can protection be guaranteed?** | ⛔ **no.** the research found no sure mechanism — it found four tracks defeated by four different things, and two gaps that stay open even when all stand |
| does consent settle it? | ⛔ **no** — consent answers `§ 227(b)`. `§ 227(c)`'s statutory defense has **no consent element** |
| what is the one layer most likely to be skipped? | ⭐ **layer 3** — a suppression program feels redundant to a sender who has consent, and it is the only layer the statute names a defense for |
| what is the one layer that governs the dollars? | **layer 5** — the multiplier, not the per-message rate, is where the exposure lives |
| what is the deadline nobody sets for you? | **layer 6** — cover is fixable only before a claim |
| which layer is strongest and weakest at once? | ⭐ **layer 3** — a named statutory defense whose *"due care"* standard **no read decision applies** |
| is a written program enough? | ⛔ **no** — the text requires practices *"established **and** implemented, with due care"* |
| what beats the whole federal stack? | ⚠️ **California's live-announcement requirement** (`§ 2874`) — no written record satisfies it. (Indiana states a live-operator route too, but as an alternative to consent, so a record still reaches it) |
