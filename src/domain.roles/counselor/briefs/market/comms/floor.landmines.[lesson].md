# floor.landmines

## .what

the catalogue of **landmines** found across this subject — each one a place where a reasonable,
careful design step produces a wrong result, and the person who took it has no signal that it did.

⭐ **63 entries in 11 groups, and every one is marked for who steps on it.** 🪨 **type ⓪** is the plain
rule that defeats a reader with **no model** of this law; ⚡ **type I / II** is the surprise that
defeats a reader **with** one. ⛔ **both kinds are enumerated — the obvious is never omitted for being
obvious** — and only the surprises carry the ⚡, so each reader can find their own half at a glance.

⭐ **it holds both directions of the sweep, and group `J` is why.** the same instrument was read twice —
once for what it **demands**, once for what it **grants** — because a sweep returns what it was sent to
find. ⛔ **a catalogue built from the first read alone leads a reader to over-reserve** against rules
that never reached them, and no event ever corrects that: an unknown duty arrives as a demand letter,
an unknown protection never arrives at all.

⚠️ **an earlier cut of this engagement put the grants half in a separate file** (`floor.armory`), split
into "armor" and "shields" on the claim that the first kind was passive and unloseable. ⛔ **that claim
was false on its own entries** — every protection here carries conditions you must establish. the
entries were folded into group `J`, and the defect itself is recorded as `J6`.

⭐ **a landmine is not "a risk."** a risk is an outcome you have weighed. a landmine is what you step
on. ⛔ **some are buried and some sit in plain sight, and both blow up the same.** that is why they are
catalogued apart from the law itself: the law is in the other briefs, and this file maps **the whole
minefield** — marked for which mines you can see from where you stand.

### ⭐ nightwalkers and lightwalkers — this file serves both

⛔ **an earlier cut of this file served the lightwalker only.** every entry was framed as a
**correction to a belief** — *"you thought X; here is why X is false"* — which hands a reader who
never held X no help at all. that reader is the **nightwalker**: someone who does not yet know this
body of law reaches them.

| the walker | what they hold | ⛔ what defeats them |
|---|---|---|
| 🌞 **lightwalker** | a live model of the law, learned in the trade | ⚡ the **surprise** — where the model is wrong, or has a hole |
| 🌑 **nightwalker** | ⭐ **no model at all** — they may not know the statute reaches them | 🪨 the **plain** rule, which a catalogue never bothers to state because it surprised no author |

⭐ **the rule this file holds itself to: enumerate every mine, and mark the surprises.** a plain mine
is **never omitted for obviousness** — it is stated flatly and marked 🪨, so a lightwalker skims past
it in one glance. a surprise is marked ⚡, so a nightwalker knows exactly which rows their intuition
will not carry them through.

### the three kinds, and why the difference is operational

| | 🪨 **type ⓪** — **no belief** is held | ⚡ **type I** — the belief is **wrong** | ⚡ **type II** — the belief is **absent** |
|---|---|---|---|
| what it is | ⭐ the **plain rule** — obvious to anyone who has read the instrument, invisible to anyone who has not | a correct read of a **partial** source yields a false conclusion | a requirement that is perfectly obvious **once stated** — you simply never encountered it |
| the tell | *"is this law even about us?"* | *"we checked that"* | *"nobody raised it"* |
| ⭐ how it is found | ✅ ask *"what does this demand of someone who has never heard of it?"* — ⛔ **the question an expert forgets to ask** | ⛔ **only by contradiction.** a re-read cannot find it — your read was already correct on its own terms; what was wrong was the **scope** you gave it | ✅ **by an exhaustive read.** the requirement sits in the instrument, and a sweep end-to-end surfaces every one |
| who steps on it | 🌑 the **nightwalker** | 🌞 the lightwalker | 🌞 the lightwalker |
| what it costs to find | ⭐ a deliberate refusal to use what you already know | a counter-example, which you cannot schedule | an afternoon with the full text |
| what fixes it | an **orientation** — the trigger question, answered | a correction | a checklist |
| ⚠️ does it recur? | **no** — once you know the law is about you, you know it | **yes** — the intuition survives the correction and reasserts on the next adjacent question | **no** — once written down, it stays written |

⭐ **type II is the largest half, and it is the one most often left out of a hazard list**, because a
list built from "what went wrong" only captures beliefs that failed loudly. an absent belief fails
**quietly** — the requirement is simply never met, and no alarm sounds.

⚠️ **and neither ⓪ nor II is only more duties.** `D11` is a threshold that cuts in the **defendant's**
favor, and the whole of group `J` runs that way. an unknown is an unknown in both directions.

⛔ **type ⓪ is the kind a catalogue is structurally worst at, and the reason deserves a plain
sentence.** a research pass is run by people who have just read the instrument, so **what is obvious to
the author is invisible to the artifact** — the blind spot grows with the expertise. group `K` exists
to close it, and `K7` records the defect itself.

⚠️ **the faces can sit on one entry.** `G1` is the clean example: you do not know California sets a
different window (II), so you believe your federal-window dialer is compliant (I). the marks below are
an aid to the reader, not a taxonomy that carries weight — where an entry has more than one face it is
marked for the one that governs how you would find it.

⛔ **this file prescribes no action.** it states what is false or absent, and what is true, with the
authority for each. the lines to hold are in `floor.redlines.[lesson].md`, which cites back into this
inventory by ID — so a redline with no landmine behind it is visible as invented caution.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

> ⚠️ **this inventory is not complete, and cannot be.** each entry is here because research tripped
> over it. an absence from this list is **not** evidence that a step is safe — it is evidence that
> nothing in this engagement's sources contradicted it. **treat a new landmine as the most valuable
> reply this document can receive.**

> ⚠️ **a note on gerunds.** this file trips `rule.forbid.gerunds` almost entirely on text that cannot
> be altered: verbatim statutory language (`"an automatic telephone dialing system"`, `"using an
> artificial or prerecorded voice"`, `"bearing the signature"`, `"maintaining a list"`, `"records
> documenting this process"`, `"evidencing a transaction involving commerce"`, `"the making of the
> arbitration agreement"`, `"an agreement in writing"`, `"arising out of"`), verbatim judicial text
> (`"Lead Tracking System"`, `"calling lists"`, `"purchasing a vehicle from Toyota"`, `"without
> considering or resolving that issue"`, `"regarding the operation"`), a party name (`Insurance
> Marketing Coalition`), and terms of art (`telemarketing`, `advertising`, `marketing call`,
> `mailing list`, and `holding` in its judicial sense). to reword a quote is citation fraud; to
> rename a party breaks the search path to the case.

## 🌑 K — the plain rules, for the walker who arrived in the dark

⭐ **this group is first because it is the orientation every other group presumes.** groups `A`–`J`
correct beliefs. a reader who holds no belief needs the rule stated flatly before a correction to it
can mean anything.

🪨 **every row here is type ⓪ — obvious to anyone who has read the instrument.** they carry no ⚡
because they surprise no practitioner. ⛔ **they defeat a nightwalker exactly as hard.**

| ID | the question you would have to think to ask | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|--------------------------------------------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **K1** | *does this law reach my company at all?* | ⛔ **it reaches the call, not the industry.** `47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A)(iii)` bars a call to a number *"assigned to a … cellular telephone service"* placed *"using any automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice"*. no sector, size, or volume threshold appears in the text — ⭐ **one call can be a violation**, and there is a **private** right of action | 🪨 **⓪** | `(b)` |
| **K2** | *if I have consent, is there anything else?* | ⛔ **yes — a whole second track.** `§ 227(c)(5)` gives its own private right to *"a person who has received more than one telephone call within any 12-month period"* to a registered number. it turns on **suppression practices**, and its affirmative defense has **no consent element**. ⭐ **two tracks, two artifacts** — a consent record and a suppression program are not substitutes | 🪨 **⓪** | `(c)` |
| **K3** | *is a promotional call different from any other call?* | ⛔ **yes, and it is the stricter one.** `§ 64.1200(a)(2)` requires *"prior express written consent"* for a call that *"includes or introduces an advertisement or constitutes telemarketing"*. a non-solicitation call to the same number takes the lower *prior express* standard. ⭐ **purpose decides which standard, never whether consent is needed** | 🪨 **⓪** | `(b)` |
| **K4** | *who decides whether my message is "telemarketing"?* | ⛔ **the content does, never your label for it.** the text reaches a call that *"includes or introduces an advertisement"* — so **one promotional sentence appended to an order confirmation** puts that message inside the paragraph. an internal classification of a campaign as "transactional" decides the question not at all | 🪨 **⓪** | `(b)` |
| **K5** | *does it matter whose phone it is — a consumer's or a business contact's?* | ⛔ **the hook attaches to the NUMBER, not to the recipient's role.** a business contact's mobile is *"a number assigned to a … cellular telephone service"*, and the text does not ask who the person is or why they were called. ⭐ so *"we are B2B, so the TCPA is not about us"* does not follow from the text. 🔶 the outer edge of *"assigned to a cellular telephone service"* is litigated (`Breda v. Cellco Partnership`, 934 F.3d 1 (1st Cir. 2019)) and no survey of it was run | 🪨 **⓪** | `(b)` |
| **K6** | *we only text — is a text a call?* | the FCC reads *"call"* to include text messages, and `§ 64.1200(a)(9)` says so in its own words: *"the term 'call' includes a text message, including a short message service (SMS) call"*. ⚠️ **the Supreme Court assumed rather than decided the point** — `Duguid` footnote 2: *"we **assume that it does without considering or resolving that issue**"*. ⭐ so a text-only program is inside the regime on the agency's reading, and the judicial question is open | 🪨 **⓪** | `(b)` |
| ⭐ **K7** | *whose landmines does a landmine catalogue actually hold?* | ⛔ **this row is about our own artifact.** every group `A`–`J` was framed as a correction to a belief, which serves a reader who **already has a model** and hands no help at all to one who does not. ⚠️ **the omission was not random**: a research pass is run by people who just read the instrument, so **the plain rule is invisible to the artifact precisely because it was obvious to the author** — ⭐ **a catalogue's blind spot is proportional to its authors' expertise**. `floor.mustcants` promises *"not defeated"*, and for a nightwalker that promise was **false** while the plain rules went unwritten | ⚡ **I** | all |

⭐ **`K7` is a landmine about the method rather than the subject, and it is the second of its kind** —
`J6` was the first. it was found the way a type I always is: ⛔ **by contradiction**, when a reader
asked why a landmine had to be a surprise at all. ⚠️ **and note it is the one ⚡ row in a 🪨 group** —
that plain rules were missing is itself a surprise, which is exactly why it went unnoticed.

⛔ **this group is the least complete in the file, and its incompleteness is structural.** the six
plain rules above are the trigger axes of `define.boundary.when-consent-is-required-at-all`, projected
here. a genuine type ⓪ sweep would need a reader who has **not** read the instrument, and no such
reader was available. ⚠️ **treat `K` as a floor on the plain rules, never a peak.**

## A — track confusion

⭐ **the largest family, and the one that produces the most expensive errors**, because every member
has the same shape: a defense that genuinely works is believed to work against a track it never
touched.

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **A1** | "our consent records are flawless, so we are covered" | ⛔ `§ 227(c)`'s affirmative defense has **no consent element** — it asks whether you *"established and implemented, with due care, reasonable practices and procedures"*. the subsection is framed around **objection**, not agreement. a perfect consent file is not an answer to it | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **A2** | "we do not autodial, so `§ 227(b)` is not our problem" | ⛔ the statute bars a call made *"using an automatic telephone dialing system **or** an artificial or prerecorded voice"* — two independent hooks joined by "or". a prerecorded message dialed by hand is inside the second | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **A3** | "the do-not-call rules are about robocalls" | ⛔ `§ 227(c)` is **dialer-agnostic**. a manually-dialed, live, human-voice marketing call sits outside every `§ 227(b)` hook and squarely inside this one | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **A4** | "we have federal written consent, so we are consented nationwide" | ⛔ two of five surveyed states require a **different kind** of consent, not a stronger version of the same kind. IN accepts a message *"immediately preceded by a live operator who obtains the subscriber's consent"*; `Cal. Pub. Util. Code § 2874` requires an *"unrecorded, natural voice announcement"* that must *"[i]nquire as to whether the person called consents"*. ⭐ **no written record satisfies either** — the artifact is a live exchange | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` `(c)` |
| **A5** | "the arbitration clause caps our exposure" | ⛔ it reaches **private** claims. a state attorney general's action is not a class action, so no waiver affects it — and the largest figures found carry no private right at all | ⚡ **I** | none — it is the gap |

## B — the two authorities most often misread

⚠️ **both misreads run the same direction: they overstate how much protection was removed.** a reader
who acts on either loosens a program the law did not loosen.

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **B1** | "the 2025 vacatur struck down the FCC's consent rule" | ⛔ one **part** of one **2023** order was vacated. `IMC` footnote 1 says: *"The 2012 Order is not at issue in this case."* — and the 2012 Order is what carries the written-consent baseline | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **B2** | "*Duguid* narrowed the TCPA" | it narrowed the **autodialer** hook. the Court noted that *"The statute separately prohibits calls using 'an artificial or prerecorded voice'"* — that hook is untouched | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **B3** | "*Duguid* settled that texts are covered" | ⛔ it **assumed** it. the opinion records that *"Neither party disputes"* the point, and the Court proceeded *"without considering or resolving that issue"*. an assumption is not a holding | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **B4** | a brief cites `135 F.4th 230` | ⛔ that opinion was **withdrawn and substituted** on 2025-08-22. the live one is `AT&T v. FCC`, No. 24-60223 (5th Cir. Aug. 22, 2025). ⚠️ **a pure type II** — no intuition is wrong here; a withdrawal is knowable only by a check | ⚡ **II** | — |

## C — the record exists and proves nothing

⭐ **every entry here describes a system that passes an internal audit and fails in court**, because
the audit asks "do we have consent?" and the court asks "produce it, and put on a witness."

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **C1** | "our CRM holds every contact, so it holds our consent" | ⛔ `Dish` found a system that *"included the contact information for anybody who came in contact with Dish for almost any reason and provided contact information."* a system that cannot separate **who authorized contact** from **who merely touched the business** holds a mailing list | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **C2** | "the record points at our terms" | the consent `§ 64.1200(f)(9)` defines is an agreement *"bearing the signature of the person called that clearly authorizes"* delivery **to the telephone number specified**. a pointer to *current* terms cannot show what was on screen then | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **C3** | "we have the data; we can assemble the proof at claim time" | ⛔ `Dish` also failed for want of a **witness**: *"no testimony from any representative … who had pe[rsonal] knowledge to testify regarding the operation of the Lead Tracking System"*, and *"very little competent evidence on how the Lead Tracking System was formulated or how calling lists were derived"* | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **C4** | "our lead vendor certifies the leads are compliant" | a vendor's assurance is **a contractual promise between the vendor and the sender**. it is not the artifact a court asks for, and it does not travel to the plaintiff's side of the caption | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **C5** | "we will fix the record design later" | ⛔ **none of it is retro-fittable.** a record of what was displayed cannot be reconstructed after the display changed; a custodian cannot acquire personal knowledge of a system retroactively. every requirement here is **build-time** | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **C6** | *the claim window was never asked about* | the TCPA states **no limitations period of its own**, so a claim runs on the federal catch-all at `28 U.S.C. § 1658(a)` — **four years**. ⚠️ **that it governs a TCPA claim is an inference, not a read holding**; the statutory text is quoted, its application is not. ⭐ the consequence is a **retention floor**, and a record destroyed inside the window is a defense destroyed with it | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |

## D — the suppression program

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **D1** | "we honor `STOP`, so revocation is handled" | ⛔ `§ 64.1200(a)(10)` says a caller *"may not designate an exclusive means"* to revoke. ⭐ a program that accepts only literal `STOP`, or routes revocation solely to a web form, is **non-compliant by construction** — not by a judgment call | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **D2** | "our vendor keeps the list, so it is their exposure" | ⛔ *"the person or entity on whose behalf the call is made **will be liable** for any failures to honor the do-not-call request."* the custody is delegable; the liability is not | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **D3** | "we pass suppressions to our partners — that is more protective" | ⚠️ a consent duty hides inside the non-consent track: to forward a request beyond the caller or an affiliate you *"must obtain a consumer's **prior express permission** to share or forward"* it. the protective act is itself gated | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **D4** | "we run the safe harbour, so an opt-out slip is sheltered" | ⛔ `Dish` records: *"The TSR safe harbor applied to Internal List Calls and Registry Calls. **The FCC Rule safe harbor only applied to Registry Calls.**"* ⭐ an **internal-list** failure has no enumerated FCC shelter — only the unconstrued *"due care"* sentence | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **D5** | "we scrub the registry; the logs are just ops telemetry" | in `§ 64.1200(c)(2)(i)(D)` the standard is a registry version obtained *"no more than 31 days prior to the date any call is made"* **and** that the caller *"maintains records documenting this process"*. ⭐ **the record is part of the standard, not evidence of it** | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **D6** | "we have a written do-not-call policy" | ⛔ `§ 227(c)(5)` requires that the defendant *"has **established and implemented**, with due care"*. read the conjunction — the artifact and its exercise are **both** elements. a policy nobody runs does not match the text | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **D7** | *the policy is internal, so its form was never asked about* | `§ 64.1200(d)(1)`: you *"must have a written policy, **available upon demand**, for maintaining a do-not-call list."* ⭐ a policy that cannot be **produced on request** does not meet the standard, however well it is run | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **D8** | *training was scoped to our own staff* | `§ 64.1200(d)(2)` requires personnel be *"informed and trained in the existence and use of the do-not-call list"* — and the safe harbour at `(c)(2)(i)(B)` reaches *"any entity assisting in its compliance."* ⭐ **the duty crosses the org boundary into your vendors** | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **D9** | *the script was written for conversion, not for `(d)(4)`* | `§ 64.1200(d)(4)` requires *"the name of the individual caller"*, the entity's name, and a contact number or address — and that number *"may not be a 900 number"*. ⭐ four required elements in the call itself, none of them intuitive to a script author | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **D10** | *registry access was procured like any other data feed* | `§ 64.1200(c)(2)(i)(E)` requires purchase from the administrator **directly**, with no cost-share arrangement that divides access costs among client sellers. ⭐ a shared or resold subscription can defeat the safe harbour on procurement alone | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **D11** | *exposure was modelled as though every violative call is actionable* | ⭐ **this one cuts your way.** `§ 227(c)(5)` reaches only a person who received *"**more than one** telephone call within any 12-month period"* by or for the same entity. a single violative call creates **no** `§ 227(c)` claim — while a single autodialed message can create a `§ 227(b)` one. ⚠️ a program unaware of the asymmetry **over-reserves** | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |

## E — formation, and the clause that does not reach

⭐ **the whole family turns on one inversion:** the question is never *was this person a customer?*
but *did this person assent, at an act, with notice?*

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **E1** | "our terms of service contain a class waiver" | ⛔ `9 U.S.C. § 2` makes a provision enforceable inside *"a contract evidencing a transaction involving commerce"*. **where there is no contract, there is no provision to enforce** — a perfectly drafted waiver in an unformed contract is worth zero | ⚡ **I** | `×` |
| **E2** | "the arbitrator will sort out whether they agreed" | ⛔ `9 U.S.C. § 4`: *"If the making of the arbitration agreement … be in issue, the court shall proceed summarily to the trial thereof"*, and *"If the jury find that no agreement in writing for arbitration was made … the proceeding shall be dismissed."* formation is a fact question **a plaintiff may put to a jury** | ⚡ **I** | `×` |
| **E3** | "we link to the terms on the signup page" | ⚠️ two of `Soliman`'s five defeating features are the ones most likely to sit in a flow that otherwise looks careful: **no statement that the act would constitute agreement**, and a destination page headed as terms *for the website*, readable as unrelated. a link labeled only "terms" satisfies neither | ⚡ **I** | `×` |
| **E4** | "the clause covers our whole send list" | ⛔ `Knutson` is the **purchased-list problem, stated by a court**: *"A reasonable person in Knutson's position could not be expected to understand that purchasing a vehicle from Toyota would simultaneously bind him or her to any contract with Sirius XM."* a number from outside your own contract flow is a number whose holder formed no contract with you | ⚡ **I** | `×` |
| **E5** | "we answered the formation question for our program" | the answer is **per source**, and one source's answer does not carry to another. a campaign assembled from several sources has several answers | ⚡ **I** | `×` |
| **E6** | "non-customers cannot be bound" | ⭐ **customer status is not the axis.** in `Dahdah` a **nonsignatory lender** enforced a referral service's clause against a consumer who had never contracted with it — because notice and assent were validly formed | ⚡ **I** | `×` |
| **E7** | *the clause was reviewed once, against federal law* | `Dahdah`: *"state law likewise governs whether the parties formed a binding contract to arbitrate."* ⭐ so **one flow can form a contract in one state and fail in another**, and a single federal review does not answer the question for a national footprint | ⚡ **II** | `×` |

## F — cover

⛔ **every entry here is one that cannot be repaired after a claim arrives.**

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **F1** | "we have the certificate of insurance" | the certificate is a summary. what decides the question is the **complete policy**, the **full endorsement schedule**, and the **form edition stamp** | ⚡ **I** | `$` |
| **F2** | "there is no TCPA exclusion in the policy" | ⚠️ search by **substance**, never by title. a clause that bars injury arising out of an act *that violates a statute* reaches the TCPA without a mention of it | ⚡ **I** | `$` |
| **F3** | "we have cyber cover" | ⭐ read the grant's nouns. if they are *information*, *data*, *records*, the product is keyed to a **breach** — and a claim about **contact** falls outside it. this is why "we have cyber" is not an answer to a robotext class action | ⚡ **I** | `$` |
| **F4** | "we negotiated the exclusion out" | a buy-back is worth what its **replacement text** says, never what its deletion says | ⚡ **I** | `$` |
| **F5** | "we will sort out cover when something happens" | ⛔ the coverage fight is won or lost **at purchase time**; neither condition is curable after a claim | ⚡ **I** | `$` |
| **F6** | *the limit was read; the defence terms were never asked about* | ⭐ on a per-message statute the **defence spend on a class action can rival the indemnity** — so whether defence costs **erode the limit** can be worth more than the limit's headline number. ⚠️ also unasked by default: is there a **sublimit**, and is it a **duty to defend** or indemnity only | ⚡ **II** | `$` |

## G — the state layer

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **G1** | "we call inside the federal window" | ⛔ `Cal. Pub. Util. Code § 2872(c)` sets California's window at **9 p.m. to 9 a.m.** — stricter at the early end than the federal 8 a.m. boundary. a federal-window dialer is **non-compliant for an hour every day** in California. ⚠️ **the clean both-faces case**: you do not know CA differs (II), so you believe you are inside the window (I) | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **G2** | "ten business days is stricter than fifteen days, so the federal clock covers Florida" | ⚠️ they are **not the same deadline in different units**: the federal duty is a compliance obligation in *business* days; the `Fla. | **G3** | "five states surveyed, so we can generalize to fifty" | ⛔ the five produced **three different consent architectures** — written record, live per-call, and suppression-list. ⭐ the variance is a **measured result**, not a worry: a uniform sample could be generalized; this one cannot | ⚡ **I** | all |

## I — the message classes and the exemptions

⭐ **this group came out of one sweep**, of the paragraphs of `§ 64.1200(a)` and `(e)` that earlier
passes never asked for. eight of its ten entries are **type II** — which is what a sweep produces, and
is the clearest demonstration in this file that the type II half is a matter of the read rather than
of experience.

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **I1** | "transactional and informational messages need no consent" | ⛔ `§ 64.1200(a)(1)` bars **any** call made with an autodialer or an artificial or prerecorded voice to a wireless number *"(other than a call made for emergency purposes or is made with the **prior express consent** of the called party)"*. ⭐ purpose decides **which consent standard** applies — written for a solicitation, oral-sufficient otherwise — never **whether** consent is needed at all | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **I2** | *the exemption list was never read* | `§ 64.1200(a)(9)` exempts four named classes from the wireless prohibition — package delivery, inmate collect call service, financial institutions, healthcare providers — each on a **conjunctive** condition set: content limits, a message-count cap, a length bound, and an opt-out deadline that ranges from *"immediately"* to *"six business days"*. ⭐ it also defines the unit: *"the term 'call' includes a text message, including a short message service (SMS) call"* | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **I3** | "no caller may designate an exclusive way to opt out" | ⚠️ **that is `(a)(10)` — and `(a)(9)` says the opposite for the classes it exempts.** a financial-institution or healthcare text must inform recipients of the ability to opt out by reply of *"STOP," which will be the exclusive means by which consumers may opt out of such messages*. ⭐ **two paragraphs of one section pull opposite ways**, and which governs turns on whether the message sits inside `(a)(1)` or is exempted from it. ⬜ no decision that reconciles them was read | ⚡ **I** | `(b)` |
| **I4** | *the revocation confirmation was treated as a courtesy* | `§ 64.1200(a)(12)` makes a one-time confirmation lawful only where it *"merely confirms the text recipient's revocation request and does not include any marketing or promotional information, and is the only additional message sent"*. ⭐ **and it sets a clock**: within **five minutes** it is presumed inside consent; beyond that *"the sender will have to make a showing that such delay was reasonable"* | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **I5** | *revocation was scoped to the listed channels* | `§ 64.1200(a)(11)`: a method **not** listed — a voicemail or email to any number or address intended to reach the caller — *"creates a rebuttable presumption that the consumer has revoked consent"* once the called party produces evidence of it. ⭐ the burden then sits on the **caller**, under a totality-of-circumstances test | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **I6** | *the dialer's cadence was an operations option* | `§ 64.1200(a)(6)`–`(7)` make it regulation: no disconnect *"prior to at least 15 seconds or four (4) rings"*; no abandonment of *"more than three percent of all telemarketing calls that are answered live by a person, as measured over a 30-day period for a single calling campaign"*; a live representative within **two seconds** or a prescribed identification-and-opt-out message carried by *"an automated, interactive voice- and/or key press-activated opt-out mechanism"*. ⭐ and `(a)(7)(iii)`: *"The seller or telemarketer must maintain records establishing compliance"* — a **third** record duty | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **I7** | "the do-not-call program rules are about residential landlines" | ⛔ `§ 64.1200(e)`: *"The rules set forth in paragraph (c) and (d) of this section are applicable to any person or entity making telephone solicitations or telemarketing calls **or text messages to wireless telephone numbers**"*. ⚠️ but only *"to the extent described in"* an FCC Report and Order (CG Docket No. 02-278, FCC 03-153) **which was not read** — so the extension is certain and its **bounds are not** | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| **I8** | *a suppression request was assumed to cover the corporate family* | `§ 64.1200(d)(5)`: absent a specific contrary request, a do-not-call request *"shall apply to the particular entity making the call … and will not apply to affiliated entities unless the consumer reasonably would expect them to be included"*. ⭐ **a second entry that cuts your way** — the default is narrow, so a group-wide suppression is stricter than the rule demands | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **I9** | *every marketing call was treated as a "telephone solicitation"* | `§ 64.1200(f)(15)` excludes a call *"(i) To any person with that person's prior express invitation or permission; (ii) To any person with whom the caller has an established business relationship; or (iii) By or on behalf of a tax-exempt nonprofit organization."* ⭐ **the established-business-relationship axis lives in the definition, not in an exemption** — so a call inside it is not a solicitation at all. ⚠️ `(f)(6)` defines that term *"for purposes of paragraph (a)(4) of this section on the sending of facsimile advertisements"*, so the term as `(f)(15)` uses it was **not defined in what was read** | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **I10** | *how the dialer seizes lines was never a legal question* | `§ 64.1200(a)(5)` bars use of an autodialer *"in such a way that two or more telephone lines of a multi-line business are engaged simultaneously"*, and `(a)(8)` bars use of *"any technology to dial any telephone number for the purpose of determining whether the line is a facsimile or voice line."* ⭐ two prohibitions on **how the equipment behaves**, independent of consent, purpose, or list hygiene | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |

## ⭐ J — the protections you do not know you hold

⭐ **this group came out of the sweep run in the OTHER direction** — of the same instrument, for what it
**grants** rather than what it demands. ⚠️ **that sweep is the one a hazard pass loses by default**,
because a sweep returns what it was sent to find.

⛔ **and the asymmetry is why the group earns its place.** an unknown **duty** eventually announces
itself — a demand letter arrives. an unknown **protection** announces itself **never**. you simply
spend more than you had to, forever, and no event ever corrects you. so every entry below is a
**silent** cost, and `D11` and `I8` above are two more of the same kind.

⚠️ **a protection is still a landmine, and how it is framed matters.** the entry is not *"here is a
shelter"* — it is *"you believe a rule reaches you, and it does not."* that is a false belief exactly
like every other row in this file, and it is catalogued here for the same reason: the law does not
announce it.

| ID | the belief | what is actually so | t | blast |
|----|-----------|---------------------|---|-------|
| **J1** | *a number that recently moved to wireless was treated as an ordinary wireless number* | `§ 64.1200(a)(1)(iv)` gives a grace: no liability under `(a)(1)(iii)` where ⛔ **all four hold** — it is a **voice** call, *"not knowingly made to a wireless number"*, placed within **15 days** of *"the porting of the number from wireline to wireless service"*, and the number *"is not already on the national do-not-call registry or caller's company-specific do-not-call list"*. ⭐ **this one cuts your way**, and a program unaware of it treats a lawful window as a violation | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **J2** | *every prerecorded call to a residential line was assumed to need consent* | `§ 64.1200(a)(3)(ii)`–`(v)` substitute a **numerical limit** for consent across several classes: **no more than three calls within any consecutive 30-day period** for non-commercial calls, commercial calls that carry no advertisement, and tax-exempt nonprofit calls; and for HIPAA health care, *"no more than one call per day to each patient's residential line, up to a maximum of three calls combined per week"*. ⚠️ each still requires the opt-out duties of `(b)` and `(d)` be honored. ⭐ **cuts your way** | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **J3** | *the written-consent standard was applied to every message that felt promotional* | `§ 64.1200(a)(2)` carves **tax-exempt nonprofit** and HIPAA-defined **"health care"** messages down to *prior express* consent where a solicitation would take *prior express written* consent. ⭐ the carve-out turns on **identity and message content**, never on the sender's intent — and the HIPAA terms are as defined at `45 CFR 160.103`. ⭐ **cuts your way** | ⚡ **II** | `(b)` |
| **J4** | *the registry duty was assumed to reach every call to a registered number* | `§ 64.1200(c)(2)(iii)` excepts a call where *"the telemarketer making the call has a personal relationship with the recipient"*, and `(f)(17)` defines that as *"any family member, friend, or acquaintance of the telemarketer making the call."* ⭐ **cuts your way** — and ⚠️ *"acquaintance"* is undefined in what was read, so its edge is unlocated | ⚡ **II** | `(c)` |
| **J5** | *consent and the registry were treated as one question* | `§ 64.1200(c)(2)(ii)` gives an **independent** route out of the registry duty: the subscriber's *"prior express invitation or permission"*, evidenced by *"a signed, written agreement … [that] includes the telephone number to which the calls may be placed."* ⛔ **but it is a proof, not a status** — the permission may genuinely exist and be unprovable, which is `C3` exactly. ⚠️ it is a **narrower** artifact than `(f)(9)` consent and does not substitute for it | ⚡ **I** | `(c)` |
| ⭐ **J6** | *a protection was treated as a fact you HAVE rather than a condition you must PROVE* | ⛔ **this entry is about our own earlier artifact, and it is the reason this group replaced it.** a prior brief split these into "armor" (passive, unloseable) and "shields" (built and proven) — and the split was **false on its own entries**. to *rely* on `D11` you must count calls per number per 12 months; to rely on `A2` you must show your dialer is not an autodialer, which is the entire `Duguid` litigation; `I2`'s exemptions are **conjunctive**, so one missed condition puts you back under `(a)(1)(iii)` with no consent on file. ⭐ **every protection in this file has conditions, and the conditions are yours to establish** | ⚡ **I** | all |

⭐ **`J6` is the most valuable row in the group, and it is a landmine about the method rather than the
subject.** it was found the only way a type I ever is — ⛔ **by contradiction**, when a reader asked why
armor was different from a shield and the answer did not survive contact with the entries. it is
recorded here rather than quietly repaired, because a defect the artifact's own gate caught is exactly
the kind this inventory exists to hold.

## the honest gap

- ⛔ **this paragraph is a correction, and the correction is the point.** an earlier draft of this file
  said *"no method was applied to find the mines nobody stepped on, and no such method is proposed
  here."* that was true when the file held only **type I** entries, and it is **half-wrong** now. ⭐ for
  **type II a method does exist and is named above**: read the instrument end to end. the type II
  entries below were found that way — `D7`–`D11` came out of a sweep of `§ 64.1200(d)` and
  `(c)(2)(i)`, not out of an incident. ⚠️ **for type I the original sentence stands unchanged** — a
  belief that is wrong on a correct read cannot be found by reading more carefully.
- ⚠️ **so the halves are unequally complete, and in different ways.** the type II set is complete
  **for the instruments actually swept** and empty for those that were not. the type I set is complete
  for **no group at all** — it is a record of the contradictions this engagement happened to meet.
- ⛔ **and the type ⓪ set is the weakest of the three, for a reason that cannot be engineered away.**
  group `K` was assembled by a projection of one brief's trigger axes, **not** by a sweep — because the
  sweep it would need is *"what does this demand of someone who has never heard of it?"*, and that
  question cannot be honestly asked by anyone who just spent a week with the instrument. ⭐ **the only
  real instrument for type ⓪ is a genuinely uninitiated reader**, and none was available. `K7` records
  the defect; ⚠️ **`K`'s six rows are a floor on the plain rules, never a peak.**
- ⬜ **the state groups are five-state.** group G's entries come from the surveyed five; the other
  forty-five are unresearched, and per **G3** the sample forbids extrapolation.
- ⬜ **no entry has been tested against a decision that applies it.** several rest on statutory or
  regulatory text whose construction no read case supplies — `§ 227(c)(5)`'s *"due care"* most of all.
- ⚠️ **`Dish` is one case**, and its safe-harbour comparison (**D4**) is descriptive of that record. it
  is the clearest statement found that the two shelters differ in scope; it is not a holding that they
  always do.
- ✅ **the residential/wireless boundary is resolved, and this bullet records the correction.** an
  earlier draft said *"unresolved, so every group-D entry carries an unstated scope question"*. `(e)`
  answers it: `(c)` and `(d)` reach *"text messages to wireless telephone numbers"*. ⚠️ **but the
  answer arrives with a narrower gap attached** — the reach runs only *"to the extent described in"*
  FCC 03-153, and **that order was not read**. so group D is scoped to wireless with **bounds unread**,
  which is a better-shaped hole than the one it replaces, not an absent one. see **I7**.
- ⬜ ⚠️ **`(a)(9)` and `(a)(10)` are unreconciled, and this file cannot reconcile them.** `I3` records a
  direct textual tension inside one section. ⛔ **no decision that construes it was read**, so the file
  states the conflict and stops — a reader who needs an answer needs counsel, not this table.
- ⬜ **the established-business-relationship term is used but not defined in what was read.**
  `(f)(15)` turns on it; the only definition captured, at `(f)(6)`, is expressly scoped to facsimile
  advertisements. **`I9` therefore names an axis whose boundary is unknown.**
