## ⚠️ scope of this set

briefs **1–7** are **federal only** — the TCPA (`47 U.S.C. § 227`), the FCC's rules
(`47 C.F.R. § 64.1200`), and the federal decisions that construe them. each says so in its own
`honest gap`; this line exists so a reader knows it before they open any of them.

⭐ **brief 8 is the exception, and it is the reason to read this line rather than skim it.**
`ref.mini-tcpa-five-state` carries the state layer the other seven disclaim — and its result is **not
"federal, times five."** **California** requires a **live, unrecorded mid-call announcement** that no
written record satisfies (`§ 2874`); Indiana states a live-operator route too, but as an
**alternative** to consent, so a valid written record still reaches Indiana. a program built to
briefs 1–7 alone is compliant federally and can still be non-compliant in California **on the consent
step itself**.

⭐ **read `define.boundary.when-consent-is-required-at-all` first.** every other brief here presumes
consent was required and asks what it must contain, how it is proved, or what it costs to lack it.
that brief answers the prior question — and its answer is sometimes *no*.

## the vocabulary gap

the single largest hazard here is that the colloquial word and the term of art do not mean the same.
a program built on the left column fails the right one.

| the colloquial word | the term of art | why the gap bites |
|---------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| "permission" / "opted in" | **prior express written consent** | oral or implied assent does not meet the telemarketing standard |
| "robocall" | **automatic telephone dialing system**, *or* **artificial or prerecorded voice** | two independent hooks; `Duguid` narrowed only the first |
| "unsubscribe" | **revocation of consent** | the legal duty carries a **deadline** the word does not imply |
| "the do-not-call list" | **`§ 227(c)`** | a **separate** private right of action, with its own damages and its own defense |
| "a text is not a call" | the FCC reads "call" to include text messages | ⚠️ and the Supreme Court **assumed** rather than decided this |
| "covered by general liability" | the policy's **exclusion** and **grant** language | a broker's summary is not the policy's words |
| "consent settles it" | consent answers `§ 227(b)`, not `§ 227(c)` | a perfect consent record is no defense to a call to a registered number |

## what this folder does NOT cover

named here so an absence is legible rather than mistaken for an answer:

- ⚠️ **state mini-TCPAs — NO LONGER ABSENT.** brief **8** now carries the five-state matrix
  (TX · IN · FL · CA · NY) on both the consent and the penalty dimension, with a verbatim cite or an
  explicit named null per cell. what brief 8 does **not** do is read **case law** — it is a
  statutory-text sweep, so how any of these provisions has been applied is unresearched. ⚠️ **and
  five is not all fifty**: any state outside the baseline remains uncovered, and whether these five
  are even the right five for this subject is a gap that brief names rather than closes
- **individual states named in briefs 1–7** — Florida's consent definition, its pre-suit `STOP`
  condition, a null result for a state analogue of the federal safe harbor — each is cited there for
  the one point it makes and **is not a survey of that state's law**. brief **8** is the survey
- ⬜ **a schema, a vendor, or a storage technology.** the properties a record must carry are traceable
  to the regulation and the case law; **the shape that carries them is a business decision, and no
  brief in this set states one**
- ⚠️ **time-of-day windows, caller-identification duties, and the internal do-not-call list
  obligation — NO LONGER ABSENT.** brief **7** now quotes `(c)(1)`'s 8 a.m./9 p.m. window, `(d)(4)`'s
  identification duty, and all six of `(d)`'s minimum standards. what brief 7 does **not** settle is
  the **residential/wireless boundary** — `(d)` is written to a *"residential telephone subscriber"*,
  and whether these duties reach a wireless number was not researched
- **how a court has construed `§ 227(c)(5)` "due care"** — the defense's text is quoted in brief 7;
  no decision that applies the standard was read
- **individual/officer exposure** — whether liability reaches past the corporate entity
- **email** — the same insurance exclusion names CAN-SPAM alongside the TCPA, so a parallel gap
  exists on that channel; it is out of scope here
