# define.what-the-2025-vacatur-did-and-did-not-do

## .what

in January 2025 the Eleventh Circuit vacated part of an FCC order about telephone-consent rules. the
popular shorthand — *"the FCC's consent rule was struck down"* — is wrong in several directions, and
each error points the same way: it overstates how much of the consent regime moved.

this brief separates **what the vacatur reached** from **what it left untouched**, on the primary
documents — and traces the **effective dates**, which decide when each rule actually bound anyone and
which no summary carries.

## .why

- the vacated text and the text that stands sit in the **same regulation**, `47 C.F.R. § 64.1200`, so
  a reader who takes the shorthand can conclude that the written-consent baseline fell with it
- the same misread has a twin at the Supreme Court: `Facebook v. Duguid` is popularly read as a
  narrower TCPA, and it expressly reserved two of the questions people cite it for
- **the dates are not obvious from the codified text.** the vacated rule was published in 2024 and
  never bound anyone; the revocation rule was published as *"delayed indefinitely"* and later did
  take effect. neither fact is visible in the regulation as it reads today

the opinion states was not before it.

## what the court actually held

`Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd. v. FCC`, No. 24-10277 (11th Cir. Jan. 24, 2025), verbatim:

> "we agree with IMC that the FCC exceeded its statutory authority under the TCPA because the 2023
> Order's new consent restrictions impermissibly conflict with the ordinary statutory meaning of
> 'prior express consent.' Accordingly, we grant IMC's petition for review, vacate Part III.D of the
> 2023 Order, and remand for further proceedings."

disposition: **"PETITION GRANTED; ORDER VACATED IN PART AND REMANDED."**

⭐ **footnote 1 is the whole point of this brief**, verbatim:

> "The 2012 Order is not at issue in this case."

the 2012 Order is the one that established that *"prior express consent"* means prior express
**written** consent for a telemarketing purpose. the vacatur removed restrictions the 2023 Order
**added on top** of that baseline. it did not disturb the baseline.

**source**: [IMC v. FCC, No. 24-10277](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202410277.pdf)

---

## the regulation itself corroborates the footnote

the point does not rest on an inference from the opinion. the codified text, current as of
**7/28/2026**, still conditions telemarketing on written consent — `47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(2)`:

> "No person or entity may initiate, or cause to be initiated, any telephone call that includes or
> introduces an advertisement or constitutes telemarketing, using an automatic telephone dialing
> system or an artificial or prerecorded voice, to any of the lines or telephone numbers described in
> paragraphs (a)(1)(i) through (iii) of this section, other than a call made with the prior express
> written consent of the called party…"

and the definition at `(f)(9)` reads as it did before the 2023 amendment:

> "An agreement, in writing, bearing the signature of the person called that clearly authorizes the
> seller to deliver or cause to be delivered to the person called advertisements or telemarketing
> messages using an automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice, and the
> telephone number to which the signatory authorizes such advertisements or telemarketing messages to
> be delivered."

that this is the **prior** version, restored rather than never-changed, is stated by the FCC itself —
see the conforming order below.

**source**: [47 C.F.R. § 64.1200 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200)

## ⭐ the vacated rule never bound anyone — and not for the reason the dates suggest

on **August 29, 2025** the FCC published a ministerial order that conforms its rules to the court's
decision. paragraph 1 of its Synopsis states the sequence, verbatim:

> "Pursuant to F. R. App. P. 41(b), the court issued its mandate on **April 30, 2025**, which
> vacated, **as of that date**, the rule change for the first full paragraph of § 64.1200(f)(9) that
> the Commission adopted in 2023. This action reinstates in the Commission's rules the prior version
> of § 64.1200(f)(9). **Prior to the court's mandate, the Commission had postponed the effective date
> of the revised rule and the revised rule had not gone into effect.**"

two independent points sit in that passage, and each defeats a natural but wrong inference.

**① a vacatur bites on the mandate, not on the opinion.** the opinion issued January 24, 2025; the
mandate issued April 30, 2025, and the FCC records the vacatur as effective *"as of that date"*. a
reader who dates the vacatur from the opinion is off by roughly three months — a window that matters
to anyone who has to say what the rule was on a given day.

**② the amended rule never took effect — because the Commission postponed it**, not because any date
arithmetic ran out. this deserves precision, because the published effective date invited the
opposite conclusion. on **November 6, 2024** the FCC announced OMB approval and set the compliance
date, verbatim:

> "Compliance with 47 CFR 64.1200(f)(9) is required as of January 27, 2025."

taken alone, that line plus the January 24 opinion looks like a three-day near-miss. it was not. the
Commission postponed the date, and the FCC's own conforming order is the authority for the outcome:
**the revised rule had not gone into effect.**

| date | event |
|------|-------|
| 2024-01-26 | 89 FR 5098 — the 2023 Order published, with a delayed effective date for the `(f)(9)` amendment |
| 2024-03-12 | 89 FR 17762 — a correction: the revision reaches **`(f)(9)(i)`**, not the whole of `(f)(9)` |
| 2024-11-06 | 89 FR 87983 — OMB approval announced; compliance *"required as of January 27, 2025"* |
| — | the Commission **postpones** that effective date |
| 2025-01-24 | the Eleventh Circuit opinion issues |
| **2025-04-30** | **the mandate issues under F.R.A.P. 41(b); the rule change is vacated as of this date** |
| 2025-08-29 | 90 FR 42138 — the FCC conforms its rules and **reinstates the prior version** of `(f)(9)` |

so the popular frame implies a before-and-after: a stricter rule that operated and was then removed.
there was no "before" — the amended text never governed a call.

[89 FR 87983 — OMB approval](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/11/06/2024-24908/rules-and-regulations-implementing-the-telephone-consumer-protection-act-of-1991) ·

## ⭐ the revocation duty runs on its own clock — in fact on two of them

a **separate** FCC order — not the one that was vacated — created the duty to honor a revocation of
consent. it is frequently mentioned alongside the vacatur and is unrelated to it, and its effective
date follows a pattern worth understanding on its own.

`89 FR 15756` (Mar. 5, 2024) is the Report and Order. its **SUMMARY**, verbatim:

> "the Commission adopts rules to make clear that revocation of consent can be made in any reasonable
> manner, require that callers honor do-not-call and consent revocation requests within a reasonable
> time not to exceed ten business days of receipt, and limit text senders to a one-time text message
> confirming a consumer's request…"

⚠️ but its **DATES** section, read alone, says the opposite of what most readers assume:

> "Amendatory instruction 2 (adding 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(12)) is effective April 4, 2024, and amendatory
> instruction 3 (revising 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(9)(i)(F) and (d)(3) and adding 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10) and
> (11)) is delayed indefinitely. The Federal Communications Commission will publish a document…"

**the promised document exists.** on **October 11, 2024** the FCC published a *"Final rule;
announcement of effective dates"* at `89 FR 82518`, verbatim:

> "The effective date for the amendments to 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(9)(i)(F) and (d)(3) and the addition of
> 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10) and (11), published March 5, 2024, at 89 FR 15756 is **April 11, 2025**."

with the mechanism stated:

> "This document announces that the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) approved the information
> collection requirements in 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(9)(i)(F), (a)(10) and (11), and (d)(3) on September
> 26, 2024. In the TCPA Consent Order, the Commission concluded that the appropriate timeframe for
> implementation of these amended rules is six months following publication in the Federal Register
> of notice that OMB has completed any required review of the adopted rules."

**so the ten-business-day revocation duty at `47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a)(10)` took effect April 11,
2025.** it is live, and the eCFR text is correct. a reader who stops at the adopting order's DATES
line would conclude the opposite.

⚠️ **the "delayed indefinitely" pattern recurs**, so it is worth recognition rather than a one-off:
the same construction appears in a March 2025 FCC order for `§ 64.1200(o)`, which says the amendments
*"will become effective following publication of a document in the Federal Register announcing
approval of the information collection and the relevant effective date."* **for any FCC rule marked
delayed indefinitely, the operative date lives in a later, separate document** — and that later
document earns no eCFR source credit, because it amends no text.

⚠️ **and the duty then split into two clocks.** the account above was complete as of the October 2024
announcement. it is not complete now. the FCC's **December 5, 2025** further notice, `2025-22063`,
records a later Bureau action at ¶79, verbatim:

> "The Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau delayed until **April 11, 2026** implementation of
> this rule "to the extent that it requires callers to treat a request to revoke consent made by a
> called party in response to one type of message as applicable to all future robocalls and robotexts
> from that caller on unrelated matters.""

so `(a)(10)` did take effect on April 11, 2025 — but **its cross-campaign reach did not**. for the
twelve months that followed, a revocation sent in reply to one campaign did not, as an enforced rule,
stop that same sender's unrelated campaigns. that component became operative **April 11, 2026**, and
is live today. a brief that quotes only the 2025 date describes a duty narrower than the one now in
force, and described it too broadly for the year in between.

⭐ **and the same rule is under active proposal to change.** the December 2025 notice does not merely
report the delay — it proposes to amend `(a)(10)`, verbatim at ¶81:

> "We also propose to amend § 64.1200(a)(10). For example, commenters in the Delete Proceeding asked
> us to permit callers to designate the exclusive means by which consumers may revoke prior express
> consent rather than requiring callers to honor all revocation requests made using "reasonable
> means.""

**this is a live posture, not a settled one.** a reader who builds a revocation process against the
"any reasonable means" standard should know the Commission has an open proposal to let senders name
an exclusive channel instead. 🔶 whether that proposal is adopted, and in what form, is **undecided**
— a docket is not a rule, and no part of it changes what `(a)(10)` requires today.

⚠️ **provenance.** 🔭 **observed**: the two passages above, read off the Federal Register render of
`2025-22063`. 🔶 **not observed**: the Bureau's underlying delay order itself — a Federal Register
search across three probes did not surface it as a separate FR document, so the delay is cited here
from the Commission's own account of its Bureau's action rather than from that order's own text.

**sources**: [89 FR 82518 — announcement of effective dates](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/10/11/2024-23605/strengthening-the-ability-of-consumers-to-stop-robocalls) ·
[89 FR 15756 — Report and Order](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/05/2024-04587/strengthening-the-ability-of-consumers-to-stop-robocalls) ·
[90 FR 13425 — the same pattern for § 64.1200(o)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/24/2025-04811/advanced-methods-to-target-and-eliminate-unlawful-robocalls) ·
[2025-22063 — Advanced Methods To Target and Eliminate Robocalls (Dec. 5, 2025), ¶¶ 79–83](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/05/2025-22063/advanced-methods-to-target-and-eliminate-robocalls)

## ⚠️ a documents trap: the order and the notice share a title

`89 FR 15802` carries the **same title** as `89 FR 15756`, sits one docket number away, and was
published the same day. it is a **Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking** — it *seeks comment*; it
does not adopt a rule. probes of its full text for `"ten business days"` and `"any reasonable method"`
return **no match**.

a citation to `89 FR 15802` as the source of the revocation duty would attribute an obligation that
binds to a request for comment. the two are distinguishable only by a read of the document, not by
title.

**source**: [89 FR 15802 (Federal Register)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/05/2024-04586/strengthening-the-ability-of-consumers-to-stop-robocalls)

## the twin misread at the Supreme Court

the same shape appears in the other most-cited authority in this area. `Facebook, Inc. v. Duguid`,
No. 19–511 (Apr. 1, 2021), is popularly read as *"the Supreme Court narrowed the TCPA."* it narrowed
one definition and **expressly reserved** two questions people cite it for.

**what it held** (slip op. at 12):

> "We hold that a necessary feature of an autodialer under §227(a)(1)(A) is the capacity to use a
> random or sequential number generator to either store or produce phone numbers to be called."

**reservation 1 — the Court did not decide that the TCPA reaches text messages** (footnote 2, slip
op. at 3):

> "Neither party disputes that the TCPA's prohibition also extends to sending unsolicited text
> messages. See *Campbell-Ewald Co.* v. *Gomez*, 577 U. S. 153, 156 (2016). We therefore assume that
> it does without considering or resolving that issue."

**reservation 2 — it does not touch the prerecorded-voice prohibition** (slip op. at 12):

> "The statute separately prohibits calls using 'an artificial or prerecorded voice' to various types
> of phone lines, including home phones and cell phones, unless an exception applies. See 47 U. S. C.
> §§227(b)(1)(A) and (B). Our decision does not affect that prohibition."

`47 U.S.C. § 227(b)(1)(A)` reaches a call made *"using any automatic telephone dialing system **or**
an artificial or prerecorded voice"* — two independent hooks joined by "or". `Duguid` construed the
first. the second stands unchanged, and the equipment used is irrelevant to it.

**sources**: [Duguid slip opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-511_p86b.pdf) ·
[47 U.S.C. § 227](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title47-section227&num=0&edition=prelim)

## where the two frames diverge, point by point

| the popular frame | what the documents say |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| "the FCC's consent rule was struck down" | one **part** of one **2023** order was vacated; the 2012 written-consent baseline was "not at issue" |
| "written consent is no longer required" | `§ 64.1200(a)(2)`, `(a)(3)` and `(f)(9)` still require it in text current 7/28/2026 |
| "the stricter rule operated, then was removed" | *"the revised rule had not gone into effect"* — the FCC postponed it before the mandate |
| "the rule was struck down in January 2025" | the **mandate** issued April 30, 2025, and the FCC records the vacatur *"as of that date"* |
| "*Duguid* narrowed the TCPA" | it narrowed the **autodialer** hook; the prerecorded-voice hook is expressly unaffected |
| "*Duguid* settles that texts are covered" | the Court **assumed** it "without considering or resolving that issue" |
| "the revocation rule was delayed indefinitely" | a later document set its effective date: **April 11, 2025** |
| "so the revocation duty has been fully live since April 2025" | its **cross-campaign** reach was delayed a further year, to **April 11, 2026** |
| "the revocation standard is settled" | the FCC has an **open proposal** (Dec. 2025) to let senders name an exclusive revocation channel |

## the honest gap

- **the postponement document itself was not located.** the FCC's conforming order states that the
  Commission had postponed the `(f)(9)` effective date before the mandate; the separate Federal
  Register document that effected that postponement was not found. the **outcome** is attested by the
  FCC; the instrument is not in hand.
- **the FCC's substantive post-remand action was not located.** the Eleventh Circuit remanded "for
  further proceedings"; the August 2025 order is expressly *"ministerial"* and implements the mandate
  only. whether the Commission has since re-proposed anything on Part III.D is **unresearched here**.
- **no circuit-split analysis was run.** a vacatur by one court of appeals is not automatically the
  rule in every circuit; how other circuits treat the vacated restrictions is not addressed.
- **the `Duguid` pin cite is partial.** the slip prints *"Cite as: 592 U. S. \_\_\_\_ (2021)"* — the
  volume is confirmed, the page is blank in the slip. cite the docket number and date until the bound
  page is checked.
- **the FCC orders were read at the Federal Register render**, not at the Commission's own release
  documents. the `DATES`, `SUMMARY` and `Synopsis` text quoted here is the Federal Register's
  published text; each Federal Register document links a matched PDF on govinfo.gov that was not
  pulled.
- **state law is not addressed here — and ⭐ that is a stronger statement now than when it was
  written.** the vacatur moved a federal rule; a state definition is untouched by an Eleventh Circuit
  decision on an FCC order. **the five-state matrix now exists** in `ref.mini-tcpa-five-state`, and
  it shows that **California does not turn on a written-consent definition at all** — `§ 2874`
  requires a live announcement on the call. (Indiana adds a live-operator route too, but as an
  **alternative** to ordinary consent, so a written record still reaches Indiana.) ⚠️ **so a reader
  who concluded "the consent rules loosened in 2025" has a claim that is contested even federally and
  is simply inapplicable in California**, whose requirement this decision could not have moved in
  either direction. **a sender's
  actual standard is the strictest that applies in a served state, which may not be the federal one.**

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what did the vacatur reach? | Part III.D of the FCC's **2023** Order — the one-to-one and "logically and topically associated" restrictions, carried by the amendment to the first full paragraph of `§ 64.1200(f)(9)` |
| did it remove the written-consent requirement? | no — footnote 1: *"The 2012 Order is not at issue in this case."* |
| does the regulation still require written consent? | yes — `§ 64.1200(a)(2)`, `(a)(3)`, `(f)(9)`, current 7/28/2026 |
| when did the vacatur take effect? | on the **mandate**, **April 30, 2025** — not on the January 24, 2025 opinion |
| did the vacated amendment ever operate? | no — *"the revised rule had not gone into effect"*; the FCC had postponed it |
| is the ten-business-day revocation duty live? | **yes — effective April 11, 2025**, per the FCC's announcement at 89 FR 82518 |
| does one revocation stop that sender's *unrelated* campaigns? | **yes, but only since April 11, 2026** — the Bureau delayed that component a further year |
| is the revocation standard stable? | **no** — the FCC proposed in Dec. 2025 to amend `(a)(10)`; the docket is open |
| did *Duguid* decide that the TCPA covers texts? | no — it **assumed** it "without considering or resolving that issue" |
| did *Duguid* narrow the prerecorded-voice prohibition? | no — *"Our decision does not affect that prohibition."* |
