# ref.dnc-suppression-program

## .what

the **do-not-call** track's own requirements, quoted from the regulation and the statute that create
them — the program `47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(d)` calls *"minimum standards"*, the registry duty at `(c)`,
and the two **separate** safe harbours that sit over them.

this is the half of phone-contact liability that **consent does not answer**. every other brief in
this folder asks what consent must be, how it is proved, or what it costs to lack it. `§ 227(c)`
asks none of those questions.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate. this brief is **federal
> only** — states impose their own suppression duties, and those are not addressed here.

---

## .why

⭐ **a flawless consent record is no defense on this track, and a flawless program is no defense on
the other.** the two tracks have different triggers, different defenses, and different records — and
the statute says so in its own scope language. `47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(1)` directs the Commission to
protect

> "residential telephone subscribers' privacy rights to avoid receiving telephone solicitations to
> which they object"

which is a duty about **objections and list practices**, not about agreement. so a program built
entirely around consent capture can be complete, correct, current — and lose.

---

## the threshold — one call is not enough

`47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5)` gives the private right to

> "A person who has received **more than one telephone call within any 12-month period** by or on
> behalf of the same entity in violation of the regulations prescribed under this subsection"

who may, *"if otherwise permitted by the laws or rules of court of a State bring in an appropriate
court of that State"*, seek an injunction, *"actual monetary loss … or to receive up to $500 in
damages for each such violation, whichever is greater"*, or both.

⭐ **two features of that sentence are easy to miss.** the right has a **two-call threshold** — a
single call does not open it — and it is framed as an action **in state court**, conditioned on that
state's own laws or rules of court. neither feature has a counterpart on the `§ 227(b)` track.

and the multiplier is discretionary:

> "If the court finds that the defendant willfully or knowingly violated the regulations prescribed
> under this subsection, the court may, in its discretion, increase the amount of the award to an
> amount equal to not more than 3 times the amount available under subparagraph (B)"

---

## the defense the statute names

`§ 227(c)(5)` closes with the sentence this research lost once to a summarizer and recovered only
from the source:

> "It shall be an affirmative defense in any action brought under this paragraph that the defendant
> has established and implemented, **with due care**, reasonable practices and procedures to
> effectively prevent telephone solicitations in violation of the regulations prescribed under this
> subsection."

⭐ **note what it does not contain: any consent element at all.** and note the two verbs — the
practices must be **established** *and* **implemented**, with due care. a written program that was
never run does not satisfy a sentence with that shape.

⚠️ **it is scoped to *"any action brought under this paragraph."*** it is not a defense to a
`§ 227(b)` autodialer or prerecorded-voice claim.

---

## the program — `§ 64.1200(d)`'s six minimum standards

the paragraph's own frame: a person may not make the calls it covers

> "unless such person or entity has instituted procedures for maintaining a list of persons who
> request not to receive such calls made by or on behalf of that person or entity. The procedures
> instituted must meet the following **minimum standards**"

| # | standard | what the text requires |
|---|----------|------------------------|
| **1** | **written policy** | *"must have a written policy, **available upon demand**, for maintaining a do-not-call list"* |
| **2** | **training of personnel** | personnel *"must be informed and trained in the existence and use of the do-not-call list"* |
| **3** | **recording, disclosure** | record the request and list the number *"at the time the request is made"*; honor within *"ten (10) business days"* |
| **4** | **identification of callers** | give *"the name of the individual caller"*, the entity's name, and a contact number or address — and it *"may not be a 900 number"* |
| **5** | ⭐ **affiliated persons** | a request applies to *"the particular entity making the call"* and **not** to affiliates, with one exception (below) |
| **6** | **maintenance** | *"A do-not-call request must be honored for **5 years** from the time the request is made."* |

### ⭐ three provisions inside those standards that carry more weight than their titles suggest

**(a) the delegation trap, stated twice.** `(d)(3)` says:

> "If such requests are recorded or maintained by a party other than the person or entity on whose
> behalf the call is made, the person or entity on whose behalf the call is made **will be liable**
> for any failures to honor the do-not-call request."

⭐ **and the same paragraph adds a duty in the other direction** — a duty this research had not
previously captured: an entity

> "must obtain a consumer's **prior express permission to share or forward** the consumer's request
> not to be called to a party other than the person or entity on whose behalf a call is made or an
> affiliated entity."

so a suppression request cannot simply be broadcast to partners. **the request itself is
consent-gated for onward transfer**, which is a consent duty *inside* the non-consent track.

**(b) `(d)(5)` — a suppression request does not reach affiliates by default.** the text:

> "In the absence of a specific request by the subscriber to the contrary, a residential subscriber's
> do-not-call request shall apply to the particular entity making the call (or on whose behalf a call
> is made), and will not apply to affiliated entities **unless the consumer reasonably would expect
> them to be included** given the identification of the caller and (for telemarketing calls) the
> product being advertised."

⚠️ **that exception is a reasonableness test tied to `(d)(4)`'s identification duty** — how the
caller identified itself helps decide which entities the request reached. the two standards are
coupled, and neither reads that way on its own.

**(c) two different clocks.** a registry registration under `(c)(2)` *"must be honored indefinitely,
or until the registration is cancelled by the consumer or the telephone number is removed by the
database administrator."* an internal `(d)(6)` request must be honored **5 years**. the same
consumer, two lists, two durations.

---

## ⭐ the two safe harbours are NOT the same, and one is narrower than it looks

this is the point most likely to be misread, because both are commonly called "the safe harbor".

| | `§ 64.1200(c)(2)(i)` — the regulatory one | `§ 227(c)(5)` — the statutory one |
|---|---|---|
| **source** | the FCC's rule | the Act |
| **shape** | five enumerated standards, plus two alternatives | one sentence: *"reasonable practices and procedures"*, with due care |
| **what it answers** | liability for a call to a **registered** number | *"any action brought under this paragraph"* |
| **enumerated?** | yes — see below | ⛔ **no** — the statute does not list what qualifies |

the regulatory version requires the caller to *"demonstrate that the violation is the result of
error"* and that **as part of its routine business practice** it meets five standards:

| # | standard | the operative requirement |
|---|----------|---------------------------|
| A | **written procedures** | *"established and implemented written procedures to comply with the national do-not-call rules"* |
| B | **training of personnel** | trained its own people *"and any entity assisting in its compliance"* |
| C | **recording** | *"maintained and recorded a list of telephone numbers that the seller may not contact"* |
| D | **accessing the national do-not-call database** | a registry version obtained *"no more than **31 days** prior to the date any call is made"*, and *"maintains records documenting this process"* |
| E | **purchasing the national do-not-call database** | purchase access from the administrator, and no cost-share arrangement that divides access costs among client sellers |

⭐ **and `(c)(2)` names two alternatives to the safe harbor that are not a program at all**: `(ii)`
the subscriber's *"prior express invitation or permission"* evidenced by *"a signed, written
agreement … [that] includes the telephone number to which the calls may be placed"*, or `(iii)` where
*"the telemarketer making the call has a **personal relationship** with the recipient."*

### ⚠️ the scope limit a court stated plainly

`United States v. Dish Network` (C.D. Ill. 2017) records how narrowly the regulatory harbour reaches:

> "The TSR safe harbor applied to Internal List Calls and Registry Calls. **The FCC Rule safe harbor
> only applied to Registry Calls.** Neither safe harbor provision applied to …"

⛔ **so a failure of the *internal* list has no FCC regulatory safe harbor over it.** the statutory
`§ 227(c)(5)` defense remains available, but the enumerated five-standard shelter does not extend
there. a program documented against the `(c)(2)(i)` list has evidence for one kind of call.

## the adjacent duties on the same track

these are independent violations, each unaffected by consent:

- ⭐ **time of day.** `(c)(1)` bars a solicitation to a residential subscriber *"before the hour of
  8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. (**local time at the called party's location**)"* — the called party's clock,
  not the caller's.
- **caller identification.** `(d)(4)`, above.
- **the revocation clock reaches this track.** `(a)(10)`'s ten-business-day duty applies to calls
  under *"paragraphs (a)(1) through (3) and **(c)(2)**"*, so it is not confined to the autodialer
  regime. it became effective **2025-04-11** per `89 FR 82518`, after `89 FR 15756` created it and
  delayed it.

## ⭐ what `(d)` does NOT reach — the scope nobody states

`(d)` opens by cover of calls *"to a **residential telephone subscriber**"*, and `§ 227(c)(1)`
frames the whole subsection around *"residential telephone subscribers' privacy rights"*.

⚠️ **so the internal-DNC program duty, as written, is addressed to residential subscribers** — a
scope limit that the shipped briefs' frame of `§ 227(c)` as simply "the do-not-call track" does not
convey. ⛔ **but do not read the inverse from this brief**: whether, and how, these duties reach a
wireless number is a question of how the FCC and courts have treated wireless subscribers as
residential, and **no source on that point was read here**. the text is quoted; the boundary is
flagged, not drawn.

## the honest gap

- ⛔ **the residential/wireless boundary is named and NOT answered**, per the paragraph above. it is
  the largest open question in this brief, because it decides who the `(d)` program must cover.
- ⚠️ **`(d)(7)` was requested and did not exist** in the current text — the enumeration ends at
  `(d)(6)`. recorded as a null result rather than as silence, so a later reader does not assume a
  seventh standard was overlooked.
- ⭐ **the established-business-relationship definition captured at `(f)(6)` is expressly
  fax-scoped** — it opens *"for purposes of paragraph (a)(4) of this section on the sending of
  facsimile advertisements"*. but `(f)(15)`'s definition of *telephone solicitation* excludes a call
  *"to any person with whom the caller has an established business relationship"*. **so what EBR
  means for a voice solicitation is not settled by the definition this capture returned**, and no
  other definition was located.
- **no case law on what satisfies the `§ 227(c)(5)` "due care" standard was read.** `Dish` is cited
  for the safe harbours' scope, not as a construction of due care.
- ⬜ **whether a program artifact must be ADMISSIBLE, not merely extant, is unresearched here.** the
  statute's two verbs — *established* **and** *implemented* — imply a record that proves the second,
  and a record that cannot be put into evidence proves none of it. **what a court has required of
  such a record (business-record certification, custodian testimony, contemporaneity) was not
  established.** the gap cuts both ways and is named rather than half-answered.
- 🔶 **the state analogue of this defense is UNCONSTRUED, not absent — and the distinction has now
  been demonstrated.** a CourtListener sweep of TX / CA / NY / IN returned **NO-CONSTRUCTION-FOUND**
  in each (recorded in `ref.tcpa-exposure-tracks`). ⭐ **a later statutory-text sweep then found that
  Indiana carries one in statute** — `Ind. Code § 24-4.7-5-2` conditions its penalty on a defendant
  who cannot show it *"did not know; and (2) in the exercise of reasonable care could not have
  known"*. so the null was a null about **case law**, exactly as it said, and the provision itself
  was there the whole time. **whether TX, CA, or NY carry analogues is still unsearched at the text
  level.** see `ref.mini-tcpa-five-state`.
- 🔶 **state suppression duties are now partly addressed, elsewhere.** `ref.mini-tcpa-five-state`
  records that **New York** frames its regime as a registry with a 31-day lookback and an
  established-business-relationship exception, over a definition that reaches *"any telemarketing
  sales call or electronic messaging text"*. ⬜ **what it does not do is compare the six federal
  minimum standards below against each state's own list duties** — that per-standard map is
  unwritten, and the absence of a duty in that brief is an absence of search.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| does consent answer this track? | ⭐ **no** — `§ 227(c)(5)`'s defense has **no consent element**, and the subsection is framed around objections, not agreement |
| is one call actionable? | **no** — the right needs *"more than one telephone call within any 12-month period"* |
| where does the claim run? | in **state court**, *"if otherwise permitted by the laws or rules of court of a State"* |
| what does the statutory defense require? | practices *"established **and** implemented, with due care"* — a written program never run does not match the text |
| are the two safe harbours the same? | ⭐ **no** — and per `Dish`, *"The FCC Rule safe harbor only applied to Registry Calls"*, so an internal-list failure has no enumerated shelter |
| how fresh must the registry copy be? | *"no more than **31 days** prior to the date any call is made"* |
| how long does a request last? | registry: *"indefinitely"* until cancelled or removed. internal: **5 years** |
| does a request reach affiliates? | ⭐ **not by default** — only where *"the consumer reasonably would expect them to be included"*, judged against how the caller identified itself |
| may we forward a suppression request to a partner? | ⚠️ **only with the consumer's prior express permission** — a consent duty inside the non-consent track |
| who is liable if a vendor keeps the list? | *"the person or entity on whose behalf the call is made **will be liable**"* |
| when may a solicitation be placed? | not before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. — **local time at the called party's location** |
