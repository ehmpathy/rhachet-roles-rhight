# define.boundary.spend-only-shuts-three-regimes

## .what

a **spend-only closed-loop credit** — redeemable only for the issuer's own goods or services, and
**never** cashable, **never** transferable, **never** refundable — sits **outside three
financial-services regimes at once**: money transmission, stored value, and escheat. **cashability** is
the single trait that would drop it into any of the three.

## .why

- each regime has an exclusion or an absent element that a spend-only credit satisfies.
- all three turn on the **same trait** — whether the credit can become cash or move to another person.
  hold that trait at "no" and the three exclusions hold together.
- this is a map of where the legal lines sit, not a design recommendation: it lays each regime's text
  against a spend-only credit.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

---

## door 1 — money transmission stays shut (no transmit element)

money transmission is built on **two conjunctive elements** — an acceptance from one person **and** a
transmission to another. a spend-only merchant credit never closes the second element:

> "acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and the
> transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another location or
> person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(i)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

the federal registration statute reaches the same conduit — a business in the **transmission** of value
that substitutes for currency, not an issuer of a merchant credit that never leaves its own loop:

> "any other person who engages as a business in the transmission of currency, funds, or value that
> substitutes for currency"

**source**: [31 U.S.C. § 5330(d)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5330)

florida frames the covered actor as an **intermediary** that receives value to move it from one person
to another — again, the transmit shape a spend-only credit never takes:

> "receives currency, monetary value, a payment instrument, or virtual currency for the purpose of
> acting as an intermediary to transmit currency, monetary value, a payment instrument, or virtual
> currency from one person to another location or person by any means"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.103(24)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.103)

a practitioner memo underlines why the cashable case is the dangerous one: the moment value is issued as
a retrievable balance with no identified payee, the agent-of-payee shield falls away —

> "the issuance of stored value is not exempt 'because when stored value is issued, no payee is
> identified and therefore, there is no immediate obligation to deliver funds to a specific person.'"

**source**: [Cooley — the California agent-of-a-payee exemption, final rules](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2021/2021-06-28-california-rulemaking-agent-of-a-payee-exemption-money-transmission-licensing)

## door 2 — stored value stays shut (closed-loop + loyalty exclusion)

indiana removes closed-loop value **and** loyalty/rewards/promotional value from the definition of
stored value itself. the definition:

> "monetary value representing a claim, against the issuer, that is evidenced by an electronic or digital
> record and that is intended and accepted for use as a means of redemption for money or monetary value,
> or payment for goods or services."

and the term expressly **does not include**:

> "a payment instrument"

> "closed loop stored value"

> "stored value not sold to the public but issued and distributed as part of a loyalty, rewards, or
> promotional program"

**source**: [Ind. Code § 28-8-4.1-201](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-28-financial-institutions/in-code-sect-28-8-4-1-201/)

a spend-only, promotional credit hits **two** of those three exclusions: it is closed-loop, and it is
issued as part of a rewards/promotional program. the federal rule defines the closed-loop shape the same
way — value usable only at a **defined merchant**:

> "Prepaid access to funds or the value of funds that can be used only for goods or services in
> transactions involving a defined merchant or location (or set of locations), such as a specific
> retailer or retail chain, a college campus, or a subway system"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(kkk)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

## door 3 — escheat stays shut (RUUPA loyalty-card exclusion)

a balance redeemable **only** for goods, services, or a discount — and **not** monetized — is a loyalty
card, which RUUPA excludes from unclaimed property. the D.C. enactment states it verbatim:

> "A record given without direct monetary consideration under an award, reward, benefit, loyalty,
> incentive, rebate, or promotional program that may be used or redeemed only to obtain goods or services
> or a discount on goods or services. The term does not include a record that may be redeemed for money
> or otherwise monetized by the issuer."

**source**: [D.C. Code § 41-151.02](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/41-151.02)

indiana reaches the same result by an exclusion of a loyalty card from the very definition of
"property":

> "The term does not include property held in a plan described in Section 529A of the Internal Revenue
> Code, game related digital content, a financial organization loyalty program, a loyalty card, or an
> in-store credit for returned merchandise."

with the same monetizable carve-back on the loyalty-card definition:

> "The term does not include a record that may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the
> issuer."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 32-34-1.5-3](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-32-property/in-code-sect-32-34-1-5-3/)

florida reaches the loyalty result through its gift-certificate statute — a certificate handed over as
part of a promotional program, with no separate charge, is treated as loyalty/promotional, not a
purchased certificate:

> "provided in conjunction with a loyalty or promotional program when the recipient does not pay a
> separate identifiable charge for the certificate"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.95(2)(a)](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

## the through-line

each door stays shut **only** while the credit is spend-only. the moment the credit becomes
**cashable**, three things happen at once:

- the transmit element can close (a retrievable balance can move to another person),
- the stored-value exclusions fall away (a cash-out credit is not goods-or-services-only), and
- the loyalty-card escheat exclusion breaks (a record "redeemed for money or otherwise monetized").

so cashability is the single lever that moves a credit across all three lines together.

## the redline

| safe side (outside all three regimes) | the line | unsafe side (inside one or more) |
|----------------------------------------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| redeemable **only** for the issuer's own goods/services | "cashable?" | redeemable for **money** → transmit element + monetized escheat |
| usable **only** within the issuer's own defined loop | "closed-loop?" | usable at **third parties** / general-purpose → stored value / prepaid access |
| **non-transferable** — cannot move person-to-person | "transferable?" | **transferable** → the transmit element can close |

**the separator:** can the credit become **cash**, leave the **issuer's own loop**, or move to **another
person**? if no on all three, it sits outside money transmission, stored value, and escheat together.

## the honest gap

- **no case law on this exact synthesis.** no court has held that a spend-only credit escapes all three
  regimes at once. the read is text-level — the two-element transmitter test, the closed-loop and
  loyalty/rewards stored-value exclusions, and the RUUPA loyalty-card escheat exclusion — strong, but
  counsel-confirmable.
- **florida's stored-value silence.** florida's stored-value definition carries no express loyalty
  carve-out, so the florida shield leans harder on a strictly closed-loop, non-cashable, non-transferable
  credit plus the two-element transmitter test.
- **per-state confirmation.** the stored-value and escheat mechanics differ by state, so a per-state
  check is required before reliance.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what keeps a credit out of money transmission? | no transmit element — it never moves to another person |
| what keeps it out of stored value? | closed-loop + issued under a loyalty/rewards/promotional program |
| what keeps it out of escheat? | redeemable only for goods/services, never monetized (loyalty-card exclusion) |
| the single lever across all three? | cashability — hold it at "no" and all three exclusions hold |
