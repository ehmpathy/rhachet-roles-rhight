# hazard.cash-redeemed-reward-breaks-loyalty-escheat

## .what

a loyalty / reward record escapes unclaimed-property (escheat) law **only** while it is redeemable
**solely** for goods, services, or an in-transaction discount. the moment the issuer lets the holder
redeem it for **cash** — a **cash rebate**, a cash-out, any money in the holder's hand — the record is
**monetized**, falls **out** of the loyalty-card escheat exclusion, and its **dormant balances become
escheatable**.

the trap: the **same** cash rebate is **outside** tax law (a purchase-price adjustment, not income) and
**outside** money-transmission law (a self-funded price adjustment on the holder's own purchase, not a
third-party conduit). it clears two regimes and trips the third. the inference "the rebate is a lawful
price adjustment" is correct for tax and MT and **wrong** for escheat.

## .why

- escheat's loyalty exclusion is a **possibility test on the instrument**: does the record "may be
  redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the issuer"? if yes, the exclusion never attaches — and
  it taints **every** dormant balance of that instrument, not only the balances actually cashed.
- a **cash rebate is money in the holder's hand.** economically it is a discount; in **form** it is a
  redemption **for money**, which is exactly the carve-back the exclusion names. "a discount on goods
  or services" in the exclusion means a price *lowered in the transaction*, not a cash payment equal to
  a discount.
- the three regimes read the **same act** through **different lenses** — so a clean read under tax and
  MT does not decide escheat. each must be checked on its own.

## the escheat trigger — a monetizable record loses the exclusion

RUUPA-style law excludes a loyalty / reward record from unclaimed property **only** if it is redeemable
solely for goods, services, or a discount — and **expressly excludes** a monetizable one. the D.C.
enactment states it verbatim:

> "A record given without direct monetary consideration under an award, reward, benefit, loyalty,
> incentive, rebate, or promotional program that may be used or redeemed only to obtain goods or
> services or a discount on goods or services. The term does not include a record that may be redeemed
> for money or otherwise monetized by the issuer."

**source**: [D.C. Code § 41-151.02](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/41-151.02)

indiana reaches the same result by excluding a loyalty card from the definition of "property," with the
same monetizable carve-back:

> "The term does not include a record that may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the
> issuer."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 32-34-1.5-3](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-32-property/in-code-sect-32-34-1-5-3/)

so a **cash-redeemable** reward is not an excluded loyalty record. its dormant balances escheat as
unclaimed property under the state dormancy period (e.g. credit-memo dormancy of 3 years in Indiana, 5
years in Florida):

**source**: [NAUPA credit-memos dormancy by state — unclaimed.org](https://unclaimed.org/property-type-credit-memos/)

florida routes the loyalty / promotional carve-out through its gift-certificate statute; a certificate
issued under a promotional program with no separate charge is treated as loyalty / promotional, and its
unclaimed-property treatment follows ch. 717:

> "provided in conjunction with a loyalty or promotional program when the recipient does not pay a
> separate identifiable charge for the certificate"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.95](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

## why tax and money-transmission do NOT flag the same rebate

### tax — a rebate on the buyer's own purchase is a price adjustment, not income

> "where a payment is made from a seller to a purchaser, and the purpose and intent of the parties is
> to reach an agreed upon selling price, the payment is properly viewed as an adjustment to the
> purchase price that reduces gross sales."

**source**: [IRS Rev. Rul. 2008-26](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-08-26.pdf)

so the cash rebate is **not** an accession to wealth to the buyer — no income, no 1099. clean for tax.
(contrast: a cash reward to a **non-buyer** is income under 26 U.S.C. § 61 — a different fact.)

**source**: [26 U.S.C. § 61](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/61)

### money-transmission — a self-funded price adjustment is not a conduit

money transmission needs **both** an acceptance from one person **and** a transmission to another (a
third party):

> "acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and the
> transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another location or
> person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(i)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

a rebate the issuer funds from its **own** revenue, paid to the buyer on the buyer's **own** purchase,
is the issuer's own promotional debt discharged as a price reduction — no third party's money is
accepted and transmitted. clean for MT.

**the divergence in one line:** tax and MT ask *whose money / what character*; escheat asks *can the
record be turned into money*. a self-funded rebate answers the first two safely and the third fatally.

## the divergence table

| regime | what it asks | cash-rebate verdict | why |
|--------|--------------|---------------------|-----|
| **tax** | income, or a price adjustment? | ✅ safe | rebate on the buyer's own purchase = purchase-price adjustment, not income |
| **money-transmission** | is the issuer a conduit for a third party's funds? | ✅ safe | self-funded price adjustment on the buyer's own purchase = own promo debt, no third-party transmit |
| **escheat / unclaimed-property** | can the record be redeemed for money / monetized? | ⛔ **trips** | a cash rebate monetizes the record → loyalty exclusion never attaches → dormant balances escheat |

## the redline

| safe side (exclusion holds) | the line | unsafe side (exclusion lost) |
|-----------------------------|----------|------------------------------|
| reward redeemed **only** for goods, services, or an **in-transaction discount** / wallet credit | "redeemed for money or otherwise monetized" | reward redeemed for **cash** — a cash rebate, cash-out, or any money in the holder's hand |

**the separator:** does redemption of the reward record put **money in the holder's hand**? if the
reward value lands only as an in-loop discount or credit, the record stays an excluded loyalty
instrument. if redemption can produce cash, the whole instrument is monetizable and its dormant
balances escheat.

## where the exclusion holds and where it breaks

- **an in-loop reward redemption keeps the exclusion** — where a reward saves as an on-invoice discount
  or a wallet credit, never as cash to the holder, the record stays an excluded loyalty instrument.
- **a cash rebate sourced OUTSIDE the reward instrument does not taint it** — a rebate keyed to a
  specific purchase ("spend $X, get $Y back"), booked on its own footing rather than as a redemption of
  a reward balance, leaves the reward record cash-free (exclusion intact); the standalone rebate carries
  only its own narrow uncashed-instrument escheat.
- **the possibility test reaches the whole instrument** — one cash-redemption path anywhere taints the
  entire reward instrument's exclusion, even balances never cashed.

## the honest gap

- **no case law** squarely tests whether a cash rebate that is economically a discount is a "discount
  on goods or services" (excluded) or a redemption "for money" (carve-back). the statutory text points
  to the carve-back (cash in hand = money), but this is counsel-confirmable.
- **per-state divergence** — the loyalty / monetizable line, the dormancy periods, and the
  gift-certificate route differ by state (Indiana excludes a loyalty card from "property"; Florida
  routes through its gift-certificate statute + ch. 717). confirm each state of operation.

**source**: [escheat — Cornell LII (Wex)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/escheat)

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| does a cash rebate break the loyalty escheat exclusion? | yes — it monetizes the record, which the exclusion's carve-back names |
| is that same cash rebate a tax problem? | no — a rebate on the buyer's own purchase is a price adjustment, not income |
| is it a money-transmission problem? | no — a self-funded price adjustment on the buyer's own purchase is not a third-party conduit |
| what taints the exclusion — the rebate paid, or the option to rebate? | the **option** — the possibility of cash redemption taints every dormant balance |
| where does the exclusion hold? | where a reward redeems only in-loop (discount / credit); a cash rebate lives outside the reward instrument |
