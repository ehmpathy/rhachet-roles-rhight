# rule.prefer.clear-conspicuous-disclosures

## .what

**PREFER** to disclose **both** (a) the reward/incentive itself **and** (b) any TTL/expiration on
points — **clearly and conspicuously** (unavoidable, not behind a hyperlink or a hover), at the point
each one matters.

two disclosures, two moments:

- **the incentive** — surface it on or near the reward, at the point the customer decides. an
  incentive that shapes a customer's choice with no disclosure is a material-connection violation.
- **the expiration** — surface the TTL in the program terms **and** where points are shown. a buried
  or absent expiration loses the loyalty/promotional carve-out that lets the point expire at all.

> **recommendation disclaimer.** this recommendation is offered to the best of our knowledge, based on the information found in research — it carries **no guarantee of success or outcome**, and it must be reviewed by a licensed professional (attorney / CPA / licensed advisor) against your own real scenario and current law before any reliance. it is groundwork for that review, not a substitute for it.

## .why

- an undisclosed incentive is an FTC **material-connection** violation (16 cfr § 255.5). silence is
  the violation, not the incentive.
- an undisclosed or buried **expiration** loses the loyalty/promotional carve-out that permits
  expiration at all — florida § 501.95 conditions that carve-out on the terms, and the CARD Act
  (15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1) governs the disclosure of expiration and fees.
- **clear-and-conspicuous** is a **defined** standard (16 cfr § 465.1): "easily noticeable (i.e.,
  difficult to miss)", unavoidable — a definition counsel can hold the customer terms against.

## what must be disclosed, and where

| what | must be disclosed | where it must surface | anchor |
|------|-------------------|-----------------------|--------|
| **the incentive** | that the reward/discount is a funded incentive that could shape the choice | on/near the reward, at the point the customer decides — unavoidable, not behind a link | 16 cfr § 255.5; § 465.1 |
| **the expiration (TTL)** | that points expire, and on what schedule | in the program terms **and** surfaced where points are shown | fla. stat. § 501.95; 15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1; 12 cfr § 1005.20 |
| **the earned-point TTL** | the same expiration terms — but here the disclosure is the **condition** that keeps the expiration lawful under the loyalty carve-out | in the program terms + at the point-balance view | fla. stat. § 501.95; 12 cfr § 1005.20 |

the third row is the load-critical one: for an **earned** point, the disclosed expiration is not just
good manners — it is the **condition** on which the loyalty carve-out (which is what lets the point
expire at all) rests. drop the disclosure and the expiration is no longer a carve-out expiration; it is
a bare expiration on an instrument that may then be judged against the strict purchased-card regime.

---

## the clear-and-conspicuous standard

the 2024 FTC rule **defines** clear-and-conspicuous, so it is not a matter of taste. verbatim, 16 cfr
§ 465.1:

> "means that a required disclosure is easily noticeable (i.e., difficult to miss) and easily
> understandable by ordinary consumers"

and, decisively for a screen where the customer decides:

> "the disclosure must be unavoidable. A disclosure is not clear and conspicuous if a consumer must
> take any action, such as clicking on a hyperlink or hovering over an icon, to see it."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.1 — clear-and-conspicuous definition](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.1)

the disclosure duty itself — for the incentive — comes from the material-connection rule. when an
incentive creates a tie the audience would not expect, that tie must be disclosed clearly and
conspicuously. verbatim, 16 cfr § 255.5:

> "When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product that
> might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not
> reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously."

**source**: [16 cfr § 255.5 — disclosure of material connections](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5)

florida supplies the state hook that makes a missed material disclosure actionable outright — an unfair
or deceptive act in trade is unlawful. verbatim, fla. stat. § 501.204(1):

> "Unfair methods of competition, unconscionable acts or practices, and unfair or deceptive acts or
> practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful."

**source**: [fla. stat. § 501.204 — florida deceptive and unfair trade practices act](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/501.204)

## the expiration disclosure keeps the loyalty carve-out

the reason to disclose the TTL is not only good UX — it is the **condition** that keeps the point's
expiration lawful. florida's gift-certificate statute permits an expiration date **only** when the
certificate rides a loyalty/promotional program the recipient paid no separate charge for. verbatim,
fla. stat. § 501.95:

> "a gift certificate may have an expiration date if it is provided to the recipient, or to a
> purchaser for transfer to the recipient, as part of a loyalty or promotional program when the
> recipient does not pay a separate identifiable charge for the certificate"

**source**: [fla. stat. § 501.95 — gift certificates and credit memos](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

the federal CARD Act governs how an expiration and any fee on a covered instrument must be surfaced —
the disclosure discipline the TTL rides on. verbatim, 15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1, the five-year floor a
**purchased** card cannot go below:

> "the expiration date is not earlier than 5 years after the date on which the gift certificate was
> issued"

and the statute conditions any fee on a disclosure — the terms must clearly and conspicuously state
that a fee may be charged, the amount, and how often, and:

> "the issuer or vendor of such certificate or card informs the purchaser of such charge or fee
> before such certificate or card is purchased"

**source**: [15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1 — general-use prepaid cards, gift certificates, and store gift cards](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1693l-1)

regulation e carries the CARD Act out and names the loyalty/award/promotional exclusion — but
conditions it on a **front-of-card** label, and holds every disclosure to the clear-and-conspicuous
bar. verbatim, 12 cfr § 1005.20:

> "A statement indicating that the card, code, or other device is issued for loyalty, award, or
> promotional purposes, which must be included on the front of the card, code, or other device."

and the standard every disclosure under the section must meet:

> "Disclosures made under this section must be clear and conspicuous."

**source**: [12 cfr § 1005.20 — requirements for gift cards and gift certificates](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.20)

**the through-line**: the loyalty carve-out is what lets an earned point expire — but every quoted
carve-out is **conditioned** on a disclosure (florida's "loyalty or promotional program" fact, reg e's
front-of-card label, the CARD Act's stated terms). disclose the TTL clearly and the expiration stays
lawful and enforceable; bury it and the expiration loses its ground.

## distinguish from the review-disclosure rule

this brief is about the **reward/incentive + TTL** disclosure — the **money side** of the program. the
**review-incentive** disclosure lives in the review briefs it cross-references. there is real overlap:
both rest on the § 255.5 material-connection standard and the § 465.1 clear-and-conspicuous definition.
say so, and do **not** duplicate the review analysis here.

one guardrail worth a pointer: a reward must never be conditioned on a **positive** review — that trips
the review rule independently of any disclosure. verbatim, 16 cfr § 465.4:

> "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to provide
> compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned expressly or by implication on, the
> writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, whether positive or
> negative, regarding the product, service, or business that is the subject of the review."

**source**: [16 cfr § 465.4 — incentive-tied review sentiment](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.4)

that clause belongs to the review rule; it is cited here only to mark the seam where the two rules
meet, not to re-derive it.

## the tradeoff

- **give up**: minimal — a disclosure costs UI real estate (a line on/near the reward, a note on the
  point-balance view, a clause in the terms).
- **gain**: the incentive stays lawful (no § 255.5 material-connection exposure, no § 501.204 hook),
  and the TTL stays **lawful** and **enforceable** (the loyalty carve-out that permits expiration
  survives). the disclosure is what turns a legally-shaky expiration into an enforceable one.

it is a small cost for a large one avoided — which is why the recommendation is strong even though it
is nitpick-severity by rule.

---

## severity

**prefer → nitpick.** clear-and-conspicuous disclosure is a strong recommendation, not framed here as
an absolute blocker — but the **benefit of compliance** is concrete and cheap to capture.

the **benefit**: with both disclosures in place, (a) the incentive carries no undisclosed
material-connection exposure under § 255.5 and no § 501.204 deceptive-act hook, and (b) the point's
expiration stays lawful and enforceable because the loyalty carve-out it rides is
**conditioned** on the terms be surfaced. the cost is a few lines of UI. skip it and the downside is
disproportionate: an FTC-exposed incentive and a TTL that may not hold — so this is nitpick-severity by
rule but near-free to get right, and closely tied to the § 255.5 material-connection disclosure duty.

## the honest gap

- **§ 255.5 and part 465 are endorsement/review rules.** the reward here is not itself a paid review;
  they are cited as the **best-articulated federal test** for when and how an incentive must be
  disclosed, and for the clear-and-conspicuous **definition** — apply the standard, not a claim that
  every reward is a reviewed endorsement.
- **the CARD Act / reg e five-year floor binds a PURCHASED card.** an **earned** point sits in the
  loyalty/award/promotional exclusion, so the five-year floor does not bind it on its own terms — the
  CARD Act text is cited for the **disclosure discipline** it models, not as a claim that a five-year
  minimum binds an earned point.
- **florida's carve-out is a FACT test.** "no separate identifiable charge" is a fact about the point's
  earn mechanics; counsel must apply it to a program's exact design. where the fact is not met, the
  strict purchased-card regime attaches instead of the carve-out.
- **indiana has no enacted general gift-card expiration statute** (see the consumer-rules ref), so in
  indiana the disclosure discipline rests on the federal floor and the FTC material-connection standard,
  not a state overlay.
- **no court has ruled on any program's exact disclosure surfaces.** this is a text-level map of the two
  disclosures onto settled authorities — strong, but counsel-confirmable.
