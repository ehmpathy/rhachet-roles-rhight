# define.boundary.platform-funded-vs-conduit-held-funds

## .what

the boundary that decides whether a rewards-program money flow is money transmission: the sharp line
between **platform-settled debt** and **conduit-held third-party funds**.

- **platform-settled debt** = an actor pays its **own** obligation from its **own** revenue. it owes the
  amount (a reward-debt or an earned-commission-debt) and settles it from general revenue — payroll,
  vendor payments, Amazon-Associates-commission style. the payer is a **principal**, not a conduit. →
  outside money transmission.
- **conduit-held funds** = an actor receives person A's money to move it to a **different** person B, as
  a money-movement service. it holds and forwards someone else's value. → money transmission.

which side a given money flow sits on decides whether the regime attaches to that flow.

## .why

- money transmission needs a **conduit**: accept value from person A **and** transmit it to a **third
  party** B. a party that pays its own debt from its own revenue is not a mover of a third party's
  money.
- each money flow is analyzed on its own: a flow where a party pays its **own** debt from its **own**
  revenue is a **principal** flow, not a conduit flow — an earned commission owed, a promotional reward
  funded from revenue, a prepaid service sold are each principal.
- the separator is a single question of **whose money is it, and whose obligation is settled** — is the
  payer a mover of a third party's funds (conduit), or a payer of its own liability from its own revenue
  (principal)?
- the earned-value-vs-prepaid-credit distinction and the refund-to-same-payer-vs-on-demand-wallet
  redline are the two tests that sort each flow.

## the principal side — a party pays its own debt from its own revenue

a **principal that pays its own obligation** is the shape FinCEN carves out as a mere incident of one's
own sale of goods or services:

> "Accepts and transmits funds only integral to the sale of goods or the provision of services, other
> than money transmission services, by the person who is accepting and transmitting the funds"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(ii)(F)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

- a **prepaid service** a customer buys is the party's **own** revenue, not a held deposit.
- an **earned commission** the party owes is a **debt** it settles from its own general revenue —
  Amazon-Associates / payroll style.
- a **promotional reward** the party funds from its own revenue is principal on both legs.

the agent-of-payee doctrine reinforces the principal posture where a collection step ever appears —
receipt by the agent is receipt by the payee, not a conduit hop:

> "Payment for the goods and services is treated as received by the payee upon receipt by the agent, so
> that the payor's obligation is extinguished"

**source**: [Ind. Code § 28-8-4.1-301](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-28-financial-institutions/in-code-sect-28-8-4-1-301/)

---

## the conduit side — a party moves a third party's funds

money transmission needs the **two conjunctive elements** — an acceptance from one person **and** a
transmission to another:

> "the acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and
> the transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another
> location or person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(i)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

florida frames the covered actor as an **intermediary** that receives value to move it from one person
to another — the conduit shape:

> "receives currency, monetary value, a payment instrument, or virtual currency for the purpose of
> acting as an intermediary to transmit currency, monetary value, a payment instrument, or virtual
> currency from one person to another location or person"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.103(24)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.103)

the federal registration statute reaches the same conduit — a business in the **transmission** of
value that substitutes for currency:

> "any other person who engages as a business in the transmission of currency, funds, or value that
> substitutes for currency"

**source**: [31 U.S.C. § 5330(d)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5330)

a money flow lands on this side when it becomes a conduit flow: a refund or payout routed to a **third
party**, a **transferable** credit that moves value person-to-person, or a **bridge** that carries
prepaid credits into a cash-out wallet.

---

## the separator element

**the separator: whose obligation is settled, from whose money?**

| ask | platform-settled debt (principal) | conduit-held funds (transmission) |
|-----|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| whose money pays out? | the payer's own general revenue | a third party's funds the payer holds |
| whose obligation is settled? | the payer's own (reward-debt / earned-commission-debt) | none of the payer's — it forwards A's money to B |
| is there a third-party recipient? | no — the payer pays a party it owes | yes — a different person B receives A's value |
| the analogy | payroll, vendor pay, Amazon Associates commission | a remittance / money-transfer service |

a party that **settles its own liability** from its **own revenue** is a principal — outside money
transmission. a party that **holds and forwards a third party's funds** is a conduit — inside.

---

## the earned-value-vs-prepaid-credit distinction

a prepaid credit and an earned balance have opposite characters, and both stay safe for different
reasons:

- **prepaid credit** is **consideration for a service** — the party's revenue, booked as a
  contract liability to deliver the service, then recognized:

> "A contract liability is an entity's obligation to transfer goods or services to a customer (1) when
> the customer prepays consideration or (2) when the customer's consideration is due for goods and
> services that the entity will yet provide (ASC 606-10-45-2)—whichever happens earlier."

**source**: [presentation of contract assets and contract liabilities in ASC 606 — RevenueHub](https://www.revenuehub.org/article/presentation-of-contract-assets-and-contract-liabilities)

- **earned value** is a **debt the party owes** for work — settled from its own
  general revenue, the same way a rewards liability is carried as the issuer's own debt and recognized
  on redemption:

> "Rewards program liability is also equal to the deferred revenue."

> "The revenue gets recognized when points are redeemed."

**source**: [financial books for a rewards-program liability (ASC 606) — TrueLoyal](https://www.trueloyal.com/blog/financial-accounting-for-liability-from-rewards-programs)

neither is a **deposit** held for a third party. prepaid credit is the issuer's revenue; earned value is
the issuer's debt. both are the issuer's own money, on opposite sides of its books.

## the refund-to-same-payer vs on-demand-transferable-wallet redline

the redline that separates a prepaid balance outside stored value from a regulated stored-value balance
is **not** refundable-vs-non-refundable. it is **refundable-only-to-the-original-payer** (outside) vs
**on-demand / transferable / general-purpose wallet** (regulated).

- **refund to the same payer (outside stored value)**: a return of the **unspent** balance to the **same** person who
  paid it in, on account closure — an A→A return, not an A→B transfer. a prepaid balance that can only
  ever come back to the party that paid it never moves value to a third party. one illustration is a
  prepaid ad balance, refundable to the source account on closure:

> "If you fund your ad account using pre-paid funds instead of an automatic payment method like a credit
> card, you are entitled to a refund of any unused balance when you permanently close the account."

**source**: [how to get a refund from a Facebook ad account — Graphed](https://www.graphed.com/blog/how-to-get-a-refund-from-facebook-ad-account)

- **on-demand / transferable wallet (regulated)**: value **paid in advance** and **retrievable or
  transferable** at will — the prepaid-access core:

> "That is issued on a prepaid basis in a specified amount or not issued on a prepaid basis but capable
> of being loaded with funds thereafter"

**source**: [12 CFR § 1005.2(b)(3) — Reg E prepaid account](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.2)

the same paid-for line runs through state stored-value law — indiana pulls closed-loop and
loyalty/rewards value out of the definition entirely:

> "The term does not include: (A) a payment instrument; (B) closed loop stored value; or (C) stored
> value not sold to the public but issued and distributed as part of a loyalty, rewards, or promotional
> program"

**source**: [Ind. Code § 28-8-4.1-201](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-28-financial-institutions/in-code-sect-28-8-4-1-201/)

so a balance stays outside as a **refund to the same payer**; it becomes regulated stored value
the moment it is a **withdraw-anytime** or **transferable** wallet, or a refund routes to a **third
party**.

## red flags

| red flag | why it crosses from principal to conduit |
|----------|------------------------------------------|
| a refund or payout routed to a **third party**, not the payer | A→B transmission — the conduit shape the two-element test targets |
| the credit becomes **transferable** person-to-person | value moves between two different people → conduit |
| a **bridge** from prepaid credits into an earned cash-out wallet | prepaid revenue converts into a cashable balance → regulated stored value |
| the credit becomes an **on-demand / withdraw-anytime** wallet | it no longer serves as consideration for a service; it becomes a held, retrievable deposit |
| a party **holds and forwards** a customer's funds to another person | it moves a third party's money → money transmission |
| the wallet is marketed as a **general-purpose** balance | drifts out of the earned-income-debt character |

---

## the honest gap

the boundary is text-strong but application-untested for a modern rewards program:

- **an earned-payout flow is a real payout.** it falls on the principal side where the recipient
  **earned** the amount for work — a debt the payer owes and settles from its own revenue,
  Amazon-Associates / payroll style — not a customer's funds forwarded to a third party. that principal
  character is what the analysis turns on, and the flow still carries a 1099-NEC duty and a
  dormancy/escheat watch on stale balances.
- **a promotional-reward flow is a real money movement.** it falls on the principal side where the payer
  is both the one paid (revenue) and the one that pays out (its own reward-debt). where that character
  fails, the flow looks like a third-party transfer.
- **florida's stored-value silence.** FL carries no express loyalty carve-out, so the FL analysis leans
  on the two-element transmitter test plus a strictly closed-loop, non-transferable credit.
- state law is not uniform — per-state confirmation is required, since "managing compliance with money
  transmission laws generally remains a state-by-state proposition for money transmitters"
  ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-08-20-us-states-adopt-model-money-transmission-act-but-harmonization-remains-elusive)).

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what is THE boundary? | platform-settled debt vs conduit-held third-party funds |
| the separator? | whose obligation is settled, from whose money — the payer's own, or a third party's it holds? |
| the earned-vs-prepaid split? | prepaid credit = the payer's revenue; earned value = the payer's debt — both its own money, opposite sides of the books |
| the exit-refund redline? | refund-to-the-same-payer (principal — e.g. a prepaid ad balance) vs on-demand / transferable / third-party wallet (regulated) |
| where do distinct flows cross the line? | when a principal flow becomes a conduit flow — e.g. a prepaid credit bridged into a cash-out wallet |
| is it settled? | text is strong; application + per-state confirmation still needed |
