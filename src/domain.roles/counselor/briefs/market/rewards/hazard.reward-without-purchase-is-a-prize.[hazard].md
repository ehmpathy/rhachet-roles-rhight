# hazard.reward-without-purchase-is-a-prize

## .what

a reward paid for an action that is **not a purchase** — a mere sign-up, a form fill, an app install —
is a **prize / gift**, not a **rebate**. that one change of character carries two separate legal
consequences:

1. **tax** — a prize is an accession to wealth (income); a rebate on the recipient's own purchase is a
   price adjustment (not income).
2. **game-promotion / lottery** — a **prize** awarded with an element of **chance** (a random draw, a
   "chance to win"), offered incidental to a sale, is a regulated game-promotion / sweepstakes; add
   consideration and the same shape is an illegal lottery.

the shape outside both regimes: a reward **tethered to the recipient's own purchase** (so it reads as a
rebate) and **guaranteed, not drawn** (so no chance element is present). the shape inside them: a reward
for a non-purchase act (a prize), or one awarded by chance (a game-promotion / lottery).

## .why

- the tax character of a payment to a consumer turns on whether it adjusts the price of that consumer's
  own purchase. no purchase → no price to adjust → the payment is a prize, taxable as income.
- the lottery and game-promotion regimes turn on the trio **prize + chance + consideration**. a reward
  for a non-purchase action loses the price-adjustment defense; if any chance element is present, the
  award meets the game-promotion definition.
- both consequences attach to the **structure of the award**, not to who pays it. self-paid support does
  not cure a prize — only a tether to a real purchase does.

## consequence 1 — a non-purchase reward is taxable income, not a rebate

a rebate escapes income only because it reduces the price of the recipient's own purchase. the IRS
states the base rule:

> "where a payment is made from a seller to a purchaser, and the purpose and intent of the parties is to
> reach an agreed upon selling price, the payment is properly viewed as an adjustment to the purchase
> price that reduces gross sales."

**source**: [IRS Rev. Rul. 2008-26 (Section 61)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-08-26.pdf)

remove the purchase and the defense collapses — gross income then sweeps the reward in:

> "gross income means all income from whatever source derived, including (but not limited to) the
> following items"

**source**: [26 U.S.C. § 61](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/61)

a reward for a mere sign-up is an accession to wealth with no price to adjust — income to the recipient,
and, where it pays for a service such as a referral, reportable as nonemployee compensation.

---

## consequence 2 — a prize plus chance is a regulated game-promotion

Florida defines a game-promotion around chance and prize offered incidental to a sale:

> "a contest, game of chance, sweepstakes, or gift enterprise ... in connection with and incidental to
> the sale of consumer products or services, and in which the elements of chance and prize are present"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 849.094](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/849.094)

a reward stays out of this regime **only** while it carries **no element of chance** — a guaranteed
reward, not a random draw. a "sign up for a chance to win" mechanic supplies the chance element and the
prize-plus-chance definition closes. where consideration is also present, the same shape is an illegal
lottery.

## the redline

| safe side | the line | unsafe side |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| reward **tethered to the recipient's own purchase** (a rebate / price adjustment) | "prize" | reward for a **non-purchase** action (sign-up, install) → a taxable prize |
| reward **guaranteed** to every qualifier | "chance" | reward **by random draw** ("a chance to win") → prize + chance = game-promotion |
| no consideration required beyond the purchase | "consideration" | prize + chance + consideration = an illegal lottery |

**the separator:** did the recipient make a **purchase** the reward adjusts, and is the reward
**guaranteed** rather than **drawn**? if yes on both, it is a rebate outside the prize and
game-promotion regimes.

---

## the honest gap

- **no case law on a modern digital sign-up reward** tests the prize-vs-rebate or game-promotion line
  directly. the read is text-level — strong, but counsel-confirmable.
- **per-state confirmation.** game-promotion, sweepstakes, and lottery mechanics differ by state; the
  Florida statute is cited as a clear articulation of the chance-plus-prize test, not as the only one.
  confirm each state of operation before reliance.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| when is a reward a rebate, not a prize? | when it adjusts the price of the recipient's **own purchase** |
| what makes a reward a taxable prize? | no purchase to adjust — it is an accession to wealth (income) |
| what makes a prize a game-promotion? | an element of **chance** (a random draw) offered incidental to a sale |
| when does it become an illegal lottery? | prize + chance + consideration all present |
| which shape sits outside both? | tethered to a real purchase, guaranteed not drawn |
