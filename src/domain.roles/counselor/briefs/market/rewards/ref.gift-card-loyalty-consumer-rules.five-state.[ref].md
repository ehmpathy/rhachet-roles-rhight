# ref.gift-card-loyalty-consumer-rules.five-state

## .what

the consumer-protection rules that govern reward points, account credit, and gift certificates as
to four things: (a) **expiration** limits, (b) **disclosure** duties, (c) **cash-equivalence** and
small-balance cash-back, and (d) the **loyalty / promotional carve-out** that applies when the
recipient pays no separate identifiable charge — plus the linked **escheat** (unclaimed-property)
question. this brief lays the federal floor, then runs the five-state baseline sweep (TX, IN, FL, CA,
NY) required by `rule.require.five-state-baseline`, quotes each state's text verbatim, and names the
strictest on both the gift-card lever and the escheat lever.

the punchline: a reward **point** the consumer never bought sits inside the loyalty / promotional
carve-out in the federal rule and in **all four** states that have a gift-card statute — the trigger
in every one is **consideration** ("purchased", "sold", "money tendered", "separate identifiable
charge"). so the strict no-expiration / no-fee / cash-back regime that binds a **purchased** gift card
does **not** bind an earned point on its own terms. that changes the instant a point becomes
**cashable, transferable, or sold** — then the consideration triggers fire and the strictest state's
rule attaches.

## .why

- an earned point is not bought (see `define.points-are-a-debt-not-stored-value`). whether the gift-card
  or escheat regime attaches turns entirely on the loyalty / promotional carve-out — this brief is
  where that carve-out is quoted from primary law across five states.
- the carve-out is not a label a program picks; it is a **fact test** about whether the recipient
  paid consideration. where that fact is met, the full regime attaches — and the states diverge on
  how strict that regime is.
- the five diverge sharply: CA bans expiration outright and forces cash-back on small balances; NY
  caps expiration at nine years with a $5 cash-back floor; TX permits expiration but escheats fastest
  (3-year abandonment); FL bars expiration on purchased certificates and exempts them from escheat; IN
  has **no** enacted general gift-card statute at all.

## the federal floor — card act + regulation e

the federal card act sets the national minimum for gift certificates, store gift cards, and
general-use prepaid cards: a **five-year** floor on expiration, a **ban** on dormancy / inactivity /
service fees except under narrow conditions, and **disclosure** duties.

verbatim on the five-year floor, 15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1:

> "the expiration date is not earlier than 5 years after the date on which the gift certificate was
> issued"

verbatim on the fee ban:

> "it shall be unlawful for any person to impose a dormancy fee, an inactivity charge or fee, or a
> service fee"

**source**: [15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1 — general-use prepaid cards, gift certificates, and store gift cards](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1693l-1)

the rule that carries out the statute (regulation e) restates the same floor and names the loyalty
exclusion. verbatim on the five-year minimum, 12 cfr § 1005.20:

> "The expiration date for the underlying funds is at least the later of: Five years after the date
> the gift certificate was initially issued, or the date on which funds were last loaded to a store
> gift card or general-use prepaid card."

verbatim on the one-fee-per-month limit:

> "Not more than one dormancy, inactivity, or service fee is imposed in any given calendar month."

and — the decisive carve-out — regulation e excludes a **loyalty, award, or promotional gift card**
from the covered set, but conditions that exclusion on a front-of-card disclosure:

> "A statement indicating that the card, code, or other device is issued for loyalty, award, or
> promotional purposes, which must be included on the front of the card."

**source**: [12 cfr § 1005.20 — requirements for gift cards and gift certificates](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.20)

the federal floor is a **floor**, not a cap — a state may go further, and a state law that gives
consumers more protection is not preempted. a federal-deposit-supervised consumer resource states the
minimum plainly:

> "a gift card cannot expire until at least five years from the date it was activated."

and on fees:

> "the card issuer cannot charge a dormancy or inactivity fee on a gift card unless there has been no
> activity for one year and the card clearly states its policy toward that fee."

**source**: [fdic — what you should know about gift cards](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2024-12/what-you-should-know-about-gift-cards)

the consumer financial protection bureau frames the same three levers — expiration, fees, disclosure
— in consumer-facing terms. verbatim:

> "Under current federal law, a gift card cannot be sold that will expire in less than five years."

> "Federal rules say that gift cards cannot charge inactivity or service charges for 12 months, but
> after that first year, these fees could start to eat away at your card's value."

> "Gift cards should spell out what fees they charge, so read the fine print."

**source**: [cfpb — gift-card terms consumer blog](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/giving-or-receiving-gift-cards-know-the-terms-and-avoid-surprises/)

a consumer-law practitioner memo pins the loyalty / promotional exclusion precisely: such cards escape
the substantive fee and expiration limits **only if** the required disclosures ride along. verbatim:

> "Qualifying Loyalty/Promo Cards are not subject to the fee or expiration date restrictions if they
> include the required disclosures."

and the operative instruction to any issuer:

> "All persons involved in issuing or distributing Loyalty/Promo Cards should review and potentially
> revise disclosures that appear on or with Loyalty/Promo Cards."

**source**: [pillsbury — card act: loyalty, award and promotional card, code and other device](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/card-act-loyalty-award-and-promotional-card-code-and-other.html)

## florida — fla. stat. § 501.95 (strict, with a loyalty carve-out)

florida has an **enacted** gift-certificate statute that is stricter than the federal floor on its
face: it bars **any** expiration date and **any** post-sale fee on a purchased gift certificate.

verbatim on the definition, fla. stat. § 501.95, which reaches only an instrument **purchased for
monetary consideration**:

> "A certificate, gift card, stored value card, or similar instrument purchased for monetary
> consideration when the certificate, card, or similar instrument is redeemable for merchandise,
> food, or services"

verbatim on the no-expiration, no-fee prohibition:

> "A gift certificate purchased or credit memo issued in this state may not have an expiration date,
> expiration period, or any type of postsale charge or fee imposed on the gift certificate or credit
> memo, including, but not limited to, service charges, dormancy fees, account maintenance fees, or
> cash-out fees."

**source**: [fla. stat. § 501.95 — gift certificates and credit memos](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

the florida loyalty / promotional carve-out is the **exact fact test** for an earned point — an
expiration date **is** allowed when the recipient pays no separate identifiable charge. verbatim (per
the florida senate codification):

> "if it is provided to the recipient, or to a purchaser for transfer to the recipient, as part of a
> loyalty or promotional program when the recipient does not pay a separate identifiable charge for
> the certificate"

**source**: [fla. stat. § 501.95 (florida senate codification)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/501.95)

florida ties the consumer statute to its unclaimed-property regime by a cross-reference: an
**unredeemed** florida gift certificate is exempt from escheat, and the money the buyer paid belongs
to the issuer, not the state. verbatim, fla. stat. § 717.1045:

> "Notwithstanding s. 717.117, an unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo as defined in s. 501.95
> is not required to be reported as unclaimed property."

> "The consideration paid for an unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo is the property of the
> issuer of the unredeemed gift certificate or credit memo."

**source**: [fla. stat. § 717.1045 — unredeemed gift certificate / credit memo escheat exemption](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/717.1045)

so in florida: a **purchased** gift certificate gets the strictest treatment (no expiration, no fee,
issuer keeps the breakage); a **loyalty / promotional** certificate the recipient did not pay a
separate charge for may carry an expiration date and sits outside that strict regime.

## indiana — no enacted general gift-card expiration statute

indiana is the mirror opposite of florida on this point: it has **no enacted general gift-card
expiration statute**. the 2021 attempt to enact one, hb 1260, would have barred sale of a gift
certificate or store gift card with an expiration date after june 30, 2021, but it **died** without
passage. a legislative tracker records both the substance and the fate. verbatim on the status:

> "Dead 11/15/2021"

verbatim on what the bill would have required:

> "Provides that, after June 30, 2021, a person shall not sell or issue to an Indiana consumer any
> gift certificate or store gift card with an expiration date unless certain conditions are met."

**source**: [billtrack50 — indiana hb 1260 (2021)](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1262532)

the consequence: in indiana, the **federal card act is the only floor** for a purchased gift card,
and there is no state overlay that adds a no-expiration or no-fee rule beyond it. reward **points**,
which the consumer never buys, sit inside the federal loyalty / award / promotional exclusion and are
not reached by any indiana statute at all.

## california — cal. civ. code § 1749.5 (strictest gift-card, with a loyalty carve-out)

california is the strictest of the five on the gift-card lever: it bars an expiration date outright
and forces **cash-back** on a small balance. verbatim on the no-expiration rule:

> "It is unlawful for any person or entity to sell a gift certificate to a purchaser that contains
> any of the following: (1) An expiration date."

verbatim on the small-balance cash-back right and its dollar threshold — raised from $10 to **$15**,
operative april 1, 2026:

> "Notwithstanding paragraph (1), a gift certificate with a cash value of less than fifteen dollars
> ($15) is redeemable in cash for its cash value."

and the loyalty / promotional carve-out — the earned-point escape, conditioned on a front-of-card
expiration disclosure and, critically, on **no value given in exchange**:

> "This section does not apply to any of the following gift certificates issued on or after January 1,
> 1998, if the expiration date appears in capital letters in at least 10-point font on the front of
> the gift certificate: (1) A gift certificate that is distributed by the issuer to a consumer
> pursuant to an awards, loyalty, or promotional program without any money or other thing of value
> being given in exchange for the gift certificate by the consumer."

california does **not** escheat gift-certificate balances — the no-expiration + cash-back regime
substitutes for an unclaimed-property claim, so there is no CA escheat lever for a gift certificate.

**source**: [cal. civ. code § 1749.5 (CA Legislative Info, official)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1749.5.&lawCode=CIV)

## texas — bus. & com. code § 604 + property code § 72.1016 (permissive card, fastest escheat)

texas permits an expiration date and regulates fees rather than a ban on expiration — so it is the
**most permissive** of the four gift-card states — but it **escheats the fastest**. the loyalty
carve-out sits in the stored-value definition; verbatim, § 604.002 excludes a card that:

> "is distributed by the issuer to a person under an awards, rewards, loyalty, incentive, rebate, or
> promotional program and is not issued or reloaded in exchange for money tendered by the cardholder"

on escheat, texas presumes a stored-value card abandoned at the **three-year** anniversary — shorter
than NY's five years. verbatim, tex. prop. code § 72.1016 presumes abandonment on the earlier of the
card's expiration date, "the third anniversary of the date the card was issued, if the card is not
used after it is issued," or the first anniversary if the value represents wages. it applies only to
cards within the § 604.001 "monetary consideration" definition and expressly excludes cards "to which
Chapter 604 does not apply" — so an earned, unpurchased loyalty point is outside both the gift-card
rules and the escheat presumption.

**source**: [tex. bus. & com. code § 604.002 (public.law mirror)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_604.002)
· [tex. prop. code § 72.1016 (public.law mirror)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_72.1016)
(fallback mirrors — the official statutes.capitol.texas.gov is a javascript app that returns no text
to an automated fetch; confirm against the official pdf.)

## new york — gbl § 396-i + aband. prop. law § 1315 (mid-strict, 5-year escheat of sold cards)

new york sits between CA and TX: it permits a long expiration (nine-year floor) and a small cash
redemption, and it escheats **sold** certificates after five years. verbatim on expiration, gbl
§ 396-i:

> "a gift certificate may be subject to an expiration date which is not earlier than the later of nine
> years after the date on which the gift certificate was issued, or the date on which funds were last
> loaded"

verbatim on the $5 cash-redemption floor:

> "A gift certificate…with a remaining value of less than five dollars may be redeemed upon request
> for its cash value"

verbatim on the loyalty exclusion — again conditioned on **no consideration**:

> requirements "shall not apply to gift certificates…distributed to a consumer or employee pursuant
> to an awards, rewards, loyalty, or promotional program without any consideration being given"

on escheat, aband. prop. law § 1315 attaches only to certificates that were **sold**:

> "Any unclaimed amount representing unredeemed gift certificates sold after December thirty-first,
> nineteen hundred eighty-three…and which has remained unclaimed by the owner of such amount for five
> years, shall be deemed abandoned property."

the escheat hook is the certificate being **sold** — an earned, unpurchased point is not "sold," so
it falls outside § 1315.

**source**: [n.y. gen. bus. law § 396-i (NYS Senate, official)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/396-I)
· [n.y. aband. prop. law § 1315 (NYS Senate, official)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ABP/1315)

## the four levers, side by side

| lever | federal floor | TX (§ 604) | IN | FL (§ 501.95) | CA (§ 1749.5) | NY (§ 396-i) |
|-------|---------------|-----------|----|---------------|---------------|--------------|
| **(a) expiration** | ≥ 5 yrs for a purchased card | expiration allowed, fee-regulated | none (fed floor only) | **none** on a purchased certificate | **none** — expiration barred outright ⭐ | ≥ 9-year floor allowed |
| **(b) small-balance cash-back** | **no** federal mandate | no state mandate | none | bars "cash-out fees", no cash-back mandate | **cash-back under $15** (eff. 2026-04-01) ⭐ | cash redemption under $5 |
| **(c) loyalty / promotional carve-out** | reg e excludes loyalty/award/promo (front label) | excluded if "not… in exchange for money tendered" | no statute to carve out of | "no separate identifiable charge" | "without any money or other thing of value… in exchange" (10-pt font) | "without any consideration being given" |
| **(d) the carve-out trigger** | consideration paid | "money tendered" | n/a | "separate identifiable charge" | "money or other thing of value" | "consideration" |

**gift-card baseline = California** — § 1749.5 bans expiration outright and forces cash-back on a
balance under **$15** (the highest cash-back floor of the five), so it is the most consumer-protective.
every state's carve-out turns on the **same trigger**: an earned point given for no consideration sits
outside all of them.

## the escheat lever, side by side

| state | escheat of a purchased/sold gift certificate | earned point? | verbatim anchor | strict? |
|-------|----------------------------------------------|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| TX | presumed abandoned at the **3-year** anniversary | outside (Ch. 604 exclusion) | Tex. Prop. Code § 72.1016 | ⭐ (fastest) |
| IN | general unclaimed-property law; no gift-card-specific rule | outside (no statute reaches it) | Ind. Code tit. 32 (UPA) | |
| FL | **exempt** — issuer keeps the breakage | outside | Fla. Stat. § 717.1045 | |
| CA | **no** gift-certificate escheat (no-expiration regime substitutes) | outside | Cal. Civ. Code § 1749.5 | |
| NY | escheat after **5 years** — but only if **sold** | outside ("sold" not met) | N.Y. Aband. Prop. Law § 1315 | |

**escheat baseline = Texas** — a 3-year abandonment presumption is the shortest of the five (vs NY's
5 years; FL and CA do not escheat gift certificates at all). but **all five spare the earned point**:
each escheat hook requires the value to have been *sold* / *bought* / issued for *monetary
consideration*, and an earned point is none of those.

## the single line across all five

an **earned** point the consumer never bought stays outside both the strict gift-card regime and the
escheat presumption in **every** one of the five states — because each regime's trigger is
**consideration** ("purchased" / "sold" / "money tendered" / "separate identifiable charge" / "money
or other thing of value"). that changes the instant the point is **sold**, **loaded for a separate
charge**, or made **cashable/transferable** — then the consideration triggers fire and the strictest
served state's rule (CA on the card lever, TX on the escheat clock) attaches.

## key takeaways

| question | answer | anchor |
|----------|--------|--------|
| does the strict gift-card regime bind an **earned** point? | no — the loyalty / promotional carve-out applies in all four gift-card states | each state's "consideration" trigger |
| what triggers the strict regime? | the recipient pays **consideration** — "purchased" / "sold" / "money tendered" / "separate charge" | § 501.95; § 1749.5; § 396-i; § 604 |
| strictest gift-card of the five? | **CA** — no expiration + cash-back under $15 | cal. civ. § 1749.5 |
| strictest escheat of the five? | **TX** — 3-year abandonment presumption | tex. prop. § 72.1016 |
| which states do **not** escheat gift certs? | CA and FL | § 1749.5; § 717.1045 |
| federal floor for a **purchased** card? | ≥ 5-yr expiration; no dormancy fee before 12 mo; disclosure | 15 u.s.c. § 1693l-1; 12 cfr § 1005.20 |
| does escheat ever reach an **earned** point? | no in all five — the hook needs sale/purchase/consideration | § 1315 "sold"; § 72.1016 Ch. 604 exclusion |
