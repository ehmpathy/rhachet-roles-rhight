# ref.udap-remedy-teeth.five-state

## .what

every referral, rewards, and review mechanic carries a **civil UDAP** fallback: even a structurally-
clean design can draw an unfair-or-deceptive-acts-and-practices claim over how it is **marketed** or
**disclosed**. the five baseline states (TX, IN, FL, CA, NY) diverge sharply on the **remedy teeth** —
whether a plaintiff can win a multiplier, a fee award, or only actual damages. this brief runs the
five-state sweep required by `rule.require.five-state-baseline` and names the state whose teeth set the
operative exposure.

the punchline: **Texas has the sharpest teeth** — the DTPA allows an **uncapped** treble on economic
damages for a "knowing" violation plus **mandatory** attorney's fees to a prevailing consumer. Florida
has the **weakest** — FDUTPA gives actual damages plus fees, no multiplier. so a program's market copy
must clear a **TX-grade** bar to be safe nationwide.

## .why

- UDAP is the **catch-all**: where the referral-lottery, gift-card, or review statutes do not attach,
  the state UDAP still reaches deceptive *promotion* — so it is the residual exposure on every mechanic.
- the multiplier and fee rules decide whether a small deception is a nuisance or a class-action magnet;
  the teeth, not just the prohibition, drive the risk.
- this is a **cross-domain** reference — the referral, reviews, and rewards briefs each point here for
  the civil-remedy layer rather than repeat it.

## the five-state remedy matrix

| state | statute | private multiplier | attorney's fees | strict? |
|-------|---------|--------------------|-----------------|---------|
| TX | DTPA, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.50 | **up to 3× economic damages** (knowing) — uncapped | **mandatory** to prevailing consumer | ⭐ |
| IN | DCSA, Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4 | treble (willful), min $500 | reasonable fees | |
| FL | FDUTPA, Fla. Stat. § 501.211 | **none** — actual damages only | fees + costs | (weakest) |
| CA | UCL/CLRA/FAL (Bus. & Prof. § 17200; Civ. § 1780) | CLRA punitive + restitution (no fixed treble) | fees available | |
| NY | GBL §§ 349/350 | treble **capped at $1,000** (willful) | discretionary fees | |

**baseline = Texas** — its DTPA multiplier is **uncapped** (three times *all* economic damages on a
"knowing" verdict) and its fee award is **mandatory**, the strongest combination of the five. a design
whose market copy clears the TX DTPA clears the nationwide floor for UDAP-teeth exposure.

## the statutes verbatim

### TX — Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.50 (DTPA, the sharpest teeth)

the "knowing" multiplier, § 17.50(b)(1):

> "If the trier of fact finds that the conduct of the defendant was committed knowingly, the consumer
> may also recover damages for mental anguish … and the trier of fact may award not more than three
> times the amount of economic damages"

the mandatory fee, § 17.50(d):

> "Each consumer who prevails shall be awarded court costs and reasonable and necessary attorneys'
> fees."

the general prohibition, § 17.46(a):

> "False, misleading, or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby
> declared unlawful"

**source**: [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.50 (public.law mirror)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_17.50)
· [§ 17.46 (public.law mirror)](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_17.46)
(fallback mirrors — official portal not machine-readable.)

### IN — Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4 (DCSA, treble + $500 floor)

> a consumer may recover "the damages actually suffered as a result of the deceptive act or five
> hundred dollars ($500), whichever is greater", trebled up to "three (3) times the actual damages" for
> willful conduct, plus "reasonable attorney's fees".

**source**: [Ind. Code § 24-5-0.5-4 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-4/)

### FL — Fla. Stat. § 501.211 (FDUTPA, the weakest — actual damages only)

> a person aggrieved by a FDUTPA violation "may recover actual damages, plus attorney's fees and court
> costs"

FDUTPA contains **no** statutory multiplier — the individual remedy is actual damages plus fees and
costs only, which makes FL the weakest UDAP-teeth state of the five.

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.211 (FL Senate, official)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/501.211)

### CA — Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200 (UCL) + Civ. Code § 1780 (CLRA)

california's UCL affords **restitution and injunction** (no damages multiplier), while the CLRA adds
**actual damages, punitive damages, and fees** for enumerated deceptive practices. the UCL's core
prohibition:

> "unfair competition shall mean and include any unlawful, unfair or fraudulent business act or practice
> and unfair, deceptive, untrue or misleading advertising"

**source**: [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200 (CA Legislative Info, official)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=17200.&lawCode=BPC)

### NY — N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 349 (deceptive acts, treble capped at $1,000)

> recovery of "actual damages or fifty dollars, whichever is greater"; the court may "increase the
> award of damages to an amount not to exceed three times the actual damages up to one thousand
> dollars, if the court finds the defendant willfully or knowingly violated this section" plus
> discretionary "reasonable attorney's fees to a prevailing plaintiff."

**source**: [N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law § 349 (NYS Senate, official)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/349)

## how to read the teeth

| lever | why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| **uncapped multiplier (TX)** | a small per-consumer deception scales into large aggregate exposure |
| **fixed-cap multiplier (NY $1,000)** | limits per-plaintiff upside; still a class-action driver |
| **mandatory fees (TX)** | removes the plaintiff's cost barrier — more suits get filed |
| **actual-damages-only (FL)** | weakest; a deception with no measurable loss yields little |

the practical takeaway: **market copy is a separate review surface from structure.** a structurally-
clean referral or rewards program can still lose on UDAP if its promotion is deceptive — and TX sets
the exposure limit.

## the honest gap

- this brief maps the **individual** remedy tracks; each state also has an **AG enforcement** track
  (civil penalties, injunctions) with its own multipliers — counsel should layer that in for a full
  exposure picture.
- the **Texas** quotes rest on public-law mirrors (official portal not machine-readable); confirm
  against the official pdf.
- CA's UCL/CLRA remedy interaction is nuanced (restitution vs damages vs punitive); the summary here
  is a map, not an opinion on which claim a given fact pattern supports.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| sharpest teeth of the five? | **TX** — uncapped 3× economic + mandatory fees |
| weakest? | **FL** — actual damages only, no multiplier |
| which cap their multiplier? | NY ($1,000); IN trebles with a $500 floor |
| what does this govern? | the **market-copy / disclosure** layer on every mechanic |
