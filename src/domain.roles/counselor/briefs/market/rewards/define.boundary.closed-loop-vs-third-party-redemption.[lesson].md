# define.boundary.closed-loop-vs-third-party-redemption

## .what

the sharp question this brief settles: when a program's customers redeem rewards across **many
independent sellers**, not one store, does redemption at many third-party sellers **break** the
single-defined-merchant closed-loop exemption, or does it survive?

the answer turns on **whose sale it is**. where the program operator is the **merchant-of-record /
promisor** — the customer redeems the operator's credit for an operator-brokered purchase, and the
enrolled seller is the operator's fulfilment channel — the redemption reads as one program, a defined
**set of locations**, closed-loop. where the credit is instead spendable as general-purpose value at
unrelated third parties like cash, it reads **open-loop** — outside the closed-loop exemption and into
prepaid-access / money-services scrutiny.

this brief does **not** re-derive the money-transmitter two-element test — see the kin briefs
cross-referenced below. it isolates the closed-loop-vs-open-loop question alone.

## .why

- the closed-loop exemption is drawn around a **defined merchant or location (or set of locations)**.
  a marketplace of enrolled sellers under one program either reads as a "set of locations" or as a
  general-purpose network of unrelated sellers.
- the manufacturer-coupon-reimbursement structure places the operator as the **promisor /
  merchant-of-record**: the operator owes the discount and reimburses the seller that honors it. that
  is not a customer who spends stored value at an unrelated third party — it is a promisor that pays its
  own promotional debt.
- three separators — **merchant-of-record vs conduit**, **program-only vs general-purpose**, and
  **non-transferable vs transferable** — decide which read applies.

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

## the exemption text — a defined merchant, or a SET of locations

FinCEN draws closed-loop prepaid access around a defined merchant **or a set of locations**, and the
examples reach past a single store to a **chain**, a **campus**, a **subway system**:

> "Prepaid access to funds or the value of funds that can be used only for goods or services in
> transactions involving a defined merchant or location (or set of locations), such as a specific
> retailer or retail chain, a college campus, or a subway system."

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(kkk)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

the contrast term is **prepaid access** at large — value paid in advance that can be **retrieved or
transferred** for general use:

> "Access to funds or the value of funds that have been paid in advance and can be retrieved or
> transferred at some point in the future through an electronic device or vehicle, such as a card,
> code, electronic serial number, mobile identification number, or personal identification number."

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ww)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

a memo on the 2011 final rule reads the same line: closed-loop is tied to a specified merchant set
and **cannot be pulled out as cash**, while open-loop spans unaffiliated merchants:

> "prepaid access to funds or the value of funds that can be used only for goods and services in
> transactions involving a defined merchant or location (or set of locations)"

**source**: [Mondaq — FinCEN final rule on prepaid access](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/finance/141788/fincen-issues-final-rule-regarding-prepaid-access)

so the CFR itself contemplates a **set of locations** under one program. a marketplace of enrolled
sellers can sit on the closed-loop side — the question is whether it reads as **one program's
fulfilment channels** or as **general-purpose value at unrelated sellers**.

---

## the closed-loop read — the operator is the defined merchant / merchant-of-record

on the closed-loop read the operator is the **merchant-of-record / promisor** and the enrolled
sellers are its fulfilment channels. the merchant-of-record is the party legally on the hook for the
sale to the customer:

> "The seller of record (SoR) is the entity legally responsible for the products or services that are
> being sold to a customer."

**source**: [RetailDogma — seller of record (SoR): definition & marketplace considerations](https://www.retaildogma.com/seller-of-record/)

the **manufacturer-coupon-reimbursement** structure is what puts an operator in that seat. the operator
promised the customer the discount and owes it; the seller honors the discount at checkout; the
operator reimburses the seller — exactly the manufacturer-coupon flow, where the promisor reimburses
the store that accepts its coupon:

> "SMF will reimburse the following items for properly redeemed coupon: (a) face value of the coupon
> or if the coupon provides for free product(s), SMF shall reimburse the retail selling price but only
> up to the printed maximum value contained on the coupon; (b) 8¢ for processing each properly
> redeemed coupon"

**source**: [Inmar — coupon redemption policy (Springer Mountain Farms), § 9](https://www.inmar.com/terms-conditions/coupon-redemption-policy)

read that way, the customer does **not** spend stored value at an unrelated third party. the customer
redeems the operator's credit for an operator-brokered purchase; the operator is the merchant-of-record
that owes the discount and reimburses its fulfilment channel. the redemption never leaves the operator's
program — the "set of locations" that the CFR text expressly allows.

indiana reinforces the closed-loop read at the state level: its closed-loop carve-out reaches value
redeemable only within the issuer's own program and its affiliates or franchisees:

> "Stored value that is redeemable by the issuer only for goods or services provided by the issuer or
> the issuer's affiliate or by franchisees of the issuer or the issuer's affiliate, except to the
> extent required by applicable law to be redeemable in cash for its cash value."

**source**: [Ind. Code § 28-8-4.1-201](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-28-financial-institutions/in-code-sect-28-8-4-1-201/)

a marketplace of enrolled sellers that redeem an operator's credit under one program is the close
analogue of the issuer-plus-franchisees model — the value stays inside the issuer's own redemption
universe.

## the open-loop read — a general-purpose credit spendable at unrelated third parties

the credit reads **open-loop** where it is general-purpose value the customer spends at unrelated
sellers like cash. open-loop is defined by exactly that reach across many unrelated merchants:

> "Open-loop payment cards are much more common than closed loop cards and can be thought of as any
> electronic payment card which can be used to make purchases at a multitude of organizations."

and the counterpoint the same source draws for closed-loop:

> "Closed-loop payment cards are any type of electronic payment card that can be used only from a
> single organization."

**source**: [True Merchant — closed-loop cards vs. open-loop cards](https://truemerchant.com/closed-vs-open-loop-payments/)

if the operator is a **mere conduit** — one that hands the customer's value straight through to an
independent seller the customer chose and pays — the flow drifts toward the money-services definition,
which reaches a business in the transmission of value that substitutes for currency:

> "any other person who engages as a business in the transmission of currency, funds, or value that
> substitutes for currency, including any person who engages as a business in an informal money
> transfer system"

**source**: [31 U.S.C. § 5330(d)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5330)

florida sharpens the edge: its "stored value" is defined around value **retrievable and transferred
electronically**, with no express loyalty carve-out — a transferable, general-purpose credit lands in
the definition:

> "currency or monetary value represented in digital electronic format, regardless of whether it is
> specially encrypted, and stored or capable of storage on electronic media in such a way as to be
> retrievable and transferred electronically."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.103(35)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.103)

so the open-loop read is: a **transferable**, **general-purpose**, cash-like credit that the customer
spends at unrelated sellers, with the operator a conduit rather than the merchant-of-record.

## the separator elements

three elements decide which read applies. the closed-loop read holds only when all three sit on the
closed-loop side; any one on the open-loop side pulls toward the open-loop read.

| # | separator | closed-loop side | open-loop side |
|---|-----------|------------------|----------------|
| a | **merchant-of-record vs conduit** | the operator is the promisor / merchant-of-record — it owes the discount and reimburses the seller (coupon-reimbursement) | the operator is a mere conduit that hands the customer's value to an independent seller |
| b | **program-only vs general-purpose** | the credit redeems only within the operator's program (a defined set of enrolled sellers) | the credit is general-purpose value spendable at unrelated third parties like cash |
| c | **non-transferable vs transferable** | the credit cannot be sold, sent, or cashed person-to-person | the credit is transferable / cash-retrievable, the prepaid-access core |

element (a) is the decisive one: the coupon-reimbursement structure is what makes an operator the
merchant-of-record. the merchant-of-record is the party responsible for the sale to the customer, so
where the operator owes and reimburses the discount, the redemption is the operator's own promotional
settlement — not a customer who pays an unrelated third party with stored value.

## red flags

| red flag (breaks the closed-loop read) | why it moves toward open-loop / regulated |
|----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| the credit is spendable at **unrelated sellers** outside the operator's program | it no longer maps to a defined "set of locations" and becomes general-purpose value → open-loop |
| the credit is **transferable** or **cash-retrievable** person-to-person | it becomes the prepaid-access core — value paid in advance, retrieved or transferred at will |
| the operator is papered as a **payment conduit**, not the merchant-of-record | the coupon-reimbursement / promisor character is lost; the customer looks to pay an independent seller |
| the seller, not the operator, is the **seller-of-record** to the customer for the redeemed value | the operator is no longer the defined merchant; the redemption reads as third-party spend |
| the program is marketed as a **general-purpose** balance or wallet | drifts out of the defined-merchant closed loop |
| redemption pays the seller **more than a reimbursement** of an operator-promised discount | the flow looks like value transmitted to a third party, not a promo debt settled |

## the honest gap

no agency letter or court has held that a many-seller marketplace redemption stays closed-loop; this
is a **text-level** read of the CFR "set of locations" language, the IN issuer-and-franchisee carve-out,
and the merchant-of-record / coupon-reimbursement structure — strong but counsel-confirmable. three
soft edges:

- **the "set of locations" line is undrawn for marketplaces.** the CFR text allows a set of locations
  and a retail chain, but FinCEN never squarely settled whether an online marketplace of independent
  sellers counts. the closed-loop read rests on the operator as the merchant-of-record, not merely the
  venue.
- **florida carries no express loyalty carve-out.** FL's stored-value definition turns on
  "retrievable and transferred electronically." the FL analysis therefore leans hardest on a strictly
  **non-transferable, program-only, non-cash** credit plus the merchant-of-record character.
- **the merchant-of-record character is where the read is won or lost.** if the seller is documented
  as the seller-of-record to the customer for the redeemed value, the operator looks like a conduit and
  the closed-loop read weakens. the coupon-reimbursement flow (the operator owes and reimburses) is the
  shape that supports the closed-loop read.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| does many-seller redemption break the closed-loop exemption? | **not inherently** — the CFR allows a defined "set of locations," and a one-program marketplace can qualify |
| what keeps it closed-loop? | the operator is the **merchant-of-record / promisor** (coupon-reimbursement), the credit is **program-only** and **non-transferable** |
| what breaks it? | a **general-purpose**, **transferable / cash-like** credit spent at unrelated sellers, with the operator a mere conduit |
| the decisive separator? | **merchant-of-record vs conduit** — does the operator owe and reimburse the discount, or hand the customer's value to an independent seller? |
| a stricter state to note? | **florida** illustrates the stricter end — stored value keyed to "retrievable and transferred," no express loyalty carve-out |
| how does IN help? | its closed-loop carve-out reaches issuer-plus-franchisee redemption — the marketplace analogue |
| is it settled? | text is strong; the marketplace "set of locations" line is untested — per-state counsel confirmation needed |
