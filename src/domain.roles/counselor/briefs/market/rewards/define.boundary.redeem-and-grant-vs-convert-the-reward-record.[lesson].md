# define.boundary.redeem-and-grant-vs-convert-the-reward-record

## .what

where a loyalty reward ends up as a benefit to a **second party** (a merchant, a supplier, another
account), two structures reach the **same economic outcome** while they land on **opposite sides** of
the monetizable-record line:

- **redeem-and-grant** — the holder **redeems** the reward in-loop (a discount on the holder's own
  purchase), and the issuer **separately grants** the second party a reward from the **issuer's own**
  funds, triggered by (not sourced from) that redemption. two independent events. the record stays a
  discount → outside the monetizable-record line.
- **convert / transfer** — the holder's reward record **converts into** a second, cashable instrument
  the second party holds, or is **transferred** to the second party. one event: the record becomes
  money in another's hands → across the monetizable-record line.

the dollars are identical (holder saves $X, second party gets $X, issuer is out $X). the legal fork
is **structural, not economic** — it turns on whether the **reward record itself becomes money**, or
merely **triggers** a separate issuer-funded obligation.

## .why

- the escheat loyalty-exclusion is a possibility test on the **record**: does it "may be redeemed for
  money or otherwise monetized by the issuer"? a record that **converts** into a cashable instrument
  is monetized — even if a *third party*, not the holder, receives the cash. a record that is merely
  **redeemed as a discount** yields goods/services, not money.
- transferability is a named stored-value hallmark: a record the holder can **hand to another person**
  is a unit that circulates. a record the holder can only ever spend as their **own** discount is not.
- money transmission turns on **accept-from-A, transmit-to-B**. an issuer that **grants** the second
  party from its **own** revenue discharges its **own** debt — not a conduit. a **conversion** that
  routes the holder's balance to the second party is value accepted from A and transmitted to B.

so the same outcome is safe when built as *redeem + separate issuer grant*, and unsafe when built as
*convert/transfer the record* — across all three regimes at once.

## the possibility test reads the record, not the outcome

the loyalty-exclusion carve-back:

> "The term does not include a record that may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the
> issuer."

**source**: [D.C. Code § 41-151.02](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/41-151.02) · [Ind. Code § 32-34-1.5-3](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-32-property/in-code-sect-32-34-1-5-3/)

a **conversion** of the reward record into a cashable instrument makes the record "otherwise
monetized by the issuer" — the possibility test closes, regardless of **who** ends up with the cash.
a **redemption** that yields a discount leaves the record non-monetizable; the separate issuer grant
that follows is the issuer's **own** obligation, not a monetization of the holder's record.

---

## transferability — the record must not move to another person

> "Access to funds or the value of funds that have been paid in advance and can be retrieved or
> transferred at some point in the future ..."

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

a **transfer** of the reward record to the second party is exactly the "transferred to another
person" hallmark — it makes the record a unit that circulates and flips it toward stored value. a
**redemption** never moves the record to anyone; it settles in-loop as the holder's own discount, and
the second party's benefit arrives as a **fresh issuer grant**, not the holder's transferred record.

---

## money transmission — a grant is self-funded, a conversion is a conduit

> "the acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person
> and the transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another
> location or person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

an issuer that **grants** the second party from its own revenue accepts and transmits no third
party's funds. a **conversion** that carries the holder's balance to the second party is value
accepted from A and transmitted to B — the conduit shape.

---

## the two independence tests — how the two structures are distinguished

because the outcome is identical, the distinction is read from **structure**, not amount. two tests:

1. **can the holder, unaided, move the reward's value to the second party?**
   - **redeem-and-grant** — no. the holder's only lever is a discount on their **own** purchase; the
     second party's reward is a **separate issuer rule** the holder cannot direct.
   - **convert / transfer** — yes. the holder directs the record to the second party (a
     transfer/conversion).
2. **can the two legs vary independently?**
   - **redeem-and-grant** — yes. the issuer can grant the second party a different form, a different
     amount, or no reward at all, **without a change** to what the holder gets (still a discount). two
     independent knobs.
   - **convert / transfer** — no. the holder's record **is** the second party's instrument — one
     value, welded.

## the redline

| redeem-and-grant (record stays exempt) | the line | convert / transfer (exemption lost) |
|----------------------------------------|----------|-------------------------------------|
| holder **redeems** the record as an in-loop discount on their own purchase | "does the **record itself** become money or move to another person?" | holder's record **converts** into a cashable instrument, or **transfers** to the second party |
| issuer **grants** the second party from its **own** revenue — a separate event | "one event or two?" | one event — the record is relabeled/routed to the second party |
| the two legs can **vary independently** | "welded or independent?" | the legs are **welded** — the record *is* the second party's instrument |
| redemption **triggers** the grant | | redemption **becomes** the grant |

**the separator:** does the **reward record itself** become money or move to another person
(convert/transfer → monetized), or does an in-loop redemption merely **trigger** a **separate,
issuer-funded** grant (redeem-and-grant → the record stays a discount)? same dollars — the structure,
not the amount, decides.

---

## the sham-substance caveat

form alone does not settle a structure whose substance is a conversion. if the two legs are welded so
tightly — the second party's reward is **always exactly** the record's value, fully automatic, with
no independent issuer-funded source or discretion — the arrangement can be read as a conversion in
substance despite its two-event form. what separates the two in substance is **genuine independence**:
the issuer funds the grant from its own revenue, owns the program terms, the legs **can** diverge, and
the holder has **no** transfer right. unclaimed-property law refuses to let form defeat substance
generally:

> "the expiration of a period of limitation on the owner's right to receive or recover property,
> whether specified by contract, statute, or court order, does not preclude the property from being
> presumed abandoned."

**source**: [Revised Uniform Unclaimed Property Act — ABA Business Law Today](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2018-february/the-revised-uniform-unclaimed-property-act/)

the same substance-over-form posture means a conversion dressed as two events is still a conversion —
so the independence must be **real**, not cosmetic. this is counsel-confirmable.

---

## the honest gap

- **the independence line is fact-sensitive.** how much the two legs must be able to diverge, and how
  much issuer discretion is enough, is not settled by bright-line authority; it is a counsel-confirmable
  question of fact on a given structure.
- **per-state divergence** — the monetizable-record line, the stored-value hallmarks, and the
  money-transmitter thresholds differ by state; confirm each state of operation.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| same dollars — does the structure still matter? | yes — whether the record **becomes** money vs **triggers** a grant is the whole line |
| what keeps redeem-and-grant outside the line? | the record redeems as a discount; the second party's reward is a **separate issuer-funded** grant |
| what carries convert/transfer across it? | the record itself becomes cashable or moves to another person — monetized + transferable + conduit |
| how are the two structures distinguished? | the two independence tests — can the holder move value out? can the legs vary? |
| the caveat? | independence must be **real** — form does not defeat substance |
