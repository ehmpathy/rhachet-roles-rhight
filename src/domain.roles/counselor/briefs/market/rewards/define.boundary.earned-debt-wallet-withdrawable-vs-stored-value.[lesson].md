# define.boundary.earned-debt-wallet-withdrawable-vs-stored-value

## .what

a common worry: if a balance is **withdrawable at any time**, it must be a regulated stored-value /
prepaid-access instrument, and its issuer a money transmitter. that instinct over-reads
withdrawability. the line is not *when* the holder can take the value — it is **where the value came
from and whether it can move to others**:

- an **earned, non-transferable, self-funded** balance is a **debt the issuer owes** the holder for
  a contribution. it can be **withdrawable on demand** and still sit **outside** stored value and
  money transmission — because it is the issuer's own money, owed, paid from its own revenue.
- a **deposited** balance (the holder paid value in) or a **transferable** balance (it can move
  person-to-person) is **regulated stored value / prepaid access** regardless of when it is
  withdrawn — because it is either funds held for the holder or a circulating unit of value.

so withdraw-anytime is **not** the flip-trigger. **deposit-funding** and **transferability** are.

## .why

- the prepaid-access / stored-value definitions reach funds **"paid in advance"** and value that is
  **"retrieved or transferred"** — both key on money the **holder put in**, or a unit that
  **circulates**. an earned reward was neither paid in advance by the holder nor a bearer unit.
- the money-transmission test is **accept from one person, transmit to another**. an issuer that
  pays its **own** debt to the payee from its **own** revenue accepts and transmits no third party's
  funds — it discharges an obligation, the way a business pays accrued commissions or payroll.
- an earned balance is therefore ordinary **accounts-payable**, not custodial funds. its
  withdrawability is a feature of a debt, not a hallmark of stored value.

## the stored-value definition keys on "paid in advance"

the federal prepaid-access definition names the source of the value:

> "Access to funds or the value of funds that have been paid in advance and can be retrieved or
> transferred at some point in the future through an electronic device or vehicle, such as a card,
> code, electronic serial number, mobile identification number, or personal identification number."

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

two load-bearing phrases:

- **"paid in advance"** — the holder **put value in**. an **earned** balance is the opposite: the
  holder put no value in; the issuer **owes** it for a contribution.
- **"retrieved or transferred"** — a balance that can move **to another person** is a circulating
  unit. a **non-transferable** earned balance can only ever settle back to the one holder who earned
  it.

a balance that was never paid in and can never move to another person fails both hallmarks — so
withdraw-anytime does not, by itself, make it prepaid access.

---

## the money-transmission test keys on movement between persons

> "the acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person
> and the transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another
> location or person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

an issuer that pays a holder from the issuer's **own** revenue, to discharge a debt it **owes** that
holder, accepts no third party's funds and transmits nothing between two other persons. it is a
self-funded payout of its own obligation — outside the two-element test — even when the holder may
withdraw on demand. a purchase-tied or contribution-tied payout the issuer funds itself is the
Amazon-Associates commission shape, not a conduit.

---

## the cost that remains: an earned debt still escheats (as wages/payables)

the earned-debt characterization keeps the balance out of stored value and money transmission, but
it does **not** exempt it from **escheat**. an earned, monetizable balance the holder never claims
becomes unclaimed property — typically on a **short** dormancy for compensation:

> "an employee's paycheck might be considered dormant just one year after the date payable"

**source**: [Sales Tax Institute — dormancy periods & escheatment](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/managing-unclaimed-property-understanding-dormancy-periods-due-diligence-and-escheatment)

so a withdrawable earned wallet carries a **narrow, bounded** unclaimed-wages / unclaimed-property
escheat on dormant balances. that is ordinary accounts-payable escheat — manageable — not the
custodial-funds-held posture a deposited balance would carry.

---

## the redline

| outside stored value / MT (an earned debt) | the line | regulated stored value / prepaid access |
|--------------------------------------------|----------|------------------------------------------|
| balance is **earned** for a contribution — the holder paid no value in | "was the value **paid in advance** by the holder, or **owed** to them?" | balance is **deposited** — the holder paid value in ("paid in advance") |
| balance is **non-transferable** — settles only to the one holder | "can it move **to another person**?" | balance is **transferable** — a circulating bearer unit |
| issuer pays from its **own** revenue, discharging its **own** debt | "does the issuer **hold** the holder's funds, or **owe** its own?" | issuer **holds** the holder's funds, or forwards a third party's |
| **withdraw-anytime is allowed** on all of the above | — | withdraw-anytime is irrelevant — the deposit/transfer already flipped it |

**the separator:** did the holder **pay the value in** (deposit → stored value), can it **move to
another person** (transfer → stored value), or does the issuer **hold a third party's funds**
(conduit → money transmission)? if none — the balance is earned, non-transferable, and self-funded —
it is a debt the issuer owes, and it can be withdrawable at any time and still stay outside both
regimes.

---

## the three conditions on which withdraw-anytime stays outside both regimes

the earned-debt characterization holds only where three conditions are met, and breaks where any one
is absent:

1. **earned-only** — the balance is only ever *earned* for a contribution, never *bought, loaded, or
   deposited*. a deposit flips it to "funds paid in advance" → stored value + MT + a restricted
   custodial float.
2. **non-transferable** — it cannot be sold or sent person-to-person. a transferable balance is a unit
   that moves between holders → stored value.
3. **self-funded, one-directional** — the issuer pays it from its own revenue and it never bridges
   into a deposited or third-party-funded pool. a bridge re-opens the analysis.

where all three hold, "redeemable at any time" describes a debt. where any one is absent, the same
phrase describes regulated stored value.

---

## the honest gap

- **the earned-vs-deposited line is fact-sensitive at the edges.** a balance that mixes earned and
  purchased value, or that can be topped up by the holder, blurs the "paid in advance" test; keep
  the two strictly separate, and this is counsel-confirmable.
- **state money-transmitter and stored-value regimes vary.** the federal definitions are the
  clearest articulation, but state license thresholds and closed-loop exemptions differ; confirm
  each state of operation.
- **escheat still applies** — the earned-debt framing narrows escheat to ordinary payables, but does
  not remove it; wage/commission dormancy is short (often one year).

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| does withdraw-anytime make a balance stored value? | no — deposit-funding or transferability does, not withdrawability |
| what keeps a withdrawable earned wallet out of MT? | the issuer pays its **own** debt from its **own** revenue — no third-party conduit |
| what are the flip-triggers? | the holder **paid value in**, the balance is **transferable**, or the issuer **holds third-party funds** |
| is there any residual cost? | yes — a narrow unclaimed-wages/property **escheat** on dormant earned balances |
| the three conditions? | earned-only + non-transferable + self-funded/one-directional |
