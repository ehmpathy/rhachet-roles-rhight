# define.boundary.earned-debt-vs-deposited-value

## .what

the redline for reward-point law: the sharp line between **earned debt** and **deposited value**.

- **earned debt** = an amount owed for a contribution. it is the issuer's own money, carried as a
  liability that offsets — an ordinary accounts-payable. → outside money transmission, stored value, and
  (mostly) escheat.
- **deposited value** = money a holder deposited with the issuer. it is the holder's funds, held by the
  issuer. → stored value / prepaid access → money-transmitter regulated, with segregation and custody
  duties.

## .why

- the three regimes (money transmission, stored value, escheat) all attach to **deposited value** and
  all release **earned debt**
- so the regime a point falls under rests on **which side of this one line** it sits
- the element that separates the two is a single question of **whose money is it** — did a person
  deposit funds, or did the issuer incur a liability for a contribution?

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate.

## the redline

```
        EARNED DEBT                    │  THE LINE  │      DEPOSITED VALUE
─────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────┼────────────────────────────────────
 the issuer INCURS a liability for a       │          │  a holder DEPOSITS their own funds
 person's contribution                   │  "whose  │  with the issuer
                                         │   money  │
 the issuer's own money — it owes an offset│   is     │  the holder's money — the issuer holds it
                                         │   it?"   │
 no funds were accepted, paid in advance,│          │  funds were paid in, loaded, prefunded
 or loaded                               │          │
                                         │          │
 an accounts-payable (a trade debt)      │          │  a custody balance (stored value)
                                         │          │
 redeemed for a discount / goods, or as  │          │  redeemable / retrievable as value,
 an earned-for-service cash-out          │          │  cashable, transferable
```

**the element that separates the sides:** *whose money is it?* if a person **deposited funds**, the
issuer holds someone else's value → deposited value. if the issuer **incurred a liability** for a
contribution, it is the issuer's own money with a payable that offsets → earned debt.

---

## the separator, regime by regime

each regime states the same fork in its own words. on every one, "earned debt" fails the trigger and
"deposited value" meets it.

### money transmission — needs an "acceptance" of another's funds

the FinCEN service definition needs an actor to **accept** value from one person to move to another:

> "acceptance of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and
> the transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for currency to another
> location or person by any means"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(i)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

- **earned debt**: no acceptance — a person contributed a referral; the issuer did not take their funds.
- **deposited value**: an acceptance — a holder handed funds in, which the issuer then holds/moves.

the federal registration statute frames the regulated actor the same way, around a business that
handles **value that substitutes for currency**:

> "any person who engages as a business in the transmission of currency, funds, or value that
> substitutes for currency"

**source**: [31 U.S.C. § 5330(d)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5330)

and FinCEN carves out value moved as a mere incident of one's **own** sale of goods/services — the
earned-debt shape:

> "Accepts and transmits funds only integral to the sale of goods or the provision of services, other
> than money transmission services, by the person who is accepting and transmitting the funds."

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(ii)(F)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100#ff_5)

### stored value / prepaid access — needs "funds paid in advance"

> "Access to funds or the value of funds that have been paid in advance and can be retrieved or
> transferred at some point in the future"

**source**: [31 CFR § 1010.100(ww)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100)

- **earned debt**: no sum was paid in advance — the point was earned, not prepaid.
- **deposited value**: value paid in advance, retrievable later — the definition's core.

the Reg E "prepaid account" analogue keys on value **loaded**:

> "issued on a prepaid basis in a specified amount or not issued on a prepaid basis but capable of
> being loaded with funds thereafter"

**source**: [12 CFR § 1005.2(b)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1005.2)

- **earned debt**: never loaded.
- **deposited value**: loaded/reloaded with funds.

florida's state definitions confirm the same edge — "monetary value" and "stored value" both describe
value **represented and held** as a medium of exchange:

> "'Monetary value' means a medium of exchange, other than virtual currency, regardless of whether it
> is redeemable in currency."

> "'Stored value' means currency or monetary value represented in digital electronic format… stored
> or capable of storage on electronic media in such a way as to be retrievable and transferred
> electronically."

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 560.103(22), (35)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/560.103)

### escheat — turns on whether consideration was paid

kentucky's enacted RUUPA (KRS 393A.010, a verbatim adoption) states the redline in two definitions. a
**stored-value card** is paid-for; a **loyalty card** is not:

> "'Stored-value card': (a) Means a record evidencing a promise made for consideration by the seller
> or issuer of the record that goods, services, or money will be provided to the owner of the record
> to the value or amount shown in the record"

> "'Loyalty card': (a) Means a record given without direct monetary consideration under an award,
> reward, benefit, loyalty, incentive, rebate, or promotional program, which may be used or redeemed
> only to obtain goods or services or a discount on goods or services; and (b) Does not include a
> record that may be redeemed for money or otherwise monetized by the issuer"

**source**: [Ky. Rev. Stat. § 393A.010(30), (14)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ky/title-xxxiv-descent-wills-and-administration-of-decedents-estates/ky-rev-st-sect-393a-010/)

and only the paid-for stored-value card is escheatable — the loyalty card is removed from covered
property:

> "'Property':… (d) Does not include:… 3. A loyalty card"

**source**: [Ky. Rev. Stat. § 393A.010(24)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ky/title-xxxiv-descent-wills-and-administration-of-decedents-estates/ky-rev-st-sect-393a-010/)

that same paid-for-vs-earned line is the settled national test — the loyalty carve-out turns on
whether the holder paid money:

> "Most of the new state laws also include…exemptions for loyalty cards, provided the cardholder did
> not pay money for the card."

**source**: [unclaimed property — gift, payroll, loyalty and stored-value cards — Eversheds Sutherland](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/unclaimed-property-what-s-new-for-gift-66136/)

## the deepest cut: title vs custody

the escheat doctrine itself draws the earned-debt-vs-deposited-value line at the level of **whose
money**. the statute reaches only a card that was **prefunded** by a payment of consideration — a
customer's own value the issuer merely holds:

> "'Stored-value card':… (b) Includes a: 1. Record that contains or consists of a microprocessor
> chip, magnetic strip, or other means for the storage of information, which is prefunded and whose
> value or amount is decreased on each use and increased by payment of additional consideration"

**source**: [Ky. Rev. Stat. § 393A.010(30)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ky/title-xxxiv-descent-wills-and-administration-of-decedents-estates/ky-rev-st-sect-393a-010/)

a **deposit** keeps the value the depositor's — the issuer is a mere custodian of a prefunded balance,
and that balance is remittable. a **debt** is the issuer's own money against which it owes a payable —
there is no prefunded, owner-titled fund for a state to claim. the redline is a whose-money line.

the common-law escheat root is narrower still — title passes to the state only where an owner leaves
no taker:

> "Escheat is the passing of an interest in land to the state when a decedent has no will, no heirs,
> or devisees."

**source**: [escheat — Cornell LII (Wex)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/escheat)

## a real program on each side

| side | program | verbatim mechanic | why it sits here |
|------|---------|-------------------|------------------|
| **DEPOSITED VALUE** | Starbucks Card | "The dollar value that you load onto your Starbucks Card is a prepayment for the goods and services of participating stores." | the holder **loads their own funds** in advance → prepayment held by the issuer |
| **DEPOSITED VALUE** | Starbucks Card (reload) | "You can reload your Starbucks Card in-store or…link a payment method to your Starbucks account…and reload either manually online when you choose or set up auto-reload." | value is **loaded/reloaded with funds** — the prepaid-access core |
| **EARNED DEBT** | Amazon Associates | "the rights and obligations of the parties under Sections 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, and 11 of this Agreement… together with any payable but unpaid payment obligations under this Agreement, will survive the termination of this Agreement." | the earned commission is a **payable the issuer owes**, not a deposit the associate loaded |
| **EARNED DEBT** | ASC 606 loyalty books | "Rewards program liability is also equal to the deferred revenue." / "The revenue gets recognized when points are redeemed." | the point is carried as the issuer's **own liability**, recognized on redemption — a debt, not custodied funds |

**sources**:
[Starbucks Card Terms & Conditions](https://www.starbucks.com/terms/manage-gift-cards/) ·
[Amazon Associates Program Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement) ·
[financial accounting for liability from rewards programs (ASC 606) — TrueLoyal](https://www.trueloyal.com/blog/financial-accounting-for-liability-from-rewards-programs)

note the Starbucks card carries a "no cash out" trait — "The dollar value on your Starbucks
Card is nonrefundable and may not be redeemed for cash." — yet it is still **deposited value**, because
the holder **loaded their own funds**. the cash-out limit does not decide the side; the **separator is
the deposit**, not the redemption path. that is the subtle point: a redemption limit alone does not make
a balance earned debt — a balance a holder funded is still stored value.

## the traits that place a point on each side

each row is a factual trait of the balance; the left column falls on the earned-debt side of the law,
the right on the deposited-value side:

| earned-debt trait | deposited-value trait |
|-------------------|-----------------------|
| the point is **earned** for a contribution | a person **buys, loads, or deposits** value to get the point |
| the point is carried as the issuer's **own liability** (a payable) | the issuer holds a person's funds as a **custody balance** |
| redemption is to **discount / goods** only | a promotional balance is **cashed out** |
| a cash payout is only for **services earned** (a trade debt) | the point can be **transferred / sold / sent** person-to-person |

the florida gift-certificate carve-out draws the top line — a reward given with **no separate charge**
is a loyalty/promotional item, not a purchased (deposited) certificate:

> "if it is provided to the recipient, or to a purchaser for transfer to the recipient, as part of a
> loyalty or promotional program when the recipient does not pay a separate identifiable charge for
> the certificate"

**source**: [Fla. Stat. § 501.95(2)(a)](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0501/Sections/0501.95.html)

## severity

| a point that drifts to "deposited value" triggers… | consequence |
|-----------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| money transmission | FinCEN registration (31 U.S.C. § 5330) + state MSB licensure, surety bonds, exams |
| stored value / prepaid access | prepaid-access + Reg E custody, segregation, and disclosure duties |
| escheat | balances become abandoned property remittable to the state |

the drift is usually caused by one design choice: a **load/buy/deposit** feature, a **transfer**
feature, or a **consumer cash-out**. each turns the payable into a held balance.

---

## the honest gap

the redline is text-strong but application-untested for a modern points program:

- a **cash-out** redemption is the value that sits closest to the line. it falls on the earned-debt side
  only where the amount is earned **for services** (a trade debt owed), not where a consumer deposited
  funds. a cash-out is exactly what RUUPA's loyalty-card exclusion names ("redeemed for money or
  otherwise monetized"), so the earned-for-service character is where that path is won or lost.
- the FinCEN "integral to the sale of goods or services" exemption is the strongest money-transmission
  shield for the earned-debt read, but its scope is fact-specific; counsel should confirm an earned
  point falls inside it.
- state law is not uniform — RUUPA's loyalty-card carve-out is not adopted verbatim everywhere, and a
  minority of states still reach some rewards balances. per-state confirmation is required.

## key takeaways

| question | answer |
|----------|--------|
| what is THE redline? | earned debt vs deposited value |
| the separator? | whose money is it — a person's deposit, or an issuer's incurred liability? |
| what makes a point "deposited value"? | a load / buy / deposit of the holder's own funds |
| what makes it "earned debt"? | it is owed for a contribution — the issuer's own money, a payable that offsets |
| a real program on each side? | Starbucks Card = deposited value; Amazon accrued commission = earned debt |
| does a no-cash-out limit alone decide the side? | no — Starbucks has it and is still stored value; the **deposit** is what matters |
| is it settled? | text is strong; application + per-state confirmation still needed |
