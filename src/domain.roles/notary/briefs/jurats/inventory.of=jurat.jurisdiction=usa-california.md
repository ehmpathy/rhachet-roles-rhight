# inventory.of=jurat.jurisdiction=usa-california

## .what

the California jurat, from the statute. pulled from
`leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` with bhrowser.

California prescribes a jurat form **and** a mandatory boxed disclaimer that no
other state in this inventory requires. it is the most prescriptive of the five.

## .the statutory form

> **Cal. Gov't Code §8202(d)** — "A jurat executed pursuant to this section
> shall be in the following form:"
>
> ```
> ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
> │ A notary public or other officer completing this │
> │ certificate verifies only the identity of the    │
> │ individual who signed the document to which this │
> │ certificate is attached, and not the             │
> │ truthfulness, accuracy, or validity of that      │
> │ document.                                        │
> └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
>
> State of California
>
> County of _______________
>
> Subscribed and sworn to (or affirmed) before me on this
> _____ day of _______, 20__, by _________________________,
> proved to me on the basis of satisfactory evidence to be
> the person(s) who appeared before me.
>
> Seal                            Signature
> ```

## 🚫 §8202 CARRIES NO TITLE

the section reads, in full, as `8202.` followed by subdivisions (a)–(d): **no
heading**, and the word `JURAT` appears nowhere in it. the boxed disclaimer sits
where a title would go. a statute's title is as much a fact as its text — do not
justify a `Jurat` heading on a document by a recollected title (measured: that
recollection was wrong).

## 🎯 the mandatory boxed disclaimer

> **§8202(b)** — "To any affidavit subscribed and sworn to before a notary,
> **there shall be attached a jurat that includes a notice at the top, in an
> enclosed box**, stating: 'A notary public or other officer completing this
> certificate verifies only the identity of the individual who signed the
> document to which this certificate is attached, and not the truthfulness,
> accuracy, or validity of that document.' **This notice shall be legible.**"

three things to notice:

1. **"shall be attached"** and **"shall be legible"** — mandatory, twice.
2. the notice is a **consumer-protection device**. it tells a reader that the
   notary vouched for *who signed*, and expressly not for *whether the content
   is true*. every state's law works this way; California alone makes the
   document say so.
3. §8202(c) softens only the *format*, never the *content*:

> **§8202(c)** — "The physical format of the boxed notice at the top of the
> jurat required pursuant to subdivision (d) is an example, for purposes of
> illustration and not limitation, of the physical format of a boxed notice
> fulfilling the requirements of subdivision (b)."

so the box may be drawn differently. the words may not change, and the box must
still be a box.

## 🎯 the signature must happen in the notary's presence

> **§8202(a)** — "When executing a jurat, a notary shall administer an oath or
> affirmation to the affiant and shall determine, from satisfactory evidence as
> described in Section 1185 of the Civil Code, that the affiant is the person
> executing the document. **The affiant shall sign the document in the presence
> of the notary.**"

this is the crispest statement of the jurat rule in any of the five statutes:
**oath first, then signature, in the notary's presence.** a pre-signed document
cannot receive a California jurat.

it is also why an e-signature is useless here. a typed-name-in-cursive signature
is valid for ordinary e-signature under ESIGN/UETA and is **forbidden** on a
document that needs a jurat.

## .identification

the form's operative phrase is:

> "proved to me on the basis of satisfactory evidence to be the person(s) who
> appeared before me"

"satisfactory evidence" is defined by cross-reference:

> **§8202(a)** — "...from satisfactory evidence as described in **Section 1185
> of the Civil Code**..."

⚠️ **Civ. Code §1185 was not pulled.** the acceptable ID list is therefore not
quoted here. California's list is known to be narrower than Florida's in
practice, but that is an impression, not a verified claim.

## .venue

`State of California` is **pre-printed**; only the county is blank. same shape
as Florida and Texas — the venue names the officer.

## .the date format

`this _____ day of _______, 20__` — the three-part construction, and note the
hard-coded `20__` century.

## ⛔ RON — the article EXISTS and is NOT YET IN OPERATION

California enacted an Online Notarization Act in 2023. **it authorizes no online
notarization today.** hold both facts; either alone gives a wrong answer.

**the article:** Gov't Code ch. 3, **ARTICLE 2. Online Notarization
[8231 - 8231.20]**, *"( Article 2 added by Stats. 2023, Ch. 291, Sec. 8. )"*.
every section in it carries the credit *"(SB 696) Effective January 1, 2024."*

**the gate**, codified in the article's own first section:

> **§8231(b)** — "An online notarization platform **shall not be authorized for
> use** by a notary public in California and a notary public **shall not provide
> online notarization** for any principal pursuant to this article **prior to
> registration with the Secretary of State or prior to completion of the
> Secretary of State's technology project** necessary to implement statutes
> related to online notarization. The Secretary of State shall complete the
> technology project **no later than January 1, 2030**, unless the Secretary of
> State informs the Legislature and the Governor in writing on or before January
> 1, 2029, that the technology project necessary to implement statutes related
> to online notarization is incomplete."

a second, independent gate sits four sections later:

> **§8231.2(d)** — "A commissioned notary public **shall not provide online
> notarization for any principal before the adoption of rules by the Secretary
> of State** pursuant to this section."

and the agency states the operative position in its own words:

> "The new law **does not take effect until the Secretary of State certifies**
> that it has completed the technology project necessary to implement the new
> provisions, **or on January 1, 2030 (whichever comes first)**."
> — Cal. Secretary of State, Notary FAQs

whether the SoS has certified its own project is a fact only the SoS holds, so
the agency page is the right source here.

### 🚩 "Effective January 1, 2024" on every section is a TRAP

the credit line says *when the statute entered the code*, not *when the conduct
it authorizes becomes lawful* — up to six years apart here. **read the
article's first section for its own operative gate before you read any section
for its rule.**

- Ch. 291 is **SB 696**: it added Article 1's heading at Sec. 5, **Article 2 at Sec. 8**, and **Article 3 at Sec. 9**. a new "ARTICLE 1" heading signals an Article 2
- a single-section url (`codes_displaySection.xhtml`) cannot surface Article 2; pull the container (`rule.require.bhrowser-citations`)

## ✅ ARTICLE 3 — recognition of out-of-state acts

the same bill added **ARTICLE 3. Recognition of Notarial Acts [8232 - 8232.4]**,
*"( Article 3 added by Stats. 2023, Ch. 291, Sec. 9. )"* — California's RULONA
recognition provisions, effective January 1, 2024 and **not** gated.

> **§8232.1(a)** — "A notarial act performed in another state shall be afforded
> the same legal effect under the law of this state as if performed by a
> notarial officer of this state, if the act performed in that state is
> performed by any of the following: **(1)** A notary public of that state.
> **(2)** A judge, clerk, or deputy clerk of a court of that state. **(3)** Any
> other individual authorized by the law of that state to perform the notarial
> act."
>
> **§8232.1(b)(1)** — "The signature and title of an individual performing a
> notarial act in another state are **prima facie evidence** that the signature
> is genuine and that the individual holds the designated title."
>
> **§8232.1(b)(2)** — "The signature and title of a notarial officer described
> in **paragraph (1) or (2) of subdivision (a)** **conclusively establish** the
> authority of the officer to perform the notarial act."

⚠️ **the (a)(3) officer gets only prima facie treatment.** (b)(2) names (a)(1)
and (a)(2) and stops. so a notary or a court clerk is conclusively established;
"any other individual authorized by the law of that state" is not.

this is the analogue of **Del. §4324** — same three-item officer list, same
prima-facie/conclusive split, same carve-out of the third item — arranged
differently: California packs both rules into (b)(1)/(b)(2); Delaware splits
them across (b) and (c). slice a container to the section before a needle
search, or parallel neighbors get confused.
