## ⛔ THE HEADLINE: TEXAS HAS NO STATUTORY JURAT FORM

a widely-circulated "Texas jurat" exists. it is not a statute.

| source | covers | jurat form? |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Gov't Code ch. 406 | the office: commission, bond, powers, fees, records | ❌ no forms |
| Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 121 | certificate forms | ❌ **acknowledgments only** |
| Secretary of State educational materials | sample forms | ✅ — but as a **sample** |

the statute says so itself:

> **Tex. Gov't Code §406.008(b)** — the secretary of state shall provide, with
> the commission, materials that include "...(3) **sample forms** for an
> acknowledgment, **jurat**, and verification and for the administering of an
> oath, protest, and deposition..."

so the Texas jurat is a **sample form in an educational packet**, distributed by
the Secretary of State to new notaries. it carries no statutory force.

### the `JURAT` label on the SOS sample sheet is a catalog label

the Texas SOS "sample notarial certificates" sheet labels its jurat block
**`JURAT`** to tell it apart from its neighbors. that label belongs to the
**sample sheet**, not the certificate — executed documents overwhelmingly carry
no such title.

## .what Texas DOES prescribe: acknowledgments

> **Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §121.008** — "SHORT FORMS FOR CERTIFICATES OF
> ACKNOWLEDGMENT. (a) The forms for certificates of acknowledgment provided by
> this section may be used as alternatives to other authorized forms. They may
> be referred to as 'statutory forms of acknowledgment.'"
>
> "(b) Short forms for certificates of acknowledgment include:
> (1) For a natural person acting in his own right:
>
> ```
> State of Texas
>
> County of ____________
>
> This instrument was acknowledged before me on (date) by
> (name or names of person or persons acknowledging).
>
> (Signature of officer)
>
> (Title of officer)
>
> My commission expires: ________
> ```
> "

note **"State of Texas" is pre-printed** here, as in Florida — the venue names
the officer's location.

Texas also keeps a long-form acknowledgment:

> **§121.007** — "Before me ____________ (here insert the name and character of
> the officer) on this day personally appeared ________________, known to me
> (or proved to me on the oath of ________________ or through
> __________________ (description of identity card or other document)) to be
> the person whose name is subscribed to the foregoing instrument and
> acknowledged to me that he executed the same for the purposes and
> consideration therein expressed.
>
> (Seal) 'Given under my hand and seal of office this ________ day of
> ____________, A.D., ________.'"

## .the identification note — and its short-form carve-out

> **§121.005(b)** — "**Except in a short form certificate of acknowledgment
> authorized by Section 121.008**, the officer must note in the certificate of
> acknowledgment that: (1) he personally knows the acknowledging person; or (2)
> evidence of a witness or an identification card or other document was used to
> identify the acknowledging person."

so Texas requires an ID note — but **exempts the short form from it**. contrast
Florida, where the ID basis is a mandatory element of *every* certificate with
no short-form escape (`§117.05(4)(f)`).

## .the notary's powers and fees

> **Gov't Code §406.016(a)** — a notary public may "...(3) administer oaths..."
>
> **§406.024(6)** — "for administering an oath or affirmation with certificate
> and seal, a fee of **$10**"

the fee matches Florida's $10 cap for an in-person act. note §406.024 is a
schedule of *maximum* fees for enumerated acts, which is a different drafting
shape from Florida's single blanket cap.

## .records

> **§406.014(g)** — "A notary public shall retain the records required by
> Subsection (a) until the **10th anniversary** of the date of notarization."

Texas requires a **journal**; Florida does not for standard acts.

## what this means in practice

because no jurat form is prescribed, a Texas notary has **latitude** — and
different notaries exercise it differently. two consequences:

1. **do not pre-print a Texas jurat and expect it to be used.** the notary may
   substitute the SOS sample they were trained on.
2. **but the notary may not choose the ACT for you.** to select which notarial
   act a document needs is the practice of law. bring a document that already
   says which act it needs, and let the notary supply the certificate wording.

## ✅ RON — fully operative since 2018, and it reaches OUT-OF-STATE principals

**Gov't Code ch. 406, SUBCHAPTER C. ONLINE NOTARY PUBLIC** (§§406.101–406.113),
*"Added by Acts 2017, 85th Leg., R.S., Ch. 340 (H.B. 1217), Sec. 3, eff. July 1,
2018."* Texas RON carries **no** deferred-operation gate — contrast California,
whose Article 2 is in the code and authorizes no act until 2030 at the latest.

appearance treats RON as a first-class alternative:

> **§406.005(b)** — a person appears if "**(1)** for a notarization **other
> than an online notarization**, the person physically appears before the notary
> public … and **(2)** for an online notarization, the person appears … by an
> **interactive two-way video and audio conference technology** that meets the
> standards adopted for online notarization under Section 406.104."

for an out-of-state signer:

> **§406.110(a)** — "An online notary public may perform an online notarization
> authorized under Section 406.107 … **regardless of whether the principal is
> physically located in this state** at the time of the online notarization."

so a Texas online notary may notarize for a principal located anywhere. whether
the **destination** honors that act is a separate question answered by the
destination's own recognition statute — see the compare grid.

| provision | what it fixes |
|-----------|---------------|
| §406.102 | the subchapter "applies **only** to an online notarization" |
| §406.105 | apply to the SoS; must first qualify under Subchapter A |
| §406.107 | an online notary may perform **any** §406.016 function online — so a **jurat is available online** |
| §406.108 | a secure **electronic record** of every online act is mandatory |
| §406.111 | fee capped at **$25** per online notarization, on top of §406.024 |

⚠️ §406.104 puts the identity standards **in SoS rule**, not in the statute —
the SoS "by rule shall develop and maintain standards … **including standards
for credential analysis and identity proofing**." those rules were not pulled;
do not state a credential requirement from this brief.
