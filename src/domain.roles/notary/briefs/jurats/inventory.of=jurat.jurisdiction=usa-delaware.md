# inventory.of=jurat.jurisdiction=usa-delaware

## .what

the Delaware jurat, from the statute. pulled from `delcode.delaware.gov` with
bhrowser and reproduced verbatim.

Delaware enacted the **Revised Uniform Law on Notarial Acts** (RULONA) at
29 Del. C. ch. 43, subch. II.

> **§4315** — "This subchapter may be cited as the 'Revised Uniform Law on
> Notarial Acts.'"

RULONA is a uniform act, so these sections track the language of every other
RULONA state.

## ⚠️ Delaware does not say "jurat"

RULONA's vocabulary is different, and a search of the Delaware code for "jurat"
returns no useful hit. the act you want is a **verification on oath or
affirmation**.

> **§4316(5)b** — "'Notarial act' includes taking an acknowledgment,
> **administering an oath or affirmation, taking a verification on oath or
> affirmation**, witnessing or attesting a signature, certifying or attesting a
> copy, and noting a protest of a negotiable instrument."

| everyday word | RULONA term | Delaware § |
|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| jurat | verification on oath or affirmation | §4329(3) |
| acknowledgment | acknowledgment | §4329(1), (2) |
| signature witnessing | witnessing or attesting a signature | §4329(4) |

## .the statutory short form

> **§4329** — "The following short form certificates of notarial acts are
> sufficient for the purposes indicated, if completed with the information
> required by § 4328(a) and (b) of this title:"
>
> **(3) For a verification on oath or affirmation:**
>
> ```
> State of ________________  County of ________________
>
> Signed and sworn to (or affirmed) before me on ____Date
> by ____Name(s) of individual(s) making statement
>
> ________________________________
> Signature of notarial officer            Stamp
>
> [________________________________
> Title of office]
>
> [My commission expires: ________________]
> ```

## 🎯 the capacity split — why an officer's title does NOT go in a jurat

compare two adjacent subsections:

> **§4329(2)**, acknowledgment in a representative capacity —
> "...by ____Name(s) of individual(s) **as (type of authority, such as officer
> or trustee) of (name of party on behalf of whom record was executed)**."
>
> **§4329(3)**, verification on oath or affirmation —
> "...by ____Name(s) of individual(s) **making statement**"

the acknowledgment form has a slot for capacity. **the jurat form has none.**
that is by design: an acknowledgment can certify that someone signed *as an
officer*; a jurat certifies only that a named human swore and signed.

so an officer's title belongs in the **body** of the document and in the
signature caption — never in the jurat.

## ✅ THE PROVISION THAT MATTERS MOST TO OUT-OF-STATE SIGNERS

Delaware does not merely *tolerate* a foreign certificate. it expressly accepts
one, twice over.

**first**, the act itself is recognized:

> **§4324. Notarial act in another state.** — "(a) A notarial act performed in
> another state has the same effect under the law of this State as if performed
> by a notarial officer of this State, if the act performed in that state is
> performed by any of the following:
> (1) A notary public of that state.
> (2) A judge, clerk, or deputy clerk of a court of that state.
> (3) Any other individual authorized by the law of that state to perform the
> notarial act."
>
> "(b) The signature and title of an individual performing a notarial act in
> another state are **prima facie evidence** that the signature is genuine and
> that the individual holds the designated title."
>
> "(c) The signature and title of a notarial officer described in paragraph
> (a)(1) or (a)(2) of this section **conclusively establish** the authority of
> the officer to perform the notarial act."

### ⛔ recognition is NOT conclusive for every foreign officer

(b) is the floor and (c) is the upgrade. (c) names **(a)(1) and (a)(2) only**,
so an **(a)(3)** officer — "any other individual authorized by the law of that
state" — falls back to (b):

| the foreign officer is… | Delaware treats the signature + title as… |
|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** a notary public of that state | ⭐ **conclusive** — §4324(c) |
| **(a)(2)** a judge, clerk, or deputy clerk | ⭐ **conclusive** — §4324(c) |
| **(a)(3)** any other authorized individual | ⛔ **prima facie only** — §4324(b) |

"conclusively" forecloses rebuttal. "prima facie" invites it. same outcome in
the ordinary case; different ground in a contested one.

a commissioned **notary public** is (a)(1), so a Florida RON notary lands in the
conclusive row. the distinction bites only for an officer who is *authorized*
but not commissioned.

§4324 governs recognition of the **ACT**. it says not
one word about whether the **certificate** carries the elements §4328(a)
demands — **(a)(5)**, the commission expiry, among them. that is the axis a
submission actually gets refused on, and it is below.

**second — the foreign FORM is accepted. §4328(c) in full, all four paragraphs:**

> **§4328(c)** — "A certificate of a notarial act is sufficient if it meets the
> requirements of subsections (a) and (b) of this section **and any of the
> following**:
> (1) Is in a short form set forth in § 4329 of this title.
> (2) Is in a form otherwise permitted by the law of this State.
> **(3) Is in a form permitted by the law applicable in the jurisdiction in
> which the notarial act was performed.**
> (4) Sets forth the actions of the notarial officer and the actions are
> sufficient to meet the requirements of the notarial act as provided in
> §§ 4318, 4319, and 4321 of this title or law of this State other than this
> subchapter."

(c)(1)–(c)(3) are *form* tests; **(c)(4)** is a **substance-over-form** escape. a
certificate in no recognized form is still sufficient if it sets out what the
officer did and that satisfies §§4318 / 4319 / 4321.

read together: a certificate executed by a notary of another state, **in that
state's own statutory form**, is sufficient in Delaware. there is no need to
graft a Delaware short form onto a document a Florida or California notary will
sign.

## ⛔ THE PART EVERYONE DROPS — (c) is a CONJUNCTION, not an escape

`(a) and (b)` **and** any of `(c)(1)–(4)`. the four paragraphs of (c) are
alternatives **to each other**, never alternatives to (a) and (b).

so "Delaware accepts the Florida form" is true about the **form** and silent
about the **elements**. a foreign-form certificate still has to carry everything
§4328(a) demands — including **(a)(5)**, the commission expiry.

quote (c)(3) without its lead clause and it reads as an unconditional
acceptance.

## .what every Delaware certificate must carry

> **§4328(a)** — "A notarial act must be evidenced by a certificate. The
> certificate must meet all of the following:
> (1) Be executed contemporaneously with the performance of the notarial act.
> (2) Be signed and dated by the notarial officer and, if the notarial officer
> is a notary public, be signed in the same manner as on file with the
> Secretary of State.
> (3) Identify the jurisdiction in which the notarial act is performed.
> (4) Contain the title of office of the notarial officer.
> (5) If the notarial officer is a notary public, indicate the date of
> expiration, if any, of the officer's commission."
>
> **§4328(b)** — "If a notarial act regarding a tangible record is performed by
> a notary public, an official stamp must be affixed to or embossed on the
> certificate."

note **(a)(5)**: Delaware wants the commission expiration on the certificate.
Florida's element list does not — Florida puts it on the seal instead. a
Florida-form certificate therefore satisfies Delaware only because Florida's
**seal** carries the expiration.

## ✅ the §4328(a)(5) seam — CLOSED for paper, and it is seal-borne

for a **paper** notarization:

> **Fla. Stat. §117.05(3)(a)** — "A notary public seal shall be affixed to all
> notarized **paper** documents and shall be of the **rubber stamp** type and
> shall include the words 'Notary Public-State of Florida.' The seal shall also
> include the name of the notary public, **the date of expiration of the
> commission of the notary public**, and the commission number."

and Florida supplies a fallback for the seal-less case that carries the expiry
too:

> **Fla. Stat. §695.03(2)** — "If the acknowledgment or proof is taken … by or
> before a notary public **who does not affix a seal**, it is sufficient for the
> notary public to type, print, or write by hand on the instrument, 'I am a
> Notary Public of the State of …(state)…, and **my commission expires on
> …(date)…**.'"

### ⛔ but the expiry is ENTIRELY seal-borne — the FL jurat FORM has none

read Florida's own statutory jurat, §117.05(13)(a), element by element:

| the form supplies | |
|---|---|
| State of Florida · County | ✅ |
| the physical-presence / online checkbox | ✅ |
| date, signer name | ✅ |
| notary signature + printed commissioned name | ✅ |
| ID basis | ✅ |
| **commission expiration** | ⛔ **absent** |

so a certificate that follows the Florida form **exactly**, with no seal
impression, satisfies Florida and **fails Delaware §4328(a)(5)**. the expiry is
carried by the seal or by nothing.

### 🚩 and for a REMOTE act the chain is a construction, not a quote

the live case here is a **Florida RON on a Delaware-bound document**, and
§117.05(3)(a) is drafted for "**paper** documents" of the "**rubber stamp**
type." it reaches an online notarization only through:

> **Fla. Stat. §117.265(8)** — "Except where otherwise expressly provided in this
> part, the provisions of **part I** of this chapter apply to an online
> notarization and an online notary public."

§117.05 is in part I, and part II's own seal reference presupposes one rather
than redefining its content:

> **§117.265(7)** — "The electronic notarial certificate for an online
> notarization must include a notation that the notarization is an online
> notarization which may be satisfied by placing the term 'online notary' in or
> adjacent to **the online notary public's seal**."

⚠️ that is a sound construction and it is **not a quote**. §117.021(2), which
governs the electronic signature, requires uniqueness, independent
verification, sole control and tamper-evidence — and says nothing of an expiry.

**✅ the actionable form of this, for the live matter:** when the RON is booked,
confirm the vendor's electronic seal renders the **commission expiration date**.
do not rely on the Florida certificate text to supply it, because it does not.
that is a one-glance check on the returned PDF, and it is the single element a
Delaware filing could be refused on.

## .personal appearance, and the RON exception

> **§4319. Personal appearance required.** — "Except as provided under § 4320 of
> this title, if a notarial act relates to a statement made in or a signature
> executed on a record, the individual making the statement or executing the
> signature shall appear personally before the notarial officer."

§4320 is Delaware's remote-notarization section, and it requires the certificate
to disclose the mode:

> **§4320(i)** — "If a notarial act is performed under this section, the
> certificate of notarial act under § 4328 of this title and the short-form
> certificate under § 4329 of this title **must indicate that the notarial act
> was performed using communication technology**."
>
> **§4320(j)(2)** — a short form is sufficient if it "Is in the form under §
> 4329 of this title and contains a statement in substantially the following
> form: **This notarial act involved the use of communication technology.**"

so Delaware reaches the same place Florida does — the certificate must say
whether the act was remote — but by a **sentence** rather than a checkbox.

## .official stamp

> **§4330** — the stamp must include the notary's name as on file, **"State of
> Delaware"**, and **"My Commission expires on"** with the date.
