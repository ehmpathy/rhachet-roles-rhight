# state.comparison.delaware.wyoming.nevada.texas.florida

## .what

a comparison of the five most popular states for incorporation: Delaware, Wyoming, Nevada, Texas, and Florida. fees are cited to each state's own schedule; where a figure is a **function** (of stock, assets, or an election) it is marked so.

## 🐊 Florida — price the CORPORATION, not the LLC

Florida prices a corporation and an LLC differently on every line. `$125`
formation and `$139` annual report are the **LLC** figures.

| what the schedule says | LLC | **corporation** |
|---|---|---|
| filing fee (required) | $100.00 | **$35.00** |
| registered agent designation (required) | $25.00 | **$35.00** |
| — required subtotal | $125.00 | **$70.00** |
| certified copy + certificate of status (optional) | $35.00 | $17.50 |
| — schedule's printed TOTAL | $160.00 | **$87.50** |
| annual report (with supplemental fee) | $138.75 | **$150.00** |
| annual report received after May 1 | $538.75 | **$550.00** |

the schedule is quoted under `.florida` below. ⚠️ **the late fee is the sharper
half:** a profit annual report filed after May 1 is **$550.00**, not $150 — a
3.7× penalty on one missed date. audit the column header (entity type) before
the cells — every figure can be true and the row still wrong.

## 🌲 Wyoming — the $60 is a FLOOR over an asset-based LICENSE TAX

the schedule's footnote: *"Annual Report License tax is $60 or two-tenths of one
mill on the dollar ($.0002) whichever is greater based on the company's assets
located and employed in the state of Wyoming"* (sourced under `.wyoming` below).

| a flat read | what the schedule says |
|---|---|
| `annual fee \| $60` | **$60 or $0.0002 × WY-situs assets, whichever is greater** |
| `franchise tax \| none` | a **tax on assets**, with a $60 floor |

⚠️ an entity chartered in Wyoming and run from elsewhere has ~$0 Wyoming-situs
assets, so $60 is what it pays. an entity that **operates** in Wyoming does not
get that answer.

## .delaware — the gold standard

### why 67% of Fortune 500 companies choose Delaware

> "More than 67.6 percent of the Fortune 500 companies are incorporated in Delaware."
>
> — [Delaware Division of Corporations](https://corplaw.delaware.gov/why-businesses-choose-delaware/)

### the Court of Chancery

> "Delaware's Court of Chancery is the nation's preeminent business court."
>
> — [Delaware Division of Corporations](https://corplaw.delaware.gov/delaware-court-chancery-supreme-court/)

| advantage | description |
|-----------|-------------|
| no juries | decisions by expert judges, not laypeople |
| speed | complex disputes resolved in days or weeks |
| 200+ years precedent | predictable outcomes based on extensive case law |
| business judgment rule | courts defer to director decisions made in good faith |

> "The judges are experts in corporate law, and their decisions tend to be more predictable than those in other states, partly because the court operates without juries."
>
> — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/incorporating-in-delaware-vs-other-states-differences-and-key-considerations)

### VC expectation

> "Many investors and venture capitalists see incorporation in Delaware as a sign of a business's credibility and stability."
>
> — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/incorporating-in-delaware-vs-other-states-differences-and-key-considerations)

VCs prefer Delaware because:
- standard documents (NVCA templates assume Delaware)
- familiar legal framework
- reduced due diligence friction
- clean exit path (IPO, acquisition)

> "VCs have such a preference for Delaware corporations that they will refuse to invest in corporations not incorporated in the state."
>
> — [Capbase](https://capbase.com/why-you-shouldnt-incorporate-your-company-in-texas/)

### costs

| item | amount |
|------|--------|
| formation | **`*$109.00`** ⭐ |
| franchise tax — **default**, 10,000,000-share charter | ⚠️ **$85,165.00**/year |
| franchise tax — floor, authorized shares (needs ≤5,000 shares) | $175/year (corps), $300/year (LLCs) |
| franchise tax — floor, assumed par value (**elected**) | $400/year |
| franchise tax (max) | $200,000/year |
| registered agent | $50–$300/year 🚩 *unverified* |

⚠️ **the `$175` row is a floor almost no VC-track startup reaches** — they file
10,000,000-share charters and are invoiced $85,165 unless they elect the
assumed-par-value method.

**best for**: VC-track startups, complex cap tables, public company plans

### ⛔ "annual report" is the WRONG TERM for Texas from 2024 onward

- §171.2022 exempts a zero-tax entity from the **initial** (§171.201) and **annual** (§171.202) reports — **not** from the **public information report** (§171.203, PIR / OIR), which stays owed *"regardless of whether the entity is required to pay any tax"*
- an unfiled PIR/OIR can forfeit the right to transact business and make **each officer and director personally liable** (§171.255(a)); a later revival **does not undo it** (§171.255(d))
- the no-tax-due threshold is a **per-report-year** value — a **$2.47 million** statutory base (§171.002(d)(2)) re-indexed by §171.006; take the year's figure from the Comptroller. 🚩 a *"$2.65 million"* sentence attributed to `comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/` is not on that page (0 matches, 3,971 chars)

the statute quotes, the SB 3 carve-out, and the nexus question that precedes all
of it: `accountant` — `franchise-tax/state=texas/hazard.zero-tax-still-owes-a-report`.
the Secretary of State side: `incorporation/foreign-registration/hazard.foreign-registration.state=texas`.

### new business court

> "While Texas recently established a specialized business court, Delaware has had a business court since 1792."
>
> — [GNS Law](https://www.gnslawpllc.com/insights/delaware-vs-texas-incorporation)

### limitations

> "Investors, especially VCs, prefer Delaware corporations due to its well-established business-friendly statutory and case law, whereas Texas likely has ambiguities in business governance practice and contract interpretation."
>
> — [GNS Law](https://www.gnslawpllc.com/insights/delaware-vs-texas-incorporation)

**best for**: Texas-based operations, bootstrapped companies, avoid multi-state complexity

## .why delaware wins for VC-track startups

despite higher costs, Delaware dominates for companies that plan to raise institutional capital:

1. **predictability** — 200+ years of case law means outcomes are known
2. **speed** — Court of Chancery resolves disputes in days, not months
3. **expertise** — judges specialize in corporate law
4. **standard documents** — NVCA templates assume Delaware
5. **due diligence** — VCs know Delaware, reduces friction
6. **exit clarity** — IPO and M&A paths are well-established

> "If you plan on raising from VCs, it's in your best interest to incorporate in Delaware."
>
> — [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/blog/texas-vs-delaware-incorporation)
