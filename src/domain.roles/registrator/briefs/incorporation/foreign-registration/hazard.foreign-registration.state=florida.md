# hazard.foreign-registration.state=florida

## .what

a corporation chartered in one state that **transacts business** in Florida must
obtain a certificate of authority from the Department of State. the penalty is
**all back fees** plus a court-ordered civil penalty of **$500–$1,000 per year
or part thereof**, and the corporation cannot bring a suit in Florida until it
cures.

this duty is **entirely separate** from Florida corporate income tax. different
agency, different test, different penalties — and the statute says so itself.

## ⚠️ the statute forecloses the conflation, in terms

> "(4) This section does **not** apply in determining the contacts or activities
> that may subject a foreign corporation to service of process, **taxation**, or
> regulation under the law of this state other than this chapter."
>
> — [Fla. Stat. §607.1501(4)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/607.1501)

so an entity may sit squarely inside the registration safe harbor below and
still owe Florida income tax. the two chapters are not in conversation.

| | corporate income tax | foreign registration |
|---|---|---|
| agency | **Dept of Revenue** | **Dept of State** |
| the test | "conducting business, earning or receiving income in this state" (§220.11(1)) | "transacting business in this state" (§607.1501(1)) |
| threshold | **lower** | higher |
| what you file | a return, annually | one application for a certificate |
| the sanction | penalties + interest | back fees, $500–$1,000/yr, court bar |

## .the duty

> "(1) A foreign corporation **may not transact business in this state until it
> obtains a certificate of authority** from the department."
>
> — Fla. Stat. §607.1501(1)

no grace period appears in the text. the duty attaches at the first act of
business.

## 🎯 "transacting business" is defined only NEGATIVELY

there is no affirmative definition to apply. the analysis runs through the
§607.1501(2) list of activities that do **not** constitute it:

| § | the activity |
|---|--------------|
| (a) | maintaining, defending, mediating, arbitrating, or settling any proceeding |
| (b) | internal affairs — shareholder or board meetings |
| (c) | **maintaining accounts in financial institutions** |
| (d) | offices or agencies **for the transfer, exchange, and registration of securities**, or trustees/depositaries for those securities |
| (e) | selling through **independent contractors** |
| (f) | **soliciting or obtaining orders**, by mail or through employees or agents, **if the orders require acceptance outside this state** before they become contracts |
| (g) | creating or acquiring indebtedness, mortgages, or security interests |
| (h) | securing or collecting debts; holding property so acquired |
| (i) | **transacting business in interstate commerce** |
| (j) | an **isolated transaction** completed within 30 days, not one of a repeated series |
| (k) | owning/controlling a subsidiary; voting its shares |
| (l) | owning a limited partnership interest, absent management or control |
| (m) | **owning, protecting, and maintaining, without more, real or personal property** |

### ⛔ read what the list does NOT contain

the harbor covers an office kept **for securities transfer** (d) and property
owned **"without more"** (m). it does **not** cover an office the entity
actually runs its business from.

so an entity that works out of a Florida address is outside the harbor on its
face. and clause (i) does not rescue it — that clause protects the *interstate
character of a transaction*, not an in-state place of business.

clause (f) is the one most often over-read. it protects **solicitation** whose
orders "require acceptance outside this state." it is about where a contract is
formed, not about where the seller sits.

and the list is a floor, never a ceiling:

> "(3) The list of activities in subsection (2) is **not an exhaustive list** of
> activities that do not constitute transacting business within the meaning of
> subsection (1)."

which cuts both ways: an activity absent from the list is not thereby
*included*. §607.1501(3) is why this analysis ends with counsel rather than with
a checklist.

## 💰 the cost

> "(4) A foreign corporation which transacts business in this state without
> obtaining a certificate of authority is liable to this state **for the years or
> parts thereof** during which it transacted business… in an amount equal to
> **all fees and penalties that would have been imposed** by this chapter…
> In addition to the payments thus prescribed, the foreign corporation may, to
> the extent ordered by a court of competent jurisdiction, be liable for a
> **civil penalty of not less than $500 but not more than $1,000 for each year
> or part thereof** during which it transacts business in this state without a
> certificate of authority."
>
> — Fla. Stat. §607.1502(4)

two components, and the second is capped only by a range:

```
1. all fees and penalties that WOULD HAVE BEEN imposed  -> back annual reports
2. a civil penalty, $500-$1,000 per year or part        -> court-ordered
```

⚠️ **"year or part thereof"** is a round-up, exactly as in Texas §9.054(b). an
entity present from mid-2020 through 2026 counts **seven** years, not six.

⚠️ **do not compute this by hand** — per `rule.forbid.hand-computed-figures`.
component 1 requires the §607.0122 fee schedule applied per year, component 2 is
a range rather than a figure, and the round-up is precisely the step a hand
estimate gets wrong by a whole year.

⚠️ component 2 is **"to the extent ordered by a court"** — it is not an
automatic assessment at the counter. component 1 is collected by the Department.
they are different mechanisms and should not be quoted as one number.

## .the court bar

> "(1) A foreign corporation transacting business in this state or its successors
> **may not prosecute or maintain an action or proceeding in this state** until
> it has obtained a certificate of authority…"
>
> — Fla. Stat. §607.1502(1)

and the cure is complete — the bar lifts on registration ("**until** it has
obtained"). a court may also stay a proceeding while it decides whether a
certificate was required (§607.1502(3)).

⚠️ §607.1502(8): an unregistered corporation that transacts business here
**appoints the secretary of state as its agent for service of process**. so the
absent registration does not make the entity unreachable — it makes it reachable
without its knowledge.

## ✅ what an absent certificate does NOT do

> "(5) The failure of a foreign corporation to have a certificate of authority to
> transact business in this state **does not impair the validity of any of its
> contracts, deeds, mortgages, security interests, or corporate acts** or
> **prevent the foreign corporation from defending** an action or proceeding in
> this state.
>
> (6) A shareholder, officer, or director of a foreign corporation **is not
> liable for the debts, obligations, or other liabilities** of the foreign
> corporation solely because the foreign corporation transacted business in this
> state without a certificate of authority."

so contracts stay good, the entity can still defend itself, and the **liability
shield holds**.

> 🚩 **contrast this with the tax side.** an unregistered entity keeps its shield
> under §607.1502(6). the analogous Texas comparison is sharper still: an entity
> that skipped a Texas franchise-tax **report** can lose its shield under Tex.
> Tax Code §171.255(a) — officers liable "as if… a partner and the corporation
> were a partnership."
>
> the registration duty is the noisier one. the **tax** duty is the more
> dangerous one, and it is quieter, because it can be owed at **$0 tax**.

⚠️ whether Florida has a §171.255-style officer-liability provision on its tax
side **was not researched**. do not infer its absence from its absence here.

## .no five-year cap was found

Texas caps late registration fees at five years for an entity that produces a
clean Comptroller status. **no analogous cap appears in the Florida sections
pulled here.**

⚠️ that is an **absence in the sections read**, not a confirmed absence in
Florida law. §607.1502 and §607.0122 carry no cap; a cap could live in a
Department rule or a policy statement that was not pulled.

### ⚠️ an absent FEE cap does not mean an absent sequence constraint

| the absence | verdict |
|---|---|
| a **late-registration-FEE cap**, the §607.1502(4) analogue | ✅ none found; the tax lever below does not reach it |
| a **sequence constraint** on the revenue side | ⛔ Florida has one, and it is statutory |

the tax-side cap is in the limitations statute itself, and it turns on **who
moves first**:

> "5. **At any time** after the taxpayer … **failed to file a required
> return** … **except that** … the limitation prescribed in subparagraph 1.
> applies **if the taxpayer disclosed in writing the tax liability to the
> department before the department contacts the taxpayer**"
>
> — [Fla. Stat. §95.091(3)(a)5](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/95.091)

so a non-filer is assessable with **no time limit**, and one move — a written
disclosure that lands before the Department makes contact — restores the 3-year
cap.

⭐ **an absence established in one body of law is not an absence in another** —
§607 is the corporations code; this cap lives in ch. 95 and the Department's
rules.

## .the practical order of work

```
1. CHECK      sunbiz for a prior registration -- do not presume an absence
2. TAX        the Dept of Revenue side: back returns, and read the Voluntary
              Disclosure terms BEFORE anything is filed   (accountant brief)
3. REGISTER   application for a certificate of authority  (this brief)
```

⚠️ **step 2 before step 3 is statutory, and the wrong order is expensive.**
§95.091(3)(a)5 keeps the 3-year cap only for a taxpayer who disclosed **before**
the Department made contact, and an online registration is a first move. read
the lever brief before step 2 or 3 — its channels and its eight required items
are what keep the cap alive.

## .enforcement

- a registration question answered without the §607.1501(2) list = **blocker**
- an in-state office in active use treated as within the harbor = **blocker**
- clause (f) or (i) read as protecting an in-state place of business = **blocker**
- a §607.1501(2) safe-harbor result read as settling **taxation** = **blocker**
  (§607.1501(4) forecloses it expressly)
- a late-fee exposure computed by hand = **blocker**
  (`rule.forbid.hand-computed-figures` — the "year or part thereof" round-up)
- the §607.1502(4) civil penalty quoted as an automatic assessment = **blocker**
  (it is "to the extent ordered by a court")
- an absent registration described as a break in the shield = **blocker**
  (§607.1502(6) says otherwise)
