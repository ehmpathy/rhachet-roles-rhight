# define.why-delaware-wants-a-zero-shares-affidavit

## .what

when a corporation elects the Assumed Par Value Capital Method to recompute an inflated franchise tax assessment, Delaware's Franchise Tax section commonly conditions acceptance on two documents:

| document | fact it evidences | already seen by a third party? |
|---|---|---|
| signed copy of federal Form 1120 | total gross assets | yes — the IRS |
| notarized affidavit of zero issued shares | issued share count | no — nobody |

this brief explains why the second must be **sworn**, what weight the oath carries, and what the affidavit does *not* accomplish.

Delaware's own template reads, verbatim (typos preserved):

> REQUIRED — THE AFFIDAVIT MUST BE ON COMPANY LETTERHEAD & SIGNED BY OFFICER AND NOTORIZED. IF THE COMPANY HAS MORE THEN ONE STOCK PERIOD, THEN YOU WILL NEED TO ADD LINES FOR THE ADDITIONAL ASSETS AND DATES.
>
> I ____ (officer Name) certify that to the best of my knowledge and under penalty and perjury, the total issued shares of ____ (company name) file # ____ at ____ (date) were ____. The company has never issued shares.
>
> Respectfully Submitted: Name, Title of Officer / ____
> Notary Public: My commission expires on ____.

### ⛔ the STATEMENT is statutory; the NOTARIZATION is the agency's own condition

the recomputation is not a favor the agency grants. § 502(c) makes it an **election the corporation exercises**:

> "the amount so fixed by the Secretary of State **shall stand as the basis of taxation** under the provisions of this chapter **unless the corporation shall thereafter elect** to compute the franchise tax in the manner prescribed by § 503(a)(2) of this title **by filing the annual franchise tax report and complying with the provisions of § 503(b)** of this title."
>
> — [8 Del. C. §502(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c005/index.html) · source of record: `refs/de-title8-ch5-franchise-tax.txt`

and § 503(b) names what that election must carry — **a statement**, and no more specific form:

> "Unless a corporation shall submit to the Secretary of State, at the time of filing its annual franchise tax report, **a statement setting forth the number of shares of each class of stock actually issued**, if any, **and the amount of the total gross assets** of the corporation, as of the nearest date on which the amount is obtainable"
>
> — [8 Del. C. §503(b)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c005/index.html) · source of record: `refs/de-title8-ch5-franchise-tax.txt`

| the requirement | its source | negotiable? |
|---|---|---|
| a statement of **issued shares** | § 503(b) — statute | ⛔ no |
| a statement of **total gross assets** | § 503(b) — statute | ⛔ no |
| that the statement be **notarized** | the Division's template, above | ⚠️ a practice, not a statute |
| that it sit on **company letterhead** | the Division's template, above | ⚠️ a practice, not a statute |

- the affidavit is a **form** the Division chose for a statement the statute already demands
- do not skip the notarization — a clerk will bounce a bare letter, and the argument costs more than a notary
- know that the **substance** is statutory and the **ceremony** is the counter's, so you can tell a requirement from a preference when they conflict
- ⚠️ send **both** figures. a statement short one does not bounce — the year is **re-priced** under paragraph (a)(1), the Authorized Shares Method. see `define.amended-annual-report.state=delaware`

## .why

the franchise tax turns on three numbers. Delaware can verify two of them itself.

| input | source of truth | can the state check it independently? |
|---|---|---|
| authorized shares | the certificate of incorporation, on file with the Division of Corporations | **yes** — it is the state's own public record |
| total gross assets | "total assets" on U.S. Form 1120, Schedule L | **partly** — 8 Del. C. § 503(i) lets the Secretary of State demand a true and correct copy |
| **issued shares** | the corporation's private stock ledger | **no** — the state holds no registry of issuances |

the affidavit plugs the third row. it substitutes legal jeopardy for verification the state cannot perform.

## .what an affidavit is

> "a sworn statement a person makes before a notary or officer of the court"
>
> — [Cornell LII, *affidavit*](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/affidavit)

the affiant asserts, per that same source, "that certain facts are true to the best of that person's knowledge."

| attribute | unsworn letter | affidavit |
|---|---|---|
| form | prose, any format | first-person statement of fact |
| oath | none | oath or affirmation before a notarial officer |
| signer identity | asserted | verified by the notary |
| falsehood exposure | fraud claims only, hard to prove | perjury / false-statement statutes attach directly |
| evidentiary posture | correspondence | testimonial declaration |

the relevant Delaware notarial act is a **verification on oath or affirmation** (a jurat):

> "A declaration, made by an individual on oath or affirmation before a notarial officer, that a statement in a record is true."
>
> — 29 Del. C. § 4316(15), via [Delaware Code Online, Title 29 Ch. 43](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title29/c043/sc02/index.html)

an **acknowledgment** only declares that the signer signed; it carries no oath on the contents. a zero-shares affidavit needs the jurat.

## .why sworn matters

### the criminal hook

| statute | offense | grade | trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 Del. C. § 1221 | perjury in the third degree | class A misdemeanor | "A person is guilty of perjury in the third degree when the person swears falsely." |
| 11 Del. C. § 1222 | perjury in the second degree | class F felony | false statement "(1) Made in a written instrument for which an oath is required by law; and (2) Made with intent to mislead a public servant in the performance of official functions; and (3) Material to the action, proceeding or matter involved." |
| 11 Del. C. § 1223 | perjury in the first degree | class D felony | false statement "consists of testimony" and is material |
| 11 Del. C. § 1233 | making a false written statement | class A misdemeanor | false statement in "a written instrument bearing a notice, authorized by law, to the effect that false statements therein are punishable" |

§ 1235 defines "swear falsely" as to "intentionally make a false statement... knowing it to be false or not believing it to be true, while giving testimony or under oath in a written instrument," and defines "oath" to include "an affirmation and every other mode authorized by law of attesting the truth of that which is stated."

— [Delaware Criminal Code, Title 11 Ch. 5 subch. VI](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title11/c005/sc06/index.html)

### the parallel hook inside the franchise tax chapter

> "If any officer or director of a corporation required to make an annual franchise tax report to the Secretary of State shall knowingly make any false statement in the report, such officer or director shall be guilty of perjury."
>
> — 8 Del. C. § 502(b), via [Delaware Code Online, Title 8 Ch. 5](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c005/index.html)

the annual report is already perjury-backed. the affidavit extends that posture to an out-of-cycle correction.

### the inversion

the exposure **is** the point. Delaware does not purchase the founder's honesty — it purchases the founder's willingness to stake a perjury exposure on the number.

⚠️ inferred, unconfirmed: which charge would attach to a false zero-shares affidavit. § 1222 requires "a written instrument for which an oath is required by law"; whether an oath required by administrative practice qualifies is unsettled. § 1221 and § 1233 have lower thresholds and appear reachable regardless.

## .why the state cannot just take the taxpayer's word

### the incentive asymmetry

| number | direction that lowers tax | who benefits from the lie |
|---|---|---|
| authorized shares | fewer | taxpayer — but the certificate is public, so the lie is instantly detectable |
| total gross assets | lower | taxpayer — but Schedule L is already sworn to the IRS, so the lie is cross-checkable |
| issued shares | **depends on the method** — see below | taxpayer, and no cross-check exists |

there is no public registry of issued shares. the DGCL puts issuances in a private book:

> "(c) For purposes of this chapter, “stock ledger” means 1 or more records administered by or on behalf of the corporation in which the names of all of the corporation’s stockholders of record, the address and number of shares registered in the name of each such stockholder, and all issuances and transfers of stock of the corporation are recorded in accordance with § 224 of this title."
>
> — [8 Del. C. §219(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc07/index.html)
> · source of record: `refs/de-title8-ch1-sc07-meetings.txt`

the state never sees that book unless it demands it.

### the statutory architecture

| statute | power |
|---|---|
| 8 Del. C. § 504(d) | "The Secretary of State has power to inquire into the truth or falsity or accuracy of every report required to be filed to carry out this chapter" — and may "require the production of the books of any corporation" and "swear or affirm and examine witnesses in relation thereto" |
| 8 Del. C. § 503(i) | "The Secretary of State may at any time require a true and correct copy of such schedule to be filed" (the Form 1120 Schedule L) |
| 8 Del. C. § 503(f) | "The Secretary of State may require the filling of a supplemental affidavit stating fully the pertinent facts upon which the claim for one-half rate is based." |
| 8 Del. C. § 505(c) | "If the Secretary of State determines the tax, interest and/or penalties fixed by the Secretary or taxes paid are excessive or incorrect, in whole or in part, the Secretary shall resettle the same and adjust the assessment" |

— all via [Delaware Code Online, Title 8 Ch. 5](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c005/index.html) and [FindLaw § 503](https://codes.findlaw.com/de/title-8-corporations/de-code-sect-8-503/)

read together: § 504(d) lets the state demand proof, § 503(i) names the Form 1120, and § 505(c) is how the ~$191,652 gets knocked down once the state is satisfied.

⚠️ inferred, unconfirmed: the § 503(f) supplemental affidavit is tied to the **one-half rate** claim for a corporation that "has not been engaged in any of the business activities for which it was granted a certificate of incorporation" — not to a zero-issued-shares claim. no statute or regulation mandates a zero-issued-shares affidavit; it appears to be **administrative practice** built on § 504(d).

## .authorized vs issued vs outstanding

**authorized ⊇ issued ⊇ outstanding.** authorized is public (the charter); issued
and outstanding are private (the ledger). 10,000,000 authorized with 0 issued is
lawful, and the tax figure for issued **includes treasury shares**. the three
counts, the § 152 / § 153 act of issuance, and the Division's quote:
`define.authorized-vs-issued-vs-outstanding-shares.md`.

## .why THIS specific fact — the two tax methods

Delaware offers two computations and the corporation may pay the lower. the
rate tables, the six steps, and the §503(a)–(c) quotes live in
`define.authorized-vs-issued-vs-outstanding-shares.md` § *.the two Delaware
franchise tax methods*; this section keeps only why the **issued** count matters.

### method 1 — authorized shares

**issued shares do not appear in this method.** it is Delaware's default, and it produced the ~$191,652 assessment.

✅ 10,000,000 authorized shares → **$85,165.00 per year**: `rhx franchise.tax.delaware --authorized 10000000 --par 0.0001 --issued 8000000 --assets 0` prints `method A · authorized shares §503(a)(1) → $85,165.00 / year`.

⚠️ **still open:** the **$191,652** total. two delinquent years plus the $200/year penalty plus interest lands near it, but the state's year-by-year breakdown was never confirmed, and a single-March-1 accrual understates whenever §504(a) installments apply. test it, and ask the Franchise Tax section for the itemization:

```sh
rhx franchise.tax.delaware.accrue --tax 85165 --years 2022,2023 --fit 191652.43
```

- measured: best accrual 2023-11-01 → tax $170,330.00 + penalty $400.00 + interest $20,184.10 = **$190,914.10**, a **residual of $738.33**
- the skill flags it: *"residual over $500 — the statute does NOT reproduce this bill."*
- ⇒ "lands near it", never "reproduces". the itemization is the only settler

### method 2 — assumed par value capital

step 1 is total gross assets ÷ **total issued shares**. **issued shares sit in the
denominator** — that is the whole reason Delaware cares about the number. run
`rhx franchise.tax.delaware` for the figure, never the table
(`rule.forbid.hand-computed-figures`).

## .what zero issued shares actually does to the math

| issued shares | assumed par (assets ÷ issued) | direction of the tax |
|---|---|---|
| many | small | **down** — toward the $400 floor |
| few | large | **up** |
| one | assets × 1 | very large |
| **zero** | **undefined — division by zero** | the method does not evaluate |

**fewer issued shares makes method 2 worse, not better.**

> "If you have a large number of authorized shares, but you've issued only a tiny number of them, you could end up owing a large tax bill—even if your gross assets are relatively small."
>
> — [Capbase](https://capbase.com/no-your-startup-doesnt-owe-thousands-of-dollars-in-delaware-franchise-tax/)

at exactly zero the state's own calculator refuses to run:

> "If the total gross assets and/or issued shares equal zero, then please contact Franchise Tax at (302) 739-3073, option 3."
>
> — [Delaware Division of Corporations, Franchise Tax Calculator & Disclaimer](https://corp.delaware.gov/taxcalc/)

**confirmed:** zero issued shares breaks method 2; it does not reduce tax under it.

**⚠️ inferred, unconfirmed:** what Delaware substitutes. no published rule, form, or FAQ states the computation for a never-issued corporation. any confident formula online for this case is a guess.

## .what relief the zero-issued fact plausibly unlocks

| path | statute | what zero-issued establishes | confirmation status |
|---|---|---|---|
| discretionary resettlement | 8 Del. C. § 505(c) | that the report's stock figures were wrong, so the assessment is "excessive or incorrect" and the Secretary "shall resettle" | ✅ statute confirmed; ⚠️ its application to this fact pattern is inferred |
| one-half rate for a dormant corp | 8 Del. C. § 503(f) | that the corp "has not been engaged in any of the business activities for which it was granted a certificate of incorporation" — zero issuance is strong corroboration but is *not* the statutory test | ✅ statute confirmed; ⚠️ that Delaware treats the zero-shares affidavit as the § 503(f) supplemental affidavit is inferred |
| retroactive reduction of authorized shares | 8 Del. C. § 241 | that "the corporation has not received any payment for any of its stock" — the precondition for a § 241 amendment, which relates back to inception | ✅ statute confirmed; ⚠️ that the Franchise Tax section will recompute *prior* years off a § 241 amendment is inferred and no source was found either way |

a majority of directors may adopt a §241 amendment — no shareholder vote, since there are no shareholders — and it relates back to the original effective date, **except** as to *"those persons who are substantially and adversely affected"*, for whom it runs from the filing date (§241(b), quoted with the §242 contrast in `define.authorized-vs-issued-vs-outstanding-shares.md` § *the retroactivity question*; bytes at `refs/de-title8-ch1-sc08-amendments.txt`).

**⚠️ inferred, unconfirmed — the highest-value open question here:** is the State, as holder of an assessed franchise tax for a closed year, such a person? a cut from 10,000,000 authorized at inception would erase an $85,165/yr assessment; if the carve-out reaches the State, the amendment reaches no closed year. no Delaware authority settles it either way. **put it to the Franchise Tax section directly, on paper.**

### ⛔ § 503(e) sentence 2 — read it before you price any retroactive fix

§ 503(e) (quoted whole in `howto.reinstate-void-delaware-corp.md` § *§ 204 — the certificate of validation, and why it lands on STEP 4*) is a one-way ratchet on a § 204 certificate of validation:

| the move | direction |
|---|---|
| **cannot reduce** prior-period tax | ⛔ down is barred |
| **no refund** | ⛔ money already paid is gone |
| additional tax computed at **current rates** | ⚠️ **up is repriced to today** |

### ⚠️ it names § 204, not § 241

§ 503(e) binds a certificate of **validation** (§ 204). § 241 is a certificate of **amendment** before stock issuance.

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| § 503(e) forecloses § 241 relation-back | ❌ **no.** it does not name § 241 |
| § 503(e) is *silent on relation-back* | ❌ **no.** it is directly on point, for § 204 |
| the legislature knows how to bar retroactive franchise-tax relief, and did so expressly for one instrument | ✅ **yes** — which is why its silence as to § 241 is a question to ask, never an answer to assume |

- § 312(g) re-opens the revival cap for a validation that *"relates to a time **during which**"* the charter was void — keyed on the **act's** date
- § 503(e) reaches *"any period **prior to the filing** of such certificate"* — keyed on the **certificate's** date, so broader
- ⛔ three provisions bar retroactive relief through a § 204 validation: § 503(e), § 312(g), and § 505(a) (*"No refund shall be granted in connection with the filing of a certificate of correction under § 103(f) ... or ... validation under § 204."*). each closes a different door

revival under 8 Del. C. § 312 requires payment of "all franchise taxes, penalties and interest thereon due at the time its certificate of incorporation became forfeited or void" — or three times the current annual tax if void more than 5 years. so the assessment must be corrected *before* revival.

## .the common solo-founder error

founders routinely copy the authorized number into the adjacent issued box.

| what the founder enters | effect on method 2 | effect on the record |
|---|---|---|
| issued = 10,000,000 (copied from authorized) | assumed par = assets ÷ 10,000,000 ≈ 0 → tax floors at $400 | creates a sworn state record that 10M shares were issued |
| issued = 0 (correct, in a later year) | method 2 undefined | contradicts the prior year → audit flag |

> "Another mistake we see is incorrect share information being listed. An example is 10,000,000 shares showing issued one year on the annual report and then 0 shares showing issued the next year. This will trigger the state to request additional information about share issuance or require a correction to be filed. Either of these issues can affect the standing of your corporation. Clients may also claim Zero gross assets in multiple years. This will also trigger additional scrutiny and may require corrections to be filed."
>
> — [Harvard Business Services, Common Mistakes on the Delaware Annual Report](https://www.delawareinc.com/blog/common-annual-report-mistakes/)
> · source of record: `refs/pulledAt=2026-08-04.hbs-common-annual-report-mistakes.txt`

the mis-entry buys a cheap $400 year and plants a self-contradictory sworn record. when the corp later goes delinquent and the state falls back to the authorized-shares default, it will not accept a bare correction — hence the affidavit.

⚠️ inferred, unconfirmed: that causal chain is a reconstruction. Delaware publishes no internal trigger logic.

## .why letterhead + officer signature + notary

| requirement | what it independently establishes | what fails without it |
|---|---|---|
| **company letterhead** | corporate provenance — the statement issues from the entity, not from a private individual who happens to know about it | the state gets a personal claim about a third party's books, with no corporate act behind it |
| **signature by an officer** | authority to bind — an officer is the corporation's agent for statements to the state, which mirrors 8 Del. C. § 502(b)'s "officer or director" perjury hook and the federal rule that Form 1120 must be signed by "The president, vice president, treasurer, assistant treasurer, chief accounting officer; or Any other corporate officer... authorized to sign" ([IRS Instructions for Form 1120](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120)) | a bookkeeper, spouse, or advisor could attest, with no corporate authority and no officer-level perjury exposure |
| **notarization (jurat)** | identity verification and the oath — the notary confirms personal appearance (29 Del. C. § 4319) and satisfactory evidence of identity (29 Del. C. § 4321), and administers the oath that converts the text into sworn testimony | the state cannot tie the signature to a human it can later prosecute; without the oath there is no perjury hook at all |

the template's per-period lines ("IF THE COMPANY HAS MORE THEN ONE STOCK PERIOD, THEN YOU WILL NEED TO ADD LINES FOR THE ADDITIONAL ASSETS AND DATES") match § 503(e) **sentence 1**'s proration of mid-year changes in authorized stock. sentence 2 is the § 204 ratchet above — a different rule.

## .what this does NOT do

| misconception | reality |
|---|---|
| "a notary verified my statement is true" | **false.** a notary verifies who signed and that an oath was administered. Delaware's notarial act statute expressly preserves challenges: "The validity of a notarial act under this chapter does not prevent an aggrieved person from seeking to invalidate the record or transaction that is the subject of the notarial act." — 29 Del. C. § 4337 |
| "the affidavit is independent evidence" | **false.** it remains the affiant's own uncorroborated claim. its value is the jeopardy attached, not a second source |
| "the affidavit binds Delaware" | **false.** § 505(c) makes resettlement a determination by the Secretary. the affidavit supports the claim; it does not compel a specific number |
| "'to the best of my knowledge' softens it" | **partly.** that qualifier goes to your state of mind, not to your diligence. under 11 Del. C. § 1235 you swear falsely if you affirm a statement "not believing it to be true" — willful blindness is not a shield |
| "the affidavit fixes the certificate" | **false.** it corrects the tax record. it does not reduce authorized shares. that needs a separate amendment (§ 241 if no payment was ever received for stock, § 242 otherwise) |
| "this ends the matter" | **false.** revival still needs the § 312 certificate and payment of the corrected back taxes |

## .how to verify before you swear

do not sign from memory. founder stock, an advisor grant, or a service-provider issuance may exist that the founder forgot.

| artifact | where to look | what its presence means | what its absence means |
|---|---|---|---|
| stock certificates | corporate minute book, safe, formation-service portal, or a lawyer's file | shares were issued | weak signal — Delaware permits uncertificated shares, so absence proves little |
| board consent that authorizes issuance | minute book, e-signature archive, formation-service portal | issuance was authorized under 8 Del. C. § 152 | ⚠️ **weak — the row most likely to mislead.** a formation service executes this consent by e-signature at inception and files it in a portal the founder has not opened since. see the trap below |
| stock purchase / subscription agreement | same, plus email archive at inception | shares were sold, and for what consideration | ⚠️ **weak, same mechanism** — generated and e-signed by the same service |
| proof of payment for the stock | bank statements, cancelled check, wire record at inception | consideration was paid → § 241 is unavailable | ⚠️ **weak at nominal par.** at `$0.00001` the consideration for 8,000,000 shares is a two-figure amount, often part-paid in IP; a bank search misses it either way |
| 83(b) election | your own tax records, certified mail receipt, or IRS [Form 15620](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f15620.pdf) | restricted stock was actually transferred to you — an 83(b) presupposes an issuance | supportive of zero, not conclusive; an 83(b) is not required for fully vested shares |
| cap table | Carta / Pulley / spreadsheet / the § 219(c) stock ledger | the authoritative record — § 219(c) makes the ledger *"the only evidence as to who are the stockholders"* ⚠️ **for the vote and inspection rights only; the sentence ends *"entitled by this section to examine the list … or to vote"***, so it does not bar other proof of issuance | if no ledger was ever opened, that is itself consistent with zero issuance |
| prior annual reports | [icis.corp.delaware.gov](https://icis.corp.delaware.gov/ecorp/entitysearch/namesearch.aspx) | what you previously told the state, under § 502(b) perjury | you must reconcile any prior nonzero issued figure — the affidavit contradicts it, and you should expect to explain the discrepancy |

- ⛔ an **absence** in the minute book or safe does not discriminate: a formation service produces the consent, the CSPA, and the payment record at inception into a portal, so a founder who searches from memory finds none of them whether or not shares were issued
- measured: the reinstatement demo carries `atlas.founder-stock-board-consent.transform.md` — a § 141(f) unanimous written consent that resolves *"the officers are authorized to sell and issue on behalf of the Company the shares of stock as set forth in Exhibit A"* — plus the `atlas.cspa.*` purchase agreement: **8,000,000 shares issued at inception**
- ✅ one artifact discriminates: the **§ 219(c) stock ledger**. a formation service does not manufacture one, so its *presence* settles the question — an argument from provenance, not from statutory exclusivity. see `define.stock-ledger` § 1

### the balance-sheet cross-check

| Schedule L item | zero-issued corp | shares-were-issued corp |
|---|---|---|
| total assets (line 15, column (d)) | ~$0 | ≥ the consideration paid |
| capital stock | $0 | ≥ aggregate par value |
| additional paid-in capital | $0 | the excess over par |

> "Such total assets and total gross assets shall be those “total assets” reported to the United States on U.S. Form 1120 Schedule L, relative to the company’s fiscal year ending in the calendar year prior to filing with the Secretary of State pursuant to this section."
>
> — [8 Del. C. §503(i)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c005/index.html), second paragraph · source of record: `refs/de-title8-ch5-franchise-tax.txt`

### ⛔ § 503(b) says WHEN; § 503(i) says WHERE — and they are not in conflict

| provision | the asset figure is… |
|---|---|
| § 503(b) | "as of the nearest date on which the amount is obtainable" |
| § 503(i) ¶2 | "those “total assets” reported to the United States on U.S. Form 1120 Schedule L" |

§ 503(i) fixes the **document**; § 503(b)'s "nearest obtainable" is latitude on the **date**. neither licenses a figure from outside a Schedule L.

⇒ **a Schedule L is a precondition.** a year with no filed Form 1120 has no figure the statute will accept, and the § 502(c) election cannot be completed for that year.

### 🚩 the STATUTE and the DIVISION state different year mappings — and only a LATE filer notices

> "Total Gross Assets shall be those “total assets” reported on the U.S. Form 1120, Schedule L (Federal Return) relative to the company’s fiscal year ending the calendar year of the report."
>
> — [corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/) · source of record: `refs/pulledAt=2026-08-26.de-frtaxcalc.txt`

| source | the anchor |
|---|---|
| § 503(i) ¶2 | the fiscal year ending in the calendar year **prior to filing** |
| the Division's page | the fiscal year ending the calendar year **of the report** |

on time, they agree: the 2023 report filed by March 1, 2024 → **FY2023** either way. delinquent, they diverge by the delinquency:

```
§ 503(i), literally  ->  the year prior to FILING   ->  FY2025   🚫
the Division's page  ->  the year of the REPORT     ->  FY2023   ✅
```

⚠️ **do not settle this from the page alone.** confirm with the Franchise Tax section which fiscal year's Schedule L belongs on each amended year before any report is submitted — a wrong-year figure is a wrong figure sworn to, and § 503(b)'s failure mode is a **silent re-price** under the Authorized Shares Method.

Schedule L already carries its own perjury declaration — "Under penalties of perjury, I declare that I have examined this return, including accompanying schedules and statements, and to the best of my knowledge and belief, it is true, correct, and complete." — which is why Delaware asks for the Form 1120 *and* the affidavit together.

- ⚠️ inferred, unconfirmed: line 15(d) as total assets is confirmed from the IRS instructions; the line numbers for capital stock and additional paid-in capital are not. verify against the current-year form
- ⚠️ shares can be issued for non-cash consideration under § 153 (property, or "any benefit to the corporation"), recorded at nominal value
- 🚨 **for a solo-founder C-corp, zero equity is not even corroboration.** at `$0.00001` par, 8,000,000 shares cost a two-figure amount — booked as $80 or $0, the line reads the same as a charter with no issuance
- ⇒ the Schedule L is evidence in **one direction only**: real paid-in capital rules zero issuance out; zero equity rules no case out
- measured: this inference was refuted in a live matter by the ledger (8,000,000 shares issued). the remedy flipped from this affidavit to an attestation of the real count. **get the § 219(c) ledger before you sign anything on this page**

## ⚠️ the formation-service trap — read this before you swear

the most common way a founder signs a false zero-shares affidavit: the corporation was formed through **Stripe Atlas, Clerky, Carta Launch, Firstbase**, or similar, and the service **already completed the issuance** at inception, in a dashboard the founder has not opened since.

the founder remembers "I never issued stock to anyone" — meaning *no outside investor bought in*. **founder stock is stock.**

Stripe's own documentation describes the standard flow ([stripe.com/guides/atlas/equity](https://stripe.com/guides/atlas/equity)):

> "founders who purchase shares at the time of incorporation ... this is generally an exchange of a nominal amount of money (**par value times the number of shares received**) for the shares or an assignment of relevant IP generated prior to incorporation, with founders using Stripe Atlas **purchasing their shares with a combination of money and IP**."

and the same service files the 83(b) ([stripe.com](https://stripe.com/resources/more/how-to-issue-stock-to-founders)):

> "Stripe Atlas allows founders to file an 83(b) tax election ... Atlas will file it for both US and non-US founders with USPS Certified Mail and tracking."

⚠️ **an 83(b) election presupposes that shares were received.** if one was filed, that is strong evidence of issuance.

### the three-element test

| # | element | authority | verbatim |
|---|---------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | board authorizes | [8 Del. C. §152](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc05/index.html) | "The board of directors may authorize capital stock to be issued for consideration consisting of cash, any tangible or intangible property or any benefit to the corporation" |
| 2 | **consideration received** | [8 Del. C. §152](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc05/index.html) | "deemed to be fully paid and nonassessable stock **upon receipt by the corporation of such consideration**" |
| 3 | recorded in the ledger | [8 Del. C. §219(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc07/index.html) | "'stock ledger' means 1 or more records ... in which ... **all issuances and transfers** of stock of the corporation are recorded" |

and the price floor, [§153](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc05/index.html):

> "Shares of stock with par value may be issued for such consideration, having a value **not less than the par value** of the shares so issued"

at $0.00001 par, 8,000,000 shares requires **$80.00** — small enough to forget, large enough to be dispositive.

### what to pull from the formation-service dashboard

| # | document | proves |
|---|----------|--------|
| 1 | Founder Stock **Board Consent** + Exhibit A | element 1 |
| 2 | Founder **Stock Purchase Agreement** | the consideration promised |
| 3 | **proof of payment** or IP assignment | element 2 — the pivot |
| 4 | **stock ledger** / cap table | element 3 |
| 5 | **83(b) election** + certified mail receipt | shares were received |

> ⚠️ **inferred**: a board consent *alone*, with no consideration and no ledger entry, does **not** issue shares — §152 conditions "fully paid" on *receipt*. that is a fine distinction on which to stake a perjury exposure. if the documents are ambiguous, **get counsel** before you sign.

## .who else has hit this — the published analogs

no one has publicly documented the **zero-issued** outcome. the adjacent pattern is well documented.

### analog 1 — Fourscore Business Law (near-exact)

**verbatim** ([fourscorelaw.com](https://www.fourscorelaw.com/resources/delaware-franchise-tax-calculation/)):

> "Let's use a prototypical early stage startup company that has **gross assets of less than $1,000,000**, with **10,000,000 authorized shares, each with a par value of $0.00001 per share** and 3,600,000 shares issued (all common stock).
>
> Under the Authorized Shares Method, the tax due would be **$85,165** (gulp), because the company pays $250 on the first 10,000 authorized shares, plus an additional $85 for every 10,000 authorized after that (999 blocks of 10,000 x $85 = $84,915 + $250 = $85,165).
>
> Under the Assumed Par Value Method, the tax due is **$400**."

### analog 2 — Kruze

10,000,000 authorized @ $0.0001, **8,000,000 issued to founders**, ~$5,000 assets → **"the company will owe the minimum of $400."** ([kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/delaware-franchise-tax/))

| | Fourscore | Kruze |
|---|---|---|
| authorized | 10,000,000 | 10,000,000 |
| par value | $0.00001 | $0.0001 |
| **issued** | **3,600,000** | **8,000,000** |
| gross assets | < $1M | ~$5,000 |
| **result** | **$400** | **$400** |

🔑 the issued count differs by more than 2× and the answer does not move — at low gross assets the numerator erases it. **a founder in this posture may already be entitled to the $400 minimum with the share count already reported.** confirm that before you conclude a zero-shares affidavit is needed at all.

### the documented trigger pattern

([delawareinc.com](https://www.delawareinc.com/blog/common-annual-report-mistakes/)):

> "An example is **10,000,000 shares showing issued one year** on the annual report and then **0 shares showing issued the next year**."
> "This will trigger the state to **request additional information about share issuance** or require a correction to be filed."
> "Clients may also claim **Zero gross assets in multiple years**. This will also trigger additional scrutiny and may require corrections to be filed."
> "Either of these issues can affect the standing of your corporation."

⚠️ **inferred**: the zero-shares affidavit is most likely the "additional information about share issuance" this passage describes — a **substantiation** step, not a tax-method election.

### the right statutory lever for a void corp

🎯 for a corporation already void on back years, **§505(b) is the correct cite** — keyed to the revival certificate rather than the calendar; §505(a) **expires** March 1 of the second year, §505(c) makes resettlement a **duty**, §505(d) gives **60 days** to Chancery. the quotes: `howto.reinstate-void-delaware-corp.md` § *⛔ and if that sum is WRONG, § 505(b) is your only door — it shuts at step 4*.
