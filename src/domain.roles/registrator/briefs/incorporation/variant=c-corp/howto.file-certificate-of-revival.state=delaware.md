# howto.file-certificate-of-revival.state=delaware

the last step of a Delaware revival: the **Certificate for Revival of Charter**
under 8 Del. C. § 312. what it costs, who may file it, whether it can be done
online, and the four things that are not what they look like.

⚠️ not legal advice.

## ⭐ yes, it can be filed ONLINE — and the service INVOICES rather than charges

> "The Delaware Division of Corporations provides a service that allows the
> ability to electronically submit a business entity document for filing or a
> certificate request.
>
> IMPORTANT: This service is for **submission only** of business entity filings
> or certificate/document requests. This service **does not provide for direct
> online filing** or retrieval of business entity documents and certificates.
> The service **does not calculate fees**. If you need information regarding
> fees, please refer to our fee schedule, or contact our office for further
> assistance. Please Note: The fees required for the request **will not be
> charged to the credit card/ACH account upon submission. When the order is
> processed, the charges will be applied. An invoice will be sent with the
> completed request.**"
>
> — DE Division of Corporations, *Document Filing and Certificate Request
> Information*, embedded at
> `refs/pulledAt=2026-08-28.de-document-upload-service.txt`

- you upload the executed certificate; Delaware processes it, applies the charge, and invoices. the card is not hit at submission
- ⇒ where the final number is uncertain, submit and let the Division price the order
- ⚠️ it is a *document upload*: you draft and execute the certificate yourself. the annual-report flow, by contrast, **is** a true online filing inside DCIS eCorp

| the thing | the mechanism |
|---|---|
| amended annual franchise tax report | ✅ **true online filing** — DCIS eCorp computes and takes payment |
| ⭐ the Certificate of Revival | ⚠️ **upload only** — you execute it, they price it, they invoice |

### the hours are NOT the Division's hours

> "This service is available Monday thru Thursday from 7:45am to 11:59pm (EST)
> and on Friday from 7:45am to 10:30pm (EST). This service is not available
> during weekends, State holidays and unexpected closures of the Division of
> Corporations."

⚠️ Friday shuts at **10:30pm**, not 11:59pm. *"unexpected closures"* are posted
as alert banners on the Division's pages — read them before a deadline upload.

### the account is OPTIONAL

> "Users may have the Submitter Information pre-populated on the Document Filing
> and Certificate Request form by creating a My eCorp account."

⇒ an account is a convenience, never a gate. **"To submit a business entity
filing or certificate request, go to our eCorp Business Services and select
'Document Filing and Certificate Request'."**

## ✅ you may file it YOURSELF — no registered agent required

**1. the statute puts the agent nowhere in the chain.** § 312(c) names exactly
two acts, and an agent performs neither:

> "The revival of the certificate of incorporation may be procured **as
> authorized by the board of directors** or members of the governing body of the
> corporation in accordance with subsection (h) of this section and by
> **executing, acknowledging and filing a certificate of revival in accordance
> with § 103** of this title."
>
> — 8 Del. C. § 312(c), embedded at `refs/de-title8-ch1-sc12-revival.txt`

and § 312(h) lets a **sole** director do the authorizing alone:

> "The requirement for authorization by the board of directors under subsection
> (c) of this section shall be satisfied if a majority of the directors or
> members of the governing body then in office, even though less than a quorum,
> **or the sole director or member of the governing body then in office,
> authorizes the revival** of the certificate of incorporation of the
> corporation and the filing of the certificate required by subsection (c) of
> this section.""
>
> — 8 Del. C. § 312(h)

the agent appears only as **content of article 2** — a fact the certificate
states, not a party that files it.

**2. the Division's own form says an officer signs it:**

> "Execution Block - The document must be signed by an authorized officer of the
> corporation pursuant to Section 103 of Title 8. The name of the person must be
> typed or written legibly underneath the signature."
>
> — DE Division of Corporations, *Special Instructions – Revival for Void*,
> embedded at `refs/effectiveAt=2023-08-01.de-revival-void-form.txt`

### ⚠️ the two sources that LOOK like an agent requirement

| what it says | what it actually means |
|---|---|
| the eCorp verification block: *"Please contact your Registered Agent or the Franchise Tax Section at 302-739-3073 option 3 for assistance."* | an **either/or**, offered for **assistance** on a flagged tax year. not a filing requirement, and not about the certificate at all |
| a Franchise Tax clerk: *"If you need assistance please contact the information center or your registered agent as I do not process filings."* | a **channel boundary** — the tax section does not process filings. the sentence is conditioned on *"if you need assistance"* and offers two routes, neither mandatory |

both are **conditional offers of help** with an equal alternative. read either
as a mandate and you hire an agent to do what a sole director may do alone.
(the upload-service page mentions "Registered Agent" only in its nav and
footer, never in its body.)

## ⛔ the $189 is the FEE, and the fee is NOT the bill

the fee schedule states the trap in its own row label:

```
Revival (plus taxes to renew)      $189.00     $50.00     $200.00     $100.00
Revival (exempt)                     $5.00     $50.00     $200.00     $100.00
```
— [DE Division of Corporations, Fee Schedule, revised 2026-08-01](https://corpfiles.delaware.gov/Fee_Schedule/AugustFee2026.pdf),
embedded at `refs/effectiveAt=2026-08-01.de-doc-fee-schedule.{pdf,txt}`

**"plus taxes to renew."** § 312(g) says which taxes, and closes with a sentence
that carves one year *out* of its own cap:

> "Any corporation that revives its certificate of incorporation under this
> chapter shall pay to this State a sum equal to all franchise taxes, penalties
> and interest thereon due **at the time its certificate of incorporation became
> forfeited or void** pursuant to this title; … **No payment made pursuant to
> this subsection shall reduce the amount of franchise tax due under Chapter 5
> of this title for the year in which the revival is effected.**"
>
> — 8 Del. C. § 312(g)

| what | inside the § 312(g) sum? |
|---|---|
| delinquent years, up to the **void date** | ✅ yes — that is the measure |
| ⛔ **the year the revival is effected** | **no** — owed separately, in full |
| ⛔ years **between** the void date and the revival | ⚠️ **not settled by § 312(g)** — see below |
| the $189 filing fee | no — a fee, not a tax |

🎯 **budget three lines, not one.**

### ⭐ § 312(g) is a MEASURE, not a separate assessment

the sum § 312(g) names **is** the delinquent annual reports and their taxes —
there is no distinct "revival assessment" to obtain from a clerk:

> "Before the Certificate can be filed, all taxes due to the State at the time
> the corporation became void must be paid and all applicable Annual Franchise
> Tax Reports must be filed. **The Annual Franchise Tax Reports and Franchise
> Taxes must be filed and paid online at corp.delaware.gov.**"
>
> — DE Division of Corporations, cover letter to the Certificate of Revival of
> Charter, `refs/effectiveAt=2023-08-01.de-revival-void-form.txt`

⇒ pay the back years **through the tax portal**, then file the certificate. the
certificate stage carries the fee and the current year's tax, not a re-billing
of the arrears.

### 🚩 the open question this brief does NOT answer

whether the years **between** the void date and the revival carry their own
report-and-tax duty. § 312(g) measures to the void date and excludes the revival
year; it is silent on the span between. § 510 makes a void corporation's powers
*"inoperative"*, which is not obviously the same as relieving it of § 502's
report duty.

⇒ **do not reason it out.** the account balance in the tax portal states what
Delaware believes is owed, and the upload service's invoice states what it
charges. both are cheaper and more authoritative than an inference.

## ⚠️ the 5-YEAR FLIP — and why the ORDER of filings decides its price

buried mid-sentence in § 312(g), and it reverses the whole calculation:

> "provided, however, that any corporation that revives its certificate of
> incorporation under this chapter whose certificate of incorporation **has been
> forfeited or void for more than 5 years** shall, **in lieu of** the payment of
> the franchise taxes and penalties otherwise required by this subsection, pay a
> sum equal to **3 times the amount of the annual franchise tax that would be
> due and payable by such corporation for the year in which the revival is
> effected**, computed at the then current rate of taxation."
>
> — 8 Del. C. § 312(g)

past 5 years void, the measure stops being *"all the back taxes"* and becomes
*"3× this year's tax."*

⭐ **and "this year's tax" is the § 503 election, which is a variable you
control.** on the standard startup charter — 10,000,000 authorized, par
$0.00001 — the two methods differ by a factor of 213:

| the revival-year report | 3× is |
|---|---|
| filed **with** the § 503(b) assumed-par-value election | 3 × $400 = **$1,200** |
| ⛔ **absent**, so § 503(a)(1) authorized-shares governs | 3 × $85,165 = **$255,495** |

⚠️ figures via `rhx franchise.tax.delaware --authorized 10000000 --par 0.00001
--issued 8000000 --assets 0` — never hand-derived, per
`rule.forbid.hand-computed-figures`. substitute your own charter.

⇒ **for a >5-year revival, file the current year's report WITH the election
before the sum is computed.** the other order costs two hundred times more.

⚠️ *"in lieu of"* cuts both ways: the 5-year alternative can be **cheaper**
than the back taxes it replaces.

## .the certificate itself — five articles

§ 312(d) prescribes the contents; the Division's form numbers them.

| article | § 312(d) | what goes in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (d)(1) | the current name **exactly as it appears in Delaware's records**, and the original name if different |
| 2 | (d)(2) | registered **office** street, city, **county**, zip — and the registered **agent's** name at that address |
| 3 | (d)(1) | the date the **original** certificate of incorporation was filed |
| 4 | (d)(3) | pre-printed: *"was organized under the laws of this State"* — no action |
| 5 | (d)(4)+(5) | the **actual day** the charter became void, plus the by-authority-of-directors statement |

⚠️ § 312(d)(1) names **four** facts where the form shows a blank for two. read
the statute, not only the form:

> "(1) The date of filing of the corporation's original certificate of
> incorporation; the name under which the corporation was originally
> incorporated; **the name of the corporation at the time its certificate of
> incorporation became forfeited or void** pursuant to this title; and the new
> name under which the corporation is to be revived to the extent required by
> subsection (f) of this section;"

the third and fourth come apart from the first two whenever the name changed
mid-life, or must change now — see § 312(f), below.

### 🚩 article 5's date is the one that can bounce the filing

> "5. The actual day the corporation was voided for non-payment of Franchise
> Taxes and/or failure to file a complete Annual Report."
>
> — the form's own Special Instructions

this is **Delaware's record**, not your computation. § 510's mechanism gives a
derivable date — tax due March 1, one year of neglect, mandatory notice by
November 30, void the following March 1 absent cure — but a good-cause extension
moves it later and the unfiled-report prong can bite sooner.

⇒ **ask.** *"what day does your record show the charter became void?"* is one
question to the Franchise Tax Section at (302) 739-3073 option 3, and it fills
the only blank you would otherwise guess. the same holds for article 3's
incorporation date: **the registry's date governs**; a reconstruction is a
hypothesis.

### ⚠️ § 312(f) — the name may not be yours any more

> "If, since the certificate of incorporation became forfeited or void … any
> other corporation organized under the laws of this State shall have adopted
> the same name … or shall have adopted a name so nearly similar thereto as not
> to distinguish it … then in such case the corporation to be revived **shall
> not be revived under the same name** which it bore when its certificate of
> incorporation became forfeited or void pursuant to this title, but shall be
> revived under some other name as set forth in the certificate to be filed
> pursuant to subsection (c) of this section."

⇒ **check name availability before you execute.** a void charter does not hold
its name, and the discovery that it is gone belongs before the notary and the
$189, not after.

### execution

signed by an **authorized officer** per § 103, with the name **typed or printed
underneath** the signature — the form asks for that twice, because an illegible
execution is a rejection.

> "It is important that the execution be legible, we request that you print or
> type the name of the person signing under the signature line."

🎯 **the form carries no notary block.** § 312(c) says *"executing,
acknowledging and filing … in accordance with § 103"*, and § 103 is what
"acknowledging" resolves to — which the Division's own form satisfies with a
signature and a printed name. ⚠️ this brief has not pulled § 103 itself; treat
the absence of a notary block as strong operational evidence rather than a
settled reading.

## ⭐ what revival buys — and the one duty it starts

§ 312(e) is fully retroactive, and says so three times in one subsection:

> "the corporation shall be revived with the same force and effect **as if its
> certificate of incorporation had not been forfeited or void** … Such revival
> shall **validate all contracts, acts, matters and things** made, done and
> performed … during the time when its certificate of incorporation was
> forfeited or void … **as if the certificate of incorporation had at all times
> remained in full force and effect.**"

⇒ every contract signed while void is validated, and property acquired during
the void period vests as though the gap never happened.

### 🚩 § 312(i) — the void period COUNTS toward your annual-meeting clock

almost never mentioned, and it fires the instant revival lands:

> "After a revival of the certificate of incorporation of the corporation shall
> have been effected, the provisions of § 211(c) of this title shall govern and
> **the period of time during which the certificate of incorporation of the
> corporation was forfeited or void … shall be included within the calculation
> of the 30-day and 13-month periods** to which § 211(c) refers."

⇒ a corporation void for two years is, on the day it revives, **two years
overdue** for an annual meeting — and § 211(c) lets any stockholder or director
petition the Court of Chancery to order one. low stakes for a sole founder,
real for anyone with outside holders. ⭐ § 312(h) supplies the cure in advance:
a special meeting held under it *"shall be deemed an annual meeting … for
purposes of § 211(c)."*

## ⛔ one door this filing shuts

8 Del. C. § 505(b) opens *"Prior to the filing of a certificate required by
§ 312(c)"* — so the petition route to contest a franchise-tax assessment closes
at this filing.

⚠️ weigh it honestly rather than fear it: if the account already sits at zero or
in credit after the amended reports, there is no assessment left to contest and
the closed door leads nowhere you wanted to go. if a **disputed** balance
remains, the order matters and § 505(b) should be exercised first.

## .the sequence

```
1. ✅ amended annual reports for every delinquent year, WITH the § 503(b) election
2. ✅ pay the resulting tax through the tax portal        <- this IS the § 312(g) sum
3. ⚠️ confirm the void DATE with the Division              <- fills article 5
4. ⚠️ check NAME availability                              <- § 312(f)
5. ⬜ sole director authorizes; officer executes            <- § 312(h), § 103
6. ⬜ upload to the Document Filing service, or mail with a check
7. ⬜ Delaware processes, charges, and INVOICES
8. ⬜ the current year's franchise tax, separately          <- § 312(g) excludes it
9. ⬜ good standing certificate, if needed                  <- § 502(f)
```
