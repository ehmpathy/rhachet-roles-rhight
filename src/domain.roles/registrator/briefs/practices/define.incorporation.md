# define.incorporation

## .what

incorporation is the act of creating a legal entity separate from its owners.

the entity becomes a "legal person" — it can:
- own property
- enter contracts
- sue and be sued
- incur debt
- pay taxes

all in its own name, distinct from the humans behind it.

---

## .why it exists

### the liability problem

without incorporation, business = owner.

| scenario | without incorporation | with incorporation |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| business debt | owner pays from personal assets | entity pays from entity assets |
| lawsuit against business | owner is defendant | entity is defendant |
| business bankruptcy | owner may go bankrupt | entity goes bankrupt, owner walks away |

### the continuity problem

without incorporation, business dies with owner.

| scenario | without incorporation | with incorporation |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| owner dies | business ends | entity continues |
| owner sells | complex asset transfer | ownership transfer via shares |
| owner retires | business dissolves | new management takes over |

### the capital problem

without incorporation, raising money is personal debt.

| scenario | without incorporation | with incorporation |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| raise investment | personal loan | sell equity (shares) |
| investor risk | tied to owner's life | tied to entity's performance |
| exit for investor | repayment only | sell shares to others |

---

## .how it protects

### the corporate veil

incorporation creates a "veil" between owners and entity.

```
creditors → [corporate veil] → owners' personal assets
              ↓
         entity assets only
```

creditors can only reach entity assets, not owner assets.

### limited liability

owners risk only what they put in.

| investment | risk | potential loss |
|------------|------|----------------|
| $10,000 capital contribution | limited | $10,000 max |
| personal guarantee (avoid!) | unlimited | everything |

### asset segregation

personal and business assets are legally separate.

| asset type | owned by | reachable by business creditors |
|------------|----------|--------------------------------|
| business equipment | entity | yes |
| business bank account | entity | yes |
| owner's house | owner | no |
| owner's savings | owner | no |

---

## .when the veil can be pierced

the protection is not absolute. courts can "pierce the veil" if:

| violation | description |
|-----------|-------------|
| commingling | mixing personal and business funds |
| undercapitalization | starting with inadequate funds to operate |
| failure to observe formalities | no meetings, no minutes, no separation |
| fraud | using entity to defraud creditors |
| alter ego | entity is just a shell for personal dealings |

### how to maintain the veil

- keep separate bank accounts
- hold annual meetings (even if just yourself)
- document major decisions
- maintain adequate capital
- sign contracts as the entity, not personally
- use entity name on all business dealings

---

## .pros

| benefit | description |
|---------|-------------|
| limited liability | owners protected from business debts |
| perpetual existence | entity survives owner changes |
| transferable ownership | shares can be bought and sold |
| credibility | formal structure signals legitimacy |
| tax flexibility | choose taxation method (C-corp, S-corp, etc.) |
| raising capital | can issue stock to investors |
| employee incentives | stock options, equity compensation |

---

## .cons

| drawback | description |
|----------|-------------|
| formation cost | filing fees, registered agent, legal setup |
| ongoing compliance | annual reports, franchise tax, meetings |
| second layer of tax (C-corp) | corp pays tax, then dividends taxed again — ⚠️ **only when distributed**; retained earnings bear the corporate tax and no more |
| formality burden | must maintain records, minutes, resolutions |
| public disclosure | some states require officer/director names |
| complexity | more accounting, legal, and admin overhead |
| piercing risk | veil can be pierced if formalities ignored |

---

## .the tradeoff

incorporation trades **simplicity for protection**.

| aspect | sole proprietor | incorporated |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| setup | none | file articles, pay fees |
| maintenance | none | annual filings, meetings |
| liability | unlimited | limited |
| taxes | simple (schedule C) | complex (1120, K-1s) |
| credibility | informal | formal |
| growth potential | limited | scalable |

the protection is worth it when:
- business has real liability exposure
- business has significant assets to protect
- owners have personal assets worth shielding
- business needs outside investment
- business should outlive its founders

---

## .the fundamental insight

> incorporation separates the person from the enterprise.

the entity can fail without the person failing. the entity can be sued without the person being sued. the entity can die without the person dying.

this separation is what enables risk-taking at scale — the foundation of modern commerce.
