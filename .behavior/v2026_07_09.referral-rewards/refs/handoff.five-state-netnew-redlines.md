# net-new redlines surfaced by the five-state sweep (TX / IN / FL / CA / NY)

> **not legal advice.** informational groundwork for counsel to validate. verify current text before
> any reliance. generic public-law facts only — no applied design.

## .what

a two-state (IN + FL) view of referral-rewards law misses redlines that appear only once the sweep
runs across the five high-population baseline states (TX, IN, FL, CA, NY) per
`rule.require.five-state-baseline`. this note records those **deltas** — the redlines a two-state view
has no analogue for — as neutral statements of where each state draws the line. consumer, criminal,
and privacy law attaches by the **state the consumer is served in**, not the state of incorporation,
so each served market carries its own duty. the strictest of the five per redline sets a defensible
nationwide floor.

full matrices with verbatim cites live in the counselor role briefs under `market/` (named per item).

## the net-new redlines

### 1. CA data opt-in inverts the loyalty default
`Cal. Civ. §1798.125(b)` — where a reward varies price or service level **because** a consumer shares,
sells, or lets the business retain data, that is a "financial incentive" that requires **prior opt-in
consent** (revocable, 12-month cooldown). TX/IN/FL take the **opposite** posture: a bona fide loyalty
program is an express **safe harbor**. NY has no rule. **the line**: a reward priced on a purchase or
referral is outside the rule; a reward priced on a data-share choice triggers CA's opt-in.
- brief: `define.boundary.ccpa-financial-incentive-opt-in`

### 2. CA criminalizes the referral structure and adds a private suit
`Cal. Penal §327` (endless chain — a wobbler, up to 3 years) + `Cal. Civ. §1689.2` (private
rescission + attorney's fees). FL treats contingent referral-selling as a criminal lottery, but CA
adds a **consumer** rescission remedy FL and IN do not. **the line**: a reward for a real end-consumer
sale is outside the endless-chain definition; a reward for the recruitment of the next participant is
inside it.
- brief: `hazard.contingent-reward-referral-selling.five-state` (baseline = CA)

### 3. CA fixed penalties for review-gating
`Cal. Civ. §1670.8` (the "Yelp bill") — $2,500 / $5,000 / $10,000 per violation for a
non-disparagement clause or a penalty imposed for a review. IN and FL have **no analogue** — only
weaker general UDAP + the federal review rule. **the line**: a sentiment-blind, disclosed,
verified-purchaser reward is outside the statute; a reward gated on a positive score is inside it.
- brief: `hazard.review-gating-penalty`

### 4. NY pre-launch registration + bond once chance is present
`N.Y. GBL §369-e` — a prize-**by-chance** with a pool over **$5,000** needs a filing **30 days
before launch** plus a trust account or surety bond equal to the full prize pool; a missed filing is a
class B misdemeanor. FL §849.094 imposes a parallel trust-fund / bond duty. TX / CA / IN have no such
registration gate. **the line**: a guaranteed reward for every qualifier has no chance element and
does not trigger registration; a prize-by-chance does.
- brief: `hazard.sweepstakes-registration-and-bond` (baseline = NY)

### 5. NY chain scheme = a security
`N.Y. GBL §359-fff` recharacterizes a chain distributor scheme as a **security** under the Martin Act
(AG civil + criminal enforcement) — an enforcement doorway IN/FL do not have.
- brief: `hazard.contingent-reward-referral-selling.five-state`

### 6. TX sets the top of the UDAP-teeth range
`Tex. Bus. & Com. §17.50` (DTPA) — **uncapped** treble on economic damages for "knowing" conduct plus
**mandatory** attorney's fees. FL FDUTPA is the weakest of the five (actual damages only). **the
line**: market copy, not just structure, is a review surface, and TX sets the top of the remedy range.
- brief: `ref.udap-remedy-teeth.five-state` (baseline = TX)

### 7. TX has the fastest escheat clock
`Tex. Prop. Code §72.1016` — a 3-year abandonment presumption vs NY's 5. it still spares earned points
(the hook needs the value to be sold / bought / issued for monetary consideration), but it is the
tightest dormancy clock of the five.
- brief: `ref.gift-card-loyalty-consumer-rules.five-state` (escheat baseline = TX; gift-card baseline
  = CA, which bars expiration outright and forces cash-back under $15)

### 8. money-transmitter: two states offer no carve-out
`Fla. Stat. §560` (broad stored-value net) and `N.Y. Banking Law §§640–641` (activity-based scope)
both **lack** the express loyalty / closed-loop carve-out that TX (§152.003), IN (§28-8-4.1-201), and
CA (§2003) write into their stored-value definitions. **the line**: where two states offer no
carve-out, the analysis rests on the two-element transmitter test itself (accept-from-one +
transmit-to-another), which an earned / closed-loop / own-debt structure fails to satisfy in all five.
- brief: `hazard.cashout-money-transmitter-license.CONDITIONAL`

## the universal tripwire

across all consideration-gated regimes — gift-card, escheat, money-transmitter — each carve-out holds
only while the point stays **non-cashable, non-transferable, discount-only**. the instant a point
becomes **cashable, transferable, or sold**, the consideration triggers fire and the strictest served
state's rule attaches (CA on the card lever, TX on the escheat clock, and the money-transmitter
two-element test comes live).

## the strictest-per-redline summary

| redline | strictest of the five | why |
|---------|-----------------------|-----|
| data / privacy opt-in | CA | §1798.125(b) opt-in inverts the TX/IN/FL safe harbor |
| referral criminality | CA | §327 endless chain + §1689.2 private rescission |
| review-gating | CA | §1670.8 fixed penalties; others = federal CRFA only |
| sweepstakes registration | NY | §369-e registration + bond, 30-day, misdemeanor |
| UDAP remedy teeth | TX | §17.50 uncapped treble + mandatory fees |
| escheat clock | TX | §72.1016 3-year abandonment |
