## the verdict

> **is this set safe to publish openly? no.**

with a diagnosis worth more than the verdict:

> **almost none of what is wrong with these files is legal content.**

the leaks fall into four narrow classes, no one of them substantive law:

1. **engagement framing** — first-person and second-person voice, the commissioner's own intake
   questions, repeated use of a phrase that names a paid engagement
2. **process exhaust** — corrections, gate notes, citation-quota arithmetic, repo rule vocabulary
3. **an index that publishes what it withholds** — see finding #3
4. **a handful of imperative sentences** in files that attest they issue no recommendation

⚠️ **the lesson is the same one, one level up.** finding #3 was diagnosed as *"the index publishes
what it withholds"* — so the fix was applied to the index. but the defect was never a property of the
index; it was a property of **cross-reference discipline across the whole set**, and four instances
of it sat outside the file the reviewer happened to name. **a finding names an instance; the fix is
owed to the class.** this is the third consecutive round in which a repair aimed at the reported
symptom left the actual defect live.

## the two fulcrums

per `rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last`, both were best-guessed and carried to the end rather than
raised mid-drive.

### fulcrum 1 — the `refs/*.play.ts` citations

**question:** the briefs cite reproducible-pull playbooks by path, which reveals the toolchain.

**decision: KEEP the files; the outward *citation* is the leak, not the files.** the human has
standing instruction that the playbooks are the reproducibility guarantee and are never to be
stripped. the red team answered the narrow remainder decisively: what a published reader should not
see is the **path reference**, not the artifact. so the reproducible-pulls section is a
**publication-time strip**, and the files stay in the repo untouched.

### fulcrum 2 — the four-ways taxonomy

**question:** does `hazard.unprovable-consent-record`'s "four ways a record becomes unproducible"
read as a neutral failure taxonomy, or as a document-request outline aimed at a real program?

**decision: keep the taxonomy, generalize its leaves.** each of the four is a distinct legal cause
traceable to a distinct quoted provision — that is legal terrain, not tactics. what made it read as
an outline was the **leaf enumeration** under way 1, which described capture shapes rather than legal
causes. those leaves are now generalized. ⚠️ **the red team re-flagged this at HIGH in round 2**, so
this is a best-guess held against a live objection, and it is the item most likely to warrant the
wisher's own call.

## ⚠️ publication-time transform checklist

**repo source ≠ published artifact.** several red-team findings are no repo defect at all — they are
content that must be **removed at publication**, and that must **stay** in the repo because it is the
audit trail.

| transform | why it stays in the repo |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **strip every `## .publishability` block** | the block **is** the triage tag; it is the record that the gate ran |
| **strip every `.behavior/**` and `.route/**` path reference** | they are the engagement record and must remain traceable internally |
| **strip repo rule vocabulary** (`rule.require.*`, `rule.forbid.*`) | the rules are the authoring contract; a published reader has no use for them |
| **strip citation-count arithmetic** ("11 urls / 7 distinct statutes") | it is the quota check, and the quota is an internal bar |
| **strip the reproducible-pulls sections** (`refs/*.play.ts` paths) | ⭐ **the files themselves are KEPT** — only the outward citation is stripped |
| **restate `.date researched` as a plain currency date** | the internal form records the research pass; the published form records currency |

⛔ **this checklist has not been executed.** it is the transform a publication step must run; the
repo files are deliberately left in their internal form.

## ⭐ the transferable lesson, re-tested and confirmed

> **author intuition cannot see selection bias in its own corpus.**

every file passes the four author-intuition tests individually. what a blind reader recovers comes
not from any sentence but from **which questions were asked and which were skipped** — a property of
the set, invisible from inside any one file. round 2 confirmed it in the sharpest possible form:
**the index was again the top finding**, because the round-1 fix had been *added beside* the defect
rather than *substituted for* it, and no author re-read caught that. only a blind reader did.
