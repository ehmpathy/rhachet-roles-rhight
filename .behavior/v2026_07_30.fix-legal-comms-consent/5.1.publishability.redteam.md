# P0.2 — phase-2 blind red-team

`rule.require.publishability-triage` requires a **blind red-team reconstruction** before any fullsun
set is published. this is that gate. it had been checked `[ ]` in the ledger while eight briefs sat
in `src/` and were registered in two `boot.yml` files — an open gate behind a live artifact. the r010
peer reviewer named it, correctly, as a blocker.

## method

a red-team analyst was given **exactly eight file paths and an explicit prohibition** on reads of
`.behavior/`, `.route/`, any `boot.yml`, or git history — so it worked from the published surface
alone, with no access to the engagement's internal notes. it was asked four questions:

| # | the question |
|---|--------------|
| Q1 | reconstruct the commissioner — industry, model, channels, jurisdiction, posture |
| Q2 | reconstruct the triggering event — why now? |
| Q3 | find leaked business decisions — operational choices the law does not compel |
| Q4 | find tactical disclosures — what helps an adversary more than a neutral reader |

it was instructed to be aggressive **and** honest: *"a red team that finds nothing is a red team that
did not try. but do not manufacture a leak that is not there."*

## ⭐ the verdict, and why it justified the gate

**the fullsun tag did NOT hold as applied.** the four author-intuition tests had passed on every one
of these files; the blind reconstruction found leaks anyway. **that is precisely the failure the
two-phase workflow exists to catch, and it caught it.**

### ⭐⭐ the finding that no per-file review could have produced

> "the pattern that actually gives the commissioner away is not any one sentence — **it is the shape
> of the set**. … Each brief is defensible in isolation; published together, under one index that
> orders them and names the four questions they answer, they reconstruct a composite."

⚠️ **and the artifact it named as the most revealing of the eight was `_.glossary` — which was
authored in this same round, to close a navigation gap the r010 reviewer had flagged.** the fix for
one review created the exposure the next gate caught. **same species as the r2 round, where four of
five blockers came from my own r7/r9 fixes: a remedy is a change, and a change deserves its own
review.**

the tension is real and both sides are right — r010 wanted an index because a reader lands on six
files with no map; the red team says the index is what assembles the composite. **the resolution is
to keep the index and strip the engagement commentary from it**, which is what was done: the index
now orders the briefs and states each one's bound, and no longer reproduces a question set or
attributes a framing to whoever commissioned it.

## what was fixed, and what was flagged

### ✅ fixed — the first-person and second-person voice

the red team recovered a set of first-person objections that read as a transcript of an opening
position: *"our B2B calls"*, *"we're covered"*, *"we have consent, so we're fine"*. all recast in the
third person, as propositions the text does or does not support.

⛔ **one of those — *"our B2B calls"* — I wrote today, in `define.boundary.when-consent-is-required-at-all`,
minutes after I filed a contemplation about stated recall.** it is the same defect species,
committed again, and the blind read is what caught it.

the insurer brief carried the sharper version: **second-person imperatives addressed to an insured**
(*"a search of **your own** policy…"*, *"⚠️ editions differ — **check which one you have**"*), which
the `comms/` briefs never use. all recast.

### ✅ fixed — the self-contradiction the red team called out by name

> "The brief prohibits and then performs the same act on the same page."

the insurer brief's `.publishability` block declared that *"what to ask a broker to endorse … is a
business decision and does not belong in a fullsun brief"* — and the same file then carried *"the
question to put to a broker"*, *"what should go to a broker?"*, and *"what should I ask about a
privacy-labeled product?"*. each is now a statement of what **determines** the answer rather than an
instruction on what to ask. the *"negotiation lever"* gloss is now the underlying fact (*"a negotiated
removal is attested in a reported case"*), and the *"reuse it"* pointer is now a plain cross-reference.

### ✅ fixed — the engagement commentary

`_.glossary` reproduced the commissioning question set verbatim and attributed a framing to the
party that framed it. both removed. the index now states each brief's **bound** rather than the
question it was asked.

### ⚠️ flagged, not fixed — two fulcrums

per `rule.always.defer-fulcrums-to-last`, these carry a best guess and a note rather than a halt:

1. **the `refs/*.play.ts` reproducibility paths.** the red team is right that they are dead links to
   an external reader and leak the engagement date and internal tooling. **best guess: keep them.**
   they are the reproducibility guarantee that `rule.require.bhrowser-citations` and two prior
   reviews demanded, and they point inside this repo, which is where these files live. **the rework
   is clean** — a strip pass at actual publication time costs one grep. flagged so the choice is
   deliberate rather than unnoticed.
2. **`hazard.unprovable-consent-record`'s tactical asymmetry.** the red team reads its "four ways a
   record becomes unproducible" as a document-request outline, and *"the per-number element is the
   one most often missed"* as a statement about where senders are weak rather than about the text.
   **best guess: keep it.** the failure modes ARE the legal content of an affirmative-defense
   posture — a reference that states elements and withholds how they fail is less useful and no more
   neutral. but the observation is fair and the framing could be tightened, so it is recorded for
   the wisher rather than decided unilaterally.

### ✅ credited — what the red team found genuinely clean

worth recording, because a gate that only reports failures teaches paranoia rather than method:

> "They quote rather than paraphrase, they mark inference as inference … they mark null results as
> nulls … they state the authority that cuts toward permission as plainly as the authority that cuts
> toward liability … and they decline to state rules they do not have. **Nothing in them identifies a
> client by name, industry, or transaction.** The leaks I found are leaks of **framing, emphasis, and
> process metadata** — not of facts."

## the transferable lesson

⭐ **author intuition cannot see selection bias in its own corpus.** every one of these files passed
the four intuition tests individually. what the blind reader recovered came from **which questions
were asked and which were skipped** — an artifact of the set, invisible from inside any one file, and
invisible to the author of all of them.

**that is the whole argument for phase 2, demonstrated rather than asserted.** the rule says
"intuition leaks"; this run shows the specific direction it leaks in — not toward disclosed facts,
but toward disclosed *emphasis*.
