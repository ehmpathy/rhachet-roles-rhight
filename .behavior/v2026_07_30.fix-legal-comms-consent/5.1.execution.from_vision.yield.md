- [x] **R7** ✅ ⭐ **closed — arbitration + class waivers (H9), the largest caveat on the vision's
      lead result.** three opinions read end-to-end off CourtListener
      (🔭 `refs/find.tcpa-arbitration.play.ts`, `refs/read.arbitration-opinions.play.ts`).

  the vision named class aggregation as the engine of its $20M figure and **never named the standard
  defeater**. the defeater is real. it is also **narrower than a reader would assume**, and the two
  halves have to travel together — a brief that carried only the rule would over-comfort a defendant,
  and one that carried only the limit would under-warn a plaintiff's target.

  **① the rule — class waivers are enforceable.** `AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion`, 563 U.S. 333
  (2011) (Scalia, J.), holds the FAA preempts a state rule that conditions enforceability on the
  availability of classwide procedures:

  > "class arbitration, to the extent it is manufactured by *Discover Bank* rather than consensual,
  > is inconsistent with the FAA"

  so a company whose terms carry an arbitration clause with a class waiver can, against **its own
  customers**, convert a class action into a stack of individual arbitrations — and the per-message
  multiplier that produces the $20M headline does not survive that conversion.

  **② the limit — a clause binds the people who agreed to it, and the archetypal TCPA plaintiff did
  not.** two courts of appeals **reversed** orders that compelled TCPA plaintiffs to arbitrate:

  | decision | the plaintiff | the words, verbatim |
  |----------|---------------|---------------------|
  | ⭐ `Knutson v. Sirius XM Radio Inc.`, 771 F.3d 559 (9th Cir. 2014) | got a trial subscription bundled with a car he bought from **Toyota** | *"A reasonable person in Knutson's position could not be expected to understand that purchasing a vehicle from Toyota would simultaneously bind him or her to any contract with Sirius XM, let alone one that contained an arbitration provision without any notice of such terms."* — **no mutual assent, so no agreement at all** |
  | ⭐ `A.D. v. Credit One Bank, N.A.`, 885 F.3d 1054 (7th Cir. 2018) | a **minor**, called on a phone tied to a cardholder's account; she was not the cardholder | *"A.D. is not bound by the terms of the cardholder agreement to arbitrate with Credit One, and she has not directly benefited from the cardholder agreement such that equitable principles convince us to apply the arbitration clause against her."* — direct-benefits estoppel **rejected** |

  `Credit One` also enumerates the five theories under which a nonsignatory may be bound
  (incorporation by reference · assumption · agency · veil-piercing/alter ego · estoppel) and finds
  each unavailable on those facts — which makes it the useful map of *where the argument even runs*.

  ⭐ **the corrected synthesis.** H9 was right that the $20M presumes aggregation and that arbitration
  is the standard defeater. the accurate boundary is **narrower and different** from what I first
  wrote: the clause reaches whoever **formed the contract that carries it**, and formation turns on
  objective notice at the moment of the act said to be acceptance — **not** on whether the claimant
  was ever the caller's customer. a stranger to the caller can be bound (`Dahdah`); a person who
  transacted can be unbound (`Soliman`); and *who may enforce* is a **second, independent** question
  that can decide the case on its own (`Dahdah` yes, `Credit One` no).


`helpful-errors@1.5.3` ships a `dist/index.js` concatenated three times; the **first** block — the

⭐ **the transferable point: the type gate is not the runtime.** `tsc` green on an error path proves
the *shape*, never the *existence*. an error class is only proven by a throw that actually happens —
which is why the r2 verification table ends with an executed throw and not a compile.
